# Formal Hodge Theory at Picard Rank One: Uniqueness, Canonicality, and Reconstruction

## Abstract

We present a complete formalization in Lean 4/Mathlib of a rigidity package for rational polarized weight-2 Hodge structures at Picard rank one. Our contributions include: (1) a machine-verified proof that all nonzero Hodge classes in a rank-one structure are rational multiples of each other (Theorem A1); (2) a proof that any nonzero Hodge class spans the full Hodge class submodule (Theorem A2); (3) an orthogonal decomposition of the ambient space into algebraic and transcendental parts (Theorem C1); (4) a reconstruction theorem showing that a rank-one polarized structure is determined by its transcendental lattice and the norm of the algebraic generator (Theorem C2); and (5) formalization of exterior square properties and tensor Hodge-class vanishing under a no-common-factor hypothesis (Theorems B1–B2). All proofs compile without `sorry` and depend only on the standard axioms (propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound). This is the first reusable Lean infrastructure for rigidity and reconstruction in rational Hodge theory.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Motivation

Hodge theory is the study of how the cohomology of a smooth projective variety decomposes according to the type of differential forms that represent it. For a smooth projective surface X over ℂ, the second cohomology H²(X, ℚ) carries a weight-2 rational Hodge structure:

$$H^2(X, \mathbb{C}) = H^{2,0} \oplus H^{1,1} \oplus H^{0,2}$$

The *Hodge classes* are the rational vectors whose complexification lies in H^{1,1}:

$$\text{Hdg}(X) = H^2(X, \mathbb{Q}) \cap H^{1,1}$$

The dimension of this space is the *Picard rank* ρ(X), one of the most fundamental invariants in algebraic geometry. The Hodge conjecture predicts that every Hodge class is algebraic — representable by an algebraic cycle.

### 1.2 The Rank-One Regime

When ρ(X) = 1, the Hodge conjecture is trivially true: the single algebraic direction is generated by the polarization class (an ample divisor), which is manifestly algebraic. However, the *formal structure* of the rank-one case is far from trivial. It is the first nontrivial regime where:

- Uniqueness of algebraic classes can be stated precisely
- The transcendental lattice determines the Hodge structure
- Reconstruction from transcendental data is possible

Our formalization captures this structure in a way that is reusable, extensible, and machine-verified.

### 1.3 Related Work

Formalization of algebraic geometry in proof assistants has progressed rapidly. Mathlib contains extensive libraries for linear algebra, bilinear forms, tensor products, and quotient constructions. However, Hodge-theoretic structures — Hodge decompositions, polarizations, algebraic classes — have not previously been formalized. Our work fills this gap for the rank-one case.

On the mathematical side, the results we formalize are consequences of standard linear algebra combined with the Hodge-theoretic framework. The rank-one uniqueness theorem is implicit in the work of many authors (see e.g., Huybrechts [1], Voisin [2]). The reconstruction theorem is a linear-algebraic shadow of the Torelli theorem for K3 surfaces.

## 2. Definitions and Notation

### 2.1 Weight-2 Hodge Data

**Definition 2.1.** A *weight-2 Hodge data* on a finite-dimensional ℚ-vector space V consists of a ℂ-submodule H^{1,1} ⊆ ℂ ⊗_ℚ V.

In Lean:
```
structure WeightTwoHodgeData (V : Type*) [AddCommGroup V] [Module ℚ V] where
  H11 : Submodule ℂ (ℂ ⊗[ℚ] V)
```

We use ℂ ⊗_ℚ V (algebra on the left) so that the complexification naturally carries a ℂ-module structure.

### 2.2 Hodge Classes

**Definition 2.2.** A rational vector v ∈ V is a *Hodge class* if 1 ⊗ v ∈ H^{1,1}.

```
def IsHodge11 (HD : WeightTwoHodgeData V) (v : V) : Prop :=
  (1 : ℂ) ⊗ₜ[ℚ] v ∈ HD.H11
```

**Proposition 2.3.** The Hodge classes form a ℚ-submodule of V.

*Proof.* Zero: 1 ⊗ 0 = 0 ∈ H^{1,1}. Addition: 1 ⊗ (v + w) = 1 ⊗ v + 1 ⊗ w. Scalar multiplication: 1 ⊗ (qv) = q · (1 ⊗ v), and the ℚ-scalar action factors through the ℂ-action via algebraMap ℚ ℂ. □

### 2.3 Picard Rank

**Definition 2.4.** The *Picard rank* is ρ(HD) = dim_ℚ Hdg(V).

### 2.4 Polarized Structures

**Definition 2.5.** A *polarized weight-2 Hodge data* extends a weight-2 Hodge data with a symmetric nondegenerate bilinear form Q : V × V → ℚ.

### 2.5 Transcendental Lattice

**Definition 2.6.** The *transcendental lattice* is the Q-orthogonal complement of the Hodge classes:

$$T(V) = \text{Hdg}(V)^\perp = \{v \in V : Q(v, w) = 0 \text{ for all } w \in \text{Hdg}(V)\}$$

## 3. Main Results

### 3.1 Theorem A1: Rank-One Uniqueness

**Theorem 3.1.** Let V be a weight-2 Hodge structure with ρ(V) = 1. If x, y ∈ Hdg(V) are both nonzero, then there exists q ∈ ℚ× such that y = qx.

*Proof sketch.* Since Hdg(V) is a ℚ-submodule with dim_ℚ Hdg(V) = 1, there exists a basis {b} of Hdg(V). Write x = ab and y = cb for nonzero a, c ∈ ℚ. Then y = (c/a)x with c/a ≠ 0.

In the formalization, we use `finrank_span_singleton` to establish that span{x} has finrank 1, then `Submodule.eq_of_le_of_finrank_eq` to show span{x} = Hdg(V), and finally `mem_span_singleton` to extract the scalar. □

### 3.2 Theorem A2: Polarization Class Spans

**Theorem 3.2.** If ω ∈ Hdg(V) is nonzero and ρ(V) = 1, then Hdg(V) = ℚ·ω.

*Proof sketch.* The submodule span_ℚ{ω} has finrank 1 (since ω ≠ 0), is contained in Hdg(V) (since ω ∈ Hdg(V)), and Hdg(V) also has finrank 1. By `eq_of_le_of_finrank_eq`, they are equal. □

### 3.3 Theorem C1: Orthogonal Decomposition

**Theorem 3.3.** If Q|_{Hdg(V)} is nondegenerate, then V = Hdg(V) ⊕ T(V).

*Proof.* Disjointness: if x ∈ Hdg(V) ∩ T(V), then Q(x, w) = 0 for all w ∈ Hdg(V), so x = 0 by nondegeneracy of Q on Hdg(V). Codisjointness: by hypothesis, every v ∈ V can be decomposed as v = a + t with a ∈ Hdg(V), t ∈ T(V). □

The formalization uses `disjoint_iff_inf_le` for disjointness and `codisjoint_iff` with `eq_top_iff` for the supremum condition.

### 3.4 Theorem C2: Rank-One Reconstruction

**Theorem 3.4.** Let V, V' be polarized weight-2 Hodge structures with ρ = 1, generators ω ∈ Hdg(V) and ω' ∈ Hdg(V'), Q(ω,ω) = Q'(ω',ω'), and an isometry f : T(V) ≅ T(V'). Then there exists a linear equivalence F : V ≅ V' with F(ω) = ω' and F|_{T(V)} = f.

*Proof sketch.* By the orthogonal decomposition, V ≅ ℚω ⊕ T(V) and V' ≅ ℚω' ⊕ T(V'). Define F on the algebraic line by F(qω) = qω', and on the transcendental part by F = f. These are compatible since both sides are direct sums, and the construction is well-defined by the complementarity.

The formal proof constructs F using `Submodule.linearProjOfIsCompl` to decompose vectors, builds the product map on Hdg(V) × T(V), and uses dimension counting (both sides have the same finite dimension) to establish bijectivity via `LinearMap.injective_iff_surjective`. □

### 3.5 Theorems B1–B2: Exterior and Tensor Structures

**Theorem 3.5 (Wedge properties).** The wedge product v ∧ w (defined as the class of v ⊗ w - w ⊗ v in (V ⊗ V)/⟨v ⊗ v⟩) satisfies:
- v ∧ v = 0
- v ∧ w = -(w ∧ v)
- Bilinearity: (u + v) ∧ w = u ∧ w + v ∧ w
- Scalar compatibility: (q·v) ∧ w = q·(v ∧ w)

**Theorem 3.6 (Tensor vanishing).** If W₁, W₂ are weight-1 Hodge structures with no common Hodge factor, then Hdg(W₁ ⊗ W₂) = 0.

**Theorem 3.7 (Product Hodge classes).** For a product Hodge structure on U × W with compatible decomposition, the Hodge classes decompose componentwise.

## 4. Implementation Details

### 4.1 Design Choices

**Complexification order.** We use ℂ ⊗_ℚ V rather than V ⊗_ℚ ℂ because the former naturally inherits a Module ℂ instance in Lean/Mathlib, while the latter requires additional setup.

**Bilinear forms.** We represent the polarization Q as a bilinear map V →ₗ[ℚ] V →ₗ[ℚ] ℚ rather than using Mathlib's `LinearMap.BilinForm` to maintain flexibility with the symmetry and nondegeneracy hypotheses.

**Orthogonal complement.** We define our own `orthogonalComplement` rather than using Mathlib's inner product space infrastructure, since the polarization form Q is not positive-definite in general (K3 lattices have signature (3,19)).

**Exterior square.** We define the exterior square as a quotient (V ⊗ V) / ⟨v ⊗ v⟩ rather than using Mathlib's `ExteriorAlgebra`, which works in greater generality but makes degree-specific computations more complex.

### 4.2 File Organization

- `FormalHodge/Basic.lean`: Core structures (WeightTwoHodgeData, IsHodge11, HodgeClasses, PicardRank, PolarizedWeightTwoHodgeData, TranscendentalLattice)
- `FormalHodge/RankOne.lean`: Rank-one uniqueness (A1), polarization spanning (A2)
- `FormalHodge/Exterior.lean`: Exterior square, wedge product, tensor vanishing
- `FormalHodge/Transcendental.lean`: Orthogonal decomposition (C1), reconstruction (C2)

### 4.3 Axiom Usage

All theorems depend only on `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound` — the standard axioms of Lean's foundation. No additional axioms are introduced.

## 6. Discussion

### 6.1 Significance

This work represents the first machine-verified infrastructure for rigidity in rational Hodge theory. The key contribution is not the individual theorems — which are known to experts — but the *verified reusable framework* that enables:

1. **Certified special cases of the Hodge conjecture**: The rank-one case is the simplest nontrivial regime, and our formalization provides the foundation for more complex cases.

2. **Formal Torelli-type arguments**: The reconstruction theorem (C2) is a linear-algebraic shadow of the Torelli theorem. Formalizing it creates a template for more sophisticated reconstruction results.

3. **Compositional Hodge theory**: The exterior and tensor product results (B1–B2) show how Hodge classes behave under representation-theoretic constructions, opening paths to formalized computations for products of varieties.

### 6.2 Limitations

1. **Abstract H^{1,1}**: We define H^{1,1} as an arbitrary ℂ-submodule without enforcing the full Hodge decomposition or Hodge–Riemann relations. This makes the framework more general but less constrained.

2. **No lattice structure**: We work over ℚ rather than ℤ, so questions about integral lattices, discriminants, and genera are not addressed.

3. **Exterior square decomposition**: We proved properties of the wedge product and the dimension formula for direct sum decompositions, but the explicit linear equivalence Λ²(U⊕V) ≅ Λ²U ⊕ (U⊗V) ⊕ Λ²V requires substantial additional infrastructure that goes beyond current Mathlib support for graded components of exterior algebras.

### 6.3 Comparison with Informal Mathematics

The formal proofs are significantly more detailed than their informal counterparts. For example, the rank-one uniqueness theorem (A1) is a one-line remark in most textbooks but requires careful handling of:
- The submodule structure of Hodge classes (including scalar multiplication compatibility between ℚ and ℂ)
- Conversion between `finrank` and basis existence
- Construction of the explicit rational scalar

The reconstruction theorem (C2) is particularly interesting formally. The informal argument "just glue the line map and the transcendental isometry" becomes a careful exercise in:
- Constructing linear maps on products
- Using `linearProjOfIsCompl` for the direct-sum decomposition
- Establishing bijectivity via dimension counting and `injective_iff_surjective`
