# Arithmetic Rigidity in the Three-Halves Steering Word

## Abstract

Let $m_n$ be the nearest integer to $(3/2)^n$, with the rounding convention

$$
\left(\frac32\right)^n=m_n+\varepsilon_n,
\qquad -\frac12\leq\varepsilon_n<\frac12,
$$

and define the steering word $T=(t_n)_{n\geq0}$ by $t_n=2m_{n+1}-3m_n$. We develop the finite-block arithmetic governing this symbolic sequence. First, every correction belongs to the five-letter alphabet $\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}$. Second, a length-$k$ block reconstructs its endpoint through an explicit weighted sum:

$$
2^k m_{n+k}=3^k m_n+
\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}3^{k-1-j}2^j t_{n+j}.
$$

Consequently, two equal length-$k$ factors beginning at $a$ and $b$ force the exact Diophantine relation

$$
2^k(m_{a+k}-m_{b+k})=3^k(m_a-m_b).
$$

A zero block of length $k$ forces $2^k\mid m_n$, and an infinite zero tail can begin only at a zero rounded value. We also give the universal finite-prefix factor bound $p(k)\leq5^k$, algorithms for generating the word and testing its identities, and a precise account of how the finite-block results provide the arithmetic interface needed for future anti-repetition and superlinear-complexity arguments. No asymptotic lower bound is inferred from finite computation alone.

## 1. Introduction

The real orbit generated by multiplication by $3/2$ is explicit:

$$
x_n=\left(\frac32\right)^n.
$$

Its nearest-integer shadow is less transparent. At each time $n$, choose the unique integer $m_n$ and error $\varepsilon_n$ satisfying

$$
x_n=m_n+\varepsilon_n,
\qquad -\frac12\leq\varepsilon_n<\frac12.
$$

The half-open error interval supplies a definite tie convention. The first rounded values are

$$
1,2,2,3,5,8,11,17,\ldots.
$$

The exact orbit obeys $2x_{n+1}=3x_n$. Its integer shadow generally fails to obey the same recurrence, and the integer discrepancy

$$
t_n=2m_{n+1}-3m_n
$$

measures that failure. The sequence $T=(t_n)_{n\geq0}$ is called the three-halves steering word. It begins

$$
1,-2,0,1,1,-2,1,\ldots.
$$

The word is a symbolic dynamical record of nearest-integer rounding. Its letters remain bounded while the orbit grows exponentially. This combination makes it possible to translate repeated symbolic patterns into exact equations involving powers of $2$ and $3$.

The principal objective of this paper is to establish that translation completely at the finite-block level. The central endpoint formula says that a block of steering symbols, together with its initial rounded value, recovers the rounded value at the end. If the same block occurs twice, its weighted correction cancels, leaving a rigid scaling relation between endpoint differences. Zero blocks yield a particularly clean divisibility consequence.

A motivating asymptotic question concerns factor complexity. For an infinite word $T$, let $p_T(k)$ be the number of distinct factors, or consecutive blocks, of length $k$ occurring anywhere in $T$. The anticipated behavior for the three-halves steering word is superlinear:

$$
\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{p_T(k)}{k}=+\infty.
$$

The results below identify the exact arithmetic constraint created by every repeated factor. They do not, without an additional global Diophantine anti-repetition theorem, imply this asymptotic conclusion. We therefore separate established finite-block statements from the motivating complexity conjecture.

## 2. Definitions and elementary structure

### 2.1. Nearest-integer orbit

**Definition 2.1 (Rounded three-halves orbit).** For every integer $n\geq0$, let $m_n\in\mathbb Z$ and $\varepsilon_n\in[-1/2,1/2)$ be determined by

$$
\left(\frac32\right)^n=m_n+\varepsilon_n.
$$

The integer $m_n$ is the nearest integer under the fixed half-open convention, and $\varepsilon_n$ is its signed rounding error.

**Definition 2.2 (Steering correction and steering word).** The correction at time $n$ is

$$
t_n=2m_{n+1}-3m_n.
$$

The infinite integer word $T=(t_0,t_1,t_2,\ldots)$ is the steering word. Equivalently, its letters make the rounded orbit satisfy

$$
2m_{n+1}=3m_n+t_n.
$$

The latter form shows that $t_n$ is not auxiliary notation: it is exactly the input needed to steer one rounded state to the next.

### 2.2. Factors and complexity

**Definition 2.3 (Factor).** A length-$k$ factor beginning at position $n$ is the tuple

$$
(t_n,t_{n+1},\ldots,t_{n+k-1}).
$$

The unique length-$0$ factor is the empty tuple.

**Definition 2.4 (Infinite factor complexity).** The factor complexity $p_T(k)$ is the cardinality of the set of all length-$k$ factors beginning at positions $n\geq0$.

For a finite word $u=(u_0,\ldots,u_{N-1})$ and $k\leq N$, its finite factor complexity $p_u(k)$ is the number of distinct windows $(u_i,\ldots,u_{i+k-1})$ with $0\leq i\leq N-k$.

### 2.3. Weighted block contribution

**Definition 2.5 (Block weight).** For $n,k\geq0$, define

$$
W_T(n,0)=0
$$

and, for $k\geq1$,

$$
W_T(n,k)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}3^{k-1-j}2^j t_{n+j}.
$$

The recursive form is

$$
W_T(n,k+1)=3W_T(n,k)+2^k t_{n+k}.
$$

This weight is adapted to the recurrence: older corrections acquire additional factors of $3$, while a correction inserted at the $j$th step carries the denominator-clearing factor $2^j$.

## 3. The five-symbol alphabet

**Theorem 3.1 (Five-Symbol Alphabet Theorem).** Every steering correction satisfies

$$
t_n\in\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}.
$$

**Proof sketch.** Use the exact orbit equation $2x_{n+1}=3x_n$ and substitute $x_r=m_r+\varepsilon_r$. Rearrangement gives

$$
2m_{n+1}-3m_n=3\varepsilon_n-2\varepsilon_{n+1},
$$

so

$$
t_n=3\varepsilon_n-2\varepsilon_{n+1}.
$$

From $-1/2\leq\varepsilon_n<1/2$ and the same inequalities at $n+1$, the right side is bounded tightly enough that the integer $t_n$ lies between $-2$ and $2$, inclusive. The only possibilities are the five displayed integers. $\square$

The alphabet cannot be reduced to $\{-1,0,1\}$: with the stated rounding convention, $m_1=2$ and $m_2=2$, so

$$
t_1=2m_2-3m_1=4-6=-2.
$$

The theorem is deliberately stated with all five possible endpoint symbols. The half-open bounds on the errors do not justify deleting either extreme in general.

**Corollary 3.2 (Finite factor-count ceiling).** If $u$ is a finite steering prefix of length $N$ and $0\leq k\leq N$, then

$$
p_u(k)\leq5^k.
$$

**Proof sketch.** Every length-$k$ factor is a word of length $k$ over an alphabet of cardinality at most $5$. There are exactly $5^k$ such words in the unrestricted alphabet, so the set realized by the prefix has at most this cardinality. $\square$

This is a universal alphabet bound, not a sharp description of the steering word. Arithmetic dependencies can exclude most nominally possible blocks.

## 4. Endpoint reconstruction

**Theorem 4.1 (Endpoint Reconstruction Theorem).** Let $(m_n)_{n\geq0}$ be any integer sequence and define $t_n=2m_{n+1}-3m_n$. For every $n,k\geq0$,

$$
2^k m_{n+k}=3^k m_n+W_T(n,k),
$$

where

$$
W_T(n,k)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}3^{k-1-j}2^j t_{n+j}.
$$

**Proof sketch.** Induct on $k$. For $k=0$, both sides equal $m_n$. Assume the identity at length $k$. Multiply it by $3$ and use

$$
2m_{n+k+1}=3m_{n+k}+t_{n+k}.
$$

After multiplying this one-step relation by $2^k$, one obtains

$$
2^{k+1}m_{n+k+1}
=3\bigl(2^k m_{n+k}\bigr)+2^k t_{n+k}.
$$

Substitution of the induction hypothesis gives

$$
2^{k+1}m_{n+k+1}
=3^{k+1}m_n+3W_T(n,k)+2^k t_{n+k},
$$

and the final two terms are $W_T(n,k+1)$ by its recursive definition. $\square$

For example, at lengths one, two, and three the theorem reads

$$
2m_{n+1}=3m_n+t_n,
$$

$$
4m_{n+2}=9m_n+3t_n+2t_{n+1},
$$

and

$$
8m_{n+3}=27m_n+9t_n+6t_{n+1}+4t_{n+2}.
$$

Thus each factor is naturally evaluated as a mixed power sum. The formula is exact for every integer sequence once its steering corrections are defined; nearest-integer assumptions enter only when bounding the alphabet and interpreting the sequence as an orbit shadow.

**Lemma 4.2 (Equality of weights for equal factors).** If

$$
t_{a+j}=t_{b+j}\qquad(0\leq j<k),
$$

then

$$
W_T(a,k)=W_T(b,k).
$$

**Proof sketch.** The two weighted sums have identical coefficients $3^{k-1-j}2^j$ and, by hypothesis, identical letters at each corresponding index. Equality follows term by term. Equivalently, one may induct using the recursive definition of $W_T$. $\square$

## 5. Repeated-block rigidity

**Theorem 5.1 (Repeated-Block Rigidity Theorem).** Let $(m_n)_{n\geq0}$ be any integer sequence with steering corrections $t_n=2m_{n+1}-3m_n$. Suppose the length-$k$ factors beginning at $a$ and $b$ are equal. Then

$$
2^k\bigl(m_{a+k}-m_{b+k}\bigr)
=3^k\bigl(m_a-m_b\bigr).
$$

**Proof sketch.** Apply endpoint reconstruction at $(a,k)$ and $(b,k)$:

$$
2^k m_{a+k}=3^k m_a+W_T(a,k),
$$

$$
2^k m_{b+k}=3^k m_b+W_T(b,k).
$$

Equal factors have equal weights by Lemma 4.2. Subtract the second equation from the first and cancel this common weight. $\square$

The theorem converts a combinatorial coincidence into a sparse exponential Diophantine identity. If $a\neq b$, the difference of two rounded orbit values at the right endpoint must equal $(3/2)^k$ times the corresponding difference at the left endpoint. Since all four quantities are integers, the relation imposes both size and divisibility restrictions.

One immediate reformulation is that $2^k$ divides $3^k(m_a-m_b)$. Coprimality of $2^k$ and $3^k$ therefore implies

$$
2^k\mid(m_a-m_b).
$$

Similarly, the displayed identity implies $3^k$ divides $m_{a+k}-m_{b+k}$. These consequences show why long repeated factors are arithmetically expensive: they demand high prime-power divisibility in differences that arise from two nearby approximations to powers of $3/2$.

The theorem alone does not count how often this can happen across all pairs $(a,b)$. A superlinear lower bound for $p_T(k)$ requires a global argument showing that a hypothetical abundance of repetitions would create an impossible family of such identities. The finite-block result provides exactly the equation to which such an argument must be applied.

## 6. Zero blocks and binary divisibility

**Theorem 6.1 (Zero-Block Divisibility Theorem).** Let $(m_n)$ be an integer sequence with steering word $T$. If

$$
t_n=t_{n+1}=\cdots=t_{n+k-1}=0,
$$

then

$$
2^k\mid m_n.
$$

**Proof sketch.** The weight $W_T(n,k)$ vanishes, so endpoint reconstruction reduces to

$$
2^k m_{n+k}=3^k m_n.
$$

Thus $2^k$ divides $3^k m_n$. Since $2^k$ and $3^k$ are coprime, Euclid's lemma yields $2^k\mid m_n$. $\square$

The assertion is meaningful also for $k=0$, where $2^0=1$ divides every integer. For positive $k$, every additional zero in the block requires one additional factor of $2$ in the initial rounded value.

**Theorem 6.2 (Infinite-Zero-Tail Theorem).** Let $(m_n)$ be an integer sequence with steering word $T$. If for some $n$,

$$
t_{n+j}=0\qquad\text{for every }j\geq0,
$$

then

$$
m_n=0.
$$

**Proof sketch.** For every $k\geq0$, the initial $k$ symbols of the tail are zero. Theorem 6.1 gives $2^k\mid m_n$ for every $k$. If $m_n\neq0$, choose $k$ with $2^k>|m_n|$. A nonzero integer divisible by $2^k$ must have absolute value at least $2^k$, a contradiction. Hence $m_n=0$. $\square$

**Corollary 6.3 (No permanent silence for the positive orbit).** The nearest-integer steering word of $(3/2)^n$ has no infinite zero tail.

**Proof sketch.** Every exact orbit value is at least $1$, and its nearest integer is positive. Therefore $m_n\neq0$ at every position. Apply Theorem 6.2. $\square$

The zero-block theorem provides a local obstruction, while the infinite-tail theorem turns it into a global qualitative statement. A quantitative uniform bound on finite zero runs would need further information relating the binary valuation of $m_n$ to its location $n$.

## 7. Algorithms

### 7.1. Exact generation without floating point

Direct floating-point evaluation of $(3/2)^n$ eventually risks incorrect rounding. Exact integer arithmetic avoids this. Since

$$
\left(\frac32\right)^n=\frac{3^n}{2^n},
$$

nearest-integer rounding with half-up ties is computed by

$$
m_n=\left\lfloor\frac{3^n+2^{n-1}}{2^n}\right\rfloor
$$

for $n\geq1$, with $m_0=1$. The steering symbols then follow from $t_n=2m_{n+1}-3m_n$.

For a prefix of length $N$, powers of $2$ and $3$ can be updated iteratively. Measured in arithmetic operations, generation is linear in $N$. In bit complexity, the integers have $O(N)$ bits, so the cost depends on the multiplication model; using ordinary integer arithmetic gives a polynomial-time procedure and modern big-integer multiplication improves the practical cost.

### 7.2. Block-weight evaluation

The definition

$$
W_T(n,k)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}3^{k-1-j}2^j t_{n+j}
$$

can be evaluated in one pass via

$$
W\leftarrow3W+2^j t_{n+j},
$$

while doubling the current power of $2$ at each step. This uses $O(k)$ integer arithmetic operations and $O(1)$ auxiliary big integers beyond the input.

### 7.3. Factor-complexity enumeration

Given a finite prefix $(t_0,\ldots,t_{N-1})$, insert each of its $N-k+1$ length-$k$ tuples into a set. The set cardinality is $p_u(k)$. A straightforward tuple implementation takes $O((N-k+1)k)$ symbol processing and $O(p_u(k)k)$ storage. Rolling hashes or suffix-based data structures can reduce repeated copying, but exact tuples are preferable for transparent numerical demonstrations.

### 7.4. Identity and rigidity tests

Endpoint reconstruction can be checked for every pair $(n,k)$ fitting in a computed prefix by evaluating both sides exactly. Repeated-block rigidity can be demonstrated by grouping starting positions according to their length-$k$ factors and testing every selected pair in a group. These computations illustrate the theorems; they do not replace the arguments above or establish asymptotic complexity.

## 8. Numerical illustrations

The rounded values through index $7$ are

$$
(m_n)_{n=0}^7=(1,2,2,3,5,8,11,17),
$$

which produce

$$
(t_n)_{n=0}^6=(1,-2,0,1,1,-2,1).
$$

Take the block of length $3$ beginning at $n=0$. Its weight is

$$
W_T(0,3)=3^2t_0+3\cdot2t_1+2^2t_2
=9-12+0=-3.
$$

Endpoint reconstruction predicts

$$
2^3m_3=3^3m_0-3.
$$

Indeed, both sides equal $24$.

For a zero block of length $1$ beginning at $n=2$, the theorem says $2\mid m_2$. Since $m_2=2$, the condition holds. Longer zero blocks, if found, would demand correspondingly larger powers of $2$ in their initial rounded values.

The prefix also contains repeated single-symbol factors. Whenever two equal letters are selected, Theorem 5.1 with $k=1$ gives

$$
2(m_{a+1}-m_{b+1})=3(m_a-m_b).
$$

For example, $t_0=t_3=1$, and

$$
2(m_1-m_4)=2(2-5)=-6,
$$

while

$$
3(m_0-m_3)=3(1-3)=-6.
$$

This small calculation displays the cancellation mechanism underlying the general theorem.

## 9. Applications and extensions

### 9.1. Symbolic dynamics of rounded systems

The steering construction separates a rounded expanding system into a deterministic linear recurrence plus a bounded symbolic input. Endpoint reconstruction then permits movement in both directions: arithmetic states generate letters, while a starting state and a letter block recover the terminal state. This interface is useful whenever one studies pattern recurrence, coding, or compressibility in quantized dynamics.

### 9.2. Arithmetic obstructions to repetition

Repeated factors are usually handled combinatorially. Here they can be studied through divisibility and exponential equations. The identity

$$
2^k(m_{a+k}-m_{b+k})=3^k(m_a-m_b)
$$

provides a concrete target for Diophantine anti-repetition estimates. Any theorem limiting solutions of families of such equations can be translated into a lower bound on the number of distinct factors.

### 9.3. Rational multipliers

Let $a>b\geq1$ be coprime integers, and round $(a/b)^n$ to $m_n$. Define

$$
s_n=bm_{n+1}-am_n.
$$

The same induction yields an endpoint formula with coefficients $b^k$ and $a^k$. Bounded rounding errors constrain $s_n$ to a finite integer alphabet depending only on $a$ and $b$. Equal blocks force

$$
b^k(m_{u+k}-m_{v+k})=a^k(m_u-m_v).
$$

Thus the architecture of finite alphabet, weighted reconstruction, and repetition rigidity is uniform across coprime rational multipliers. Degenerate cases, including integer multipliers or zero states, must be classified separately in any asymptotic theorem.

### 9.4. Data integrity and recurrence diagnostics

The endpoint identity is also a consistency check. Given a claimed sequence of rounded states and steering symbols, one can verify each local recurrence or verify blocks in aggregate. A corrupted symbol changes the weighted checksum by a known coefficient $3^{k-1-j}2^j$. This is not proposed as a cryptographic hash—the weights have substantial algebraic structure—but it illustrates how recurrence identities can diagnose errors in numerical orbit data.

## 10. Discussion

The principal results divide naturally into two layers. The bounded-error layer yields a five-letter alphabet. The recurrence layer, which applies to every integer sequence, yields endpoint reconstruction and repeated-block rigidity. Coprimality then sharpens zero blocks into binary divisibility statements.

Several limitations should be explicit. First, the alphabet theorem gives an inclusion, not a claim that all five symbols occur infinitely often. Second, $p_u(k)\leq5^k$ is only the unrestricted finite-alphabet ceiling. Third, examples from a finite prefix cannot establish the limiting growth of $p_T(k)$. Finally, repeated-block rigidity is a necessary condition for repetition, but converting it into a sharp global count requires additional number theory.

The value of the finite-block theory lies in its exactness. There are no asymptotic error terms in the endpoint formula and no losses in the repetition identity. The rounding errors disappear entirely when equal blocks are subtracted. This makes the theory a suitable foundation for stronger estimates: every prospective anti-stammering theorem knows precisely which Diophantine relation must be controlled.

## 11. Future work

The central open direction is to prove

$$
\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{p_T(k)}{k}=+\infty.
$$

A quantitative refinement would seek a constant $c>0$ and an unbounded range of lengths for which

$$
p_T(k)\geq ck\log k,
$$

or ideally the same bound for all sufficiently large $k$. Repeated factors create simultaneous powers-of-two versus powers-of-three constraints; a quantitative count of mutually compatible constraints may lead beyond qualitative superlinearity.

A second direction is a uniform anti-stammering theorem for coprime rational multipliers $a/b$ that are not integers. The finite-block proof uses only recurrence iteration, bounded errors, and coprimality, suggesting that the final Diophantine layer may admit a common formulation.

A third problem asks for an explicit upper bound on the length of a zero run. Theorem 6.1 gives the exact local condition $2^k\mid m_n$. Combining this with the approximation $|m_n-(3/2)^n|\leq1/2$ may constrain the possible pairs $(n,k)$.

Finally, the extreme symbols $-2$ and $2$ deserve a frequency analysis. The identity $t_n=3\varepsilon_n-2\varepsilon_{n+1}$ shows that an extreme correction forces neighboring errors toward opposite ends of the rounding interval. It is natural to ask whether both extremes have zero upper density or whether one eventually disappears.

## 12. A proof architecture for complexity growth

The finite-block results suggest a disciplined route toward asymptotic complexity. Fix a length $k$ and inspect a long prefix containing $L-k+1$ windows. If only $p_T(k)$ different factors occur, elementary counting forces some factors to recur many times. Each pair of occurrences at positions $a$ and $b$ yields

$$
2^k(m_{a+k}-m_{b+k})=3^k(m_a-m_b).
$$

A successful global argument must retain enough distinct pairs that these equations cannot all be explained by trivial coincidences. The rounded-orbit estimate

$$
\left|m_n-\left(\frac32\right)^n\right|\leq\frac12
$$

then places every integer difference close to a difference of two powers of $3/2$. Substitution transforms symbolic repetition into a family of sparse approximate exponential relations, while the exact rigidity theorem supplies the integrality and divisibility needed to remove approximation at the decisive stage.

There are three logical steps. First, a combinatorial repetition lemma should turn a hypothetical linear bound $p_T(k)\leq Ck$ into a controlled collection of repeated factors, preferably with separated starting positions and several compatible lengths. Second, the Repeated-Block Rigidity Theorem converts that collection into exact equations. Third, a Diophantine anti-concentration theorem should show that such equations cannot persist as $k$ grows. This separation clarifies which parts are elementary and which require deeper arithmetic input.

The same architecture guards against two common errors. A single repeated factor does not contradict low complexity; repetitions are inevitable in every finite-alphabet word. Conversely, an apparently rapid rise in prefix complexity does not prove an infinite limiting law. What matters is a theorem uniformly controlling the arithmetic families forced by a sustained shortage of factors.

### 12.1. Boundary cases and conventions

The half-open interval $[-1/2,1/2)$ is part of the definition, not a cosmetic choice. A different tie rule can change isolated rounded values and therefore neighboring steering symbols. The endpoint and rigidity theorems remain valid for any integer sequence because they depend only on the recurrence, but the precise orbit word and the derivation of its alphabet must use one fixed convention consistently.

Length zero also deserves mention. The empty block has weight $0$, endpoint reconstruction reduces to $m_n=m_n$, and the divisibility assertion becomes $1\mid m_n$. Including this boundary case makes recursive algorithms and inductive arguments uniform.

Finally, equal factors may begin at the same position. The rigidity theorem then reads $0=0$. Applications to anti-repetition must select distinct positions, and usually positions separated enough that the associated orbit differences carry useful size information. These qualifications do not weaken the finite-block results; they specify how those results should be deployed in a global complexity proof.

## 13. Conclusion

The three-halves steering word compresses the nearest-integer orbit of $(3/2)^n$ into five symbols, but its factors retain exact arithmetic information. A block carries the weighted correction

$$
\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}3^{k-1-j}2^j t_{n+j},
$$

which reconstructs the terminal rounded value. Equal blocks erase this correction and force a power-of-two versus power-of-three scaling identity. Zero blocks force increasing binary divisibility, and permanent zero behavior is impossible away from the zero state.

These results establish a self-contained arithmetic-to-symbolic interface. They explain why repetition in the steering word is constrained and identify the exact equations that a future superlinear-complexity proof must control.
