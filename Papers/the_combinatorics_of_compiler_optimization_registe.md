# Computational evidence: register allocation conjectures

## Small-case calculations

The finite graph selected for formal analysis has eight vertices and is the disjoint union
`K₃ ⊔ K₁,₄`. Its degree sequence is

| vertex | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| degree | 2 | 2 | 2 | 4 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |

Thus `Δ = 4`, while the triangle gives `ω = χ = 3`. The proposed expression
`max(Δ+1, ω)` is therefore `5`, not `χ = 3`.

For a two-register budget, spilling the unique maximum-degree vertex 3 leaves `K₃` and is
insufficient. Spilling vertex 0 (degree 2) destroys the triangle and leaves a bipartite graph,
so two registers suffice. These facts are proved structurally in
`Catalog/Novelty/RegisterAllocationDegreeSpill.lean`; finite degree calculations are also
kernel-checked there.

## OEIS search

No sequence is naturally generated by the chosen structural theorem, so an OEIS search is
not applicable.

## Counterexample hunt

Several standard families already contradict the proposed exact formula:

| graph | Δ | ω | χ | max(Δ+1,ω) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| path with at least 3 vertices | 2 | 2 | 2 | 3 |
| star `K₁,₄` | 4 | 2 | 2 | 5 |
| `K₃ ⊔ K₁,₄` | 4 | 3 | 3 | 5 |

The last example was chosen because it simultaneously tests the degree-spilling claim and is
chordal (it has an explicit perfect elimination ordering in the Lean development).

## Table of one-vertex spills for two registers

| spilled vertex | residual obstruction | two-colourable? |
|---:|---|:---:|
| 3 (degree 4, maximum) | triangle 0–1–2 remains | no |
| 0 (degree 2) | triangle is broken; star remains | yes |

This evidence guided the formal theorem: maximum-degree spilling is not always optimal, even
within the chordal graph class relevant to SSA interference graphs.
