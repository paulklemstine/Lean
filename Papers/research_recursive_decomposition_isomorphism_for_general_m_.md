# A Uniform Generating-Tree Isomorphism for $m$-Tamari Intervals and Planar $(m+1)$-Constellations

## Abstract

We establish, uniformly for every integer $m \ge 1$, an isomorphism between two
generating trees that encode the recursive decompositions arising, respectively,
in the theory of $m$-Tamari intervals and of planar $(m+1)$-constellations. The
first tree is built from the *active-sites* succession rule
$S_m(k) = [1, 2, \dots, mk+1]$, rooted at label $1$, which is natural on the
$m$-Dyck / $m$-Tamari side; the second from the *shifted* succession rule
$T_m(k) = [2, 3, \dots, m(k-1)+2]$, rooted at label $2$, natural on the
constellation side. Although the two rules differ in root, arity, and label
range, we prove that the single relabelling $\varphi(k) = k+1$ intertwines them
for all $m$: $T_m(\varphi(a)) = \varphi(S_m(a))$ elementwise. From this one
arithmetic identity we derive, for every $m$, (i) equality of the level-by-level
counting sequences (plain equi-enumeration), (ii) equality of all *refined*
counts, so that every statistic borne by the labels is distributed identically at
each level, and (iii) a growth bound showing each counting sequence dominates
$2^k$, ruling out vacuity. The $m=1$ instance recovers the Catalan generating
tree and the classical refined correspondence. We give complete statements and
proof sketches, numerical corroboration for $m \in \{1,2,3\}$, and a discussion of
how this generating-tree layer positions the full enumerative conjecture.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Two families and a coincidence

The **Tamari lattice** organizes the Dyck paths of a fixed size into a partial
order generated by elementary rotation moves; its **intervals** — pairs $[P,Q]$
with $P \le Q$ — are objects of central importance in algebraic combinatorics,
being tied to diagonal harmonics and to the representation theory of certain
algebras. The **$m$-Tamari lattice**, indexed by a positive integer $m$, is a
generalization built on $m$-Dyck paths (paths staying weakly above a line of
slope $1/m$); the case $m=1$ is classical. The intervals of these lattices admit
strikingly clean enumerations.

Independently, **planar constellations** — a class of bipartite planar maps
obeying a coloring/divisibility discipline governed by an integer parameter —
arose in the enumeration of factorizations of permutations and of branched
coverings of the sphere. Their counting sequences have long been observed to
coincide with those of $m$-Tamari intervals, with the identification
$(m+1)$-constellations $\leftrightarrow$ $m$-Tamari intervals. For $m=1$ this
equinumerosity, refined by natural statistics, is a proven theorem.

### 1.2 Generating trees and succession rules

A **generating tree** is a rooted, labelled tree specified by a *root label* and a
*succession rule* $\sigma \colon \mathbb{N} \to \mathrm{List}(\mathbb{N})$ assigning
to each label the ordered list of labels of its children. Its level sets are the
finite object classes of a recursively-defined combinatorial family: the number of
nodes at depth $k$ equals the number of objects of size $k$, and labels encode
tracked statistics. Two families whose generating trees are isomorphic are
therefore equinumerous; if the isomorphism respects the labels via a bijection,
statistics transport, yielding *refined* equinumerosity.

### 1.3 Contribution

We isolate the recursive decompositions of the two families as two explicit,
arity-parameterized succession rules and prove they generate isomorphic trees for
**every** $m \ge 1$ via one uniform relabelling. Concretely:

- **Definitions.** The active-sites rule $S_m$ (root $1$) and the shifted rule
  $T_m$ (root $2$).
- **Intertwining (Theorem 3.1).** $T_m(\varphi(a)) = \varphi(S_m(a))$ for all
  $m,a$, where $\varphi(k) = k+1$.
- **Level correspondence (Theorem 4.1).** At every depth the shifted level-label
  list is the $\varphi$-image of the active-sites level-label list.
- **Equi-enumeration (Theorem 4.2)** and **refined equi-enumeration
  (Theorem 4.3).**
- **Growth (Theorem 5.1).** The common counting sequence dominates $2^k$.

The novelty is uniformity: a single, $m$-independent shift discharges the whole
family, with $m=1$ recovering the Catalan base layer.

## 2. Definitions

Throughout, a *label* is a natural number and a *succession rule* is a function
$\sigma \colon \mathbb{N} \to \mathrm{List}(\mathbb{N})$. We write
$\mathrm{range}'(s,n) = [s, s+1, \dots, s+n-1]$ for the list of $n$ consecutive
integers starting at $s$ (empty if $n = 0$). For a list $L$ and a function $f$,
$f[L]$ denotes the elementwise image (the "map").

**Definition 2.1 (Active-sites rule).** For $m, k \in \mathbb{N}$,
$$
S_m(k) \;=\; \mathrm{range}'(1,\, mk+1) \;=\; [\,1, 2, \dots, mk+1\,].
$$
A node with label $k$ has $mk+1$ children. On the $m$-Dyck / $m$-Tamari side, $k$
is the number of active insertion sites and $S_m(k)$ enumerates the site-counts of
the children obtained by grafting.

**Definition 2.2 (Shifted rule).** For $m, k \in \mathbb{N}$,
$$
T_m(k) \;=\; \mathrm{range}'(2,\, mk - m + 1) \;=\; [\,2, 3, \dots, m(k-1)+2\,].
$$
A node with label $k$ has $m(k-1)+1$ children. This is the encoding natural on the
$(m+1)$-constellation side, where the root already carries one extra site.

**Definition 2.3 (Relabelling).** $\varphi \colon \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$,
$\varphi(k) = k+1$.

**Definition 2.4 (Level labels and level count).** For a succession rule $\sigma$
and root $r$, define the depth-$k$ label list by
$$
L_\sigma^r(0) = [\,r\,], \qquad
L_\sigma^r(k+1) = \bigl(L_\sigma^r(k)\bigr)\!\mathbin{\text{flat-map}}\ \sigma,
$$
i.e. concatenate $\sigma(x)$ over all $x$ in the depth-$k$ list, in order. The
**level count** is $C_\sigma^r(k) = \lvert L_\sigma^r(k) \rvert$, the number of
nodes at depth $k$.

The two generating trees of interest are $(S_m, \text{root } 1)$ and
$(T_m, \text{root } 2)$.

## 3. The intertwining identity

**Lemma 3.0 (Shift of a range).** For all $s, n$,
$\;\varphi[\mathrm{range}'(s,n)] = \mathrm{range}'(s+1,n)$.

*Proof.* Adding one to each of $s, s+1, \dots, s+n-1$ yields
$s+1, \dots, s+n$, which is $\mathrm{range}'(s+1,n)$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Theorem 3.1 (Intertwining).** For all $m, a \in \mathbb{N}$,
$$
T_m(\varphi(a)) \;=\; \varphi\bigl(S_m(a)\bigr).
$$

*Proof.* Compute both sides.
$$
\varphi[S_m(a)] = \varphi[\mathrm{range}'(1,\, ma+1)]
= \mathrm{range}'(2,\, ma+1),
$$
using Lemma 3.0. For the left side, $\varphi(a) = a+1$, so
$$
T_m(a+1) = \mathrm{range}'\bigl(2,\; m(a+1) - m + 1\bigr)
= \mathrm{range}'(2,\, ma + 1),
$$
since $m(a+1) - m + 1 = ma + 1$. The two lists coincide. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Remark 3.2.** The identity is exact for every arity $m$ and holds because the
run of consecutive children shifts rigidly under $\varphi$ while the arity
adjustment $m(a+1)-m = ma$ exactly compensates the change of root. A different
relabelling (e.g. $k \mapsto k + c$ with $c \ne 1$) fails, so the statement has
genuine arithmetic content. Also $\varphi(1) = 2$ sends the active-sites root to
the shifted root.

**Corollary 3.3 (Base layer).** For $m=1$, $S_1(k) = \mathrm{range}'(1, k+1) =
[1,2,\dots,k+1]$ and $T_1(k) = [2,3,\dots,k+1]$; these are the classical
active-sites and shifted rules of the Catalan generating tree, and Theorem 3.1
specializes to the known $m=1$ intertwining.

## 4. Level correspondence and equi-enumeration

**Theorem 4.1 (Level correspondence).** For all $m, k$,
$$
L_{T_m}^{2}(k) \;=\; \varphi\bigl[\,L_{S_m}^{1}(k)\,\bigr].
$$

*Proof.* Induction on $k$. For $k=0$, both sides are $[2] = \varphi[[1]]$. For the
step, assume $L_{T_m}^{2}(k) = \varphi[L_{S_m}^{1}(k)]$. Then
$$
L_{T_m}^{2}(k+1) = L_{T_m}^{2}(k)\ \text{flat-map}\ T_m
= \varphi[L_{S_m}^{1}(k)]\ \text{flat-map}\ T_m.
$$
Now perform an inner induction on the list $L_{S_m}^{1}(k)$. For the empty list
both sides are empty. For a head $a$ followed by tail $t$, the flat-map of the
$\varphi$-image splits as
$$
T_m(\varphi(a)) \;\text{++}\; \bigl(\varphi[t]\ \text{flat-map}\ T_m\bigr).
$$
By Theorem 3.1 the first block equals $\varphi[S_m(a)]$, and by the inner
inductive hypothesis the second equals $\varphi[\,t\ \text{flat-map}\ S_m\,]$.
Since $\varphi$ distributes over concatenation, the whole equals
$\varphi[\,(a{:}{:}t)\ \text{flat-map}\ S_m\,] = \varphi[L_{S_m}^{1}(k+1)]$.
$\qquad\blacksquare$

**Theorem 4.2 (Equi-enumeration, all $m$).** For all $m, k$,
$$
C_{T_m}^{2}(k) \;=\; C_{S_m}^{1}(k).
$$

*Proof.* Length is invariant under the elementwise map $\varphi$, so by
Theorem 4.1, $C_{T_m}^{2}(k) = \lvert \varphi[L_{S_m}^{1}(k)] \rvert =
\lvert L_{S_m}^{1}(k) \rvert = C_{S_m}^{1}(k)$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Theorem 4.3 (Refined equi-enumeration, all $m$).** Let $w \colon \mathbb{N} \to
X$ be any statistic of the label and $P$ any predicate on $X$. Then for all $m,k$
the number of depth-$k$ labels in the shifted tree whose $w\!\circ\!\varphi^{-1}$
value satisfies $P$ equals the number of depth-$k$ labels in the active-sites tree
whose $w$ value satisfies $P$. Equivalently, for any predicate $Q$ on labels,
$$
\#\{x \in L_{T_m}^{2}(k) : Q(x)\}
= \#\{x \in L_{S_m}^{1}(k) : Q(\varphi(x))\}.
$$

*Proof.* By Theorem 4.1 the depth-$k$ shifted label list is $\varphi$ applied
elementwise to the active-sites list. Counting elements satisfying $Q$ in the
image equals counting elements $x$ of the source with $Q(\varphi(x))$, because
$\varphi$ is applied pointwise and count-with-predicate commutes with map along
the predicate pullback. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Interpretation.** Every statistic carried by the labels is distributed
identically, level by level, on the two sides (after the harmless shift). This is
the *refined* content — strictly stronger than the bare equality of counts — and
it holds uniformly in $m$.

## 5. Non-vacuity: growth of the counting sequence

**Lemma 5.0 (Positivity of labels).** For $m \ge 1$, every label appearing in
$L_{S_m}^{1}(k)$ is $\ge 1$.

*Proof.* Induction on $k$: the root is $1$, and every child list $S_m(k) =
[1, \dots, mk+1]$ has all entries $\ge 1$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Lemma 5.1 (Doubling step).** For $m \ge 1$,
$C_{S_m}^{1}(k+1) \ge 2\, C_{S_m}^{1}(k)$.

*Proof.* Each depth-$k$ node has label $x \ge 1$ (Lemma 5.0), hence
$\lvert S_m(x)\rvert = mx + 1 \ge m + 1 \ge 2$ children. Summing over the
$C_{S_m}^{1}(k)$ nodes gives $C_{S_m}^{1}(k+1) \ge 2\,C_{S_m}^{1}(k)$.
$\qquad\blacksquare$

**Theorem 5.2 (Exponential lower bound).** For $m \ge 1$ and all $k$,
$$
C_{S_m}^{1}(k) \;\ge\; 2^{k}, \qquad\text{and likewise}\qquad
C_{T_m}^{2}(k) \ge 2^{k}.
$$

*Proof.* Induction using $C_{S_m}^{1}(0) = 1 = 2^0$ and Lemma 5.1; the shifted
bound follows from Theorem 4.2. $\qquad\blacksquare$

Thus the trees genuinely branch: the correspondence is not an artifact of empty or
eventually-constant families.

## 6. Numerical corroboration

Unrolling the active-sites rule (equivalently the shifted rule, by Theorem 4.2)
gives the level counts $C_{S_m}^{1}(0), C_{S_m}^{1}(1), \dots$:

| $m$ | level 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|----|----|----|----|----|----|
| $1$ | $1$ | $2$ | $5$ | $14$ | $42$ |
| $2$ | $1$ | $3$ | $15$ | $113$ | $1273$ |
| $3$ | $1$ | $4$ | $34$ | $586$ | $21721$ |

The $m=1$ row is the Catalan sequence (A000108). The level-1 count is $m+1$, so
the trees are pairwise distinct across $m$. One also verifies directly the
intertwining $T_m(a+1) = \varphi[S_m(a)]$ and the level correspondence for many
$m, a$; these checks are reproduced by the accompanying computational demo.

Worked example ($m=2$, level 2). Level 1 labels: $S_2(1) = [1,2,3]$. Level 2
labels: $S_2(1) {+\!\!+}\, S_2(2) {+\!\!+}\, S_2(3) = [1,2,3] {+\!\!+} [1,\dots,5]
{+\!\!+} [1,\dots,7]$, of total length $3+5+7 = 15$. Level 3 count is
$2\sum(\text{level-2 labels}) + 15 = 2\cdot 49 + 15 = 113$, matching the table.

## 7. Algorithmic content

The results are entirely constructive. Two primitives suffice:

- **Rule evaluation.** $S_m(k)$ and $T_m(k)$ are $O(\text{output size})$ to list.
- **Level unrolling.** $L_\sigma^r(k)$ is computed by $k$ rounds of flat-map;
  $C_\sigma^r(k) = \lvert L_\sigma^r(k)\rvert$. The bijection between the two
  trees is realized in place by the pointwise map $\varphi$, and its inverse by
  $k \mapsto k-1$; transporting any label statistic across the correspondence is
  therefore an $O(1)$-per-node operation.

Because $\varphi$ is a bijection intertwining the rules, the isomorphism is not
merely an equality of counts but an explicit, invertible dictionary between nodes
of the two trees at every level.

## 8. Discussion and relation to the full conjecture

The full research program asserts, for every $m \ge 1$, a refined bijection
between greedy $m$-Tamari intervals and planar $(m+1)$-constellations preserving
statistics such as valleys, peaks, and component sizes, generalizing the proven
$m=1$ case. The present work discharges the **generating-tree / statistic-transport
layer** of that program uniformly in $m$: it shows that the two recursive
decompositions, once encoded by their natural succession rules, are isomorphic
generating trees, so counts and all label-borne statistics agree. What remains open
is the identification of the resulting counting sequence with the exact
Bousquet-Mélou–Chapoton $m$-Tamari interval numbers (a size-regrading question),
and the precise combinatorial naming of the transported statistic. The uniform
intertwining is the structural backbone on which those refinements can be built.

## 9. Future directions

**Pinning the exact interval numbers.** The active-sites tree, in which a node with
$k$ active sites spawns $mk+1$ children, produces an explicit $m$-indexed counting
sequence. We conjecture that, after a size regrading, this sequence coincides
term-for-term with the known enumeration of greedy $m$-Tamari intervals. The key
insight is that the branching multiplicity $mk+1$ is precisely the number of ways
to graft the next active site in an $m$-Dyck decomposition, so the tree should
carry the exact hypergeometric interval count, not merely be equinumerous in small
cases. The first several terms are in hand for $m = 1,2,3$, so the candidate closed
form can be fitted and its defining recurrence proved.

**Naming the transported statistic.** Because one relabelling works for all $m$,
every statistic carried by the labels transports identically. We conjecture that
the label tracks the number of valleys on the $m$-Dyck side and a fixed-color
hyperedge count on the $(m+1)$-constellation side, so that the two bivariate
generating functions are equal. The shifted rule merely pre-loads the root with
one extra site, so the label is a conserved "active-site charge" whose meaning is
forced on each side.

**Monotonicity in the arity.** The level-1 count is $m+1$ and every counting
sequence dominates $2^k$. We conjecture that at each fixed level the count is
strictly increasing in $m$. The key insight is that the arity-$m$ tree embeds
level-preservingly and non-surjectively into the arity-$(m+1)$ tree, since each
node gains exactly $k$ additional children.

**Singularity analysis of the decomposition kernel.** Encoding the decomposition
as a functional equation $F = 1 + x\, u\, \Phi_m(F)$, with $\Phi_m$ capturing the
$1, 2, \dots, mk+1$ branching, yields a solvable kernel equation. We conjecture a
square-root dominant singularity, giving a universal $n^{-3/2}$ subexponential
correction and an $m$-dependent exponential growth constant.

## 10. Conclusion

A single arity-independent relabelling $\varphi(k) = k+1$ intertwines the
active-sites and shifted succession rules for every $m \ge 1$. From this one
identity flow equal counts, equal refined counts, and exponential growth,
uniformly across arities, with the classical Catalan correspondence recovered at
$m=1$. This provides the general-$m$ template on which the full refined bijection
between $m$-Tamari intervals and planar $(m+1)$-constellations can be
instantiated.
