# Why computational evidence was skipped

The principal claims are structural statements about relative computability, cardinality, diagonal noncomputability, and strict order embeddings. Finite calculations cannot provide meaningful evidence for noncomputability or for universal strictness of a Turing-jump hierarchy: every finite table can be reproduced by a computable function. The appropriate evidence is therefore kernel-checked deductive proof rather than numerical sampling. No OEIS sequence or informative numerical plot is naturally associated with these claims.
