# Computational Evidence Justification

Finite numerical sampling is not probative for the claims studied here. The central obstructions are diagonal non-enumerability, uncountability, and universal nonreducibility; every finite restriction of the relevant predicate spaces is enumerable and decidable, so small cases would systematically conceal rather than test the phenomenon. No numerical sequence or quantitative parameter is produced, and therefore no OEIS comparison or plot is applicable. The appropriate adversarial tests are structural counterexamples: computable oracles refute unconditional strict growth, while identity and constant operators refute any definition of a jump lacking strict nonreducibility. These boundary cases are addressed directly in the accompanying theorems.
