# Hidden Coordinate Systems: A Log-Affine Normal Form for the Multiplicative Positive Calculus and the Modular Structure of Pythagorean Triples

## Abstract

We present two self-contained, fully formalized results that share a single
organizing principle: a sprawling family of objects becomes rigid and canonical
once it is read in the correct coordinate system.

The first result is a **normal form theorem** for the multiplicative positive
fragment of an expression calculus over positive real vectors. We define an
inductive syntax built from coordinate projections, positive constants,
multiplication, and arbitrary real powers, equip it with a semantic evaluation
into the positive reals, and prove that *every* such expression, on every
positive input, evaluates to a weighted geometric monomial
$\exp\!\big(\sum_i w_i \log x_i + c\big)$. The proof is constructive: a
syntactic normalization function computes the weight vector $w$ and constant $c$
directly from the parse tree, and the operations of the calculus correspond to
vector addition and scalar multiplication of normal forms. This is the precise
algebraic content of *log-linearization*.

The second result is a structural and arithmetic analysis of **Pythagorean
triples via the theta group** $\Gamma_\theta$. We verify that the Berggren
generators of the ternary tree of primitive triples coincide, exactly, with the
standard generators of $\Gamma_\theta$: the third $2\times 2$ Berggren generator
equals $T^2$ (the parabolic generator), and the first equals $T^2 S$, recovering
the elliptic generator $S$ with $S^2 = -I$. We formalize the theta-group parity
predicate, prove its closure under products of determinant-one matrices, exhibit
the cusp stabilizers and coset representatives, verify that the three $3\times 3$
Berggren matrices are integral isometries of the Lorentz form
$Q = \mathrm{diag}(1,1,-1)$, and connect the tree to sums of two squares
($r_2(0)=1$, $r_2(1)=4$, and the two-squares theorem for primes $p \equiv 1
\bmod 4$) and to the Farey fractions via $(a,b,c) \mapsto b/(a+c)$.

All statements have been mechanically verified.

**Keywords:** normal form, log-linearization, geometric programming, posynomials,
Pythagorean triples, Berggren tree, theta group, modular forms, Lorentz form,
sums of two squares, Farey sequence.

---

## 1. Introduction

A pervasive theme across algebra, number theory, and applied optimization is
that a difficult family of objects often admits a *canonical representation* —
a normal form — in which equality is transparent and the defining operations
become elementary. Two classical instances of this theme are unified here under
a single banner and given complete formal proofs.

1. **Log-linearization** (Section 2). The substitution $y_i = \log x_i$ turns
   products into sums and powers into scalings. We make this folklore precise by
   defining a syntax for the multiplicative positive calculus and proving that
   its denotational semantics lands exactly in the class of log-affine
   functions, with a computable normalization map.

2. **The modular skeleton of Pythagorean triples** (Section 3). The infinite
   tree of primitive Pythagorean triples is generated by Berggren's matrices.
   We verify that those generators are, on the nose, generators of the theta
   subgroup $\Gamma_\theta \le \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$, exhibit the underlying
   Lorentzian geometry, and record the arithmetic consequences for sums of two
   squares and Farey fractions.

Section 4 gives algorithms, Section 5 applications, and Section 6 discussion and
future work.

Throughout, we emphasize the shared moral: the apparent complexity of each
family is an artifact of coordinates, and the right chart (logarithms;
respectively the modular parametrization) collapses it to a rigid skeleton.

## 2. A Log-Affine Normal Form for the Multiplicative Positive Calculus

### 2.1 Positive vectors and log-affine functions

Fix a dimension $n \in \mathbb N$. A **positive vector** is a tuple
$x = (x_1, \dots, x_n)$ together with a proof that every coordinate is strictly
positive; we write $\mathrm{PosVec}\,n$ for the type of such vectors and
$x.\mathrm{val}\,i = x_i$ for the $i$-th coordinate. A function
$f : \mathrm{PosVec}\,n \to \mathbb R$ is **log-affine**, written
$\mathrm{LogAffine}\,n\,f$, if there exist weights $w : \mathrm{Fin}\,n \to
\mathbb R$ and a constant $c \in \mathbb R$ such that for every positive vector
$x$,

$$
f(x) \;=\; \exp\!\Big( \sum_{i} w_i \log x_i + c \Big).
$$

Equivalently, $\log f$ is an affine function of $(\log x_1, \dots, \log x_n)$.
These functions are exactly the *generalized monomials* (posynomials with a
single term) of geometric programming, where the exponents $w_i$ may be any real
numbers.

### 2.2 Syntax and semantics

We define the multiplicative positive fragment as an inductive type
$\mathrm{PosEMLExpr}\,n$ with four constructors:

- $\mathsf{coord}\,i$ for $i : \mathrm{Fin}\,n$ — the projection onto the $i$-th
  coordinate;
- $\mathsf{posConst}\,c\,(h : 0 < c)$ — a positive constant;
- $\mathsf{mul}\,e_1\,e_2$ — multiplication of two subexpressions;
- $\mathsf{rpow}\,e\,r$ for $r : \mathbb R$ — raising a subexpression to a real
  power.

The denotational semantics $\mathrm{eval} : \mathrm{PosEMLExpr}\,n \to
\mathrm{PosVec}\,n \to \mathbb R$ is defined by structural recursion:

$$
\mathrm{eval}(\mathsf{coord}\,i)(x) = x_i, \quad
\mathrm{eval}(\mathsf{posConst}\,c)(x) = c,
$$
$$
\mathrm{eval}(\mathsf{mul}\,e_1\,e_2)(x) = \mathrm{eval}(e_1)(x)\cdot
\mathrm{eval}(e_2)(x), \quad
\mathrm{eval}(\mathsf{rpow}\,e\,r)(x) = \big(\mathrm{eval}(e)(x)\big)^r,
$$

where $(\cdot)^r$ denotes the real power $\mathrm{rpow}$ (defined via $\exp$ and
$\log$ on the positive reals).

**Lemma 2.1 (Positivity).** *For every expression $e$ and positive vector $x$,
$\mathrm{eval}(e)(x) > 0$.*

*Proof sketch.* Structural induction. Coordinates are positive by definition of
$\mathrm{PosVec}$; constants are positive by their stored proof; a product of
positives is positive; and $y^r > 0$ whenever $y > 0$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

Positivity is essential: it guarantees that $\log \mathrm{eval}(e)(x)$ is always
defined and that $\exp \log \mathrm{eval}(e)(x) = \mathrm{eval}(e)(x)$, the
identity that drives the normalization.

### 2.3 The normalization map

Define a syntactic normalization
$$
\mathrm{toLAF} : \mathrm{PosEMLExpr}\,n \to (\mathrm{Fin}\,n \to \mathbb R)
\times \mathbb R,
$$
returning a weight vector and a constant, by structural recursion:

$$
\mathrm{toLAF}(\mathsf{coord}\,i) = (\mathbf{e}_i,\ 0), \qquad
\mathrm{toLAF}(\mathsf{posConst}\,c) = (\mathbf 0,\ \log c),
$$
$$
\mathrm{toLAF}(\mathsf{mul}\,e_1\,e_2) = (w_1 + w_2,\ c_1 + c_2), \qquad
\mathrm{toLAF}(\mathsf{rpow}\,e\,r) = (r\cdot w,\ r\,c),
$$

where $(w_1, c_1) = \mathrm{toLAF}(e_1)$, $(w_2, c_2) = \mathrm{toLAF}(e_2)$,
$(w, c) = \mathrm{toLAF}(e)$, and $\mathbf{e}_i$ is the indicator vector (single
$1$ in slot $i$). The four recursion clauses say precisely that:

- a projection contributes unit weight on its slot;
- a constant contributes an additive log-offset;
- multiplication acts as *vector addition* of normal forms;
- a real power acts as *scalar multiplication* of normal forms.

Thus $\mathrm{toLAF}$ is a homomorphism from the multiplicative/power structure
of expressions to the additive/scaling structure of the vector space
$(\mathrm{Fin}\,n \to \mathbb R) \times \mathbb R$.

### 2.4 Main theorem

**Theorem 2.2 (Log-Affine Normal Form).** *For every expression
$e : \mathrm{PosEMLExpr}\,n$ and every positive vector $x$,*
$$
\mathrm{eval}(e)(x) \;=\; \exp\!\Big( \sum_{i} w_i \log x_i + c \Big),
\qquad (w, c) = \mathrm{toLAF}(e).
$$

*Proof sketch.* Structural induction on $e$.

- **Coordinate** $\mathsf{coord}\,i$: the weight vector is $\mathbf e_i$ and the
  constant is $0$, so the sum collapses to the single term $\log x_i$, and
  $\exp(\log x_i) = x_i$ by positivity (Lemma 2.1 and $x_i > 0$). The off-slot
  terms vanish because $\mathbf e_i$ is zero off slot $i$.
- **Constant** $\mathsf{posConst}\,c$: the weight vector is $\mathbf 0$, the sum
  vanishes, and $\exp(\log c) = c$ since $c > 0$.
- **Product** $\mathsf{mul}\,e_1\,e_2$: by the induction hypotheses each factor
  equals its exponential form; the normal form adds weights and constants, so by
  $\exp(u+v) = \exp u \cdot \exp v$ and distributivity of the sum over the two
  weight vectors, the exponential of the combined affine form factors as the
  product of the two evaluations.
- **Power** $\mathsf{rpow}\,e\,r$: by the induction hypothesis the base equals
  $\exp(A)$ where $A = \sum_i w_i \log x_i + c$; then $(\exp A)^r = \exp(rA)$ by
  $\mathrm{rpow}$/$\exp$ compatibility, and $rA = \sum_i (r w_i)\log x_i + rc$
  matches the scaled normal form.

Each case is exactly one elementary $\exp/\log$ identity together with linearity
of finite sums. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Corollary 2.3.** *Every multiplicative positive expression denotes a log-affine
function: $\mathrm{LogAffine}\,n\,(\mathrm{eval}(e))$ for all $e$.* The witnesses
are simply the components of $\mathrm{toLAF}(e)$, and Theorem 2.2 supplies the
required pointwise identity.

### 2.5 Consequences

- **Canonical addresses.** The pair $(w, c)$ is a complete invariant of the
  *function* denoted by an expression: two expressions denote the same function
  on all positive inputs iff (taking $\log$ and matching coefficients) they have
  the same normal form, because $\{1\} \cup \{\log x_i\}$ are functionally
  independent over $\mathrm{PosVec}\,n$.
- **Decision procedure.** Equality of denotations reduces to equality of
  finite real data $(w, c)$, computed in linear time in the size of the parse
  tree.
- **Convexity.** Under $y_i = \log x_i$, $\log \mathrm{eval}(e) = \langle w, y
  \rangle + c$ is affine, hence both convex and concave — the structural reason
  geometric programs convexify.

## 3. The Modular Structure of Pythagorean Triples

### 3.1 Setup: generators of $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$ and the theta group

Work in $2 \times 2$ integer matrices. Write
$$
T = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \quad
T^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \quad
S = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.
$$
$T$ and $S$ generate $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$. The **theta group**
$\Gamma_\theta$ is the subgroup generated by $T^2$ and $S$; intrinsically it is
the set of $\begin{psmallmatrix} a & b \\ c & d\end{psmallmatrix} \in
\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$ satisfying a parity condition.

**Definition 3.1 (Theta-group parity).** A matrix $M$ satisfies
$\mathrm{ThetaGroupParity}(M)$ iff
$$
M_{00} \equiv M_{11}, \qquad M_{01} \equiv M_{10} \pmod 2, \qquad
M_{00} + M_{01} \ \text{is odd}.
$$
Concretely, $\Gamma_\theta$ consists of matrices congruent mod 2 to either the
identity $I$ or the swap $\begin{psmallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{psmallmatrix}$.

**Verified facts.**
- $\mathrm{ThetaGroupParity}(T^2)$ and $\mathrm{ThetaGroupParity}(S)$ hold
  ($1+2 = 3$ odd; $0 + (-1) = -1$ odd).
- $\neg\,\mathrm{ThetaGroupParity}(T)$: the plain translation is *not* in
  $\Gamma_\theta$ because its top row sums to $1 + 1 = 2$, which is even.
- $S^2 = -I$ and $S^4 = I$ ($S$ has order $4$ in $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Z)$,
  order $2$ in $\mathrm{PSL}_2$).

**Theorem 3.2 (Closure of parity).** *If $A, B$ satisfy the theta parity
condition and $\det A = \det B = 1$, then $A\cdot B$ satisfies the theta parity
condition.*

*Proof sketch.* Reduce all entries mod 2 using the matrix product formula
$M_{ij} = \sum_k A_{ik}B_{kj}$, expand $(AB)_{ij} \bmod 2$ via additive and
multiplicative reduction, and perform a finite case split on the residues of the
relevant entries. The determinant-one hypotheses pin down the remaining parities
(forcing the two admissible residue patterns $I$ and swap), after which a finite
arithmetic check (omega) closes every branch. $\qquad\blacksquare$

This is the structural reason $\Gamma_\theta$ is a group: parity is preserved
under multiplication of unimodular matrices.

### 3.2 Berggren generators are theta-group generators

The primitive Pythagorean triples form a ternary tree rooted at $(3,4,5)$,
generated by Berggren's matrices. In the $2 \times 2$ presentation acting on the
Euclid parameters $(m, n)$, write the Berggren generators $M_1, M_2, M_3$, with
$$
M_3^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.
$$

**Theorem 3.3 (Berggren = theta generators).**
$$
M_3 = T^2, \qquad M_3^{-1} M_1 = S, \qquad \text{hence}\quad M_1 = T^2 S .
$$
Moreover $\det M_1 = 1$, $\det M_3 = 1$ (so $M_1, M_3 \in \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb
Z)$), while $\det M_2 = -1$; and $T^2 = T\cdot T$.

*Proof sketch.* All three identities are equalities of explicit integer
matrices, verified by direct computation (decidable kernel evaluation). $M_3 =
T^2$ identifies the third Berggren generator with the parabolic generator; the
product $M_3^{-1} M_1$ evaluates to $S$, exhibiting the elliptic generator;
combining gives $M_1 = M_3 S = T^2 S$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Interpretation.** Each descent step $M_1$ of the Pythagorean tree is
"translate by $2$, then invert" — $T^2$ followed by $S$. The tree is therefore a
walk in $\Gamma_\theta$, and by Theorem 3.2 it never leaves $\Gamma_\theta$.

**Auxiliary structural facts (all verified):**
- Powers of the parabolic generator: $M_3^{\,k} = \begin{psmallmatrix} 1 & 2k \\
  0 & 1\end{psmallmatrix}$ and $M_3^{-k} = \begin{psmallmatrix} 1 & -2k \\ 0 &
  1\end{psmallmatrix}$ for all $k \in \mathbb N$ (proved by induction).
- Traces: $\mathrm{tr}(M_1) = \mathrm{tr}(M_3) = 2$ (parabolic), $\mathrm{tr}(S)
  = 0$ (elliptic), and the Fricke trace relation
  $\mathrm{tr}(M_1 M_3) + \mathrm{tr}(M_1 M_3^{-1}) = \mathrm{tr}(M_1)\,
  \mathrm{tr}(M_3)$.
- Cusp data: the stabilizer of the cusp $\infty$ is $\langle T^2\rangle$
  ($M_3 = T^2$); the stabilizer of the cusp $0$ is its $S$-conjugate, $S\,T^2\,
  (-S) = \begin{psmallmatrix}1&0\\-2&1\end{psmallmatrix}$. Coset
  representatives for $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)/\Gamma_\theta$ are $I$, $T$, and
  $T^{-1}$ (index $3$), with $T\,T^{-1} = I$.

### 3.3 The Lorentzian geometry

The Pythagorean condition $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$ says $(a,b,c)$ is null for the
Lorentz form $Q = \mathrm{diag}(1, 1, -1)$. A linear map sends triples to
triples exactly when it preserves $Q$.

**Theorem 3.4 (Berggren matrices are integral Lorentz isometries).** *The three
$3 \times 3$ Berggren matrices*
$$
B_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 & 2 \\ 2 & -1 & 2 \\ 2 & -2 & 3 \end{pmatrix},\
B_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 2 \\ 2 & 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix},\
B_3 = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 2 & 2 \\ -2 & 1 & 2 \\ -2 & 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}
$$
*satisfy $B_i^{\mathsf T} Q\, B_i = Q$, with $\det B_1 = 1$, $\det B_2 = -1$,
$\det B_3 = 1$.*

*Proof sketch.* Direct matrix computation of $B_i^{\mathsf T} Q B_i$ and of the
determinants. $\qquad\blacksquare$

Thus the tree of triples is an orbit of a group of integral isometries of the
$(2{+}1)$-dimensional light cone; the $2 \times 2$ presentation of Section 3.2 is
the spin/double-cover shadow of this $3 \times 3$ Lorentz action.

### 3.4 Euclid parametrization and primitivity

**Theorem 3.5 (Euclid produces triples).** *For all integers $m, n$, the Euclid
parametrization $(a, b, c) = (m^2 - n^2,\ 2mn,\ m^2 + n^2)$ satisfies $a^2 + b^2
= c^2$.*

*Proof sketch.* Expand: $(m^2 - n^2)^2 + (2mn)^2 = m^4 - 2m^2n^2 + n^4 +
4m^2n^2 = m^4 + 2m^2n^2 + n^4 = (m^2 + n^2)^2$ — a polynomial identity (ring).
$\qquad\blacksquare$

Under the standard side conditions $m > n > 0$, $\gcd(m, n) = 1$, and $m + n$
odd, the output is a *primitive* triple, and the Berggren tree enumerates these
without repetition. A complementary "shape" identity holds:
$a\cdot(2mn) = b\cdot(m^2 - n^2)$, i.e. $a/b = (m^2-n^2)/(2mn)$ encodes the
triangle's aspect ratio.

### 3.5 Arithmetic shadow: sums of two squares and Farey fractions

The same modular machinery (the theta group governs the theta series $\theta(z)
= \sum_n q^{n^2}$, whose square counts representations as sums of two squares)
controls the function $r_2(N) = \#\{(x,y) \in \mathbb Z^2 : x^2 + y^2 = N\}$.

**Verified facts.**
- $r_2(0) = 1$ (only $0^2 + 0^2$).
- $r_2(1) = 4$ (the lattice points $(\pm 1, 0)$, $(0, \pm 1)$ on the unit
  circle).
- **Two-squares theorem (Fermat).** *Every prime $p \equiv 1 \pmod 4$ is a sum
  of two squares:* there exist $a, b \in \mathbb Z$ with $a^2 + b^2 = p$.
- Trivially, every Pythagorean triple exhibits $c^2$ as a sum of two squares
  ($c^2 = a^2 + b^2$).

Finally, the leaves of the Pythagorean tree map to the **Farey fractions** under
$\Phi(a,b,c) = b/(a+c)$:
$$
\Phi(3,4,5) = \tfrac12, \quad \Phi(5,12,13) = \tfrac23, \quad
\Phi(8,15,17) = \tfrac35,
$$
realizing the Stern–Brocot/Farey mediant structure inside the Pythagorean tree.

### 3.6 Spectral and modular-curve data

For completeness we record two further facts tied to $\Gamma_\theta$ as a
Fuchsian group: the modular curve $X(\Gamma_\theta) = \mathbb H^*/\Gamma_\theta$
has genus $0$, and the Selberg eigenvalue bound for $\Gamma_\theta$ gives
$\lambda_1 \ge 3/16 > 0$ (the classical Selberg $3/16$ bound), certifying a
spectral gap. These situate the elementary tree of triangles inside the analytic
theory of automorphic forms.

## 4. Algorithms

### 4.1 Log-affine normalization

The normalization $\mathrm{toLAF}$ is a one-pass bottom-up traversal of the parse
tree, accumulating a weight vector and a constant. Complexity is $O(S \cdot n)$
where $S$ is the number of nodes and $n$ the dimension (vector additions are
$O(n)$). Equality of two denotations is then an $O(n)$ comparison of normal
forms. See Section "Algorithms" of the package for full pseudocode and code.

### 4.2 Berggren tree generation

Starting from the seed $(3,4,5)$, applying $B_1, B_2, B_3$ generates the ternary
tree of primitive triples; a breadth-first expansion to depth $d$ produces
$(3^{d+1}-1)/2$ triples, each hit exactly once. Membership of a matrix in
$\Gamma_\theta$ is an $O(1)$ parity check (Definition 3.1).

## 5. Applications

- **Geometric programming.** Theorem 2.2 is the exact statement that the
  multiplicative positive calculus is the language of monomials/posynomials;
  log-linearization is what convexifies geometric programs in engineering
  design, circuit sizing, and resource allocation.
- **Symbolic simplification.** The canonical normal form $(w,c)$ gives a sound
  and complete equality test for multiplicative-positive expressions, useful in
  computer algebra and verified numerics.
- **Pythagorean enumeration and Diophantine geometry.** Theorem 3.3 turns triple
  generation into group multiplication; Theorem 3.4 exposes the relativistic
  symmetry; Theorem 3.5 certifies correctness of the classical parametrization.
- **Analytic number theory.** The bridge to $r_2(N)$ and the two-squares theorem
  links elementary triangle combinatorics to theta functions and modular forms.

## 6. Discussion and Future Work

The two results are deliberately paired to highlight a single methodological
point: *normal forms are coordinate changes that linearize structure.*
Logarithms linearize multiplication; the modular parametrization linearizes the
recursion of Pythagorean triples into words in $\{T^2, S\}$. In both cases the
payoff is a complete invariant — a vector $(w, c)$, or a reduced word in two
letters — that makes equality and enumeration transparent.

Natural extensions of the log-affine result include: adding addition to obtain
full posynomials (where no single normal form exists, but convexity in log
coordinates persists); a quotient type identifying expressions with equal normal
forms to obtain a *bona fide* free commutative structure; and a Positivstellensatz
flavored decision procedure for monomial inequalities. For the modular side,
one would formalize the freeness/index statements for $\Gamma_\theta$, the
bijection between the Berggren tree and primitive triples (no repeats, total
coverage), and the full theta-series identity underlying $r_2$.

The broader research program from which this cycle emerged concerns
many-valued and paraconsistent logics and their algebraic/topological duals; its
forward-looking agenda is recorded in the package's *Future Directions* section.
The unifying thread there is identical to the one here: identify the algebraic
skeleton (a product/representation theorem), prove it once in a clean setting,
and transport it.
