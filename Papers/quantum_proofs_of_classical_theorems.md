# Computational Evidence Justification

Computational sampling is unnecessary for the completed results. The basis-state embedding and verifier lifting are symbolic constructions valid for arbitrary proof and input types. The finite compression obstruction is a cardinality theorem whose hypotheses already state the complete relevant finite data. The polynomial lower-bound transfer is an exact inequality derived from an assumed extractor bound. None of these claims predicts a numerical sequence or depends on behavior that small-case experiments could meaningfully distinguish.

The unresolved separation questions are deliberately stated as future conjectures rather than supported by numerical evidence: testing small verifier instances would not establish asymptotic proof-size advantage, and no canonical benchmark family or encoding was specified from which an honest table could be produced.
