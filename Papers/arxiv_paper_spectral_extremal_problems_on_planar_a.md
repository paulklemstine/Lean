# Computational Evidence Justification

The results developed here are universal symbolic inequalities for arbitrary finite matrices and finite simple graphs. Their claims do not depend on a numerical threshold, a finite exceptional range, or an experimentally inferred sequence. Small-case enumeration would therefore test only instances of identities already governed by exact Cauchy–Schwarz and degree-sum arguments, without informing the general proof.

Computational evidence for the paper's exact `C_{k,l}` extremal classifications would require a faithful executable encoding of planarity, outerplanarity, subgraph containment, and spectral optimization over graph isomorphism classes. That infrastructure is not part of this research cycle. Rather than report ad hoc floating-point searches as evidence for exact extremality, this cycle concentrates on the exact analytic bridge that any such classification uses.
