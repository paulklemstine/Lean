## Abstract

We study the **EML** (Exponential–Multiplicative–Logarithmic) function class — the closure of
the elementary algebraic operations together with the transcendental primitives $\exp$ and
$\log$ — as a vehicle for universal approximation, and we establish two complementary results.
First, a *qualitative* density theorem: on the unit cube $[0,1]^n$, the subalgebra generated by
the $n$ coordinate exponential features $\{x \mapsto e^{x_i}\}_{i=1}^n$ is uniformly dense in
the space $C([0,1]^n, \mathbb{R})$ of real continuous functions. Equivalently, every continuous
function on the cube is the uniform limit of exponential polynomials in the coordinates, and
for every error budget $\varepsilon > 0$ an explicit $\varepsilon$-approximant exists. The proof
is a family-level application of the Stone–Weierstrass theorem, in which point separation is
provided by the injectivity of a *single* nonlinearity, $\exp$, while the dimension forces the
*width* (one feature per coordinate). On the cube we further record the quantitative
shallow-feature range bound $1 \le e^{x_i} \le e$, the explicit constant that an abstract
density theorem on a general compact set cannot express. Second, a *quantitative* depth result:
the monomial $x \mapsto x^n$ admits a naive multiplication-only EML representation of depth $n$
and an exp/log representation $\exp(n \ln x)$ of constant depth $3$, both exact on $(0,\infty)$;
for all $n \ge 4$ the exp/log form is strictly shallower, and the depth gap $n-3$ grows without
bound. This is a precise separation showing that $\exp$ and $\log$ compress unbounded
multiplicative depth to a constant. All results have been formally verified.

**Keywords:** universal approximation, Stone–Weierstrass theorem, exponential polynomial,
coordinate exponential features, unit cube, density, depth compression, monomial, EML class.

## 1. Introduction

Universal approximation theorems are the theoretical foundation of function-approximation
methods across machine learning, numerical analysis, and surrogate modeling. The canonical
statement asserts that some structured family of simple functions is **dense** in a target
space of continuous functions: any continuous target can be approximated to arbitrary uniform
accuracy by a member of the family. Classical instances include polynomials (Weierstrass),
shallow neural networks with non-polynomial activations (Cybenko, Hornik, Leshno et al.), and
abstractly, any point-separating subalgebra (Stone–Weierstrass).

This paper concerns the **EML class**: functions built from the field operations of
$\mathbb{R}$ together with the two transcendental primitives $\exp$ and $\log$. Two questions
organize the study.

1. **Expressivity (density).** *Which* functions does an EML class reach? We answer this on the
   canonical domain of approximation theory, the unit cube $[0,1]^n$, for the specific and
   minimal feature set consisting of the coordinate exponentials $x \mapsto e^{x_i}$.

2. **Cost (depth).** *How expensive* is an exact EML representation, measured in compositional
   depth? We answer this for the simplest nontrivial target family, the monomials $x^n$, and
   exhibit an unbounded separation between multiplication-only depth and exp/log depth.

The two answers are complementary. Density is a soft, qualitative property: it follows from
point separation alone and is therefore indifferent to the geometry of the (compact) domain.
Depth is a hard, quantitative property: it depends on the multiplicative complexity of the
target, and it is exactly here that $\exp$ and $\log$ become indispensable rather than
incidental. Throughout, we treat $\exp$ as the source of nonlinearity and the pair
$(\exp, \log)$ as a depth-reducing lever.

## 2. Preliminaries and definitions

### 2.1 The unit cube

**Definition 2.1 (unit cube).** For $n \in \mathbb{N}$ the *unit cube* is
$$[0,1]^n \;=\; \{\, x \in \mathbb{R}^n : 0 \le x_i \le 1 \text{ for all } i \,\}
\;=\; \prod_{i=1}^n [0,1].$$
Formally we model $\mathbb{R}^n$ as the function space $\mathrm{Fin}\,n \to \mathbb{R}$ and set
$[0,1]^n = \{x : \forall i,\ x_i \in [0,1]\}$, an indexed product of the closed interval.

**Proposition 2.2 (compactness).** *The unit cube $[0,1]^n$ is compact.*

*Proof sketch.* The closed interval $[0,1]$ is compact (Heine–Borel). An arbitrary product of
compact spaces is compact (Tychonoff); for a finite product this is elementary. Hence
$\prod_i [0,1]$ is compact. As a subtype, $[0,1]^n$ therefore carries the structure of a
compact topological space. $\qquad\blacksquare$

Compactness is essential: uniform approximation of continuous functions, and the
Stone–Weierstrass theorem itself, require a compact domain.

### 2.2 The space of continuous functions

We write $C(K, \mathbb{R})$ for the $\mathbb{R}$-algebra of continuous real-valued functions on
a compact set $K$, equipped with the uniform (supremum) norm
$\|f\| = \sup_{x \in K} |f(x)|$. Convergence in this norm is uniform convergence. The algebra
operations are pointwise addition, multiplication, and scalar multiplication.

### 2.3 Coordinate exponential features and the EML subalgebra

**Definition 2.3 (coordinate feature).** For $K \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ compact and an index
$i$, the *$i$-th coordinate feature* is the continuous map
$$\mathrm{coord}_i : K \to \mathbb{R}, \qquad \mathrm{coord}_i(x) = x_i.$$

**Definition 2.4 (coordinate exponential family).** The *coordinate exponential family* is
$$\mathcal{E}_K \;=\; \{\, \exp \circ \mathrm{coord}_i : i = 1,\ldots,n \,\}
\;=\; \{\, x \mapsto e^{x_i} : i = 1,\ldots,n \,\} \;\subseteq\; C(K, \mathbb{R}).$$

**Definition 2.5 (EML subalgebra).** The *coordinate-exponential EML subalgebra* is
$\mathrm{Adj}(\mathcal{E}_K) := \mathrm{Algebra.adjoin}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{E}_K)$, the
smallest unital $\mathbb{R}$-subalgebra of $C(K,\mathbb{R})$ containing $\mathcal{E}_K$.
Because the $e^{x_i}$ commute and $e^{a}e^{b} = e^{a+b}$, every element of
$\mathrm{Adj}(\mathcal{E}_K)$ is an *exponential polynomial in the coordinates*, i.e. a finite
sum
$$p(x) = \sum_{k \in S} c_k \, e^{k_1 x_1 + \cdots + k_n x_n}, \qquad
S \subseteq \mathbb{N}^n \text{ finite},\ c_k \in \mathbb{R}.$$
Such a $p$ is the single-hidden-layer "exponential network" with integer weights on the
coordinate exponentials.

### 2.4 EML terms and depth

For the depth analysis we work with a syntactic representation of EML expressions. An **EML
term** is a finite expression tree built from a variable $x$, real constants, and the operations
of multiplication, $\exp$, and $\log$. Two functions are attached to each term $t$:

- its **evaluation** $\mathrm{eval}(t) : (0,\infty) \to \mathbb{R}$ (or $\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$
  where defined), obtained by interpreting the operations in the usual way; and
- its **depth** $\mathrm{depth}(t) \in \mathbb{N}$, the height of the expression tree, i.e. the
  maximum number of nested operations along any root-to-leaf path.

Depth is the syntactic proxy for the number of layers in a composition: a term of depth $d$ is
a composition of at most $d$ primitive operations. We define two canonical representations of
the degree-$n$ monomial.

**Definition 2.6 (naive monomial term).** The *naive* monomial term $\mathrm{monoNaive}(n)$ is
the right-nested product of $n$ copies of the variable,
$$\mathrm{monoNaive}(n) \;=\; \underbrace{x \cdot (x \cdot (\cdots \cdot x))}_{n \text{ copies}}.$$

**Definition 2.7 (exp/log monomial term).** The *exp/log* monomial term
$\mathrm{monoExpLog}(n)$ is
$$\mathrm{monoExpLog}(n) \;=\; \exp\!\big(n \cdot \log x\big),$$
i.e. the composition of $\log$, scalar multiplication by the constant $n$, and $\exp$.

## 3. Main results: density on the cube

### 3.1 Point separation

**Lemma 3.1 (separation).** *The coordinate exponential family $\mathcal{E}_K$ separates the
points of any $K \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$.*

*Proof.* Let $x \ne y$ in $K$. Since they are distinct as vectors, there is an index $i$ with
$x_i \ne y_i$ (a function is determined by its values, so unequal functions differ at some
argument). The exponential function is strictly monotone, hence injective, so $x_i \ne y_i$
implies $e^{x_i} \ne e^{y_i}$. The feature $\exp \circ \mathrm{coord}_i \in \mathcal{E}_K$
therefore assigns different values to $x$ and $y$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

The single injective nonlinearity $\exp$ is the entire engine of separation; the role of having
$n$ features is only to ensure that *whichever* coordinate distinguishes $x$ from $y$ is
covered.

### 3.2 Density

**Theorem 3.2 (EML density on the unit cube — `eml_dense_unitCube`).**
*The topological closure of the coordinate-exponential EML subalgebra on the unit cube is the
whole continuous-function algebra:*
$$\overline{\mathrm{Algebra.adjoin}_{\mathbb{R}}\big(\mathcal{E}_{[0,1]^n}\big)}
\;=\; C\big([0,1]^n, \mathbb{R}\big).$$

*Proof sketch.* Apply the **Stone–Weierstrass theorem** in its subalgebra form. The cube is
compact (Proposition 2.2), so $C([0,1]^n,\mathbb{R})$ is the setting in which the theorem
applies. The subalgebra $\mathrm{Adj}(\mathcal{E}_{[0,1]^n})$ is unital (it contains the
constants by construction) and, by Lemma 3.1, the generating family separates points; a
subalgebra generated by a point-separating family is itself point-separating. Stone–Weierstrass
then gives that its uniform closure is all of $C([0,1]^n,\mathbb{R})$. The result is obtained
by instantiating the general compact-domain density theorem at $K = [0,1]^n$, the only added
ingredient being the compactness of the cube. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Theorem 3.3 ($\varepsilon$-approximation — `eml_approx_unitCube`).**
*For every $f \in C([0,1]^n, \mathbb{R})$ and every $\varepsilon > 0$ there exists an
exponential polynomial $p \in \mathrm{Algebra.adjoin}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{E}_{[0,1]^n})$ with*
$$\|p - f\| \;=\; \sup_{x \in [0,1]^n} |p(x) - f(x)| \;<\; \varepsilon.$$

*Proof sketch.* This is the operational restatement of Theorem 3.2. Membership of $f$ in the
closure of the subalgebra is, by definition of the topology induced by the uniform norm,
exactly the existence of a subalgebra element within any prescribed uniform distance
$\varepsilon$ of $f$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

### 3.3 Width necessity (sharpness)

The family in Theorem 3.2 cannot be shrunk to a single feature once $n \ge 2$. A single *ridge*
feature has the form $x \mapsto g(w_1 x_1 + \cdots + w_n x_n)$ for fixed weights $w$, and its
separating power is limited by the linear functional $x \mapsto \sum_i w_i x_i$. A linear map
$\mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ cannot be injective for $n \ge 2$, since injectivity would force
$n = \dim \mathbb{R}^n \le \dim \mathbb{R} = 1$. Thus no single ridge feature separates points
in dimension two or higher, and at least $n$ coordinate features are genuinely required. This is
the precise sense in which *width is forced by dimension* while *nonlinearity is supplied by a
single $\exp$*.

### 3.4 Quantitative shallow-feature bounds

**Theorem 3.4 (shallow-feature range bound — `coordExp_bounds_unitCube`).**
*For every $x \in [0,1]^n$ and every coordinate $i$,*
$$1 \;\le\; e^{x_i} \;\le\; e.$$

*Proof.* From $x \in [0,1]^n$ we have $0 \le x_i \le 1$. Monotonicity of $\exp$ gives
$e^0 \le e^{x_i} \le e^1$, and $e^0 = 1$, $e^1 = e$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

This bound is the quantitative datum that the abstract, domain-agnostic density theorem cannot
express. It confines each single-layer feature to the explicit interval $[1, e]$ and thereby
controls the modulus of continuity of the shallow layer — the starting point for any explicit
approximation-rate or width estimate (cf. Conjecture 2 in Section 7).

## 4. Main results: depth compression of monomials

We now turn from *what* can be represented to *how cheaply*. Fix the positive reals
$(0, \infty)$ as the domain (so that $\log$ is defined and $x^n = e^{n \ln x}$).

### 4.1 The two representations

**Proposition 4.1 (naive monomial — `monoNaive_eval`, `monoNaive_depth`).**
*For all $x > 0$, $\ \mathrm{eval}(\mathrm{monoNaive}(n))(x) = x^n$, and
$\ \mathrm{depth}(\mathrm{monoNaive}(n)) = n$.*

*Proof sketch.* Evaluation: by induction on $n$, the right-nested product of $n$ copies of $x$
multiplies out to $x^n$. Depth: each of the $n$ leaves $x$ sits under a multiplication node, and
the right-nested tree of $n$ factors has height $n$; an induction on $n$ confirms
$\mathrm{depth} = n$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Proposition 4.2 (exp/log monomial — `monoExpLog_eval`, `monoExpLog_depth`).**
*For all $x > 0$, $\ \mathrm{eval}(\mathrm{monoExpLog}(n))(x) = x^n$, and
$\ \mathrm{depth}(\mathrm{monoExpLog}(n)) = 3$ for every $n$.*

*Proof sketch.* Evaluation: for $x > 0$, $\ \exp(n \ln x) = \exp(\ln(x^n)) = x^n$ by the
defining property $\exp \circ \ln = \mathrm{id}$ on $(0,\infty)$ and the power law
$\ln(x^n) = n \ln x$. Depth: the term is $\exp(\,(\text{const } n) \cdot \log x\,)$, a fixed
three-level tree ($\log$, then multiply-by-constant, then $\exp$) whose height does not depend
on $n$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

### 4.2 The compression theorem

**Theorem 4.3 (depth compression — `eml_depth_compression`).**
*For every degree $n \ge 4$:*
1. *(equal functions) for all $x > 0$,*
   $\ \mathrm{eval}(\mathrm{monoNaive}(n))(x) = \mathrm{eval}(\mathrm{monoExpLog}(n))(x) = x^n$;
2. *(strict depth gap)*
   $\ \mathrm{depth}(\mathrm{monoExpLog}(n)) = 3 < n = \mathrm{depth}(\mathrm{monoNaive}(n)).$

*Proof.* Part (1) combines Propositions 4.1 and 4.2: both evaluate to $x^n$ on $(0,\infty)$.
Part (2) substitutes the depth values $3$ and $n$ and uses $n \ge 4 > 3$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Theorem 4.4 (unbounded depth gap — `eml_depth_unbounded_gap`).**
*The depth gap between the two representations is*
$\ \mathrm{depth}(\mathrm{monoNaive}(n)) - \mathrm{depth}(\mathrm{monoExpLog}(n)) = n - 3,$
*and it tends to infinity with the degree:*
$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \big(n - 3\big) = \infty.$$

*Proof.* The gap is $n - 3$ by Propositions 4.1 and 4.2. As $n \to \infty$ the affine function
$n \mapsto n - 3$ diverges. $\qquad\blacksquare$

### 4.3 Interpretation

A *fixed, depth-$3$* EML circuit represents monomials of *arbitrarily high* degree exactly,
whereas the multiplication-only construction grows linearly in depth. The lever is the algebraic
identity $x^n = e^{n \ln x}$: it converts $n - 1$ nested multiplications into a single scalar
multiplication wrapped between one $\log$ and one $\exp$. This is the formal content of the
claim that *representation cost depends on depth*, and the structural justification for treating
$\exp$ and $\log$ as primitive EML operations: removing them would forfeit constant-depth
representation of high-degree behavior.

## 5. Algorithms

We summarize the constructive procedures implied by the results. Pseudocode and reference
implementations are provided in the companion `demo.py` and in `PACKAGE.json`.

### 5.1 Exponential-polynomial approximation on the cube

Given samples of a continuous target $f$ on a grid over $[0,1]^n$, build the design matrix of
exponential-monomial features $\Phi_{x,k} = e^{\langle k, x\rangle} = \prod_i e^{k_i x_i}$ for a
chosen finite multi-index set $S$, and solve the linear least-squares problem
$\min_c \|\Phi c - f\|_2$. By Theorem 3.2 the uniform error decreases to $0$ as $S$ is enriched.
The feature columns are numerically benign because Theorem 3.4 confines each base feature to
$[1, e]$.

### 5.2 Depth-aware monomial evaluation

To evaluate $x^n$, either fold $n$ multiplications (depth $n$) or compute $\exp(n \log x)$
(depth $3$). Theorem 4.3 guarantees both return $x^n$ on $(0,\infty)$; the second is the
constant-depth route.

## 6. Applications

- **Universal approximation with explicit primitives.** Theorem 3.2 furnishes a closed-form,
  fully explicit universal family on the standard ML domain $[0,1]^n$, an alternative to opaque
  activation networks in which every approximant is an exponential polynomial that can be
  written down and differentiated symbolically.
- **Conditioning and feature scaling.** Theorem 3.4 ($e^{x_i} \in [1,e]$) gives an a priori
  bound on feature magnitudes, useful for numerical conditioning and for normalizing
  exponential-feature regressors without data-dependent statistics.
- **Architecture economy.** Theorem 4.4 is a clean, provable instance of the depth/nonlinearity
  tradeoff that motivates deep architectures: the right transcendental primitives buy unbounded
  depth savings on structured targets. Power and root computations on real hardware already
  exploit exactly this identity ($a^b = e^{b\ln a}$).

## 7. Discussion and future directions

The cycle establishes two complementary facts: **density is qualitative and domain-agnostic
among compacta**, while **depth is governed by the multiplicative complexity of the target and
is collapsed by $\exp$/$\log$**. The following conjectures push on the seam between them.

**Conjecture 1 (depth lower bound for multiplication-only terms).** Every EML term in the
$\mathrm{mul}$-only fragment (no $\exp$/$\log$) that evaluates to $x^n$ on $(0,\infty)$ has
depth $\ge \lceil \log_2 n \rceil$, and the balanced binary product attains $\lceil \log_2 n
\rceil$. Hence the true multiplication-only minimal depth is $\Theta(\log n)$ — still unbounded,
so the exp/log compression to constant depth remains genuine. The key insight is that
multiplication is associative, so the minimal depth to combine $n$ copies of $x$ is the height
of an optimal binary tree on $n$ leaves, $\lceil\log_2 n\rceil$; exp/log beats even this optimum
by turning the product into one scalar multiply inside a single $\exp$.

**Conjecture 2 (quantitative depth/width law on the cube).** On $[0,1]^n$, approximating an
$L$-Lipschitz target to uniform error $\varepsilon$ with a coordinate-exponential EML network
requires width $\Theta(\varepsilon^{-n})$ at constant depth, but width $\Theta(\varepsilon^{-1})$
per coordinate if depth is allowed to grow like $\log(1/\varepsilon)$, with an explicit
depth–width exchange constant. The key insight is that the explicit feature range $[1,e]$ of
Theorem 3.4 pins the modulus of continuity of each shallow layer, turning the qualitative
density of Theorem 3.2 into a quantitative width count via a Jackson-type estimate.

**Conjecture 3 ($\exp$/$\log$ necessary for constant-depth high-degree representation).** No
EML term of depth $< 3$ represents $x^n$ on $(0,\infty)$ for $n \ge 2$, and depth $3$ is
achievable *only* by using at least one $\exp$ and one $\log$. Thus constant-depth
representation of all monomials forces the presence of both transcendental primitives. The key
insight is that depth-$\le 2$ terms are polynomials of bounded structure whose attainable
monomial degree is capped, so no fixed sub-cubic depth suffices for all $n$.

---

## 8. Conclusion

We have given two short, complete, and complementary results about the EML class. On the unit
cube, a single nonlinearity replicated across coordinates yields a universally dense
algebra — density flowing entirely from the injectivity of $\exp$ via Stone–Weierstrass — and
the cube supplies the explicit shallow range $[1,e]$. For the monomial family, $\exp$ and $\log$
compress representation depth from $n$ to a constant $3$ with an unbounded gap. Together they
delineate the two faces of expressive power: a soft qualitative reach and a sharp quantitative
cost, with the exponential and logarithm cast simultaneously as the universal builder and the
great compressor.
