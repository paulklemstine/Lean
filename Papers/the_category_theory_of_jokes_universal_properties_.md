# Computational Evidence — The Category Theory of Jokes (v19c thread)

All numbers below were produced by exact rational (`ℚ`) computation inside Lean 4
(`#eval`), before the corresponding theorems were formalised. Floating-point figures
are `Float` renderings of exact rationals. Nothing here is a *proof*; every claim that
survived is proved separately and sorry-free in
`Catalog/Applications/JokeColimitUniversality.lean`,
`Catalog/Applications/JokeSurpriseStability.lean` and
`Catalog/Applications/JokeSurpriseUniqueness.lean`.

Model recap: a setup `S` is a nonempty finite set of candidate resolutions on the
interpretive axis `ℝ`; its surprise is `H(S) = max S − min S`.

---

## 1. Exhaustive check of the submodularity law

Claim tested: `H(S ∪ T) + H(S ∩ T) ≤ H(S) + H(T)` whenever `S ∩ T ≠ ∅`.

Search space: all 63 nonempty subsets of `{0,1,2,3,4,5}`, all 3969 ordered pairs.

| condition | pairs examined | violations |
|---|---|---|
| `S ∩ T ≠ ∅` (submodularity) | all pairs with shared reading | **0** |
| `S ∩ T = ∅` (subadditivity attempted anyway) | all disjoint pairs | **258** |

Worst disjoint excess `H(S ∪ T) − H(S) − H(T) = +5`, attained at `S = {0}, T = {5}`
(two jokes with no shared reading: the juxtaposition is maximally more surprising than
either part). So the shared-reading hypothesis is *necessary*, not cosmetic.

Formalised as `JokeColimitUniversality.humor_submodular`.

## 2. The 100-joke suite and the funniness correlation

Jokes `J i`, `i = 0, …, 99`, with setups `{0, i}`, hence `H(J i) = i` (proved:
`JokeSurpriseStability.humor_jokeSetup`). Three rating models:

| rating model `R i` | interpretation | `cov(H,R)` (exact) | Pearson `r` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `min(i, 50)` | monotone with a scale ceiling | `3383/8 = 422.875` | **+0.897** |
| `i(100−i)/100` | Wundt inverted-U (too weird stops being funny) | `3333/400 = 8.3325` | **+0.039** |
| `100 − i` | anti-correlated (absurdism penalised) | negative | **−1.000** |

Variances: `Var H = 3333/4`, `Var R_sat = 4267/16`, `Var R_invU = 11122221/200000`.

Split of the inverted-U model into branches:

| sub-sample | Pearson `r` |
|---|---|
| low-surprise half `i < 50` | **+0.969** |
| high-surprise half `i ≥ 50` | **−0.967** |

**Reading.** The programme's headline claim ("funnier ⇔ further from the expected
limit") is *not* a mathematical consequence of the categorical set-up: it is an
empirical property of the rating data. It holds exactly when ratings monovary with
surprise (`empCov_nonneg_of_monovaryOn`), it collapses to `r ≈ 0.04` under a plausible
inverted-U model, and it inverts (`r = −1`) under a monotone-decreasing model
(`exists_dataset_empCov_neg`). This is the single most informative computation of the
cycle and it drove the decision to formalise the *guarded* correlation statement.

## 3. Counterexample hunt: does maximal humor imply universality?

Tested pairs `S ⊊ T` of setups with `H(S) = H(T)` over subsets of `{0,…,5}`: the
smallest witness is `S = {0,1} ⊊ T = {0, 1/2, 1}` with `H = 1` for both. Every
refinement that only adds interior readings is such a witness, so counterexamples are
generic, not exceptional. Formalised as
`JokeColimitUniversality.humor_not_reflects_refinement` and
`exists_maximal_humor_not_terminal`.

## 4. Sharpness of the paraphrase bound

Tested: paraphrases `f(x) = a x + b` on `S = {0,1}` with displacement
`ε = max_x |f x − x|`. For `f(x) = 3x − 1` the displacement is exactly `1` and the
humor changes from `1` to `3`, a change of `2 = 2ε`. No affine paraphrase of `{0,1}`
achieved a ratio above `2`. Formalised as
`JokeSurpriseStability.abs_humor_image_sub_humor_le` and `paraphrase_bound_sharp`.

### Summary of what the evidence changed

1. Submodularity was *found* computationally (0/3969 violations with shared context)
   before being proved; it strictly strengthens the catalog's subadditivity law.
2. The correlation conjecture was *falsified* computationally (inverted-U and
   decreasing models) before being replaced by its provable guarded form.
3. The maximal-humor-implies-terminal conjecture was *falsified* by an exhaustive
   small-case scan before being replaced by the one-directional theorem.
