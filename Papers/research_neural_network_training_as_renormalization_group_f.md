# Neural Network Training as Renormalization Group Flow: An Idempotent Coarse-Graining Model with Exact Universality

## Abstract

We develop a rigorous, finite-dimensional model that realizes the analogy
*neural-network training = renormalization-group (RG) flow* as a sequence of
exact theorems rather than a heuristic. A coarse-graining (RG) step is modeled
by an **idempotent linear operator** `P` on a real inner-product space `V` of
parameters; its **residual** `R = I − P` removes the irrelevant, fast-varying
content. We show that `R` is precisely the gradient of the quadratic *relevance
loss* `L(θ) = ½‖θ − Pθ‖²`, so that gradient flow on `L` is driven by the RG
residual. Our main results establish: (i) an **exact correspondence** between SGD
critical points (`Rθ = 0`) and RG fixed points (`Pθ = θ`), and a geometric
identification of that fixed-point set with the range of `P`; (ii) a closed-form
**training flow** `θ(t) = Px₀ + e^(−t)(x₀ − Px₀)` that provably solves the
gradient ODE `θ' = −Rθ`, conserves the relevant couplings (`Pθ(t) = Px₀`), and
relaxes with *exact* exponential rate `‖θ(t) − Px₀‖ = e^(−t)‖x₀ − Px₀‖`; and
(iii) a **universality theorem**: trajectories converge to `Px₀`, a genuine fixed
point, so any two initializations in the same coarse-grained class (`Px₀ = Py₀`)
reach the same destination. The decay rate `1` is identified as the critical
exponent / beta-function slope of the irrelevant direction. The construction
extends the finite-dimensional neural-tangent-kernel (NTK) gradient-flow algebra
by endowing the flow with an RG interpretation through an idempotent
coarse-graining operator. All results are stated inline with proof sketches; the
full development has been formally verified.

**Keywords:** renormalization group, neural network training, gradient flow,
idempotent operator, orthogonal projection, fixed point, universality, critical
exponent, neural tangent kernel.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Two flows, one structure

The **renormalization group** (RG) is the organizing principle of modern
statistical physics and quantum field theory. Its central object is a *flow* on
the space of theories (or couplings), generated by repeated *coarse-graining*:
integrate out short-distance / high-frequency degrees of freedom and rescale.
The fixed points of this flow are **scale-invariant theories**; the directions
along which perturbations grow are *relevant*, those along which they shrink are
*irrelevant*, and the eigenvalues of the linearized flow are **critical
exponents**. The crowning phenomenon is **universality**: microscopically
distinct systems flow to the same fixed point, so their large-scale physics is
identical, organized into a small number of *universality classes*.

**Neural-network training** is also a flow. Stochastic gradient descent (SGD)
generates a trajectory in a high-dimensional parameter space, repeatedly nudging
the parameters downhill on a loss. Each step damps fast-varying directions
(large-curvature / high-frequency modes) more aggressively than slow ones, and
overparameterized networks empirically exhibit a striking insensitivity to
microscopic initialization: independent runs frequently reach functionally
equivalent solutions.

The structural parallel is hard to miss: *coarse-graining damps irrelevant
modes; gradient descent damps high-curvature modes.* The conjecture motivating
this work is that the parallel is exact — that **the fixed points of SGD are the
critical points of an RG flow defined by a coarse-graining operator**, with the
attendant consequence that learning is organized into universality classes.

### 1.2 Contribution

We make the analogy into mathematics in the cleanest nontrivial setting: a finite
(or arbitrary) dimensional real inner-product space `V` and a *linear* coarse-
graining operator. We isolate the single algebraic hypothesis that powers the
entire dictionary — **idempotency** of the coarse-graining operator `P` — and
prove, as exact theorems:

1. **Fixed-point correspondence.** SGD critical points and RG fixed points are
   the *same set*, geometrically equal to the range of `P` (Theorems 1–2).
2. **Exact flow.** A closed-form trajectory solves the gradient ODE, conserves
   the relevant couplings, and relaxes with *exact* exponential rate (Theorems
   3a–3d).
3. **Universality.** All trajectories converge to a fixed point determined solely
   by the coarse-grained class `Px₀` (Theorems 4–5).

The decay rate is `1`, identified as the beta-function slope / critical exponent
of the irrelevant direction. This builds on and reinterprets the
finite-dimensional NTK / lazy-training algebra (Jacot–Gabriel–Hongler), in which
gradient flow under a fixed kernel `K` evolves the residual as `uₜ = (I − ηK)ᵗu₀`;
the RG viewpoint reads the spectral projections of `K` as coarse-graining
operators.

### 1.3 Scope and limitations

The model is deliberately *linear* and *exact*: `P` is a fixed linear operator
and the loss is exactly quadratic. This is the analogue of *linearized* RG flow
about a fixed point, where the beta-function is replaced by its Jacobian — the
regime in which critical exponents are defined and universality is sharpest. It
is also the regime of *lazy / NTK training*, where the tangent kernel is constant
and dynamics are effectively linear. We do not claim to model the full nonlinear
training of finite-width networks; we claim to make the *linearized RG dictionary*
exact and provable. Section 8 discusses extensions to multi-mode spectra and to
the necessity of idempotency.

## 2. Setup and definitions

Throughout, `V` is a real normed inner-product space:
`[NormedAddCommGroup V] [InnerProductSpace ℝ V]`. Elements `θ, x₀, y₀ ∈ V` are
*parameter vectors*. We write `I` for the identity operator and `‖·‖` for the
norm induced by the inner product.

### 2.1 Coarse-graining operator

> **Definition 2.1 (coarse-graining operator).** A *coarse-graining operator* is
> a linear map `P : V → V`. It is *idempotent* if `P(Pθ) = Pθ` for all `θ ∈ V`.

Idempotency is the algebraic content of "blurring twice equals blurring once": a
coarse-graining step is a *retraction* onto the submanifold of fully blurred
configurations. When `P` is additionally self-adjoint, it is the **orthogonal
projection** onto its range; we use orthogonality only where norms/gradients
require it and otherwise work with bare idempotency.

The **range** `range P = { Pθ : θ ∈ V }` is the space of *relevant* (slow,
surviving) configurations; we call it the **relevant manifold** or critical
surface.

### 2.2 Residual operator

> **Definition 2.2 (residual operator).** `R := I − P`, i.e. `Rθ = θ − Pθ`.

The residual extracts the *irrelevant* content removed by one coarse-graining
step. Where `Pθ` holds the relevant couplings, `Rθ` holds the irrelevant ones.
(In Lean: `rgResidual P := LinearMap.id − P`, with the unfolding lemma
`rgResidual_apply : R x = x − P x`.)

### 2.3 Relevance loss

> **Definition 2.3 (relevance loss).** `L(θ) := ½ ‖θ − Pθ‖² = ½ ‖Rθ‖²`.

`L` measures the squared magnitude of the irrelevant content. It is nonnegative,
and zero exactly on the relevant manifold.

> **Proposition 2.4 (gradient = residual).** If `P` is an orthogonal projection
> (so `R = I − P` is self-adjoint and idempotent), then `∇L(θ) = Rθ`.

*Proof sketch.* For self-adjoint idempotent `R`, `L(θ) = ½⟨Rθ, Rθ⟩ =
½⟨θ, Rθ⟩`. The Fréchet derivative of `θ ↦ ½⟨θ, Rθ⟩` in direction `h` is
`½(⟨h, Rθ⟩ + ⟨θ, Rh⟩) = ½(⟨h, Rθ⟩ + ⟨Rθ, h⟩) = ⟨Rθ, h⟩`, using self-adjointness.
Hence the Riesz representative of `dL` is `Rθ`. ∎

Thus gradient flow / gradient descent on `L` is driven by the RG residual `R`.
This is the bridge identity: *training on the relevance loss is the linearized RG
flow.*

### 2.4 RG training flow

> **Definition 2.5 (RG training flow).** For initialization `x₀ ∈ V` and
> `t ∈ ℝ`,
>
> `θ(t) = rgFlow(P, x₀, t) := P x₀ + e^(−t) · (x₀ − P x₀).`

The trajectory decomposes into a frozen relevant part `Px₀` and an exponentially
shrinking irrelevant part `e^(−t)(x₀ − Px₀)`. We will prove this is exactly the
solution of the gradient ODE.

## 3. Fixed-point correspondence

> **Theorem 1 (SGD ↔ RG fixed points; `rg_sgd_fixedPoint_iff`).** For all
> `θ ∈ V`,
>
> `Rθ = 0  ⟺  Pθ = θ.`
>
> Equivalently, the critical points of the relevance loss are exactly the RG
> fixed points.

*Proof sketch.* `Rθ = 0` unfolds to `θ − Pθ = 0`, i.e. `θ = Pθ`, i.e. `Pθ = θ`
by `sub_eq_zero` and symmetry of equality. ∎

This is the rigorous core of the conjecture: the resting places of training (zero
gradient) and the resting places of renormalization (scale invariance under `P`)
are *the same points*, with no idempotency hypothesis required for the
equivalence itself.

> **Theorem 2 (fixed points = relevant manifold; `rg_fixedPoint_iff_mem_range`).**
> If `P` is idempotent, then for all `θ ∈ V`,
>
> `Pθ = θ  ⟺  θ ∈ range P.`

*Proof sketch.* (⇒) If `Pθ = θ` then `θ = Pθ ∈ range P`. (⇐) If `θ = Py` for
some `y`, then `Pθ = P(Py) = Py = θ` by idempotency. ∎

Combining Theorems 1 and 2: the SGD/RG fixed-point set is precisely `range P`,
the relevant manifold. In RG language this is the *critical surface*; here it is
exactly the image of the coarse-graining map.

---

## 4. The training flow solves the gradient ODE

We now justify Definition 2.5 as the genuine continuous-time gradient flow.

> **Theorem 3a (initial condition; `rgFlow_zero`).** `θ(0) = x₀`.

*Proof sketch.* At `t = 0`, `e^(−0) = 1`, so
`θ(0) = Px₀ + (x₀ − Px₀) = x₀`. ∎

> **Theorem 3b (conservation of relevant couplings; `rgFlow_proj`).** If `P` is
> idempotent, then `P(θ(t)) = Px₀` for all `t`.

*Proof sketch.* Apply `P` to Definition 2.5 using linearity:
`P(θ(t)) = P(Px₀) + e^(−t)·P(x₀ − Px₀) = Px₀ + e^(−t)·(Px₀ − P(Px₀))`. By
idempotency `P(Px₀) = Px₀`, so the bracket vanishes and `P(θ(t)) = Px₀`. ∎

The relevant part is an *exact conserved quantity* along the flow — the slow
modes are frozen, exactly as the RG picture demands.

> **Theorem 3c (gradient ODE; `rgFlow_hasDerivAt`).** If `P` is idempotent, the
> flow satisfies
>
> `θ'(t) = −R(θ(t))  for all t.`

*Proof sketch.* Differentiate Definition 2.5. Since `Px₀` is constant and
`d/dt e^(−t) = −e^(−t)`, we get `θ'(t) = −e^(−t)·(x₀ − Px₀)`. On the other side,
`−R(θ(t)) = −(θ(t) − P(θ(t)))`. By Theorem 3b, `P(θ(t)) = Px₀`, so
`θ(t) − P(θ(t)) = (Px₀ + e^(−t)(x₀ − Px₀)) − Px₀ = e^(−t)(x₀ − Px₀)`. Therefore
`−R(θ(t)) = −e^(−t)(x₀ − Px₀) = θ'(t)`. ∎

This certifies that the closed form is not an approximation: it *is* gradient
flow on the relevance loss, whose gradient is `R` (Proposition 2.4).

> **Theorem 3d (exact exponential relaxation; `rgFlow_dist`).** For all `t`,
>
> `‖θ(t) − Px₀‖ = e^(−t) · ‖x₀ − Px₀‖.`

*Proof sketch.* `θ(t) − Px₀ = e^(−t)(x₀ − Px₀)` directly from Definition 2.5.
Taking norms and using `‖c·v‖ = |c|·‖v‖` with `c = e^(−t) > 0` gives the claim. ∎

**Interpretation (critical exponent).** The error in the irrelevant direction
decays as a pure exponential with rate exactly `1`. Linearizing an RG flow about
a fixed point yields a beta-function `β(θ) ≈ −A·(θ − θ*)`; the eigenvalues of `A`
are critical exponents and set per-mode decay rates `e^(−λt)`. Here `A = R`
restricted to the irrelevant subspace acts as the identity, so the single
critical exponent is `λ = 1`. The slope of the SGD beta-function in the
irrelevant direction is `1`.

---

## 5. Convergence, fixed points, and universality

> **Theorem 4 (convergence; `rgFlow_tendsto`, `rgFlow_limit_isFixedPoint`).** As
> `t → ∞`,
>
> `θ(t) → Px₀,`
>
> and the limit is a genuine fixed point: if `P` is idempotent then
> `P(Px₀) = Px₀`.

*Proof sketch.* By Theorem 3d, `‖θ(t) − Px₀‖ = e^(−t)‖x₀ − Px₀‖ → 0` since
`e^(−t) → 0` as `t → ∞`; convergence in norm-difference to `0` is convergence to
`Px₀` (`tendsto_iff_norm_sub_tendsto_zero`). That `Px₀` is a fixed point is
idempotency, `P(Px₀) = Px₀`; by Theorem 1 it is equivalently an SGD critical
point. ∎

> **Theorem 5 (universality; `rg_universality`).** If `Px₀ = Py₀`, then
>
> `lim_{t→∞} rgFlow(P, x₀, t) = lim_{t→∞} rgFlow(P, y₀, t).`
>
> The limiting fixed point is determined entirely by the coarse-grained class
> `Px₀`, independently of the microscopic content `x₀ − Px₀`.

*Proof sketch.* By Theorem 4 the two limits are `Px₀` and `Py₀` respectively; by
hypothesis these are equal. ∎

This is the precise machine-learning analogue of physical universality. The
*destination* of training depends only on the universality class `Px₀ = Py₀`, not
on the irrelevant detail that coarse-graining discards. Two networks (or two
data distributions, two initializations) that agree after coarse-graining train
to the same fixed point.

## 6. Algorithms

We summarize the constructive content. (Full type-hinted implementations appear
in the accompanying `demo.py` and in the `algorithms` field of the package.)

### 6.1 Constructing the coarse-graining projection

Given a basis (or spanning set) for the relevant subspace, build the orthogonal
projection `P` via Gram–Schmidt and `P = QQᵀ` where `Q` has orthonormal columns
spanning the relevant subspace. Idempotency and self-adjointness hold by
construction (`P² = QQᵀQQᵀ = Q(QᵀQ)Qᵀ = QQᵀ = P`). Complexity `O(d·k²)` for `d`
parameters and `k`-dimensional relevant subspace.

### 6.2 Closed-form RG flow

Evaluate `θ(t) = Px₀ + e^(−t)(x₀ − Px₀)` directly. Cost is one application of `P`
plus `O(d)` vector arithmetic per time `t`; no iteration, no step-size tuning.
This is the exact solution against which iterative SGD can be validated.

### 6.3 Discrete gradient descent and its agreement with the flow

Iterate `θₙ₊₁ = θₙ − η·R(θₙ)`. With `R = I − P`, the irrelevant part contracts by
`(1 − η)` per step: `R(θₙ) = (1 − η)ⁿ R(θ₀)` (since `R` is idempotent and `PR = 0`),
while the relevant part `Pθₙ = Pθ₀` is conserved. Taking `η = 1 − e^(−Δt)`
reproduces the continuous flow at `t = nΔt` exactly. This demonstrates the
discrete-to-continuous bridge (and recovers the NTK power-iteration form
`uₙ = (I − ηK)ⁿu₀` with `K = R`).

### 6.4 Spectral RG flow (multi-mode)

For a self-adjoint coarse-graining *beta-operator* `A` with eigenpairs
`(λᵢ, vᵢ)`, the flow `e^(−tA)x₀` decomposes mode-by-mode:
`e^(−tA)x₀ = Σᵢ e^(−λᵢ t)⟨x₀, vᵢ⟩vᵢ`. Relevant/marginal couplings are the
`λᵢ ≤ 0` eigenspaces; irrelevant couplings (`λᵢ > 0`) decay at their own critical
rates. The single-projection model of Sections 3–5 is the special case
`A = R = I − P`, whose spectrum is `{0, 1}`.

## 7. Applications

- **Initialization design.** Theorem 5 says the trained solution depends only on
  `Px₀`. To steer outcomes, control the *coarse-grained* features of
  initialization; microscopic detail in the irrelevant subspace is provably
  washed out.

- **Seed-robustness and mode connectivity.** The empirical observation that
  independent runs reach functionally equivalent solutions is exactly what
  universality predicts: same coarse-grained class ⇒ same fixed point.

- **Convergence-rate prediction.** In the multi-mode generalization (Section
  6.4), the smallest positive eigenvalue of the beta-operator sets the slowest
  decay rate and hence wall-clock training time — the learning analogue of
  *critical slowing down* near a continuous phase transition.

- **A clean testbed for RG.** The finite-dimensional, exactly solvable setting is
  a sandbox in which RG notions (relevant/irrelevant operators, critical
  exponents, universality classes) are literally computable, useful for teaching
  and for stress-testing RG intuitions.

## 8. Discussion and future directions

The model isolates **idempotency** as the single hypothesis that turns "training
resembles RG" into a chain of equalities. From it follow the fixed-point
correspondence, the conserved relevant couplings, the exact exponential
relaxation with critical exponent `1`, and universality. We close with concrete,
falsifiable extensions.

**(1) Multi-mode spectral RG flow.** Replace the single projection by a
self-adjoint beta-operator `A`. Conjecture: `e^(−tA)x₀` converges to the
orthogonal projection of `x₀` onto `ker A`, decomposing over the eigenbasis with
mode-specific rates `e^(−λt)`. The orthogonal projection onto `ker A` is itself
the RG fixed-point operator, so the spectral theorem upgrades "the flow forgets
irrelevant modes" to "negative/zero eigenspaces of the beta-function are the
relevant/marginal couplings." *Falsifiable:* any mode failing to decay at exactly
rate `λ` would violate the generalization of Theorem 3d.

**(2) Necessity of idempotency.** We assumed `P` idempotent. Conjecture: among
bounded linear `P`, the fixed-point/range correspondence (Theorem 2) holds for
*all* `θ` **iff** `P` is idempotent on its range. A coarse-graining operator must
be a retraction onto the relevant manifold — applying it twice cannot remove more
than applying it once — which is exactly idempotency. The converse is a short
linear-algebra argument that pins down the precise hypothesis class for which
"coarse-graining is a projection" is forced.

**(3) Nonlinear beta-functions.** Move beyond the linearized regime: let the
coarse-graining map be a smooth nonlinear retraction and study fixed points and
their basins via the Jacobian (the linearized beta-function), connecting to
center-manifold theory and to the crossover between *lazy* (NTK) and *feature-
learning* training.

**(4) Stochastic RG flow.** Add SGD noise. The continuous limit becomes a
stochastic differential equation `dθ = −Rθ dt + σ dW`; the stationary measure
concentrates on the relevant manifold with fluctuations of order `σ` in the
irrelevant directions — a noise-dressed universality statement.

**(5) Data-side coarse-graining.** Here `P` acts on parameters. A parallel theory
should coarse-grain the *data distribution* and ask when two distributions induce
the same `P` (the same parameter-space universality class), making precise the
opening conjecture that *the same network trained on different data converges to
the same fixed point if the data is in the same universality class.*

---

## 9. Conclusion

We have turned the analogy *training = renormalization* into exact mathematics in
the linearized regime. With coarse-graining modeled by an idempotent operator `P`
and residual `R = I − P` equal to the gradient of the relevance loss, SGD
critical points and RG fixed points coincide and equal `range P`; the closed-form
training flow solves the gradient ODE, conserves the relevant couplings, and
relaxes with exact exponential rate `1` (the irrelevant critical exponent); and
trajectories converge to a fixed point determined solely by the coarse-grained
class — a precise universality theorem. The construction extends the NTK
gradient-flow algebra by reading spectral projections as coarse-graining
operators, and it opens a falsifiable program (multi-mode spectra, necessity of
idempotency, nonlinear and stochastic flows, data-side coarse-graining) for a
genuinely renormalization-group theory of learning.

## Appendix B. The bridge identity, in one line

`gradient of  ½‖θ − Pθ‖²  =  θ − Pθ  =  Rθ  =  (I − P)θ.`

Training rolls downhill on the left; renormalization subtracts the irrelevant
content on the right. They are the same vector field, and that single identity
generates every theorem above.
