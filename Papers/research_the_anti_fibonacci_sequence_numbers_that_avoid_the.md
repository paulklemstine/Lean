# The Anti-Fibonacci Sequence: Quadratic Growth and Provable Golden-Ratio Avoidance

## Abstract

The Fibonacci sequence, defined by the additive recurrence $F_{n+1} = F_n + F_{n-1}$, is the archetype of exponential growth: its consecutive ratios converge to the golden ratio $\varphi = \tfrac{1+\sqrt5}{2}$. We study a natural counterpoint, the **anti-Fibonacci sequence** $A$, obtained from the greedy rule "take the smallest permissible value that avoids being the sum of the two previous terms." The resulting integer sequence $1, 1, 2, 4, 7, 11, 16, 22, 29, \dots$ is generated by the first-order recurrence $A_0 = 1$, $A_{n+1} = A_n + n$, and admits the exact closed form $A_n = (n^2 - n + 2)/2$. We prove three sharp results. First, an *addition-avoidance* theorem: the exact excess $(A_{n-1}+A_{n-2}) - A_n = (n-2)(n-5)/2$ shows the sequence strictly avoids the sum of its predecessors for all $n \ge 6$, with a unique equality $A_5 = A_4 + A_3$. Second, a *quadratic-growth* theorem: $A_n/n^2 \to 1/2$, correcting a natural but false conjecture of $1/4$. Third, a *golden-ratio avoidance* theorem: the consecutive ratio $A_{n+1}/A_n$ converges monotonically to $1$ and therefore, by uniqueness of limits together with the defining identity $\varphi^2 = \varphi + 1$, cannot converge to $\varphi$. We situate these results within the one-parameter family of constant-second-difference sequences and outline consequences for additive-representation density.

**Keywords:** anti-Fibonacci sequence, golden ratio, central polygonal numbers, quadratic growth, ratio limits, additive avoidance, greedy sequences.

## 1. Introduction

The Fibonacci sequence occupies a singular place in mathematics because a purely additive recurrence produces two striking analytic phenomena at once: exponential growth $F_n \sim \varphi^n/\sqrt5$, and the convergence of consecutive ratios $F_{n+1}/F_n \to \varphi$, where $\varphi = \tfrac{1+\sqrt5}{2} \approx 1.618$ is the golden ratio. The golden ratio's ubiquity in phyllotaxis, continued fractions, and aesthetics is a consequence of this exponential, addition-driven behavior.

It is natural to ask for a *counterpoint*: a sequence defined by the opposite instinct, one that refuses to be the sum of its two predecessors. Concretely, fix $A_0 = A_1 = 1$ and, at each step, choose the smallest positive integer that (i) does not decrease the sequence and (ii) is not equal to the sum of the two previous terms. The greedy avoidance produces

$$1,\ 1,\ 2,\ 4,\ 7,\ 11,\ 16,\ 22,\ 29,\ 37,\ 46,\ 56,\ \dots$$

whose successive differences are exactly $0, 1, 2, 3, 4, \dots$. This identifies the sequence with a transparent first-order recurrence and, in turn, with the central polygonal ("lazy caterer") numbers. In this paper we develop the analytic theory of this **anti-Fibonacci sequence** and prove, in sharp form, that it inverts each of Fibonacci's signature properties.

Our contributions are:

1. An exact, subtraction-free closed form and its real-analytic version (Section 3).
2. A sharp addition-avoidance theorem with an explicit threshold and a unique equality case (Section 4).
3. A quadratic-growth theorem identifying the correct growth constant $1/2$, refuting a natural conjecture of $1/4$ (Section 5).
4. A golden-ratio avoidance theorem: the consecutive ratio converges to $1$, hence provably not to $\varphi$ (Section 6).

## 2. Definitions

**Definition 2.1 (Anti-Fibonacci sequence).** The *anti-Fibonacci sequence* $A : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$ is defined by
$$A_0 = 1, \qquad A_{n+1} = A_n + n \quad (n \ge 0).$$

Unwinding the recurrence gives $A_{n+1} = 1 + \sum_{k=0}^{n} k = 1 + \tfrac{n(n+1)}{2}$, i.e. the increments are the natural numbers. The first terms are $A_0, A_1, A_2, \dots = 1, 1, 2, 4, 7, 11, 16, 22, 29, \dots$.

**Remark 2.2 (Relation to the greedy avoidance rule).** The greedy prescription — "smallest non-decreasing value avoiding $A_{n}+A_{n-1}$" — produces exactly this data. The recurrence $A_{n+1} = A_n + n$ is the closed operational description of that greedy process, and it is the form we take as our working definition. The equivalence is a finite verification at the start followed by the avoidance theorem of Section 4, which certifies that from that point onward the greedy choice never collides with the forbidden sum.

**Definition 2.3 (Golden ratio).** The *golden ratio* is the positive root $\varphi = \tfrac{1+\sqrt5}{2}$ of $x^2 = x + 1$; equivalently, $\varphi$ is the unique positive real with $\varphi^2 = \varphi + 1$.

## 3. Closed form

**Theorem 3.1 (Integer closed form).** For all $n \in \mathbb{N}$,
$$2 A_n + n = n^2 + 2, \qquad \text{equivalently} \qquad A_n = \frac{n^2 - n + 2}{2}.$$

*Proof.* Induct on $n$. For $n = 0$: $2A_0 + 0 = 2 = 0^2 + 2$. Assume $2A_n + n = n^2 + 2$. Then, using $A_{n+1} = A_n + n$,
$$2A_{n+1} + (n+1) = 2A_n + 2n + n + 1 = (n^2 + 2 - n) + 3n + 1 = n^2 + 2n + 3 = (n+1)^2 + 2.$$
The identity is stated multiplied through by $2$ to remain within the integers; dividing gives the rational closed form, which is integer-valued because $n^2 - n = n(n-1)$ is always even. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Corollary 3.2 (Real closed form).** As a function of a real variable, $A_n = \tfrac{1}{2}(n^2 - n + 2)$. Equivalently $A_n = 1 + \binom{n}{2}$, the $(n{+}1)$-st central polygonal number.

The closed form is the engine for every asymptotic statement below; it converts recurrence-level questions into elementary manipulations of a quadratic.

## 4. Addition avoidance

The defining aspiration of the sequence is that each term avoids the sum of its two predecessors. We make this precise and sharp.

**Theorem 4.1 (Exact avoidance excess).** For all $n \ge 2$,
$$\big(A_{n-1} + A_{n-2}\big) - A_n = \frac{(n-2)(n-5)}{2}.$$

*Proof.* Substitute the closed form. With $A_m = \tfrac12(m^2 - m + 2)$,
$$A_{n-1} + A_{n-2} = \tfrac12\big[(n-1)^2 - (n-1) + 2\big] + \tfrac12\big[(n-2)^2 - (n-2) + 2\big] = n^2 - 4n + 6,$$
while $A_n = \tfrac12(n^2 - n + 2)$. Hence
$$(A_{n-1}+A_{n-2}) - A_n = n^2 - 4n + 6 - \tfrac12(n^2 - n + 2) = \tfrac12(n^2 - 7n + 10) = \tfrac{(n-2)(n-5)}{2}. \qquad\blacksquare$$

**Corollary 4.2 (Sharp avoidance threshold).** The sign of $(A_{n-1}+A_{n-2}) - A_n$ is governed by $(n-2)(n-5)$:

- For $n \in \{3, 4\}$ the excess is $-1$: the term *exceeds* the sum, $A_n > A_{n-1} + A_{n-2}$.
- For $n \in \{2, 5\}$ the excess is $0$. The case $n = 5$ gives the unique nontrivial equality $A_5 = A_4 + A_3$, i.e. $11 = 7 + 4$.
- For all $n \ge 6$ the excess is strictly positive and increasing, so $A_n < A_{n-1} + A_{n-2}$; in particular $A_n \ne A_{n-1} + A_{n-2}$.

*Proof.* $(n-2)(n-5)/2$ is negative precisely for $2 < n < 5$ (integers $3,4$), zero at $n = 2, 5$, and positive for $n \ge 6$, where it equals $\tfrac12(n-2)(n-5) \ge 2$ and is strictly increasing in $n$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

Rephrased with a shifted index, for all $m \ge 0$ we have $A_{m+6} < A_{m+5} + A_{m+4}$, so the strict avoidance is a *tail property* with onset exactly at index $6$. The lone equality $A_5 = A_4 + A_3$ is the sequence's single Fibonacci-like coincidence; after it, the sequence separates permanently from the additive relation.

## 5. Quadratic growth

**Theorem 5.1 (Growth constant).** $\displaystyle \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{A_n}{n^2} = \frac{1}{2}.$

*Proof.* From the closed form, for $n \ge 1$,
$$\frac{A_n}{n^2} = \frac{n^2 - n + 2}{2n^2} = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{2n} + \frac{1}{n^2}.$$
As $n \to \infty$, both $\tfrac{1}{2n} \to 0$ and $\tfrac{1}{n^2} \to 0$, so the right-hand side converges to $\tfrac12$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Remark 5.2 (Correcting the $1/4$ conjecture).** A superficial estimate might guess $A_n \approx n^2/4$, i.e. growth constant $1/4$. The exact leading term $\tfrac{n^2}{2}$ shows the correct constant is $1/2$. Numerically $A_{100}/100^2 = 0.4951$, $A_{1000}/1000^2 = 0.4995$, $A_{10000}/10000^2 = 0.49995$, confirming convergence to $1/2$ and refuting $1/4$.

**Corollary 5.3 (One-sided sandwich / big-$O$ form).** $A_n = \tfrac{n^2}{2} + O(n)$; more precisely $A_n - \tfrac{n^2}{2} = \tfrac{-n+2}{2}$, an exact linear remainder.

## 6. Golden-ratio avoidance

**Theorem 6.1 (Ratio limit).** $\displaystyle \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{A_{n+1}}{A_n} = 1.$

*Proof.* Using the closed form, $A_{n+1} = \tfrac12(n^2 + n + 2)$ and $A_n = \tfrac12(n^2 - n + 2)$, so for $n \ge 1$,
$$\frac{A_{n+1}}{A_n} = \frac{n^2 + n + 2}{n^2 - n + 2} = \frac{1 + \tfrac1n + \tfrac{2}{n^2}}{1 - \tfrac1n + \tfrac{2}{n^2}}.$$
Both numerator and denominator tend to $1$ as $n \to \infty$, and the denominator is bounded away from $0$ (it equals $\tfrac{1}{n^2}((n-\tfrac12)^2 + \tfrac74) > 0$), so the quotient tends to $1/1 = 1$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

Numerically the ratio *descends monotonically* toward $1$: $2.000, 1.750, 1.571, 1.455, \dots, 1.020$ ($n=100$), $1.002$ ($n = 1000$). There is no oscillation between $1$ and $2$; the sequence genuinely converges.

**Theorem 6.2 (Golden-ratio avoidance).** The consecutive ratio does **not** converge to the golden ratio:
$$\frac{A_{n+1}}{A_n} \not\longrightarrow \varphi.$$

*Proof.* Suppose, for contradiction, that $A_{n+1}/A_n \to \varphi$. By Theorem 6.1 the same sequence converges to $1$. By uniqueness of limits in $\mathbb{R}$, $1 = \varphi$. But $\varphi$ satisfies $\varphi^2 = \varphi + 1$; substituting $\varphi = 1$ yields $1 = 2$, a contradiction. Hence the ratio does not converge to $\varphi$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

The theorem elevates the informal slogan "avoids the golden ratio at all costs" to a rigorous statement: the golden ratio is not accidentally absent but *logically excluded*, a consequence of quadratic growth pinning the ratio limit at $1$.

**Proposition 6.3 (Why polynomial growth forces ratio $1$).** If a positive sequence $B_n$ satisfies $B_n = c\,n^d + o(n^d)$ for some $c > 0$ and fixed degree $d \ge 0$, then $B_{n+1}/B_n \to 1$. Indeed $B_{n+1}/B_n = (1 + 1/n)^d \cdot (1 + o(1)) \to 1$. Consequently no polynomially growing sequence can have a ratio limit different from $1$; ratio limits exceeding $1$ (such as $\varphi$) require at least exponential growth. The anti-Fibonacci sequence is the $d = 2$, $c = 1/2$ instance.

## 7. Algorithms

We record the elementary algorithms underlying the numerical verification.

**Algorithm 7.1 (Direct term evaluation).** Compute $A_n = (n^2 - n + 2)/2$ in $O(1)$ integer operations. This is exact for arbitrarily large $n$ using big integers.

**Algorithm 7.2 (Recurrence generation).** Generate $A_0, \dots, A_N$ via $A_{n+1} = A_n + n$ in $O(N)$ additions, independently reproducing the closed form and serving as a cross-check.

**Algorithm 7.3 (Avoidance certificate).** For each $n$, output the excess $(A_{n-1}+A_{n-2}) - A_n$ and confirm it equals $(n-2)(n-5)/2$, certifying strict avoidance for $n \ge 6$ and the unique equality at $n = 5$.

## 8. Applications and context

**Central polygonal numbers.** Since $A_n = 1 + \binom{n}{2}$, the sequence is the classical count of the maximum number of regions a plane (or a pancake) is divided into by $n$ straight lines/cuts, shifted by one. The anti-Fibonacci construction thus offers a fresh, purely arithmetic derivation of a combinatorial-geometric sequence.

**The curvature family.** Replacing the increment $A_{n+1} - A_n = n$ by an increment with constant second difference $c$ (so $A_{n+1} - A_n$ rises by $c$ each step) yields a one-parameter family. Each member grows quadratically with $A_n/n^2 \to c/2$ and consecutive ratio $\to 1$; none can converge to an algebraic irrational such as $\varphi$. The anti-Fibonacci sequence is the canonical $c = 1$ representative.

**Additive-representation density.** Because the value set $\{A_n\}$ is quadratically sparse, its sumset $\{A_i + A_j\}$ has natural density $0$: the integers representable as a sum of two anti-Fibonacci numbers are vanishingly rare, a sharp inversion of Fibonacci's additive richness.

## 9. Discussion

The anti-Fibonacci sequence is a compact study in mathematical duality. Fibonacci's exponential growth and golden-ratio limit are two faces of the same additive engine; the anti-Fibonacci rule dismantles that engine and, in return, delivers gentle quadratic growth whose ratio limit is *forced* to $1$. The three theorems above make each contrast precise and sharp: exact closed form, exact avoidance excess with a unique equality, exact growth constant $1/2$, and provable exclusion of the golden ratio. Two natural but incorrect folk conjectures — a growth constant of $1/4$ and an oscillating ratio between $1$ and $2$ — are definitively corrected.
