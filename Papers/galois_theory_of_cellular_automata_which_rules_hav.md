# Computational evidence: elementary reversible cellular automata

## Small-case calculations

All 256 Wolfram elementary rules were tested for bijectivity of their induced
global map on binary cyclic configurations.  The cumulative counts are:

| tested cycle lengths | rules passing every test |
|---|---:|
| 1 | 128 |
| 1–2 | 64 |
| 1–3 | 20 |
| 1–4 | 6 |

After length 4 the survivors are exactly
`15, 51, 85, 170, 204, 240`.
The theorem `reversible_small_cycles_iff` certifies this exhaustive result in
Lean; it is not merely an external calculation.

## OEIS search

No OEIS identification is asserted.  The short cumulative sequence
`128, 64, 20, 6` comes from a one-off finite classification rather than a
naturally indexed infinite sequence, so an OEIS match would not add reliable
information here.

## Counterexample hunt and correction of the conjectural framing

The proposed claim that an elementary CA local rule is a permutation of its
eight neighborhoods is ill-typed mathematically: a local rule has type
`Bool^3 → Bool`, with 8 inputs but only 2 possible outputs, so it cannot be a
permutation of the 8 neighborhoods.  Consequently, the suggested subgroup of
`S_8` generated by these local rules, and its proposed order 10080, is not
defined without introducing a different neighborhood-level action.

Reversibility instead applies to the induced global map on configurations.
The finite search found that every rule outside the six-rule list fails
bijectivity on a cyclic configuration space of length at most 4.  Conversely,
the six survivors are projections onto left/center/right, optionally followed
by Boolean complement; Lean proofs show that these maps are reversible on
every nonempty finite cycle.

## Table of the six survivors

| Wolfram rule | local formula | induced dynamics |
|---:|---|---|
| 15 | `¬left` | complemented cyclic shift |
| 51 | `¬center` | pointwise complement |
| 85 | `¬right` | complemented cyclic shift |
| 170 | `right` | cyclic shift |
| 204 | `center` | identity |
| 240 | `left` | inverse cyclic shift |
