# Why a Separate Computational Evidence Stage Was Not Used

The principal results are uniform symbolic inequalities for arbitrary finite sets, kernels, depths, and branching bounds. Small numerical samples cannot test the decisive universal quantifiers or expose the cancellation needed beyond the cardinality threshold. The one concrete specialization included in the development—two binary-branching supports at depth three in a five-ary tree—has the exact contraction factor $64/125$, derived from the general theorem. No sequence arises naturally, so an OEIS search would not be relevant.

A counterexample analysis is instead built into the hypotheses: the elementary Hilbert–Schmidt method contracts exactly when the product of the two branching bounds is smaller than the ambient branching number. At and above that boundary it supplies no decay, which motivates the additive-energy conjectures in `FUTURE_DIRECTIONS.md`.
