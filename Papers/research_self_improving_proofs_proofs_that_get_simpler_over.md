## Abstract

We develop a structural model of self-improving certificates in which a certificate consists of an auditable proof tree together with a witness of the proposition certified. The tree language contains hypotheses, named-lemma references, modus-ponens nodes, and restatement nodes. Its cost is the sum of node count, tree depth, and named-lemma count. Refinement is generated by concrete local rewrites: redundant-restatement elimination, inlining of named trivial lemmas, and removal of restatements adjacent to modus ponens, together with contextual closure under larger trees. Every rewrite preserves the recorded conclusion and strictly decreases audited cost. It follows that refinement is well-founded and admits no infinite strict trajectory. More generally, every non-increasing sequence of audited costs is eventually constant.

We then couple certificate evolution to an ascending chain of key ideals over a commutative Noetherian ring. The certificate cost and key ideal admit a common finite stabilization stage. An explicit certificate for the irrationality of $\sqrt{2}$ illustrates a three-step rewrite chain with costs $7,5,3,1$ and a normal endpoint. We also specify a bottom-up simplification algorithm that preserves conclusions and does not increase cost. The results establish termination and stabilization for a concrete audit calculus; they do not assert confluence, global shortestness, or Kolmogorov minimality. This distinction identifies precise research questions concerning normalization distance, unique normal forms, quantitative Noetherian bounds, and preservation of cryptographic security properties.

## 1. Introduction

Cryptographic certificates are usually judged by validity: a verifier accepts or rejects a claim. Yet two valid certificates of the same proposition may differ dramatically in size, dependency depth, and reliance on named auxiliary facts. These differences affect bandwidth, auditability, verifier work, and the attack surface created by complicated dependency structures. This motivates a second question after correctness: can a certificate be simplified through local, semantics-preserving operations?

A useful theory must avoid defining “improvement” by assigning arbitrary decreasing numbers. The simplifications should be concrete transformations of certificate structure, and cost reduction should be a theorem about those transformations. It must also distinguish several notions often conflated in informal discussions:

- termination, meaning that strict refinement cannot continue indefinitely;
- stabilization, meaning that a numerical or algebraic observable eventually becomes constant;
- normality, meaning that no listed local rewrite applies;
- confluence, meaning that divergent rewrite paths can be joined;
- global minimality, meaning that an endpoint is smallest among all representations in a specified class; and
- Kolmogorov minimality, meaning shortest description relative to a universal description language.

Only the first three follow from the present calculus. In particular, natural-number descent proves termination but not confluence or global optimality.

The cryptographic setting adds a second evolving object. Key material, accumulated constraints, or algebraic knowledge can be represented by ideals. In a commutative Noetherian ring, every ascending chain of ideals stabilizes. This algebraic termination principle runs in the opposite order-theoretic direction from proof simplification: certificate costs descend while key ideals ascend. Nevertheless, their eventual constancy can be synchronized by taking the maximum of their stabilization stages.

The paper proceeds as follows. Section 2 defines formulas, proof trees, their conclusions, and audited cost. Section 3 introduces the rewrite system. Section 4 proves preservation, strict descent, and termination. Section 5 treats schedules with idle rounds. Section 6 proves synchronized certificate–key stabilization. Section 7 gives the $\sqrt{2}$ audit trail. Section 8 presents algorithms and complexity analysis. Sections 9–11 discuss applications, limitations, and future work.

## 2. Certificate trees and audited complexity

### 2.1 Propositional labels

Let $\mathcal{F}$ be a set of propositional formulas generated from atomic formulas and the usual connectives. Only formula identity and implication labels are needed below. A tree carries a recorded conclusion in $\mathcal{F}$.

### 2.2 Proof-tree syntax

**Definition 2.1 (Proof tree).** A proof tree is built recursively from the following constructors.

1. A hypothesis node $\operatorname{hyp}(A)$ is a leaf recording formula $A$.
2. A named-lemma node $\operatorname{named}(s,A)$ is a leaf recording a name $s$ and formula $A$.
3. A modus-ponens node $\operatorname{mp}(C,P,Q)$ has subtrees $P$ and $Q$ and records conclusion $C$.
4. A restatement node $\operatorname{restate}(A,P)$ has child $P$ and records conclusion $A$.

The syntax is intentionally an audit language rather than a complete foundational calculus. A modus-ponens node records its output label; a deployment may impose additional local typing checks on its children.

**Definition 2.2 (Recorded conclusion).** The conclusion function $\operatorname{concl}$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{concl}(\operatorname{hyp}(A))&=A,\\
\operatorname{concl}(\operatorname{named}(s,A))&=A,\\
\operatorname{concl}(\operatorname{mp}(C,P,Q))&=C,\\
\operatorname{concl}(\operatorname{restate}(A,P))&=A.
\end{aligned}
$$

**Definition 2.3 (Audited certificate).** For a proposition $T$, an audited certificate of $T$ is a pair $(P,w)$, where $P$ is a proof tree and $w$ is a mathematical witness of $T$. The witness secures truth of the certified proposition; the tree supplies the auditable syntactic structure. Certificates compared by refinement carry witnesses of the same proposition $T$.

This separation prevents a structural audit label from being mistaken for a foundational derivation. All refinement results concern the tree, while the common proposition type guarantees that old and new objects certify the same external claim.

### 2.3 Complexity statistics

**Definition 2.4 (Length).** The length $L(P)$ is the number of nodes:

$$
\begin{aligned}
L(\operatorname{hyp}(A))&=1,\\
L(\operatorname{named}(s,A))&=1,\\
L(\operatorname{mp}(C,P,Q))&=1+L(P)+L(Q),\\
L(\operatorname{restate}(A,P))&=1+L(P).
\end{aligned}
$$

**Definition 2.5 (Depth).** Leaves have depth $0$, and internal nodes add one level:

$$
\begin{aligned}
D(\operatorname{hyp}(A))&=0,\\
D(\operatorname{named}(s,A))&=0,\\
D(\operatorname{mp}(C,P,Q))&=1+\max\{D(P),D(Q)\},\\
D(\operatorname{restate}(A,P))&=1+D(P).
\end{aligned}
$$

**Definition 2.6 (Named-lemma count).** Let $M(P)$ count named-lemma leaves:

$$
\begin{aligned}
M(\operatorname{hyp}(A))&=0,\\
M(\operatorname{named}(s,A))&=1,\\
M(\operatorname{mp}(C,P,Q))&=M(P)+M(Q),\\
M(\operatorname{restate}(A,P))&=M(P).
\end{aligned}
$$

**Definition 2.7 (Audited cost and additive weight).** Define

$$
W(P)=L(P)+M(P)
$$

and

$$
C(P)=L(P)+D(P)+M(P)=W(P)+D(P).
$$

The integer $C(P)$ is not claimed to be a universal measure of mathematical elegance. It is an explicit audit metric whose behavior under the permitted rewrites can be proved.

## 3. Concrete refinement rules

**Definition 3.1 (One-step refinement).** Write $P\longrightarrow Q$ when $Q$ is obtained from $P$ by one of the following rules.

1. **Redundant-restatement elimination:** if $\operatorname{concl}(P)=A$, then

$$
\operatorname{restate}(A,P)\longrightarrow P.
$$

2. **Named-lemma inlining:**

$$
\operatorname{named}(s,A)\longrightarrow\operatorname{hyp}(A).
$$

3. **Modus-ponens merging on the left:**

$$
\operatorname{mp}(C,\operatorname{restate}(A,P),Q)
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{mp}(C,P,Q).
$$

4. **Modus-ponens merging on the right:**

$$
\operatorname{mp}(C,P,\operatorname{restate}(A,Q))
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{mp}(C,P,Q).
$$

The relation is closed under tree contexts: if $P\longrightarrow P'$, then the same replacement is permitted inside either child of a modus-ponens node or inside a restatement node.

For audited certificates $(P,w)$ and $(Q,w')$ of the same proposition $T$, say that $(Q,w')$ refines $(P,w)$ when $P\longrightarrow Q$.

The direction of the arrow follows computation: the larger tree rewrites to the smaller one. In verbal statements, the right-hand certificate is the refinement of the left-hand certificate.

## 4. Preservation, descent, and termination

### 4.1 Preservation of conclusions

**Theorem 4.1 (Conclusion preservation).** If $P\longrightarrow Q$, then

$$
\operatorname{concl}(P)=\operatorname{concl}(Q).
$$

**Proof sketch.** Inspect the four primitive rewrites. Redundant-restatement elimination uses the side condition $\operatorname{concl}(P)=A$. Named-lemma inlining replaces a leaf labeled $A$ by another leaf labeled $A$. Both modus-ponens merges retain the same outer node labeled $C$. For a rewrite inside a context, the root label of the context is unchanged. Structural induction over the derivation of $P\longrightarrow Q$ completes the argument. $\square$

**Corollary 4.2 (Semantic preservation).** Under any interpretation of propositional formulas, one-step refinement preserves the truth value of the recorded conclusion.

**Proof sketch.** Equal formulas have equal semantic values, and Theorem 4.1 gives formula equality. $\square$

### 4.2 Strict decrease of weight

**Lemma 4.3 (Weight descent).** If $P\longrightarrow Q$, then

$$
W(Q)<W(P).
$$

**Proof sketch.** Removing a restatement deletes one node and leaves named-lemma count unchanged. Inlining a named lemma preserves node count but lowers named-lemma count by one. Either modus-ponens merge deletes one restatement node. If a rewrite occurs in a subtree, its strict weight reduction adds to unchanged nonnegative contributions from the surrounding context. Induction over contextual closure proves the claim. $\square$

**Lemma 4.4 (Depth monotonicity).** If $P\longrightarrow Q$, then

$$
D(Q)\le D(P).
$$

**Proof sketch.** Each primitive rule either removes an internal restatement or replaces one leaf by another, so depth cannot rise. For contextual rules, monotonicity of successor and maximum propagates the subtree inequality to the whole tree. $\square$

**Theorem 4.5 (Strict audited-cost descent).** If $P\longrightarrow Q$, then

$$
C(Q)<C(P).
$$

**Proof sketch.** By Lemma 4.3, $W(Q)<W(P)$; by Lemma 4.4, $D(Q)\le D(P)$. Since $C=W+D$, adding these inequalities yields $C(Q)<C(P)$. $\square$

### 4.3 Well-foundedness

**Theorem 4.6 (Well-founded refinement).** The relation “is a one-step refinement of” on audited certificates of a fixed proposition is well-founded.

**Proof sketch.** Map every certificate to its natural-number cost. Theorem 4.5 shows that moving to a refinement strictly lowers this number. The strict order on the natural numbers is well-founded, and pulling it back along the cost map gives well-foundedness of refinement. $\square$

**Corollary 4.7 (No infinite strict refinement path).** There is no sequence of audited certificates $P_0,P_1,P_2,\ldots$ such that

$$
P_n\longrightarrow P_{n+1}
$$

for every $n\in\mathbb{N}$.

**Proof sketch.** Such a sequence would produce the infinite strictly descending sequence

$$
C(P_0)>C(P_1)>C(P_2)>\cdots
$$

of natural numbers, which is impossible. Equivalently, the range of the sequence would have a refinement-minimal element, contradicted by its successor. $\square$

A useful immediate bound is that a strict path beginning at $P_0$ has fewer than or equal to $C(P_0)$ steps, because each step decreases cost by at least one and costs remain nonnegative. This bound is representation-dependent and can be enormous.

## 5. Stabilization with idle rounds

Protocols may include rounds that leave the certificate unchanged. Strict well-foundedness alone does not describe such schedules, but monotonicity does.

**Definition 5.1 (Non-increasing audit schedule).** A sequence $(P_n)_{n\ge0}$ is a non-increasing audit schedule if

$$
m\le n\quad\Longrightarrow\quad C(P_n)\le C(P_m).
$$

**Theorem 5.2 (Eventual constancy of audited cost).** Every non-increasing audit schedule has a stage $N$ such that

$$
C(P_n)=C(P_N)
$$

for all $n\ge N$.

**Proof sketch.** The set $S=\{C(P_n):n\in\mathbb{N}\}$ is a nonempty subset of $\mathbb{N}$, so it has a least element $c$. Choose $N$ with $C(P_N)=c$. For $n\ge N$, monotonicity gives $C(P_n)\le C(P_N)=c$, while minimality gives $c\le C(P_n)$. Antisymmetry yields equality. $\square$

The conclusion is numerical. If protocol rounds permit transformations between distinct trees having equal cost, the trees themselves need not stabilize. If every non-identity transition is one of the strict refinement rules, then cost stabilization also rules out later structural changes of that kind.

## 6. Noetherian key protocols and common stabilization

### 6.1 Algebraic state

Let $R$ be a commutative ring. An ideal $I\subseteq R$ models algebraic information available to the protocol: generated key relations, constraints, or accumulated certification conditions.

**Definition 6.1 (Ascending key-ideal protocol).** An ascending key-ideal protocol is a sequence of ideals

$$
I_0\subseteq I_1\subseteq I_2\subseteq\cdots.
$$

**Definition 6.2 (Noetherian ring).** A commutative ring $R$ is Noetherian if every ascending sequence of ideals becomes constant. Thus for every ascending chain $(I_n)$ there is $N_k$ such that

$$
I_n=I_{N_k}
$$

for all $n\ge N_k$.

Polynomial rings in finitely many variables over a field are standard examples. Noetherianity captures the principle that indefinitely accumulating algebraic constraints eventually stops creating genuinely new ideals.

### 6.2 Synchronization theorem

**Theorem 6.3 (Synchronized certificate–key stabilization).** Let $(P_n)$ be audited certificates of one proposition with non-increasing costs. Let $(I_n)$ be an ascending chain of ideals in a commutative Noetherian ring. Then there exists $N$ such that for every $n\ge N$,

$$
C(P_n)=C(P_N)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
I_n=I_N.
$$

**Proof sketch.** By Theorem 5.2, choose $N_p$ after which certificate cost is constant. By Noetherianity, choose $N_k$ after which the ideal chain is constant. Set

$$
N=\max\{N_p,N_k\}.
$$

For $n\ge N$, both $n$ and $N$ are at least $N_p$, hence $C(P_n)=C(P_{N_p})=C(P_N)$. Likewise, both are at least $N_k$, hence $I_n=I_{N_k}=I_N$. $\square$

The theorem synchronizes two independent termination mechanisms. The proof component uses the well-ordering of $\mathbb{N}$; the key component uses the ascending-chain condition. No causal dependence between the two schedules is required beyond their sharing a round index.

## 7. Explicit audit trail for the irrationality of $\sqrt{2}$

### 7.1 The certified claim

**Theorem 7.1 (Irrationality of $\sqrt{2}$).** There do not exist integers $a$ and $b\ne0$ such that

$$
\sqrt{2}=\frac{a}{b}.
$$

**Proof sketch.** Suppose $\sqrt{2}=a/b$ with integers $a,b$, $b\ne0$, chosen in lowest terms. Squaring gives $a^2=2b^2$, so $a^2$ is even and therefore $a$ is even. Write $a=2k$. Then $4k^2=2b^2$, so $b^2=2k^2$ and $b$ is even. Thus $a$ and $b$ share a factor of $2$, contradicting lowest terms. $\square$

### 7.2 Four certificates

Let $A$ be an audit label for the proposition that $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational. Define

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_3&=\operatorname{restate}(A,
      \operatorname{restate}(A,
      \operatorname{restate}(A,\operatorname{hyp}(A)))),\\
P_2&=\operatorname{restate}(A,
      \operatorname{restate}(A,\operatorname{hyp}(A))),\\
P_1&=\operatorname{restate}(A,\operatorname{hyp}(A)),\\
P_0&=\operatorname{hyp}(A).
\end{aligned}
$$

Each tree is paired with a witness of Theorem 7.1. Because every subtree inside a restatement has conclusion $A$, redundant-restatement elimination gives

$$
P_3\longrightarrow P_2\longrightarrow P_1\longrightarrow P_0.
$$

**Proposition 7.2 (Measured costs).** The four costs are

$$
C(P_3)=7,
\qquad C(P_2)=5,
\qquad C(P_1)=3,
\qquad C(P_0)=1.
$$

**Proof sketch.** A chain with $r$ restatement nodes above one hypothesis has length $r+1$, depth $r$, and no named lemmas. Its cost is therefore $(r+1)+r=2r+1$. Substituting $r=3,2,1,0$ gives $7,5,3,1$. $\square$

**Proposition 7.3 (Normal endpoint).** No permitted refinement starts from $P_0$.

**Proof sketch.** The tree $P_0$ is a bare hypothesis leaf. It has no restatement, no named-lemma node, no modus-ponens node, and no proper subtree. Therefore none of the primitive or contextual rules applies. $\square$

This is normality relative to Definition 3.1. It is not a theorem that $P_0$ is a globally shortest proof of irrationality in every encoding. The audit tree labels and measures a certificate while the independent witness carries the mathematics of Theorem 7.1.

## 8. Algorithms

### 8.1 Computing tree statistics

A postorder traversal computes $L(P)$, $D(P)$, and $M(P)$ simultaneously. At a leaf, return either $(1,0,0)$ or $(1,0,1)$. At a restatement, increment length and depth. At modus ponens, sum lengths and lemma counts and take the maximum child depth.

**Proposition 8.1 (Complexity of cost computation).** For a tree with $n$ nodes, audited cost can be computed in $O(n)$ time and $O(h)$ auxiliary stack space, where $h$ is tree height.

**Proof sketch.** The traversal visits each node once and performs constant work there. The recursion stack contains at most one root-to-leaf path. $\square$

### 8.2 Bottom-up simplification

The following routine performs one comprehensive pass.

1. Leave hypotheses unchanged.
2. Replace every named-lemma leaf by a hypothesis leaf with the same formula.
3. At a modus-ponens node, recursively simplify both children and strip one outer restatement from either simplified child.
4. At a restatement node, recursively simplify the child. If the simplified child already has the wrapper’s conclusion, return the child; otherwise retain the wrapper.

**Theorem 8.2 (One-pass conclusion preservation).** For every proof tree $P$, the simplified tree $S(P)$ satisfies

$$
\operatorname{concl}(S(P))=\operatorname{concl}(P).
$$

**Proof sketch.** Use structural induction. The leaf cases are immediate. A modus-ponens node retains its outer conclusion label. For a restatement, either the wrapper remains or the equality test certifies that its removal preserves the label. $\square$

**Theorem 8.3 (One-pass cost non-increase).** For every proof tree $P$,

$$
C(S(P))\le C(P).
$$

**Proof sketch.** Structural induction shows separately that simplification does not increase $W=L+M$ or depth. Inlining decreases $M$; stripping restatements decreases length and cannot increase depth; recursive calls satisfy the induction hypotheses. Add the resulting inequalities. $\square$

With constant-time formula-label comparison, a pass takes $O(n)$ time. If formulas are represented structurally without hashing, equality checks contribute their own cost. Repeatedly applying $S$ until the tree is unchanged terminates whenever every changing pass strictly lowers cost. A direct implementation can often reach the corresponding normal form in one bottom-up pass because it simplifies children before parents.

### 8.3 Detecting stabilization

For a finite observed cost trace $c_0,\ldots,c_m$, first check that it is non-increasing. The earliest observed stable suffix begins at the least index $N$ such that $c_N=c_{N+1}=\cdots=c_m$. This is an empirical property of the supplied prefix, not a proof about unobserved future rounds.

For a finite ideal protocol represented by canonical generator sets, an analogous scan finds the earliest common observed suffix at which both cost and ideal representation are unchanged. The abstract theorem supplies eventual existence for infinite schedules under its hypotheses; a finite algorithm identifies a stage only from the data it receives.

## 9. Applications to cryptographic certificate engineering

### 9.1 Transcript compression

A certificate transcript may contain repeated annotations, administrative wrappers, and named references whose statements are already available. The rewrite calculus removes such redundancies while preserving the advertised conclusion. Lower length can reduce serialization cost; lower depth can improve parallel verification latency; lower named-lemma count can shrink dependency manifests.

### 9.2 Stable protocol state

Theorem 6.3 is a liveness statement about observables. If key knowledge grows monotonically as ideals and certificates never increase in audited cost, there is a round after which neither observable changes. This can justify a transition from an adaptive setup phase to a steady-state verification phase.

### 9.3 Audit reproducibility

Because every permitted improvement is a local tree rewrite, a party can publish both an optimized certificate and a rewrite trace from an earlier version. Auditors need not trust an opaque optimizer: each step can be checked for matching shape, preserved conclusion, and lower cost.

### 9.4 Limits of cryptographic interpretation

Conclusion preservation is weaker than preservation of a cryptographic protocol’s security. A zero-knowledge transcript carries distributions, challenges, responses, and simulation obligations not represented by a propositional conclusion alone. Before using refinement in such a protocol, one must prove that each rewrite commutes with transcript interpretation and preserves completeness, soundness or knowledge extraction, and the relevant privacy notion. The present model isolates the syntactic layer on which those stronger obligations can be stated.

## 10. Discussion and limitations

### 10.1 Termination is not confluence

A terminating rewrite system can have multiple normal forms. Different choices of local rewrite may lead to distinct endpoints. Uniqueness requires a confluence argument, often obtained by proving that local critical pairs can be joined. No such property is assumed here.

### 10.2 Normality is not global minimality

A normal tree has no outgoing permitted rewrite. It may still be larger than another tree with the same conclusion if the rewrite basis cannot reach that tree. Thus the claim that every trajectory reaches “the simplest proof” is unjustified without stronger hypotheses connecting local rules to a global optimization criterion.

### 10.3 Kolmogorov complexity is outside the metric

The cost $C(P)$ is computable and syntactic. Kolmogorov complexity is the length of the shortest program producing an object relative to a universal machine and is not computable in general. Natural-number descent ensures a minimum among costs reached by a schedule, not a Kolmogorov-minimal description.

### 10.4 Qualitative versus quantitative stabilization

Noetherianity ensures that an ideal chain stabilizes but does not, by itself, provide a practical bound on the first stable stage. Quantitative bounds require additional information such as generator degree, number of variables, and the rule by which ideals grow.

### 10.5 Tree syntax versus shared directed acyclic graphs

Real proof systems often share subproofs. Expanding a shared directed acyclic graph into a tree can duplicate nodes and distort cost. A graph-based extension should count shared lemmas once, track fan-out, and define rewrites that preserve sharing. Its termination measure may need a multigraded or lexicographic form.

## 11. Future work

First, one can augment the rewrite basis with commuting conversions and study critical pairs. Termination is already available; a local diamond theorem could lead to confluence and uniqueness of normal forms up to harmless renaming.

Second, normalization distance deserves separate study. An explicit family of polynomial-size certificates may have superpolynomial shortest paths to normal form under every fixed finite local basis. Such a result would distinguish compact endpoints from efficient routes.

Third, multigraded Noetherian methods may yield explicit bounds. For polynomial rings with bounded numbers and degrees of generators, Gröbner-theoretic control of leading-term ideals could provide primitive-recursive estimates for common stabilization stages.

Fourth, cryptographic semantics should be layered over the tree calculus. For commitment-based protocols, each rewrite should be tested against completeness, special soundness, extraction, honest-verifier zero knowledge, and verifier complexity.

Fifth, cost models can be calibrated to deployment. Weighted metrics such as

$$
C_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(P)=\alpha L(P)+\beta D(P)+\gamma M(P)
$$

with positive natural coefficients retain the descent argument when each rewrite strictly lowers a suitable component and does not increase the others. Empirical weights could reflect bandwidth, latency, or trust-management costs.

## 12. Conclusion

Concrete local refinement turns certificate improvement into a mathematical process rather than a metaphor. The audit language has four constructors, a transparent cost $C=L+D+M$, and a finite family of conclusion-preserving rewrites. Every rewrite strictly decreases cost, so strict refinement is well-founded and has no infinite path. Non-increasing cost schedules, including idle rounds, eventually become numerically constant.

When the same protocol carries an ascending chain of key ideals in a commutative Noetherian ring, algebraic stabilization and certificate stabilization share a finite stage. The $\sqrt{2}$ example displays the mechanism explicitly through costs $7,5,3,1$ and a normal leaf.

The correct conclusion is not that local simplification always discovers a universally shortest proof. It is that a carefully specified certificate calculus admits safe improvement, termination, and synchronization with algebraic protocol state. This foundation makes the stronger questions—confluence, normalization complexity, quantitative bounds, and security preservation—precise enough to investigate.
