# Computational Evidence — Skip Justification

Computational evidence was not used because the formalized results in this contribution
are structural identities, not a newly inferred numerical universal claim:

- the Hodge statement is an implication from an explicit identification of an
  enumerative count with the existing curve-moduli invariant;
- the SYZ statements are exact binomial-coefficient identities for torus cohomology;
- the zeta statements are exact rational-function identities plus the already formalized
  Weil-pair theorem;
- the point-count congruence is already proved for every dimension and integer parameter.

Small-case tables or an OEIS lookup would therefore provide weaker evidence than the
kernel-checked universal proofs and would not test the missing geometric premise.  In
particular, there is no canonical ungraded finite sequence called “the number of rational
curves on an arbitrary Calabi–Yau”: such counts depend on curve class and genus and can be
infinite without further qualifications.  The Lean theorem consequently exposes, rather
than numerically guesses, the required identification hypothesis.
