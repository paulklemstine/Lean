# Fibonacci Pythagorean Triples: A Closed-Form Family Bridging Additive Number Theory and Euclidean Geometry

## Abstract

We study an infinite family of Pythagorean triples generated by four consecutive Fibonacci numbers. Writing $F_n$ for the $n$-th Fibonacci number, define
$$a = F_n F_{n+3},\qquad b = 2 F_{n+1} F_{n+2},\qquad c = F_{n+1}^2 + F_{n+2}^2.$$
We prove that $(a,b,c)$ is a Pythagorean triple for every $n$, that its hypotenuse is itself the odd-indexed Fibonacci number $c = F_{2n+3}$, and that the triangle's area equals the product of the four generating Fibonacci numbers, $\tfrac12 ab = F_n F_{n+1} F_{n+2} F_{n+3}$. The construction is exactly Euclid's parametrization $(p^2-q^2,\,2pq,\,p^2+q^2)$ specialized to the consecutive generators $p = F_{n+2}$, $q = F_{n+1}$, and every identity reduces, after applying the Fibonacci recurrence, to a two-variable polynomial identity in $F_n$ and $F_{n+1}$. We further record the perimeter and inradius in closed Fibonacci form, exhibit the odd-indexed Fibonacci numbers as an infinite set of Pythagorean hypotenuses satisfying the linear recurrence $c_{n+1} = 3c_n - c_{n-1}$, and formulate several conjectures on primitivity, inradius-integrality, and asymptotic shape. The results connect the additive structure of the Fibonacci recurrence to the multiplicative, sum-of-squares structure of Euclidean right triangles.

**Keywords:** Fibonacci numbers, Pythagorean triples, Euclid parametrization, sum of squares, golden ratio, right triangles.

## 1. Introduction

Two of the oldest objects in mathematics are Pythagorean triples — positive integers $(a,b,c)$ with $a^2+b^2=c^2$ — and the Fibonacci sequence
$$F_0 = 0,\quad F_1 = 1,\quad F_{n+2} = F_n + F_{n+1}.$$
The famous small triples $3$–$4$–$5$ and $5$–$12$–$13$ have hypotenuses $5$ and $13$, both Fibonacci numbers. This is not accidental. In this paper we develop the complete family behind these examples: a construction that feeds four consecutive Fibonacci numbers into Euclid's classical parametrization and produces a right triangle every one of whose defining quantities has a closed Fibonacci form.

The central phenomenon is a *dictionary* between two normally distinct worlds. The Fibonacci sequence is an additive object, defined by a linear recurrence; Pythagorean triples live in the multiplicative world of squares. The bridge is Euclid's formula together with two elementary consequences of the recurrence — that the difference and the sum of consecutive Fibonacci numbers are again Fibonacci numbers. Once this substitution is made, all identities in the family become polynomial identities in the two "seed" values $F_n$ and $F_{n+1}$.

### Summary of results

For all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, with $a, b, c$ as above:

1. **(Pythagorean identity.)** $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$.
2. **(Fibonacci hypotenuse.)** $c = F_{n+1}^2 + F_{n+2}^2 = F_{2n+3}$; equivalently $a^2 + b^2 = F_{2n+3}^2$.
3. **(Euclidean bridge.)** The integer triple $(a, b, F_{2n+3})$ satisfies the Pythagorean relation over $\mathbb{Z}$, i.e. it is a bona fide Pythagorean triple.
4. **(Area identity.)** $ab = 2\,F_n F_{n+1} F_{n+2} F_{n+3}$, so the triangle's area is $F_n F_{n+1} F_{n+2} F_{n+3}$.
5. **(Nondegeneracy.)** For $n \ge 1$ all three sides are strictly positive; the only degenerate case is $n = 0$, giving $(0, 2, 2)$.

We also derive the perimeter $P = 2F_{n+2}F_{n+3}$ and inradius $r = F_n F_{n+1}$ in closed form (Section 5), and study the hypotenuse subsequence (Section 6).

## 2. Definitions and preliminaries

Throughout, $F_n$ denotes the $n$-th Fibonacci number with the convention $F_0 = 0$, $F_1 = 1$, and $F_{n+2} = F_n + F_{n+1}$.

**Definition 2.1 (The Fibonacci triple).** For $n \in \mathbb{N}$ define
$$a_n := F_n\, F_{n+3},\qquad b_n := 2\,F_{n+1} F_{n+2},\qquad c_n := F_{n+1}^2 + F_{n+2}^2.$$
When $n$ is understood we write $a, b, c$.

**Definition 2.2 (Pythagorean triple).** A triple of integers $(x, y, z)$ is *Pythagorean* if $x^2 + y^2 = z^2$. It is *primitive* if additionally $\gcd(x, y, z) = 1$.

We will use two elementary lemmas about consecutive Fibonacci numbers.

**Lemma 2.3 (Difference and sum of consecutive terms).** For all $n$,
$$F_{n+2} - F_{n+1} = F_n, \qquad F_{n+1} + F_{n+2} = F_{n+3}.$$

*Proof.* The first is the recurrence $F_{n+2} = F_n + F_{n+1}$ rearranged; the second is the recurrence with index shifted by one, $F_{n+3} = F_{n+1} + F_{n+2}$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Lemma 2.4 (Fibonacci addition law).** For all $i, j \ge 1$,
$$F_{i+j} = F_i\,F_{j+1} + F_{i-1}\,F_j.$$

*Proof.* Standard; by induction on $j$ using the recurrence, or via the matrix identity $\begin{psmallmatrix}1&1\\1&0\end{psmallmatrix}^{k} = \begin{psmallmatrix}F_{k+1}&F_k\\F_k&F_{k-1}\end{psmallmatrix}$ and $M^{i+j} = M^i M^j$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

## 3. The Euclid parametrization behind the family

The construction is best understood as Euclid's parametrization specialized to Fibonacci generators.

**Proposition 3.1 (Euclid form).** Let $p = F_{n+2}$ and $q = F_{n+1}$. Then
$$a_n = p^2 - q^2,\qquad b_n = 2pq,\qquad c_n = p^2 + q^2.$$

*Proof.* By Lemma 2.3, $p - q = F_{n+2} - F_{n+1} = F_n$ and $p + q = F_{n+2} + F_{n+1} = F_{n+3}$. Hence
$$p^2 - q^2 = (p-q)(p+q) = F_n F_{n+3} = a_n.$$
Directly, $2pq = 2F_{n+1}F_{n+2} = b_n$ and $p^2 + q^2 = F_{n+1}^2 + F_{n+2}^2 = c_n$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

Proposition 3.1 immediately reduces every identity in the family to algebra in $p$ and $q$, and — after eliminating $p = F_n + F_{n+1}$ and $q = F_{n+1}$ via the recurrence — to a polynomial identity in the two seeds $x := F_n$ and $y := F_{n+1}$. This is the mechanism that makes the whole family exact.

## 4. Main results

### 4.1 The Pythagorean identity

**Theorem 4.1 (Pythagorean identity).** For all $n \in \mathbb{N}$,
$$a_n^2 + b_n^2 = c_n^2.$$

*Proof.* By Proposition 3.1, with $p = F_{n+2}$, $q = F_{n+1}$,
$$a_n^2 + b_n^2 = (p^2 - q^2)^2 + (2pq)^2 = p^4 - 2p^2q^2 + q^4 + 4p^2q^2 = p^4 + 2p^2q^2 + q^4 = (p^2+q^2)^2 = c_n^2.$$
Equivalently, substituting $F_{n+2} = F_n + F_{n+1}$ and $F_{n+3} = F_{n+1} + F_{n+2}$ turns the claim into the polynomial identity
$$\big(x(x+2y)\big)^2 + \big(2y(x+y)\big)^2 = \big(y^2 + (x+y)^2\big)^2$$
in $x = F_n$, $y = F_{n+1}$, which expands identically on both sides. $\qquad\blacksquare$

### 4.2 The hypotenuse is an odd-indexed Fibonacci number

**Theorem 4.2 (Odd-index sum of squares).** For all $n \in \mathbb{N}$,
$$F_{n+1}^2 + F_{n+2}^2 = F_{2n+3}.$$
Consequently $c_n = F_{2n+3}$.

*Proof.* Apply Lemma 2.4 with $i = j = n+1$:
$$F_{2n+2} = F_{(n+1)+(n+1)} = F_{n+1}F_{n+2} + F_n F_{n+1}.$$
More directly, the classical identity $F_{2m+1} = F_m^2 + F_{m+1}^2$ (the odd-index case of the addition law, taking $i = m+1$, $j = m$ so that $F_{2m+1} = F_{m+1}^2 + F_m F_{m+1} + F_m F_{m+1}\cdots$) gives, with $m = n+1$,
$$F_{2n+3} = F_{2(n+1)+1} = F_{n+1}^2 + F_{n+2}^2.$$
Hence $c_n = F_{n+1}^2 + F_{n+2}^2 = F_{2n+3}$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Corollary 4.3.** $a_n^2 + b_n^2 = F_{2n+3}^2$. In particular the hypotenuses of the family are exactly the odd-indexed Fibonacci numbers $F_3, F_5, F_7, \dots = 2, 5, 13, 34, 89, \dots$

*Proof.* Combine Theorems 4.1 and 4.2. $\qquad\blacksquare$

### 4.3 The bridge to Euclidean geometry

**Theorem 4.4 (Integer Pythagorean triple).** For every $n$, the triple $(a_n, b_n, F_{2n+3})$, regarded as integers, satisfies
$$a_n^2 + b_n^2 = F_{2n+3}^2,$$
and hence is a Pythagorean triple in the standard sense.

*Proof.* Immediate from Corollary 4.3; the natural-number identity casts unchanged to $\mathbb{Z}$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

This theorem is the formal bridge: the additive number-theoretic objects $F_n$ produce a geometric object $(a_n, b_n, c_n)$ realizing a right triangle with integer sides.

### 4.4 The area identity

**Theorem 4.5 (Area identity).** For all $n$,
$$a_n\, b_n = 2\,F_n F_{n+1} F_{n+2} F_{n+3},$$
so the area of the right triangle with legs $a_n, b_n$ is
$$\mathrm{Area} = \tfrac12\, a_n b_n = F_n F_{n+1} F_{n+2} F_{n+3}.$$

*Proof.* Directly from the definitions,
$$a_n b_n = (F_n F_{n+3})\cdot(2 F_{n+1}F_{n+2}) = 2\,F_n F_{n+1} F_{n+2} F_{n+3}.$$
Dividing by two gives the area. $\qquad\blacksquare$

### 4.5 Nondegeneracy

**Theorem 4.6 (Positivity).** For $n \ge 1$, all three sides are strictly positive: $a_n > 0$, $b_n > 0$, $c_n > 0$. The only degenerate member is $n = 0$, giving $(a_0, b_0, c_0) = (0, 2, 2)$.

*Proof.* For $n \ge 1$ each Fibonacci factor $F_n, F_{n+1}, F_{n+2}, F_{n+3}$ is positive (since $F_k > 0$ for $k \ge 1$), so $a_n, b_n$ are products of positive integers and $c_n$ is a sum of positive squares. For $n = 0$, $a_0 = F_0 F_3 = 0\cdot 2 = 0$, while $b_0 = 2F_1F_2 = 2$ and $c_0 = F_1^2 + F_2^2 = 2$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

## 5. Perimeter, inradius, and the shape of the triangles

Two further quantities close in Fibonacci form.

**Proposition 5.1 (Perimeter).** $P_n := a_n + b_n + c_n = 2\,F_{n+2}F_{n+3}.$

*Proof.* In Euclid form $P = (p^2 - q^2) + 2pq + (p^2 + q^2) = 2p^2 + 2pq = 2p(p + q)$. With $p = F_{n+2}$ and $p + q = F_{n+3}$ (Lemma 2.3), $P = 2F_{n+2}F_{n+3}$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Proposition 5.2 (Inradius).** The inradius of the right triangle is $r_n = \tfrac12(a_n + b_n - c_n) = F_n F_{n+1}.$

*Proof.* For any right triangle with legs $a, b$ and hypotenuse $c$, the inradius is $r = (a + b - c)/2$. In Euclid form, $a + b - c = (p^2 - q^2) + 2pq - (p^2 + q^2) = 2pq - 2q^2 = 2q(p - q)$, so $r = q(p - q)$. With $q = F_{n+1}$ and $p - q = F_n$, $r_n = F_{n+1} F_n$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Remark 5.3 (Asymptotic shape).** Since $F_{n+1}/F_n \to \varphi = (1+\sqrt5)/2$, we have $F_{n+3}/F_n \to \varphi^3$ and $F_{n+1}F_{n+2}/F_n^2 \to \varphi\cdot\varphi^2 = \varphi^3$, so the leg ratio
$$\frac{b_n}{a_n} = \frac{2F_{n+1}F_{n+2}}{F_n F_{n+3}} \longrightarrow \frac{2\varphi^3}{\varphi^3} = 2.$$
Thus the family converges in *shape* to a fixed right triangle with leg ratio $2$ (acute angles approximately $26.57^\circ$ and $63.43^\circ$), while growing without bound in size. The area-to-perimeter ratio is $\mathrm{Area}/P = \tfrac12 F_n F_{n+1}$, which diverges, confirming unbounded growth.

## 6. The hypotenuse subsequence

By Corollary 4.3 the hypotenuses are $c_n = F_{2n+3}$, the odd-indexed Fibonacci numbers from $F_3$ onward.

**Proposition 6.1 (Linear recurrence for hypotenuses).** The sequence $c_n = F_{2n+3}$ satisfies
$$c_{n+1} = 3c_n - c_{n-1}\qquad (n \ge 1),$$
with $c_0 = 2$, $c_1 = 5$.

*Proof.* The bisected Fibonacci sequence $G_n := F_{2n+k}$ (fixed parity $k$) satisfies the second-order recurrence with characteristic polynomial $t^2 - 3t + 1$, whose roots are $\varphi^2$ and $\varphi^{-2}$ (since $\varphi^2 + \varphi^{-2} = 3$ and $\varphi^2\cdot\varphi^{-2} = 1$). Indeed, from the addition law $F_{m+2} = 3F_m - F_{m-2}$ for the even-step recurrence: $F_{m+2} + F_{m-2} = L_2 F_m = 3F_m$ where $L_2 = 3$ is the second Lucas number. Setting $m = 2n+3$ gives $c_{n+1} + c_{n-1} = 3c_n$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

Thus the hypotenuses $2, 5, 13, 34, 89, 233, \dots$ form an exactly solvable integer sequence independent of any reference to triangles, and $n \mapsto F_{2n+3}$ is strictly increasing.

## 7. Algorithms

We record the elementary algorithms used to compute and verify the family. All run in linear or better arithmetic complexity in $n$.

**Algorithm A (Triple generator).** Given $n$, compute $F_n, \dots, F_{n+3}$ by iterating the recurrence, then output $(a, b, c) = (F_n F_{n+3},\, 2F_{n+1}F_{n+2},\, F_{n+1}^2 + F_{n+2}^2)$. Cost: $O(n)$ additions (or $O(\log n)$ with fast doubling), plus $O(1)$ multiplications.

**Algorithm B (Identity verifier).** For a range $0 \le n \le N$, generate each triple and check $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$, $c = F_{2n+3}$, $ab = 2F_nF_{n+1}F_{n+2}F_{n+3}$, $a+b+c = 2F_{n+2}F_{n+3}$, and $(a+b-c)/2 = F_nF_{n+1}$. Cost: $O(N)$ triples, $O(1)$ big-integer operations each.

**Algorithm C (Hypotenuse recurrence).** Generate the odd-index Fibonacci hypotenuses directly by $c_0 = 2$, $c_1 = 5$, $c_{n+1} = 3c_n - c_{n-1}$, avoiding the full Fibonacci sequence. Cost: $O(N)$ additions/subtractions.

## 8. Applications and connections

- **A parametrized source of right triangles.** The family gives an explicit, closed-form infinite list of right triangles whose auxiliary invariants (area, perimeter, inradius) are all simple products of the generating Fibonacci numbers — convenient for constructing test cases, exercises, or examples in number-theoretic geometry.
- **Odd-indexed Fibonacci numbers as hypotenuses.** Corollary 4.3 characterizes an entire subsequence of Fibonacci numbers as Pythagorean hypotenuses, and Proposition 6.1 makes that subsequence linearly generable.
- **A worked bridge between additive and multiplicative structures.** The construction is a compact illustration of how a purely additive recurrence identity ($F_{n+2} - F_{n+1} = F_n$) can be transported, via Euclid's parametrization, into multiplicative and geometric statements ($a^2 + b^2 = c^2$, area $= \prod F$).
- **Golden-ratio asymptotics of shape.** The convergence of the leg ratio to a fixed value ties the discrete family to the continuous limit $\varphi$, linking to the broader theory of Fibonacci asymptotics.

## 9. Discussion

The distinctive feature of this family is completeness: there is no error term and no exceptional behavior beyond the single degenerate triangle at $n = 0$. Every quantity of interest reduces, via Proposition 3.1 and the Fibonacci recurrence, to a two-variable polynomial identity in the seeds $F_n$ and $F_{n+1}$. This makes the family fully transparent and each identity mechanically checkable.

Two structural remarks. First, the family is *not* the family of all Pythagorean triples with Fibonacci hypotenuse: it is exactly Euclid's construction with consecutive-Fibonacci generators, which is what forces the hypotenuse to be $F_{2n+3}$. Second, the same substitution principle applies to any pair of generators obeying a linear recurrence; consecutive Fibonacci numbers are the sharpest case because both the difference $p-q$ and the sum $p+q$ of the generators are again members of the sequence, which is special to the Fibonacci shift.

## 10. Future directions

The following conjectures arise directly from the family $a = F_n F_{n+3}$, $b = 2F_{n+1}F_{n+2}$, $c = F_{n+1}^2 + F_{n+2}^2 = F_{2n+3}$.

**Conjecture 10.1 (Primitivity dichotomy governed by index parity).** The triple $(a, b, c)$ is primitive precisely when $n \not\equiv 1 \pmod 3$; when $n \equiv 1 \pmod 3$ the greatest common divisor of the three entries is exactly $2$. The mechanism: primitivity of a Euclid triple $(p^2-q^2, 2pq, p^2+q^2)$ is controlled by the parity of the generators $p = F_{n+2}$, $q = F_{n+1}$, and consecutive Fibonacci numbers are both odd exactly when neither index is a multiple of $3$ — the positions where $F$ is even recur with period $3$. The strong-divisibility law $\gcd(F_m, F_n) = F_{\gcd(m,n)}$ together with the exact even-index pattern should settle the parity of the generators completely.

**Conjecture 10.2 (Inradius-integrality).** Every triangle in the family has integer inradius $r = (a + b - c)/2$, and $r = F_n F_{n+1}$. (This is Proposition 5.2 above, promoted from computation to a structural statement: the inradius, a geometric quantity, collapses to a bare product of the two smaller consecutive Fibonacci numbers.)

**Conjecture 10.3 (Hypotenuses are exactly the odd-index Fibonacci numbers, and they interleave).** The map $n \mapsto F_{2n+3}$ is a strictly increasing bijection from $\mathbb{N}$ onto the odd-indexed Fibonacci numbers $\ge F_3$, and consecutive hypotenuses satisfy $c_{n+1} = 3c_n - c_{n-1}$. The bisected sequence $F_{2n+1}$ obeys a second-order recurrence with characteristic root $\varphi^2$, so the hypotenuses form an exactly solvable integer sequence.

**Conjecture 10.4 (A perimeter–area law linking two Fibonacci products).** Perimeter $P = a + b + c$ and area $A = \tfrac12 ab$ satisfy $A = F_n F_{n+1} F_{n+2} F_{n+3}$ and $P = 2 F_{n+2} F_{n+3}$, so $A/P = \tfrac12 F_n F_{n+1}$. Both perimeter and area factor through consecutive Fibonacci products, so any of their normalized ratios is a pure ratio of Fibonacci numbers whose limit is a fixed power of the golden ratio; for instance the shape ratio $F_n F_{n+1}/(F_{n+2}F_{n+3}) \to \varphi^{-4}$.
