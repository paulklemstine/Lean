## Abstract

We study a discrete model of social decision systems in which a *frame* is a finite
set of atoms in the cyclic group $\mathbb{Z}_N$ and the *incoherence index* of a
frame is the length of its shortest nonempty zero-sum sequence of atoms. A natural
"threshold conjecture" asserts that for every $k \ge 1$ and every half-electorate
$n \ge 2k+1$, some *maximal* frame on $N = 2n$ voters attains incoherence index
exactly $2k+2$. We prove that this literal threshold is **false at the boundary**.
The obstruction is structural: the index of a frame is bounded above by the additive
order of each of its atoms (Lemma 1), so any index strictly exceeding $N/2$ forces
every atom to be a generator of $\mathbb{Z}_N$ (Lemma 2). At the boundary
$N = 4k+2$ the target value $2k+2 = N/2 + 1$ lies in the open interval $(N/2, N)$,
which we conjecture is *forbidden* for maximal frames on even electorates. We make
this rigorous at $k = 1$: no maximal frame on $N = 6$ voters has incoherence index
$4$ (Theorem A), refuting the literal conjecture already at $(k, n) = (1, 3)$. We
complement this with the positive side of the theory: the single-generator frame
realizes index exactly $N$ (Lemma 5), this is the greatest attainable index on $N$
states (Theorem C), every even $n \ge 4$ is realized by a maximal frame (Theorem B),
parity of atoms forces parity of the index (Theorem D), and the index spectrum is
unbounded (Theorem E). Combined with a companion cofinite-realization result, the
emerging picture is that every even index is realizable cofinitely but never down to
the naïve threshold. All results are formalized and machine-checked.

## 1. Introduction

A recurring temptation in combinatorics is to conjecture that a desirable extremal
configuration appears as soon as the ambient size crosses a simple, closed-form
threshold. Such conjectures are attractive precisely because thresholds are easy to
state. They are also frequently wrong at the boundary, where the arithmetic of the
underlying structure imposes obstructions invisible at the level of the formula.

This paper records one such phenomenon in a discrete model of social choice. The
model is deliberately minimal: collective decision states form a cyclic group
$\mathbb{Z}_N$, a voting frame is a finite set of atoms, and the fragility of a frame
is measured by the length of its shortest self-cancelling (zero-sum) coalition. The
quantity of interest, the *incoherence index*, is a girth-like invariant of the
Cayley structure generated by the atoms.

The motivating "v19d" threshold conjecture asserts a clean onset: incoherence index
$2k+2$ should be realizable by a maximal frame on $2n$ voters whenever
$n \ge 2k+1$. Our main contribution is a proof that the literal threshold fails, and
an identification of the structural reason it must fail — a forbidden interval
$(N/2, N)$ for even electorates — together with the first rigorous instance of that
gap.

### Contributions

1. **Order bound (Lemma 1).** $\operatorname{idx}(F) \le \operatorname{ord}(a)$ for
   every atom $a \in F$.
2. **Generators-only principle (Lemma 2).** If $\operatorname{idx}(F) > N/2$ then
   every atom of $F$ generates $\mathbb{Z}_N$.
3. **Boundary obstruction (Theorem A).** No maximal frame on $N = 6$ voters has
   incoherence index $4$; hence the literal threshold fails at $(k, n) = (1, 3)$.
4. **Positive realization theory (Theorems B–E, Lemmas 4–5).** Sharp realization of
   index $N$, maximality, parity, and unboundedness of the spectrum.

### The Cayley-graph viewpoint

It is worth fixing the geometric picture that makes every proof in this paper
transparent. Given a frame $F \subseteq \mathbb{Z}_N$, form the directed Cayley
graph $\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_N, F)$ whose vertices are the residues $0, 1, \dots,
N-1$ and whose edges send $r \mapsto r + a$ for each atom $a \in F$. A balanced
obstruction of length $m$ is exactly a closed walk of length $m$ that starts and
ends at $0$. The incoherence index is therefore the *return girth* of the graph:
the length of the shortest nontrivial closed walk through the identity. With this
translation, Lemma 1 says "the monochromatic cycle that repeats a single edge-color
$a$ closes after $\operatorname{ord}(a)$ steps," and the entire boundary argument
becomes a statement about which return-girths are achievable. The computational
primitive in §6 is then nothing but a breadth-first search for this return girth.

## 2. Definitions

Throughout, $N \ge 1$ and $\mathbb{Z}_N = \mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}$ is the additive
cyclic group of order $N$. For $a \in \mathbb{Z}_N$, $\operatorname{ord}(a)$ denotes
its additive order, the least $m \ge 1$ with $m \cdot a = 0$; equivalently
$\operatorname{ord}(a) = N / \gcd(\tilde a, N)$ for any integer representative
$\tilde a$.

**Definition 1 (Frame).** A *standard social decision frame* on $N$ voters is a
finite set of atoms
$$F \subseteq \mathbb{Z}_N, \qquad F \in \mathrm{Finset}(\mathbb{Z}_N).$$

**Definition 2 (Balanced obstruction).** A list $\ell = (x_1, \dots, x_m)$ of
elements of $\mathbb{Z}_N$ is a *perfectly balanced obstruction* for $F$ if
$$\ell \ne [\,], \qquad x_i \in F \ \text{for all } i, \qquad \sum_{i=1}^m x_i = 0 \ \text{in } \mathbb{Z}_N.$$
We write $\mathrm{IsBalanced}(F, \ell)$ for this predicate.

**Definition 3 (Length set).** The *set of balanced lengths* of $F$ is
$$\mathrm{balancedLengths}(F) = \{\, m \in \mathbb{N} : \exists \ell,\ \mathrm{IsBalanced}(F, \ell) \wedge |\ell| = m \,\}.$$

**Definition 4 (Incoherence index).** The *incoherence index* of $F$ is
$$\operatorname{idx}(F) = \inf\bigl(\mathrm{balancedLengths}(F)\bigr) \in \mathbb{N},$$
with the convention $\inf \varnothing = 0$ (so a frame with no balanced obstruction
has index $0$).

**Definition 5 (Maximality).** A frame $F$ is *maximal* if its atoms generate the
whole group:
$$\overline{\langle F \rangle} = \mathbb{Z}_N \qquad (\text{i.e. } \mathrm{AddSubgroup.closure}(F) = \top).$$

> **Remark.** Because the index is the length of a shortest closed walk in the Cayley
> graph $\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_N, F)$ that returns to $0$ using at least one edge,
> it is a *girth-type* invariant. The order bound below is the statement that the
> "monochromatic" cycle through a single generator gives an upper estimate.

## 3. General obstruction lemmas

### Lemma 1 (Order bound: `incoherenceIndex_le_addOrderOf`)

*For every frame $F$ on $N \ge 1$ voters and every atom $a \in F$,*
$$\operatorname{idx}(F) \le \operatorname{ord}(a).$$

**Proof sketch.** Let $r = \operatorname{ord}(a)$. The constant list
$(\underbrace{a, a, \dots, a}_{r})$ (the replicate of $a$ to length $r$) is nonempty,
has all entries equal to $a \in F$, and sums to $r \cdot a = 0$ by definition of the
order. Hence $r \in \mathrm{balancedLengths}(F)$, and since the index is the
infimum of that set, $\operatorname{idx}(F) \le r$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

### Lemma 2 (Generators-only principle: `atoms_generate_of_index_gt_half`)

*Let $F$ be a frame on $N \ge 1$ voters with $\operatorname{idx}(F) > N/2$ (integer
division). Then every atom $a \in F$ satisfies $\operatorname{ord}(a) = N$; i.e.
every atom is a generator of $\mathbb{Z}_N$.*

**Proof sketch.** Fix $a \in F$. By Lemma 1, $\operatorname{ord}(a) \ge
\operatorname{idx}(F) > N/2$. The additive order always divides the group order, so
$\operatorname{ord}(a) \mid N$; write $N = \operatorname{ord}(a) \cdot q$. A positive
divisor of $N$ that exceeds $N/2$ must equal $N$: if $\operatorname{ord}(a) < N$ then
$q \ge 2$, forcing $\operatorname{ord}(a) = N/q \le N/2$, a contradiction. Hence
$\operatorname{ord}(a) = N$. (In the formal proof the divisor case split on $q \in
\{0, 1, \ge 2\}$ is discharged by linear arithmetic.) $\qquad\blacksquare$

### Lemma 3 (Generators of $\mathbb{Z}_6$: `addOrderOf_six_eq`)

*For $a \in \mathbb{Z}_6$, if $\operatorname{ord}(a) = 6$ then $a = 1$ or $a = 5$.*

**Proof sketch.** Finite case check over the six residues: the orders are
$\operatorname{ord}(0) = 1$, $\operatorname{ord}(1) = 6$, $\operatorname{ord}(2) = 3$,
$\operatorname{ord}(3) = 2$, $\operatorname{ord}(4) = 3$, $\operatorname{ord}(5) = 6$.
Only $1$ and $5$ have order $6$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

## 4. Main result: the boundary obstruction

### Theorem A (Boundary obstruction at $k = 1$: `boundary_obstruction_k1`)

*There is no frame $F$ on $N = 6$ voters that is both maximal and has incoherence
index $4$:*
$$\neg\, \exists F \subseteq \mathbb{Z}_6,\quad \mathrm{IsMaximal}(F) \ \wedge\ \operatorname{idx}(F) = 4.$$
*Since $4 = 2 \cdot 1 + 2$ and $3 = 2 \cdot 1 + 1$, this refutes the literal v19d
threshold "$n \ge 2k+1 \Rightarrow$ index $2k+2$ realizable" already at $k = 1$,
$n = 3$, $N = 2n = 6$.*

**Proof sketch.** Suppose toward a contradiction that such an $F$ exists.

1. **Every atom is a generator.** We have $N/2 = 3 < 4 = \operatorname{idx}(F)$, so by
   Lemma 2 every $a \in F$ has $\operatorname{ord}(a) = 6$.

2. **Extract a length-4 obstruction.** Because $\operatorname{idx}(F) = 4$, the value
   $4$ lies in $\mathrm{balancedLengths}(F)$; in fact the infimum of a nonempty set
   of naturals is attained, so there is a balanced list $\ell$ with $|\ell| = 4$,
   $\ell \ne [\,]$, all entries in $F$, and $\sum \ell = 0$.

3. **Entries are $1$ or $5$.** Each entry $x \in \ell$ lies in $F$, hence has order
   $6$ (step 1), hence equals $1$ or $5$ by Lemma 3.

4. **Both $1$ and $5$ occur in $F$.** If $\ell$ used only $1$'s, then $\ell$ would be
   the replicate $(1,1,1,1)$ with sum $4 \ne 0$ in $\mathbb{Z}_6$, contradicting
   balance; symmetrically $\ell$ cannot use only $5$'s (sum $20 = 2 \ne 0$). Hence
   $\ell$ contains both a $1$ and a $5$, and since all entries lie in $F$ we get
   $1 \in F$ and $5 \in F$.

5. **A length-2 obstruction exists.** The list $(1, 5)$ is nonempty, has both entries
   in $F$, and sums to $1 + 5 = 6 = 0$ in $\mathbb{Z}_6$, so it is balanced. Thus
   $2 \in \mathrm{balancedLengths}(F)$ and $\operatorname{idx}(F) \le 2 < 4$,
   contradicting $\operatorname{idx}(F) = 4$.

The contradiction proves the claim. Note the maximality hypothesis is not used in the
argument: the obstruction holds for *every* frame on $6$ voters, maximal or not; it is
retained only to match the conjecture verbatim. $\qquad\blacksquare$

### The forbidden interval (companion conjecture)

The mechanism of Theorem A is not special to $N = 6$. At the boundary $N = 4k + 2$
the target $2k + 2 = N/2 + 1$ lies in the open interval $(N/2, N)$. Lemma 2 shows any
maximal frame with index in this range is built only from generators, and a pair of
distinct generators of an even cyclic group admits a short balanced sequence (length
$\le N/2$), pulling the index back below $N/2$; a single generator overshoots to $N$
(Lemma 5). This squeezing motivates:

> **Conjecture (forbidden interval).** For every even $N$ and every maximal frame
> $F$ on $N$ voters, either $\operatorname{idx}(F) \le N/2$ or
> $\operatorname{idx}(F) = N$; the open interval $(N/2, N)$ is never realized.

Theorem A is the $N = 6$ instance. Concrete refutations of the literal threshold at
$(k, n) = (1, 3), (2, 5), (3, 7)$ correspond to $N = 6, 10, 14$ with targets
$4, 6, 8 = N/2 + 1$. Establishing the conjecture in general requires the sharp
two-generator bound $\operatorname{idx}(\{a, b\}) \le N/2$ (see §7).

## 5. Positive realization theory

The negative boundary result is the sharp counterpart to a positive realization
theory, which we record for context. These results are independently formalized.

### Lemma 4 (Universal upper bound: `incoherenceIndex_le`)

*For $N \ge 1$ and any nonempty frame $F$ on $N$ voters, $\operatorname{idx}(F) \le N$.*

**Proof sketch.** Pick any atom $a \in F$. The replicate $(a, \dots, a)$ of length
$N$ has sum $N \cdot a = 0$ (since $\operatorname{ord}(a) \mid N$), so $N \in
\mathrm{balancedLengths}(F)$ and the infimum is at most $N$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

### Lemma 5 (Single-generator index: `incoherenceIndex_singleton_one`)

*For $N \ge 1$, $\operatorname{idx}(\{1\}) = N$.*

**Proof sketch.** ($\le$) Lemma 4 with $F = \{1\}$. ($\ge$) Any balanced list over
$\{1\}$ is a replicate $(1, \dots, 1)$ of some length $m \ge 1$ whose sum is $m$ in
$\mathbb{Z}_N$; balance forces $N \mid m$, hence $m \ge N$. Taking the infimum gives
$\operatorname{idx}(\{1\}) \ge N$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

### Theorem B (Realization of even indices: `realization_even`)

*For every even $n \ge 4$ there is a maximal frame $F$ on $n$ states with
$\operatorname{idx}(F) = n$.*

**Proof sketch.** Take $F = \{1\}$. It is maximal because $1$ generates $\mathbb{Z}_n$,
and $\operatorname{idx}(\{1\}) = n$ by Lemma 5. (The construction in fact works for
every $n \ge 1$; the evenness hypothesis is retained only to match the original
statement.) $\qquad\blacksquare$

### Theorem C (Sharpness: `incoherenceIndex_isGreatest`)

*For even $n \ge 4$, the value $n$ is the greatest incoherence index attained by any
nonempty frame on $n$ states, and it is attained (by $\{1\}$).*

**Proof sketch.** Attainment is Lemma 5; the upper bound on every competitor is
Lemma 4. Together these say $n$ is the maximum of
$\{\, m : \exists F \text{ nonempty}, \operatorname{idx}(F) = m \,\}$.
$\qquad\blacksquare$

### Theorem D (Parity transfer: `even_incoherenceIndex`)

*Let $2 \mid N$ and let $F$ be a frame all of whose atoms are "odd," i.e. map to $1$
under the parity character $\pi : \mathbb{Z}_N \to \mathbb{Z}_2$. Then
$\operatorname{idx}(F)$ is even.*

**Proof sketch.** Apply the homomorphism $\pi$ to any balanced list $\ell$: since
$\pi$ is additive and $\sum \ell = 0$, we get $\sum \pi(x_i) = 0$ in $\mathbb{Z}_2$.
Each $\pi(x_i) = 1$, so the sum is $|\ell| \cdot 1 = 0$ in $\mathbb{Z}_2$, forcing
$|\ell|$ even. Every balanced length is therefore even; in particular so is the
shortest, the index. (If no balanced obstruction exists the index is $0$, which is
even.) $\qquad\blacksquare$

### Theorem E (Unbounded spectrum: `incoherence_unbounded`)

*For every $N_0$ there exist $N$ and a frame $F$ on $N$ voters with
$\operatorname{idx}(F)$ even and $\operatorname{idx}(F) > N_0$.*

**Proof sketch.** Take $N = 2(N_0 + 2)$ and $F = \{1\}$. By Lemma 5 the index is
$N = 2(N_0 + 2)$, which is even and strictly exceeds $N_0$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

## 6. Algorithms

We isolate the computational primitive underlying every result.

### Algorithm 1: Shortest balanced obstruction via breadth-first search

The incoherence index is the length of the shortest nonempty walk in the Cayley graph
$\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_N, F)$ that starts and ends at $0$. A breadth-first search
from $0$ that records the first re-arrival at $0$ using at least one edge computes the
index exactly.

```
INPUT:  modulus N, frame F ⊆ {0,...,N-1}
OUTPUT: incoherenceIndex(F)  (0 if no balanced obstruction)

dist[0..N-1] ← ∞;  dist[0] ← 0;  queue ← [0]
while queue nonempty:
    r ← pop(queue)
    for a in F:
        s ← (r + a) mod N
        if s = 0:                 return dist[r] + 1   # first re-arrival at 0
        if dist[s] = ∞:
            dist[s] ← dist[r] + 1;  push(queue, s)
return 0
```

Because BFS dequeues vertices in nondecreasing distance, the first edge that returns
to $0$ yields the minimum length. Complexity: $O(N \cdot |F|)$ time, $O(N)$ space.

### Algorithm 2: Boundary-failure certifier

To certify that a target index $t = N/2 + 1$ on an even electorate $N$ is
unattainable by maximal frames, enumerate the generators of $\mathbb{Z}_N$ (units),
form every nonempty maximal subset built from them, and verify (via Algorithm 1) that
none attains $t$. This realizes the proof of Theorem A computationally and extends it
as evidence for the forbidden-interval conjecture.

## 7. A fully worked numerical example

To make the boundary obstruction concrete, we tabulate the incoherence index of
every relevant frame on $N = 6$ voters. Recall the additive orders in
$\mathbb{Z}_6$: $\operatorname{ord}(0) = 1$, $\operatorname{ord}(1) = 6$,
$\operatorname{ord}(2) = 3$, $\operatorname{ord}(3) = 2$, $\operatorname{ord}(4) =
3$, $\operatorname{ord}(5) = 6$. By Lemma 2 a frame of index $4 > 3 = N/2$ must use
only the order-$6$ atoms $\{1, 5\}$, so only three nonempty generator-frames are
candidates:

| Frame $F$ | atoms' orders | shortest obstruction | $\operatorname{idx}(F)$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\{1\}$ | $6$ | $(1,1,1,1,1,1)$ | $6$ |
| $\{5\}$ | $6$ | $(5,5,5,5,5,5)$ | $6$ |
| $\{1, 5\}$ | $6, 6$ | $(1, 5)$ | $2$ |

The achievable indices among generator-frames are exactly $\{2, 6\}$; the value
$4$ never appears. This is the entire content of Theorem A laid out by hand, and it
exhibits the squeezing phenomenon directly: one generator overshoots to $N = 6$,
while the pair of distinct generators undershoots to $2 \le N/2$. No configuration
lands in the open interval $(3, 6)$.

For contrast, the *cofinite* side is equally concrete. Take $k = 1$ (target index
$2k+2 = 4$) but enlarge the electorate to $N = 8$. The two-atom frame
$\{1, 5\}$ on $\mathbb{Z}_8$ is maximal ($\gcd(1, 8) = 1$) and its shortest
obstruction is $(1, 1, 1, 5)$ with sum $1+1+1+5 = 8 \equiv 0 \pmod 8$, giving
$\operatorname{idx} = 4$ — exactly the target that was forbidden at $N = 6$. The
index $4$ that is unattainable at the boundary becomes attainable once the
electorate clears the obstruction. This is the experimental shadow of the
companion cofinite-realization theorem.

## 8. Relation to zero-sum combinatorics

The incoherence index belongs to a well-studied family of zero-sum invariants of
finite abelian groups. The condition that a nonempty sequence of group elements
sums to the identity is the defining feature of *zero-sum sequences*, and the
study of the shortest or longest such sequences over a fixed generating set is the
subject of the Davenport-constant and Erdős–Ginzburg–Ziv circle of problems. What
distinguishes the present setting is the restriction to a *prescribed atom set*
$F$ rather than all of $\mathbb{Z}_N$, and the focus on *shortest* (rather than
guaranteed) zero-sums. Restricting to a small $F$ is what makes the index a
sensitive structural invariant of the frame: enlarging $F$ can only shorten the
shortest obstruction, so the extremal large indices are attained by the *sparsest*
maximal frames — single generators — which is precisely the content of Theorem C.
This monotonicity ($F \subseteq F' \Rightarrow \operatorname{idx}(F') \le
\operatorname{idx}(F)$ when both have an obstruction) is the conceptual reason the
spectrum has a clean top value $N$ and a delicate structure just below it.

## 9. Discussion and future work

The boundary obstruction, paired with the companion cofinite-realization theorem
`cofinite_realization`, yields the following picture: every even target index
$2k + 2$ is realizable by a maximal frame on all *sufficiently large* electorates,
but **not** down to the naïve threshold $n = 2k+1$. The honest onset threshold is
strictly larger than $2k+1$. Theorem A is the first rigorous witness of the gap, and
the forbidden-interval conjecture of §4 explains it uniformly.

Outstanding directions:

- **Forbidden interval $(N/2, N)$ for all even $N$.** Upgrade Theorem A from $N = 6$
  to all even $N$. The generator-forcing Lemma 2 is in hand; the missing ingredient
  is the sharp two-generator bound below.
- **Tight two-generator bound.** For even $N$ and distinct units $a \ne b$,
  $\operatorname{idx}(\{a, b\}) \le N/2$, with equality at $\{1, N/2 + 1\}$. The
  shortest relation reduces, after multiplying by $a^{-1}$, to a single congruence
  $p + qt \equiv 0$ amenable to a three-distance / continued-fraction estimate.
- **Exact onset threshold.** Pin down $n_0(k)$ with $2k+1 < n_0(k) \le 2k^2+2k+1$
  such that index $2k+2$ is realized iff $n \ge n_0(k)$, conjecturally with
  $n_0(k)/k^2 \to 2$.
- **Parity dichotomy of the realizable spectrum.** For odd $N$ exactly one value
  above $N/2$ (namely $(N+1)/2$) is omitted, whereas for even $N$ the whole block
  $\{N/2 + 1, \dots, N-1\}$ is omitted — the gap above $N/2$ is governed by the parity
  of $N$.

## 10. Conclusion

A clean threshold conjecture in a discrete social-choice model is refuted at its very
first instance by a structural obstruction: the incoherence index is bounded by the
additive order of every atom, so any index above $N/2$ forces all atoms to be
generators, and on six voters the only generators ($1$ and $5$) cancel in length two.
No maximal frame on six voters has incoherence index four. The negative result sits
beside a complete positive realization theory (sharp index $N$, maximality, parity,
unboundedness) and a companion cofinite-realization theorem, together revealing that
realization is a *cofinite* — not a *threshold* — phenomenon.
