## Abstract

We develop a complete structure theory for social aggregators that are linear
over the min-plus (tropical) semiring. An *aggregator* on a finite electorate
$\iota$ with $n = |\iota|$ voters is a map $F : \mathbb{R}^{\iota} \to \mathbb{R}$
sending a profile of real scores to a social score. We impose three axioms:
preservation of coordinatewise minima (tropical additivity), equivariance under
diagonal translation (tropical scalar multiplication), and normalization
$F(\mathbf 0) = 0$.

Our first main theorem classifies all such aggregators: every one is a *tropical
linear form* $F(x) = \min_{i \in S}(x_i + \delta_i)$ for a unique nonempty
support $S \subseteq \iota$ and unique weights $\delta_i \ge 0$ on $S$
normalized by $\min_{i \in S}\delta_i = 0$. The support consists exactly of the
*active* voters, those whom some downward perturbation renders pivotal. We show
that the sharper guess — that such an $F$ must be a minimum of coordinate
projections — is **false**, exhibiting the two-voter aggregator
$W(x_0,x_1) = \min(x_0, x_1+1)$ which satisfies all three axioms yet returns
$W(1,-1) = 0$, a value submitted by nobody; and we prove that adding the
social-choice axiom of *selectivity* (the social score is always some individual
score) forces all weights to vanish and recovers the minimum-of-projections form.

Our second main theorem identifies the Arrow threshold. The family of
*dependence sets* (coalitions whose scores determine the outcome) of a tropical
aggregator is exactly the principal filter generated by its support. This family
satisfies the ultrafilter dichotomy if and only if the support is a singleton, if
and only if $F$ is a dictatorship. Anonymity forces the opposite extreme: full
support and zero weights, i.e. $F(x) = \min_i x_i$. We then quantify the
anonymity/decisiveness tradeoff exactly: for support of size $k$, the orbit of
the minimal decisive coalition under relabellings has $\binom{n}{k}$ members, of
which exactly one is decisive, so the defect is precisely $1 - 1/\binom{n}{k}$.

Our third contribution is geometric. The profile space decomposes into convex
polyhedral chambers indexed by the support; labelling each profile by its
decisive coalition produces a polyhedral complex whose closed cells are the
finite intersections of chambers. We prove that the labelling is an
inclusion-reversing bijection onto the face lattice, that the walls containing
the cell of a label $T$ are exactly the edges of the complete graph on $T$ — the
flat of the graphic matroid of $K_S$ spanned by $K_T$, with a star of any vertex
of $T$ as a basis — and that the cell of $T$ has codimension exactly $|T| - 1$.
We establish a *single-voter exchange law*: from any profile in the chamber of
$i$, changing the score of the single voter $j$ moves the profile first onto the
wall labelled $\{i,j\}$ and then into the open cell labelled $\{j\}$.

Our fourth contribution is analytic. The log-sum-exp family
$F_\varepsilon(x) = -\varepsilon\log\sum_{i \in S}e^{-(x_i+\delta_i)/\varepsilon}$
is a family of smooth positive classical aggregators satisfying the explicit
sandwich $F(x) - \varepsilon\log|S| \le F_\varepsilon(x) \le F(x)$; it therefore
converges to $F$ as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$, uniformly in the profile, with
rate $\varepsilon\log|S|$. We prove that the deformation is *exact* — $F_\varepsilon = F$
for all $\varepsilon > 0$ — if and only if the support is a singleton. Thus
dictatorship is precisely the rigid point of Maslov dequantization.

**Keywords:** tropical semiring, min-plus algebra, social choice, Arrow's
theorem, ultrafilter, principal filter, polyhedral chamber complex, graphic
matroid, Maslov dequantization, log-sum-exp.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Arrow's theorem as an ultrafilter statement

The modern proof of Arrow's impossibility theorem proceeds by studying the family
of *decisive coalitions* of a social welfare function satisfying unanimity and
independence of irrelevant alternatives. One shows that this family is closed
upward, closed under intersection, and satisfies the dichotomy that every
coalition or its complement belongs to it: in short, it is an ultrafilter on the
electorate. On a finite set every ultrafilter is principal, generated by a single
point, and that point is the dictator. The Boolean structure of the ranking
domain is thus the engine of the impossibility.

This paper asks what remains when the Boolean structure is replaced by a
different semiring structure: the min-plus, or *tropical*, semiring
$(\mathbb{R} \cup \{+\infty\}, \min, +)$. Profiles become vectors of real scores
rather than rankings, and the aggregation axioms become tropical linearity
conditions. The results below show that the entire architecture survives, in a
form that is sharper and more quantitative than the Boolean original: instead of
an all-or-nothing impossibility, one obtains a complete classification with
dictatorship sitting as one distinguished point in a polyhedral continuum, and a
suite of exact numerical invariants measuring the distance from it.

### 1.2 Tropical algebra

Recall the tropical (min-plus) semiring: on $\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}$ define
$a \oplus b = \min(a,b)$ and $a \odot b = a+b$. Then $\oplus$ is associative,
commutative and idempotent with neutral element $+\infty$; $\odot$ is associative
and commutative with neutral element $0$; and $\odot$ distributes over $\oplus$.
The semiring has no additive inverses. A *tropical linear form* in variables
$x_i$ is $\bigoplus_{i \in S} \delta_i \odot x_i = \min_{i \in S}(x_i + \delta_i)$;
tropical polynomials are exactly the concave piecewise-linear functions with
integral slopes, and tropical geometry studies their non-differentiability loci.

Tropical arithmetic arises as the $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ limit of ordinary
arithmetic conjugated by $a \mapsto e^{-a/\varepsilon}$, a procedure known as
*Maslov dequantization*; Section 7 makes this precise in our setting.

### 1.3 Overview of results

Section 2 fixes the axioms and derives elementary consequences. Section 3 proves
the classification theorem via a dip calculus, together with uniqueness, the
refutation of the projection conjecture, the selectivity correction, and the
anonymity theorem. Section 4 develops dependence filters and the tropical Arrow

develops the chamber complex, its face lattice, its matroid structure, the
codimension formula, and the single-voter exchange law. Section 7 treats
dequantization. Section 8 gives algorithms, Section 9 applications, and
Section 10 discussion and open problems.

## 2. Setting and axioms

Throughout, $\iota$ is a finite nonempty type of *voters*, $n = |\iota|$; a
**profile** is a vector $x \in \mathbb{R}^{\iota}$, interpreted as a vector of
real scores (lower means more critical); an **aggregator** is a function
$F : \mathbb{R}^{\iota} \to \mathbb{R}$. For profiles $x, y$ we write
$(x \wedge y)_i = \min(x_i, y_i)$ and $c\mathbf 1$ for the constant profile.

**Definition 2.1 (Tropical aggregator).** An aggregator $F$ is a *tropical
aggregator* if:

* **(T1) tropical additivity:** $F(x \wedge y) = \min\bigl(F(x), F(y)\bigr)$ for all $x,y$;
* **(T2) translation equivariance:** $F(x + c\mathbf 1) = F(x) + c$ for all $x$ and $c \in \mathbb{R}$;
* **(T3) normalization:** $F(\mathbf 0) = 0$.

Axiom (T1) says $F$ is a homomorphism for tropical addition; (T2) is equivariance
for tropical scalar multiplication, and is exactly the condition making $F$
well-defined as a map from the tropical projective space
$\mathbb{R}^{\iota}/\mathbb{R}\mathbf 1$ to the tropical line
$\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{R}$; (T3) is a harmless normalization.

**Lemma 2.2 (Constants).** $F(c\mathbf 1) = c$ for all $c \in \mathbb{R}$.

*Proof.* Apply (T2) to $x = \mathbf 0$ and use (T3). $\square$

**Lemma 2.3 (Monotonicity).** If $x_i \le y_i$ for all $i$, then $F(x) \le F(y)$.

*Proof.* Then $x \wedge y = x$, so by (T1), $F(x) = \min(F(x),F(y)) \le F(y)$. $\square$

Monotonicity is therefore not an extra axiom but a consequence of tropical
additivity — the tropical analogue of the fact that a linear map preserving a
cone is order-preserving.

**Lemma 2.4 (Finite tropical additivity).** For any nonempty finite family
$(y^{(1)},\dots,y^{(m)})$ of profiles,
$$F\Bigl(\textstyle\bigwedge_{a=1}^{m} y^{(a)}\Bigr) = \min_{1 \le a \le m} F\bigl(y^{(a)}\bigr).$$

*Proof.* Induction on $m$ using (T1). $\square$

## 3. Classification of finite min-plus aggregators

### 3.1 The dip calculus

**Definition 3.1 (Dip profile).** For a voter $i$ and $t \in \mathbb{R}$, let
$d_i(t)$ be the profile with $d_i(t)_i = t$ and $d_i(t)_j = 0$ for $j \ne i$.
Write $\varphi_i(t) = F(d_i(t))$.

**Lemma 3.2 (Range of a dip).** For $t \le 0$ one has $t \le \varphi_i(t) \le 0$.

*Proof.* $t\mathbf 1 \le d_i(t) \le \mathbf 0$ coordinatewise; apply
Lemmas 2.2, 2.3. $\square$

**Lemma 3.3 (Dip functional equation).** For $t \le s \le 0$,
$$\min\bigl(\varphi_i(t),\, s\bigr) \;=\; s + \varphi_i(t-s).$$

*Proof.* Coordinatewise, $d_i(t) \wedge s\mathbf 1$ has entry $t$ at $i$ (since
$t \le s$) and $s$ elsewhere (since $s \le 0$); that is,
$d_i(t) \wedge s\mathbf 1 = d_i(t-s) + s\mathbf 1$. Apply (T1) to the left side
and (T2) plus Lemma 2.2 to the right side. $\square$

**Definition 3.4 (Active voter).** Voter $i$ is *active* for $F$ if
$\varphi_i(t) < 0$ for some $t \le 0$.

**Proposition 3.5 (Shape of a dip).** Let $F$ be a tropical aggregator and $i$ a
voter.

1. If $i$ is not active then $\varphi_i(u) = 0$ for all $u \le 0$.
2. If $i$ is active, witnessed by $t_0 \le 0$ with $\varphi_i(t_0) < 0$, then
   $\delta_i := \varphi_i(t_0) - t_0$ satisfies $\delta_i \ge 0$ and
   $$\varphi_i(u) = \min(u + \delta_i,\ 0) \qquad \text{for all } u \le 0 .$$
   In particular $\delta_i$ does not depend on the witness $t_0$.

*Proof.* (1) is immediate from Lemma 3.2 and the definition. For (2),
$\delta_i \ge 0$ because $t_0 \le \varphi_i(t_0)$ by Lemma 3.2.

First suppose $t_0 \le u \le 0$. Apply Lemma 3.3 with $t = t_0$ and
$s = t_0 - u$, which satisfies $t_0 \le t_0 - u \le 0$, and note
$t_0 - (t_0-u) = u$:
$$\min\bigl(\varphi_i(t_0),\, t_0-u\bigr) = (t_0 - u) + \varphi_i(u).$$
If $\varphi_i(t_0) \le t_0 - u$ the left side is $\varphi_i(t_0)$, giving
$\varphi_i(u) = \varphi_i(t_0) - t_0 + u = u + \delta_i$, and this value is
$\le 0$ by the case hypothesis; otherwise the left side is $t_0-u$, giving
$\varphi_i(u) = 0$ and $u + \delta_i \ge 0$. In both cases
$\varphi_i(u) = \min(u+\delta_i,0)$.

Now suppose $u \le t_0$. Monotonicity gives $\varphi_i(u) \le \varphi_i(t_0) < 0$.
Applying the previous paragraph with the roles of $u$ and $t_0$ exchanged (i.e.
using $u$ as the witness and evaluating at $t_0$) yields
$\varphi_i(t_0) = \min(t_0 + (\varphi_i(u) - u), 0)$; since $\varphi_i(t_0) < 0$
the minimum is attained by the first branch, so
$\varphi_i(t_0) = t_0 + \varphi_i(u) - u$, i.e. $\varphi_i(u) = u + \delta_i$,
which is $< 0$, hence equals $\min(u+\delta_i,0)$. $\square$

Proposition 3.5 is the crux: the two axioms rigidify the one-parameter probe
$\varphi_i$ into a *clamped translate*, encoded by the single nonnegative number
$\delta_i$ — the depth to which voter $i$ must dip before being heard.

### 3.2 The representation theorem

**Theorem 3.6 (Classification of finite min-plus aggregators).** Let $\iota$ be
finite and nonempty and let $F$ be a tropical aggregator. Then there exist a
nonempty $S \subseteq \iota$ and $\delta : \iota \to \mathbb{R}$ with

* $\delta_i \ge 0$ for all $i \in S$;
* $\min_{i \in S}\delta_i = 0$;
* $S = \{i : i \text{ is active for } F\}$;

such that
$$F(x) = \min_{i \in S}\ (x_i + \delta_i) \qquad \text{for all } x \in \mathbb{R}^{\iota}.$$

*Proof sketch.* Let $S$ be the set of active voters and, for $i \in S$, let
$\delta_i \ge 0$ be the weight furnished by Proposition 3.5(2); for $i \notin S$
put $\delta_i = 0$ (unused).

*$S$ is nonempty.* If every voter were inactive, note that
$\bigwedge_{i \in \iota} d_i(-1) = -\mathbf 1$ coordinatewise (at coordinate $j$
the term with $i = j$ contributes $-1$). By Lemma 2.4 the left-hand side has
$F$-value $\min_i \varphi_i(-1) = 0$, whereas $F(-\mathbf 1) = -1$ by
Lemma 2.2 — a contradiction.

*The formula.* Fix a profile $x$ and set $A = \min_{i \in S}(x_i + \delta_i)$ and
$K = \max\{\max_j x_j,\ A\}$. For each voter $i$ define the profile $y^{(i)}$ by
$y^{(i)}_i = x_i$ and $y^{(i)}_j = K$ for $j \ne i$. Since $x_j \le K$ for all
$j$, one has $\bigwedge_{i} y^{(i)} = x$ coordinatewise. Moreover
$y^{(i)} = d_i(x_i - K) + K\mathbf 1$ with $x_i - K \le 0$, so by (T2) and
Proposition 3.5,
$$F\bigl(y^{(i)}\bigr) = K + \varphi_i(x_i-K) =
\begin{cases}\min(x_i + \delta_i,\ K), & i \in S,\\ K, & i \notin S.\end{cases}$$
Lemma 2.4 now gives $F(x) = \min_i F(y^{(i)})$. Every term is $\ge \min(A,K) = A$
because $A \le K$; and the term at a minimizer $i_0 \in S$ of $x_i + \delta_i$
equals $\min(A,K) = A$. Hence $F(x) = A$.

*Normalization of the weights.* Evaluating the formula at $x = \mathbf 0$ and
using (T3) gives $\min_{i \in S}\delta_i = 0$. $\square$

**Theorem 3.7 (Uniqueness of support and weights).** Suppose
$F(x) = \min_{i \in S}(x_i+\delta_i) = \min_{i \in T}(x_i+\gamma_i)$ for all $x$,
where $S,T$ are nonempty, $\delta \ge 0$ on $S$ with $\min_S \delta = 0$, and
$\gamma \ge 0$ on $T$ with $\min_T \gamma = 0$. Then $S = T$ and
$\delta_i = \gamma_i$ for all $i \in S$.

*Proof sketch.* The key computation is that any such representation predicts the
dip values
$$F\bigl(d_i(t)\bigr) = \begin{cases}\min(t+\delta_i,\ 0), & i \in S,\\ 0, & i \notin S,\end{cases}
\qquad (t \le 0).$$
Indeed, plugging $d_i(t)$ into the formula, the term at $i$ (if $i \in S$) is
$t + \delta_i$, while every other term is $\delta_j \ge 0$ and the minimum of
those is $0$ by normalization. Now fix $i \in S$ and choose
$t \le -(\delta_i + |\gamma_i| + 1)$, so deep that $t + \delta_i < 0$ and
$t + \gamma_i < 0$. Then the $\delta$-representation gives
$F(d_i(t)) = t + \delta_i < 0$, which forces $i \in T$ (else the
$\gamma$-representation would give $0$) and $t+\gamma_i = t+\delta_i$, i.e.
$\gamma_i = \delta_i$. Symmetry finishes. $\square$

The support $S$ is therefore an *intrinsic* invariant of $F$: it is the set of
active voters, the *irredundant tropical support*.

### 3.3 The literal projection conjecture is false

**Definition 3.8.** $F$ is a *minimum of coordinate projections* if there is a
nonempty $S$ with $F(x) = \min_{i \in S} x_i$ for all $x$.

**Theorem 3.9 (Counterexample).** On the two-voter electorate $\{0,1\}$ define
$$W(x_0,x_1) = \min\bigl(x_0,\ x_1 + 1\bigr).$$
Then $W$ is a tropical aggregator, but $W$ is not a minimum of coordinate
projections.

*Proof.* Axiom (T1): $\min(\cdot,\cdot)$ of the two branches is checked by
splitting into the four orderings of $x_0$ vs. $y_0$ and $x_1$ vs. $y_1$; each
case is a linear inequality. Axiom (T2): both branches shift by $c$, and the
minimum of two functions shifted by the same constant shifts by that constant.
Axiom (T3): $W(0,0) = \min(0,1) = 0$.

Not a minimum of projections: at $x = (1,-1)$ we get $W(x) = \min(1,0) = 0$. A
minimum of coordinate projections always returns one of the coordinates of $x$
(namely the value at a minimizing index), but $x_0 = 1$ and $x_1 = -1$, and
neither is $0$. $\square$

So the tropical axioms genuinely permit nonzero weights; the classification of
Theorem 3.6 is sharp and the naive projection statement is a strict overreach.
The obstruction is exactly the phenomenon that a weighted tropical form can
output a value nobody submitted.

**Definition 3.10 (Selectivity).** $F$ is *selective* if for every profile $x$
there is a voter $i$ with $F(x) = x_i$.

**Theorem 3.11 (Corrected classification).** Let $F(x) = \min_{i\in S}(x_i+\delta_i)$
be a normalized tropical representation as in Theorem 3.6. If $F$ is selective,
then $\delta_i = 0$ for all $i \in S$, and hence
$$F(x) = \min_{i \in S} x_i .$$

*Proof sketch.* Suppose $\delta_i > 0$ for some $i \in S$, and consider the dip
$d_i(t)$ with $t = -(\delta_i + 1)$. By the dip computation in Theorem 3.7,
$F(d_i(t)) = \min(t+\delta_i,0) = t+\delta_i = -1$. But the coordinates of
$d_i(t)$ are only $t = -(\delta_i+1)$ and $0$, and $-1$ equals neither (the
former because $\delta_i > 0$), contradicting selectivity. $\square$

Selectivity is the tropical form of the classical demand that the social outcome
be one of the submitted alternatives; it is exactly what converts "weighted
minimum" into "minimum".

### 3.4 Anonymity

**Theorem 3.12 (Anonymity forces unanimity).** Let $F(x) = \min_{i\in S}(x_i+\delta_i)$
be a normalized tropical representation, and suppose $F$ is *anonymous*:
$F(x \circ \sigma) = F(x)$ for every permutation $\sigma$ of the electorate and
every profile $x$. Then $S = \iota$ and $\delta \equiv 0$ on $S$; that is,
$$F(x) = \min_{i \in \iota} x_i .$$
In particular an anonymous tropical aggregator is never a dictatorship when
$n \ge 2$.

*Proof sketch.* Composing a dip with the transposition exchanging $a$ and $i$
turns $d_a(t)$ into $d_i(t)$, so anonymity gives $F(d_a(t)) = F(d_i(t))$ for all
$t \le 0$ and all $a,i$. By the dip computation of Theorem 3.7, this equality of
dip functions forces $a \in S \iff i \in S$ and $\delta_a = \delta_i$. Since $S$
is nonempty this gives $S = \iota$ and $\delta$ constant on $S$; normalization
$\min_S \delta = 0$ then forces that constant to be $0$. $\square$

## 4. Dependence sets, filters, and the Arrow threshold

**Definition 4.1 (Dependence set).** A coalition $C \subseteq \iota$ is a
*dependence set* (equivalently, a *decisive coalition*) for $F$ if for all
profiles $x,y$ with $x_i = y_i$ for every $i \in C$ we have $F(x) = F(y)$. Write
$\mathcal{D}(F)$ for the family of dependence sets.

Dependence sets are exactly the coalitions whose declarations already determine
the social score; the classical Arrow argument is a study of the combinatorics of
this family.

**Theorem 4.2 (Dependence is a principal filter).** Let
$F(x) = \min_{i\in S}(x_i+\delta_i)$ be a normalized tropical representation.
Then
$$\mathcal{D}(F) = \{ C \subseteq \iota : S \subseteq C \}.$$
That is, the dependence family is precisely the principal filter generated by the
tropical support.

*Proof sketch.* ($\supseteq$) If $S \subseteq C$ and $x,y$ agree on $C$, they
agree on $S$, and the formula only reads coordinates in $S$.

($\subseteq$) Let $C \in \mathcal{D}(F)$ and suppose some $i \in S$ has
$i \notin C$. Put $t = -(\delta_i+1) \le 0$. The profiles $\mathbf 0$ and
$d_i(t)$ agree on $C$ (they differ only at $i \notin C$), so decisiveness gives
$F(\mathbf 0) = F(d_i(t))$. But $F(\mathbf 0) = 0$ while, by the dip computation,
$F(d_i(t)) = \min(t+\delta_i,0) = -1$. Contradiction. $\square$

Consequently $\mathcal{D}(F)$ is precisely the collection of members of the
principal filter on $S$: closed upward, closed under finite intersection, proper,
and containing $\iota$ — all the ultrafilter properties except the dichotomy. The
dichotomy is exactly what pins down a dictator.

**Theorem 4.3 (Tropical Arrow theorem).** Let
$F(x)=\min_{i\in S}(x_i+\delta_i)$ be a normalized tropical representation. The
following are equivalent:

1. **(Ultrafilter dichotomy)** for every $C \subseteq \iota$, either
   $C \in \mathcal{D}(F)$ or $C^{\complement} \in \mathcal{D}(F)$;
2. **(Dictatorship)** there is a voter $i_0$ with $F(x) = x_{i_0}$ for all $x$;
3. **(Singleton support)** $|S| = 1$.

Equivalently, $\mathcal{D}(F)$ is (the set of members of) an ultrafilter if and
only if $F$ is a dictatorship.

*Proof.* By Theorem 4.2, $C \in \mathcal{D}(F) \iff S \subseteq C$.

$(1)\Rightarrow(3)$. Fix $a \in S$ and suppose $b \in S$ with $b \ne a$. Test the
dichotomy on $C = \{b\}^{\complement}$. If $C$ were decisive we would need
$S \subseteq C$, contradicting $b \in S$. So $C^{\complement} = \{b\}$ is
decisive, i.e. $S \subseteq \{b\}$, contradicting $a \in S$, $a \ne b$. Hence
$S = \{a\}$.

$(3)\Rightarrow(2)$. If $S = \{a\}$, normalization gives $\delta_a = 0$, so
$F(x) = x_a$.

$(2)\Rightarrow(1)$. A dictatorship has the representation with support
$\{i_0\}$ and zero weight; by uniqueness (Theorem 3.7) $S=\{i_0\}$, so
$\mathcal{D}(F) = \{C : i_0 \in C\}$, and for every $C$ exactly one of $C$,
$C^{\complement}$ contains $i_0$. $\square$

**Remark 4.4.** Theorem 4.3 exhibits the Arrow phenomenon as a statement about
the *cardinality of the support of a tropical linear form*. In the classical
setting the axioms conspire to force the ultrafilter property; here we see that
the ultrafilter property is a strictly stronger requirement than tropical
linearity, and precisely measures the collapse of the support to a point. The
aggregator $W$ of Theorem 3.9 has support $\{0,1\}$ and is a legitimate,
non-dictatorial tropical rule; the unanimity rule $\min_i x_i$ has support
$\iota$. The tropical axioms therefore admit an entire lattice of rules, indexed
by pairs (support, weights), with dictatorship a single distinguished point.

## 5. The exact anonymity defect

Anonymity and dictatorship are incompatible (Theorem 3.12), but that is
qualitative. We now measure the failure exactly.

**Definition 5.1 (Orbit of a coalition).** For a coalition $S$ let
$$\mathcal{O}(S) = \{ T \subseteq \iota : |T| = |S| \}.$$

**Proposition 5.2 (The orbit is a permutation orbit).** $T \in \mathcal{O}(S)$ if
and only if there is a permutation $\sigma$ of $\iota$ with $\sigma(S) = T$.
Moreover $|\mathcal{O}(S)| = \binom{n}{k}$ where $k = |S|$.

*Proof sketch.* A permutation preserves cardinality, giving one direction. For
the other, a bijection $S \to T$ (which exists since $|S|=|T|$) extends to a
permutation of $\iota$ because the complements also have equal cardinality; its
image of $S$ is contained in $T$ and has the same size, hence equals $T$. The
count is the number of $k$-subsets of an $n$-set. $\square$

**Proposition 5.3 (Uniqueness of the decisive member of the orbit).**
$$\{ T \in \mathcal{O}(S) : S \subseteq T \} = \{S\}.$$

*Proof.* If $|T| = |S|$ and $S \subseteq T$, then $T = S$ by cardinality. $\square$

**Theorem 5.4 (Exact anonymity–independence tradeoff).** Let
$F(x)=\min_{i\in S}(x_i + \delta_i)$ be a normalized tropical aggregator on $n$
voters with irredundant support of size $k = |S|$. Then, among the
$\binom{n}{k}$ coalitions in the permutation orbit of the minimal decisive
coalition $S$, exactly one — namely $S$ itself — is a dependence set of $F$;
the number of non-decisive members of the orbit is $\binom{n}{k} - 1$, and
$$\frac{\#\{T \in \mathcal{O}(S) : T \notin \mathcal{D}(F)\}}{|\mathcal{O}(S)|}
= 1 - \frac{1}{\binom{n}{k}} .$$
Being an identity, the bound is sharp.

*Proof.* By Theorem 4.2, $T \in \mathcal{D}(F) \iff S \subseteq T$. Combine with
Propositions 5.2 and 5.3 and divide, noting $\binom{n}{k} \ge 1$ since
$k \le n$. $\square$

**Interpretation.** The invariant $1 - 1/\binom{n}{k}$ is a *defect of
anonymity*. The two extremes are:

| support size $k$ | orbit size | defect |
|---|---|---|
| $1$ (dictatorship) | $n$ | $1 - 1/n$ |
| $k$ | $\binom{n}{k}$ | $1 - 1/\binom{n}{k}$ |
| $n$ (unanimity, anonymous) | $1$ | $0$ |

A dictatorship on a large electorate has defect close to $1$: essentially all
individuals in the orbit of the dictator are powerless. Unanimity has defect $0$:
its decisive coalition is the unique coalition of its size, so relabelling is
invisible. The defect is maximal, in a large electorate, when $k \approx n/2$.

## 6. Polyhedral geometry: chambers, cells, and the exchange law

Throughout this section fix a nonempty support $S$ and weights $\delta$, and
write $F(x) = \min_{i \in S}(x_i + \delta_i)$. The *tropical monomials* are the
affine functions $m_i(x) = x_i + \delta_i$, $i \in S$.

### 6.1 Chambers

**Definition 6.1 (Chamber).** For $i \in \iota$,
$$\mathcal{C}_i = \{ x \in \mathbb{R}^{\iota} : m_i(x) \le m_j(x) \ \ \forall j \in S \}.$$

**Theorem 6.2.** (i) Each $\mathcal{C}_i$ is a convex polyhedron. (ii) If
$F(x) = m_i(x)$ then $x \in \mathcal{C}_i$. (iii) $\bigcup_{i \in S}\mathcal{C}_i
= \mathbb{R}^{\iota}$. (iv) If $i \in S$ and $x \in \mathcal{C}_i$ then
$F(x) = m_i(x)$. (v) If $x \in \mathcal{C}_i \cap \mathcal{C}_j$ with
$i,j \in S$ then $m_i(x) = m_j(x)$. (vi) $F$ is concave on $\mathbb{R}^{\iota}$.

*Proof sketch.* (i) $\mathcal{C}_i$ is an intersection of the closed half-spaces
$\{m_i \le m_j\}$, each defined by an affine inequality; convexity follows since
for $a,b \ge 0$, $a+b=1$, the inequality $m_i \le m_j$ is preserved by convex
combination, using $a\delta + b\delta = \delta$. (ii) and (iv) are the definition
of the minimum together with the elementary bounds $F \le m_i$ and
$F \ge \min_j m_j$. (iii) holds because the minimum over the finite nonempty set
$S$ is attained. (v) is antisymmetry of $\le$. (vi) $F$ is a pointwise minimum of
affine functions, hence concave: for $a,b\ge 0$ with $a+b=1$ and every $j \in S$,
$a F(x) + b F(y) \le a\, m_j(x) + b\, m_j(y) = m_j(ax+by)$; taking the minimum
over $j \in S$ gives $aF(x)+bF(y) \le F(ax+by)$. $\square$

Thus $F$ is a concave, piecewise-affine function whose linearity domains are the
chambers, and on the chamber of $i$ it is the projection to coordinate $i$
shifted by $\delta_i$.

### 6.2 The complex of decisive coalitions

**Definition 6.3 (Decisive coalition at a profile).**
$$D(x) = \{ i \in S : m_i(x) = F(x) \}.$$

**Theorem 6.4.** (i) $D(x)$ is always nonempty. (ii) $i \in D(x)$ if and only if
$i \in S$ and $x \in \mathcal{C}_i$.

*Proof.* (i) The minimum over the finite nonempty $S$ is attained. (ii) is
Theorem 6.2(ii),(iv). $\square$

**Definition 6.5 (Closed cell).** For $T \subseteq \iota$,
$$\mathcal{F}_T = \{ x : T \subseteq D(x) \}.$$

**Theorem 6.6 (Cells are polyhedra).** For $T \subseteq S$,
$$\mathcal{F}_T = \bigcap_{i \in T}\mathcal{C}_i ,$$
which is convex; and on $\mathcal{F}_T$ all monomials indexed by $T$ agree:
$m_i(x) = m_j(x)$ for $i,j \in T$.

*Proof.* Immediate from Theorem 6.4(ii) and Theorem 6.2(i). The last statement
follows since both equal $F(x)$. $\square$

**Theorem 6.7 (The label is genuinely decisive).** Let $x$ be a profile and let
$y$ be any profile with $y_i = x_i$ for all $i \in D(x)$ and $y_i \ge x_i$ for
all $i$. Then $F(y) = F(x)$.

*Proof.* Pick $i \in D(x)$. Then $F(y) \le m_i(y) = m_i(x) = F(x)$. Conversely,
for every $j \in S$, $m_j(y) \ge m_j(x) \ge F(x)$, so $F(y) \ge F(x)$. $\square$

So the label is a decisive coalition in the operational sense: raising the scores
of everybody outside $D(x)$ cannot move the outcome.

**Theorem 6.8 (Completeness of the labelling).** For every nonempty
$T \subseteq S$ there is a profile $x$ with $D(x) = T$. Consequently the cells of
the complex are labelled exactly by the nonempty subcoalitions of the support.

*Proof.* Set $x_i = -\delta_i$ for $i \in T$ and $x_i = 1-\delta_i$ for
$i \notin T$; then $m_i(x) = 0$ for $i \in T$ and $m_i(x) = 1$ otherwise. Since
$T \ne \emptyset$ and $T \subseteq S$, $F(x) = 0$, and the monomials attaining
this value are exactly those indexed by $T$. $\square$

### 6.3 The face lattice

**Theorem 6.9 (Order anti-isomorphism).** For nonempty $T \subseteq S$ and
arbitrary $T'$,
$$\mathcal{F}_T \subseteq \mathcal{F}_{T'} \iff T' \subseteq T .$$
Consequently $T \mapsto \mathcal{F}_T$ is an injective, inclusion-reversing map
from the nonempty subcoalitions of $S$ onto the cells; strictly larger labels
give strictly smaller faces; and
$$\mathcal{F}_{T \cup T'} = \mathcal{F}_T \cap \mathcal{F}_{T'} .$$

*Proof sketch.* ($\Leftarrow$) is Theorem 6.6 and monotonicity of intersection.
($\Rightarrow$) Take, by Theorem 6.8, a profile $x$ with $D(x) = T$; then
$x \in \mathcal{F}_T \subseteq \mathcal{F}_{T'}$, so $T' \subseteq D(x) = T$. The
union formula is a restatement of $T \cup T' \subseteq D(x) \iff T \subseteq D(x)$
and $T' \subseteq D(x)$. $\square$

The face lattice of the complex is thus the lattice of nonempty subcoalitions of
$S$, order-reversed.

### 6.4 Walls, flats and the support matroid

**Definition 6.10 (Wall).** For voters $i,j$, the wall is
$H_{ij} = \{x : m_i(x) = m_j(x)\}$.

**Theorem 6.11 (Walls containing a cell are the edges inside its label).** For
nonempty $T \subseteq S$ and any $i, j$,
$$\mathcal{F}_T \subseteq H_{ij} \iff \bigl(i \in T \text{ and } j \in T\bigr) \text{ or } i = j .$$

*Proof.* If $i,j \in T$ this is Theorem 6.6; if $i = j$ it is trivial.
Conversely, suppose $i \ne j$ and, without loss of generality, $i \notin T$.
Refine the profile of Theorem 6.8: put $x_k = -\delta_k$ for $k \in T$, and for
$k \notin T$ choose $x_k = c_k - \delta_k$ with the $c_k$ pairwise distinct and
all strictly positive. Then $m_k(x) = 0$ on $T$ and $m_k(x) = c_k$ off $T$, so
$D(x) = T$, i.e. $x \in \mathcal{F}_T$; but $m_i(x) = c_i \neq m_j(x)$, since
$m_j(x)$ is either $0$ (if $j \in T$) or $c_j \ne c_i$. Hence
$x \notin H_{ij}$ and $\mathcal{F}_T \not\subseteq H_{ij}$. $\square$

Encoding each wall $H_{ij}$ as the edge $\{i,j\}$ of the complete graph $K_S$,
Theorem 6.11 says that the set of walls containing $\mathcal{F}_T$ is exactly the
edge set of the complete subgraph $K_T$. In the graphic matroid of $K_S$, the
edge sets of complete subgraphs on the parts of a partition are exactly the
*flats*; thus the cell of $T$ corresponds to the flat spanned by $K_T$, whose
rank is $|T| - 1$. We call this matroid the **support matroid** of $F$.

**Theorem 6.12 (A star is a basis of the flat).** For nonempty $T \subseteq S$
and any $i_0 \in T$,
$$\mathcal{F}_T = \mathcal{F}_{\{i_0\}} \cap \bigcap_{i \in T} H_{i i_0} .$$
Thus the $|T| - 1$ wall equations $m_i = m_{i_0}$, $i \in T \setminus \{i_0\}$,
already cut the cell out of the chamber of $i_0$: the star of $i_0$ in $K_T$
spans the flat, and has exactly the cardinality of its rank, hence is a basis.

*Proof.* ($\subseteq$) is Theorem 6.6. ($\supseteq$) If $x$ lies in the chamber
of $i_0$ then $F(x) = m_{i_0}(x)$; the wall equations then give $m_i(x) = F(x)$
for every $i \in T$, i.e. $T \subseteq D(x)$. $\square$

### 6.5 Codimension

**Definition 6.13 (Direction space).** For $T \subseteq \iota$,
$$V_T = \{ v \in \mathbb{R}^{\iota} : v \text{ is constant on } T \}
 = \{ v : v_i = v_j \ \forall i,j \in T\},$$
a linear subspace of $\mathbb{R}^{\iota}$.

**Theorem 6.14 (Dimension count).** For nonempty $T \subseteq \iota$,
$$\dim V_T + |T| = n + 1 , \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad \dim V_T = n - |T| + 1 .$$

*Proof.* Fix $i_0 \in T$ and consider the linear map
$L : \mathbb{R}^{\iota} \to \mathbb{R}^{T \setminus \{i_0\}}$,
$L(v)_i = v_i - v_{i_0}$. Its kernel is exactly $V_T$ (constancy on $T$ is
equivalent to equality with the value at $i_0$), and $L$ is surjective (prescribe
$v_{i_0} = 0$ and the required values on $T\setminus\{i_0\}$, arbitrarily
elsewhere). Rank–nullity gives $\dim V_T + (|T|-1) = n$. $\square$

**Theorem 6.15 (Codimension of a cell).** If $x, y \in \mathcal{F}_T$ then
$y - x \in V_T$; moreover for $T \subseteq S$ nonempty there is a profile in
$\mathcal{F}_T$ at which every monomial indexed outside $T$ is *strictly* larger
than the common value, so $\mathcal{F}_T$ is relatively open in the affine
subspace it spans. Hence the cell of $T$ has codimension exactly $|T| - 1$, the
rank of the corresponding flat of the support matroid.

*Proof sketch.* If $x,y \in \mathcal{F}_T$ then for $i,j\in T$,
$x_i + \delta_i = x_j + \delta_j$ and $y_i + \delta_i = y_j + \delta_j$;
subtracting gives $(y-x)_i = (y-x)_j$. The strict profile is the one of
Theorem 6.8 (values $0$ on $T$, $1$ off $T$), whose non-$T$ monomials exceed the
common value. Combining with Theorem 6.14 gives the codimension. $\square$

The codimension formula packages the geometry combinatorially: *the codimension
of a cell equals one less than the number of voters simultaneously decisive on
it.* Chambers ($|T| = 1$) are top-dimensional; walls ($|T| = 2$) have codimension
$1$; the deepest cell ($T = S$) has codimension $|S| - 1$.

### 6.6 The single-voter exchange law

**Theorem 6.16 (Exchange onto a wall).** Let $i \ne j$ be voters of $S$ and let
$x \in \mathcal{C}_i$. Define $y$ by changing only voter $j$'s score:
$$y_j = x_i + \delta_i - \delta_j, \qquad y_k = x_k \ (k \ne j).$$
Then $\{i,j\} \subseteq D(y)$; that is, $y$ lies on the wall between the chambers
of $i$ and $j$.

*Proof.* Since $y_i = x_i$, $m_i(y) = m_i(x)$; and $m_j(y) = x_i + \delta_i = m_i(x)$
by construction. For $k \ne j$, $m_k(y) = m_k(x) \ge m_i(x)$ because
$x \in \mathcal{C}_i$. Hence $F(y) = m_i(x)$ and both $i$ and $j$ attain it. $\square$

**Theorem 6.17 (Single-voter exchange).** With the hypotheses above, for every
$\varepsilon > 0$ the profile $z$ obtained from $x$ by setting
$$z_j = x_i + \delta_i - \delta_j - \varepsilon, \qquad z_k = x_k \ (k \ne j),$$
satisfies $D(z) = \{j\}$.

*Proof.* $m_j(z) = m_i(x) - \varepsilon$. For $k \in S$, $k \ne j$,
$m_k(z) = m_k(x) \ge m_i(x) > m_j(z)$. So the minimum is attained uniquely at
$j$. $\square$

**Corollary 6.18 (Adjacency of top-dimensional cells).** Any two chambers
$\mathcal{C}_i$, $\mathcal{C}_j$ ($i \ne j$ in $S$) share a codimension-one face,
namely the cell labelled $\{i,j\}$ (which is nonempty by Theorem 6.8), and the
passage from the open cell $\{i\}$ to the open cell $\{j\}$ is realized by
changing the score of the single voter $j$ across that face.

This is a genuine *exchange law*: adjacency in the complex corresponds to a
one-element modification, mirroring the basis-exchange axiom in the support
matroid. Socially: a single voter can always seize decisiveness on their own,
without coalition-building, by lowering their own score sufficiently.

## 7. Maslov dequantization: the classical limit

### 7.1 The smoothing family

**Definition 7.1 (Log-sum-exp).** For $\varepsilon > 0$, a nonempty finite
$S$ and $a : \iota \to \mathbb{R}$, set
$$L_\varepsilon(a) = -\varepsilon\,\log\!\left(\sum_{i\in S} \exp\!\left(-\frac{a_i}{\varepsilon}\right)\right).$$
The *dequantized aggregator* at scale $\varepsilon$ is
$$F_\varepsilon(x) = L_\varepsilon\bigl(x_\bullet + \delta_\bullet\bigr)
 = -\varepsilon \log \sum_{i \in S}\exp\!\left(-\frac{x_i+\delta_i}{\varepsilon}\right).$$

$F_\varepsilon$ is real-analytic, strictly monotone in each variable, and
translation-equivariant; it is the free energy of a Gibbs measure at temperature
$\varepsilon$ with energy levels $x_i + \delta_i$, and the softmin used
ubiquitously to smooth $\min$-pooling. It is *not* tropically additive: it is a
genuinely classical (positive, "$\log$-conjugated linear") object.

**Theorem 7.2 (Explicit dequantization sandwich).** Let $A = \min_{i \in S}a_i$.
For every $\varepsilon > 0$,
$$A - \varepsilon\log|S| \ \le\ L_\varepsilon(a)\ \le\ A .$$

*Proof.* Write $\Sigma = \sum_{i\in S}e^{-a_i/\varepsilon}$. Since every term is
positive and the term at a minimizer equals $e^{-A/\varepsilon}$,
$$e^{-A/\varepsilon} \ \le\ \Sigma\ \le\ |S|\, e^{-A/\varepsilon},$$
the upper bound because $a_i \ge A$ makes each term at most $e^{-A/\varepsilon}$.
Taking logarithms, $-A/\varepsilon \le \log\Sigma \le \log|S| - A/\varepsilon$;
multiplying by $-\varepsilon < 0$ reverses the inequalities and yields the
claim. $\square$

**Corollary 7.3 (Uniform convergence with explicit rate).** For all
$\varepsilon>0$ and all profiles $x$,
$$\bigl|F_\varepsilon(x) - F(x)\bigr| \le \varepsilon \log |S| ,$$
and consequently $F_\varepsilon(x) \to F(x)$ as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$,
uniformly in $x$.

*Proof.* Apply Theorem 7.2 to $a_i = x_i+\delta_i$, noting
$\log|S| \ge 0$, and use $|u| \le c \iff -c \le u \le c$. Uniformity is manifest
since the bound is independent of $x$. Convergence follows by squeezing between
the two sides of the sandwich as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$. $\square$

### 7.2 Exactness characterizes dictatorship

**Theorem 7.4 (Dequantization stability equals dictatorship).** Let $S$ be a
nonempty support with weights $\delta$. Then
$$\Bigl(\forall \varepsilon > 0,\ \forall x,\ F_\varepsilon(x) = F(x)\Bigr)
\iff |S| = 1 .$$

*Proof.* ($\Leftarrow$) If $S = \{i_0\}$ the sum has one term and
$-\varepsilon\log e^{-(x_{i_0}+\delta_{i_0})/\varepsilon} = x_{i_0}+\delta_{i_0} = F(x)$.

($\Rightarrow$) Test at $\varepsilon = 1$ and the profile $x_i = -\delta_i$. Then
$x_i + \delta_i = 0$ for all $i$, so $F(x) = 0$ while
$F_1(x) = -\log\sum_{i \in S} e^{0} = -\log|S|$. Equality forces
$\log |S| = 0$, hence $|S| = 1$ since $|S| \ge 1$. $\square$

Combining with Theorem 4.3: *the dequantization is exact precisely for
dictatorships.* Every non-dictatorial tropical aggregator carries a strictly
positive "entropy" $\log|S|$, and its classical smoothings differ from it by
exactly $\varepsilon \log|S|$ at the balanced profile $x = -\delta$. Dictatorship
is the unique rigid point of the deformation.

**Remark 7.5.** The quantity $\varepsilon\log|S|$ is the temperature times the
Boltzmann entropy of the uniform distribution on the support. The result may be
read: *a social rule is temperature-independent if and only if its decisive
coalition is a single voter.* Thermodynamically, dictatorship is the zero-entropy
state.

## 8. Algorithms

The theory is fully constructive; four procedures suffice to compute all the
invariants above from black-box access to an aggregator.

### 8.1 Support-and-weight identification from a dip oracle

Given an oracle for a tropical aggregator $F$, Proposition 3.5 and Theorem 3.7
give an $O(n)$-query identification algorithm. For each voter $i$, query
$F(d_i(-M))$ for a single sufficiently deep $M > 0$. If the value is $0$, voter
$i$ is inactive and $i \notin S$. If the value is $v < 0$, then $i \in S$ and
$\delta_i = v + M$, provided $M > \delta_i$; one may certify the choice of $M$ by
doubling until the returned value is strictly negative or a preset ceiling is
reached. Finally verify (or enforce) $\min_{i\in S}\delta_i = 0$; if it is not,
$F$ was not normalized. Total cost: $O(n \log(\delta_{\max}))$ oracle calls, each
on a profile of size $n$.

### 8.2 Decisive-coalition labelling and cell identification

Given $S$ and $\delta$ and a profile $x$, compute $m_i(x) = x_i + \delta_i$ for
$i \in S$ in $O(|S|)$ time, take $F(x) = \min_i m_i(x)$, and return
$D(x) = \{i : m_i(x) = F(x)\}$ (with a tolerance in floating point). By
Theorem 6.15 the cell containing $x$ has codimension $|D(x)|-1$, and by
Theorem 6.12 it is cut out of the chamber of any $i_0 \in D(x)$ by the $|D(x)|-1$
equations $m_i = m_{i_0}$. Cost $O(|S|)$.

### 8.3 Exchange-path construction

Given $x \in \mathcal{C}_i$ and a target voter $j \ne i$, Theorem 6.16/6.17
produce, in $O(1)$ arithmetic operations, the wall point (set
$x_j := m_i(x)-\delta_j$) and, for any $\varepsilon>0$, a point of the open cell
$\{j\}$ (set $x_j := m_i(x)-\delta_j-\varepsilon$). Iterating over a sequence of
target voters walks a path through the chamber complex visiting prescribed
chambers; each step modifies exactly one coordinate.

### 8.4 Numerically stable dequantization

Evaluating $F_\varepsilon$ naively overflows for small $\varepsilon$. Use the
shifted form
$$F_\varepsilon(x) = A - \varepsilon\log\sum_{i \in S}\exp\!\left(-\frac{m_i(x)-A}{\varepsilon}\right),
\qquad A = \min_{i\in S} m_i(x),$$
in which every exponent is $\le 0$ and the term at a minimizer equals $1$. This
is exact in real arithmetic and stable in floating point; the returned value
always satisfies the sandwich of Theorem 7.2 up to rounding. Cost $O(|S|)$.

## 9. Applications and interpretations

**Social choice.** The framework applies verbatim to cardinal aggregation
problems where each voter reports a real-valued score (a grade, a cost, a
completion time, a risk estimate) and society must return a single score. The
theorems say: (i) the only rules respecting the "worst-case merge" and
scale-shift axioms are weighted minima; (ii) the *decisive coalitions* form a
principal filter, so decisiveness is monotone and intersection-closed but not
complemented unless the rule is dictatorial; (iii) dictatorship is exactly a
singleton support; (iv) the failure of anonymity is quantified exactly by
$1 - 1/\binom{n}{k}$; (v) any single voter can seize decisiveness unilaterally.

**Scheduling and min-plus control.** Min-plus linear systems model discrete-event
dynamics (assembly lines, network calculus): the state is a vector of dates and
the dynamics is $x \mapsto A \odot x$ with tropical matrix $A$. Theorem 3.6 is the
statement that a scalar min-plus-linear, normalized read-out of such a system is
exactly a row of a tropical matrix; the support is the set of resources on the
critical path, and $\delta_i$ is the slack of resource $i$. The chamber complex is
then the decomposition of the state space by *which resource is critical*, and
the exchange law says criticality is transferred by a single-resource adjustment.

**Piecewise-linear learning.** A single $\min$-pooling unit over affine features
is a tropical linear form, and networks built from $\mathrm{ReLU}$ and max/min
pooling are tropical rational functions. Under this dictionary the chamber
decomposition is the *linear-region decomposition* of the unit; Theorem 6.15
gives the codimension of the region boundaries; Theorem 6.9 identifies the face
lattice; and Corollary 7.3 is exactly the standard bound
$0 \le \min_i a_i - \mathrm{softmin}_\varepsilon(a) \le \varepsilon \log |S|$
justifying softmin as a differentiable surrogate. Theorem 7.4 states that the
surrogate is exact only in the degenerate one-feature case — a precise version of
the folklore that smoothing always distorts unless the argmin is trivial.

**Statistical physics.** With $\varepsilon$ the temperature and $m_i(x)$ the
energy levels, $F_\varepsilon$ is the Helmholtz free energy and $F$ its
zero-temperature limit (the ground-state energy). The sandwich says the free
energy sits within $\varepsilon \log |S|$ (temperature times maximal entropy) of
the ground state, and Theorem 7.4 says the free energy is temperature-independent
exactly for a nondegenerate single-level system.

## 10. Discussion, limitations, and future directions

### 10.1 What the results say

We have a complete dictionary between three languages:

| tropical | social choice | geometry/physics |
|---|---|---|
| support $S$ of the tropical linear form | minimal decisive coalition | set of active monomials |
| $|S| = 1$ | dictatorship | ground state nondegenerate; exact dequantization |
| weight $\delta_i$ | threshold before voter $i$ is heard | offset of the $i$-th affine piece |
| $\delta \equiv 0$ | selectivity: outcome is somebody's score | monomials pass through the origin |
| $S = \iota$, $\delta \equiv 0$ | anonymity / unanimity | fully symmetric fan |
| dependence family | principal filter on $S$ | — |
| ultrafilter | dictatorship | zero entropy |
| $|D(x)| = t$ | $t$ voters simultaneously decisive | cell of codimension $t-1$ |
| single-voter exchange | unilateral seizure of decisiveness | adjacency across a wall |
| $\varepsilon \log|S|$ | entropy of the decisive coalition | free-energy gap |

### 10.2 Limitations

The axioms are strong. Tropical additivity (T1) is a strict requirement that in
particular forces monotonicity and rules out many familiar cardinal rules: the
arithmetic mean is translation-equivariant and normalized but not
minimum-preserving, and the median is minimum-preserving only on very special
domains. The framework is therefore best understood as characterizing
*worst-case* or *bottleneck* aggregation, not average-case aggregation. A dual
theory over the max-plus semiring is obtained by $x \mapsto -x$ and describes
best-case aggregation; a self-dual theory combining both (min-max, tropical
rational) would be substantially richer and is not treated here. Finally, the
electorate is finite throughout; on infinite electorates the equivalence
"ultrafilter $\Rightarrow$ principal" fails and one should expect non-principal
dependence filters with no dictator, matching the classical infinite-population
Arrow phenomenon.

### 10.3 Open directions

1. **Tropical rational aggregators.** Characterize the maps satisfying (T2),
   (T3) and preserving *both* $\min$ and $\max$ in an appropriate sense; these
   should be the tropical rational functions, i.e. differences
   $\max_a(\cdots) - \max_b(\cdots)$, whose social interpretation is a
   coalition-versus-coalition comparison and whose geometry is that of a
   difference of polyhedral fans.
2. **Higher-codimension exchange.** Theorem 6.17 exchanges one voter across a
   wall. Is there a matroid-theoretic strong-exchange statement describing paths
   between arbitrary cells with a prescribed number of coordinate changes,
   realizing the flat lattice of the support matroid as a metric object?
3. **Defect for families of rules.** Theorem 5.4 measures a single aggregator.
   For a *probability distribution* over supports (a randomized social rule) the
   expected defect is $1 - \mathbb{E}[1/\binom{n}{K}]$; characterize the
   distributions minimizing it subject to a decisiveness constraint.
4. **Rate-optimality of dequantization.** Corollary 7.3 is sharp at the balanced
   profile. Is $\varepsilon \log|S|$ also the exact modulus of the deformation in
   $L^p$ over a bounded region of tropical projective space, and what is the
   induced metric on the space of aggregators?
5. **Infinite electorates.** Extend the dependence filter analysis to infinite
   $\iota$ with a suitable continuity axiom, and determine which non-principal
   filters arise as dependence families of tropically linear, continuous
   aggregators.

### 10.4 Summary

Replacing Boolean aggregation by min-plus aggregation replaces Arrow's
impossibility by a *classification*. Every rule obeying the three tropical axioms
is a weighted minimum with a unique support and unique weights; the decisive
coalitions form the principal filter of that support; the ultrafilter dichotomy —
the engine of Arrow's theorem — holds exactly when the support is a single voter;
anonymity forces the opposite extreme of unanimity, with an exact defect
$1 - 1/\binom{n}{k}$ in between; the geometry of decisiveness is a polyhedral
complex whose face lattice is the reversed lattice of subcoalitions, whose cells
have codimension one less than their label size, and whose adjacencies are
single-voter exchanges; and dictatorship is precisely the unique aggregator that
survives the passage from classical to tropical arithmetic with no deformation at
all.
