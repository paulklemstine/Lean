# Why computational evidence was skipped

The formal claims concern arbitrary sets of sentences, strict inclusion between theorem sets, transfer of solved problem classes, and well-foundedness induced by ordinal ranks. They have no canonical finite numerical instances or associated integer sequence. Small-case computation would therefore test only an arbitrary toy encoding and would provide no evidence for the universally quantified structural results.

The key disputed premises—whether a theorem is directly apprehended by a human and whether one foundational formalism proves a sentence unavailable to another—are semantic or cognitive claims, not finite calculations. The Lean development instead makes those premises explicit and proves their consequences deductively.
