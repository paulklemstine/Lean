# An Exact Geometric Conjugacy Between the Logistic Map and the Tent Map

## Abstract

The logistic map $f(x) = 4x(1-x)$ on the unit interval is the archetypal smooth
one-dimensional chaotic system and the computational core of many "chaos-based"
stream ciphers. The tent map $T(t) = 1 - |2t-1|$ is its piecewise-linear cousin.
We establish that these two systems, though superficially unrelated, are
*dynamically identical*: they are topologically conjugate through the single
strictly increasing homeomorphism $h(t) = \sin^2(\pi t/2)$ of $[0,1]$. Our central
result is the exact intertwining identity $f(h(t)) = h(T(t))$, valid for every real
$t$, from which we derive that $f^n(h(t)) = h(T^n(t))$ for all $n$, that $h$ is a
bijection of the unit interval, and that fixed points and periodic orbits transfer
verbatim between the two maps. As concrete corollaries we recover the logistic
fixed point $3/4$ from the tent fixed point $2/3$, and construct a genuine
period-two logistic orbit from the tent cycle $2/5 \leftrightarrow 4/5$. We discuss
the consequence for chaos-based cryptography: because the conjugacy is exact and
invertible, any invariant computable from a logistic keystream has an exact tent-map
counterpart, so the smooth cipher is no stronger than its transparent
piecewise-linear shadow.

## 1. Introduction

Chaotic dynamical systems are prized for two properties that make them tempting
building blocks for pseudorandomness: **sensitive dependence on initial
conditions**, whereby nearby trajectories diverge exponentially, and **topological
mixing**, whereby orbits spread densely over the state space. The logistic map at
its fully chaotic parameter,
$$f(x) = 4x(1-x), \qquad x \in [0,1],$$
exhibits both, with a Lyapunov exponent $\lambda = \log 2$: on average, distances
double at every step. This has motivated a large literature on "chaos ciphers,"
which mask a plaintext with the orbit of a secret seed under $f$.

The tent map,
$$T(t) = 1 - |2t - 1|, \qquad t \in [0,1],$$
is a piecewise-linear system with the same Lyapunov exponent $\log 2$. Its
analysis is elementary because it is built from two line segments, and it is
essentially the binary shift map in disguise.

The purpose of this paper is to make precise, and fully rigorous, the classical
folklore that these two maps are "the same." We exhibit an explicit
**topological conjugacy** — a homeomorphism $h$ of the interval satisfying
$f \circ h = h \circ T$ — implemented by a single smooth, monotone reparametrization.
The consequence is that every dynamical feature of one map is, verbatim, a feature
of the other. In particular, the celebrated smooth complexity of the logistic map
is revealed as a coordinate artifact of the elementary combinatorics of folding
straight lines.

### Contributions

1. **The intertwining identity** (Theorem 3.1): $f(h(t)) = h(T(t))$ for all real
   $t$, where $h(t) = \sin^2(\pi t/2)$.
2. **Iterated conjugacy** (Theorem 3.2): $f^n(h(t)) = h(T^n(t))$ for all $n \ge 0$.
3. **$h$ is a homeomorphism of $[0,1]$** (Theorem 4.3): strictly increasing,
   continuous, and bijective onto $[0,1]$.
4. **Transfer of fixed and periodic points** (Section 5): the tent fixed set
   $\{0, 2/3\}$ maps to the logistic fixed set $\{0, 3/4\}$, and periodic seeds
   transfer with period preserved; a genuine period-two logistic orbit is
   constructed.
5. **Cryptographic consequence** (Section 6): a keystream generated by $f$ is, in
   the $h$-coordinate, a keystream generated by $T$, so no invariant confers
   security beyond the piecewise-linear model.

## 2. Definitions and preliminaries

Throughout, $I = [0,1]$ denotes the closed unit interval.

**Definition 2.1 (Logistic map).** $f : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$, $f(x) = 4x(1-x)$.

**Definition 2.2 (Tent map).** $T : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$,
$T(t) = 1 - |2t - 1|$. Equivalently, $T(t) = 2t$ for $t \le 1/2$ and
$T(t) = 2 - 2t$ for $t \ge 1/2$.

**Definition 2.3 (Change of coordinates).**
$h : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$, $h(t) = \sin^2\!\big(\tfrac{\pi t}{2}\big)$.

**Definition 2.4 (Topological conjugacy).** Two continuous self-maps
$g_1 : X \to X$ and $g_2 : Y \to Y$ are *topologically conjugate* if there is a
homeomorphism $\phi : Y \to X$ with $g_1 \circ \phi = \phi \circ g_2$. Conjugacy is
an equivalence relation that preserves all dynamical invariants: fixed points,
periodic points and their periods, density of orbits, topological transitivity,
and topological entropy.

**Lemma 2.5 (Invariance of the interval).** Each of $f$, $T$, and $h$ maps $I$ into
$I$.

*Proof.* For $x \in I$, $f(x) = 4x(1-x) \ge 0$, and $1 - f(x) = (2x-1)^2 \ge 0$ gives
$f(x) \le 1$. For $t \in I$, $|2t-1| \le 1$ gives $T(t) = 1 - |2t-1| \in [0,1]$.
For $h$, $0 \le \sin^2\theta \le 1$ for all real $\theta$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Lemma 2.6 (Endpoints).** $h(0) = 0$ and $h(1) = 1$; also $f(0) = f(1) = 0$.

*Proof.* $h(0) = \sin^2 0 = 0$, $h(1) = \sin^2(\pi/2) = 1$. Direct substitution
gives $f(0) = f(1) = 0$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

## 3. The intertwining identity

**Theorem 3.1 (Conjugacy).** For every real $t$,
$$f\big(h(t)\big) = h\big(T(t)\big).$$

*Proof.* Write $\theta = \pi t/2$. Using $\cos^2\theta = 1 - \sin^2\theta$ and the
double-angle identity $\sin(2\theta) = 2\sin\theta\cos\theta$,
$$
f(h(t)) = 4\sin^2\theta\,(1 - \sin^2\theta) = 4\sin^2\theta\cos^2\theta
= (2\sin\theta\cos\theta)^2 = \sin^2(2\theta) = \sin^2(\pi t).
$$
For the right-hand side, $h(T(t)) = \sin^2\!\big(\tfrac{\pi}{2}T(t)\big)$. We split
at the fold of the tent map.

*Near branch* ($2t - 1 \le 0$): $T(t) = 1 - (1 - 2t) = 2t$, so
$h(T(t)) = \sin^2(\pi t)$.

*Far branch* ($2t - 1 > 0$): $T(t) = 1 - (2t - 1) = 2 - 2t$, so
$$
h(T(t)) = \sin^2\!\Big(\tfrac{\pi}{2}(2 - 2t)\Big) = \sin^2(\pi - \pi t)
= \sin^2(\pi t),
$$
using $\sin(\pi - \alpha) = \sin\alpha$. In both cases $h(T(t)) = \sin^2(\pi t) =
f(h(t))$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Theorem 3.2 (Iterated conjugacy).** For every $n \in \mathbb{N}$ and every real
$t$,
$$f^{\,n}\big(h(t)\big) = h\big(T^{\,n}(t)\big).$$

*Proof.* By induction on $n$. The base case $n=0$ is $h(t) = h(t)$. Assuming the
claim for $k$,
$$
f^{\,k+1}(h(t)) = f\big(f^{\,k}(h(t))\big) = f\big(h(T^{\,k}(t))\big)
= h\big(T(T^{\,k}(t))\big) = h\big(T^{\,k+1}(t)\big),
$$
using the inductive hypothesis and then Theorem 3.1. $\qquad\blacksquare$

The identity of Theorem 3.1 also explains the standard semiconjugacy of the
logistic map to angle doubling: $f(\sin^2\theta) = \sin^2(2\theta)$. The tent map
is exactly the real-interval realization of that doubling, with the absolute value
implementing the fold that brings the doubled angle back into range.

## 4. The change of coordinates is a homeomorphism

**Theorem 4.1 (Strict monotonicity).** $h$ is strictly increasing on $I$.

*Proof.* Let $0 \le a < b \le 1$. Then $\pi a/2, \pi b/2 \in [0, \pi/2]$ with
$\pi a/2 < \pi b/2$. Since $\sin$ is strictly increasing on $[-\pi/2, \pi/2]$,
$0 \le \sin(\pi a/2) < \sin(\pi b/2)$. Squaring a strictly increasing chain of
nonnegative reals preserves the strict inequality, so
$h(a) = \sin^2(\pi a/2) < \sin^2(\pi b/2) = h(b)$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Theorem 4.2 (Continuity and surjectivity).** $h$ is continuous, and it maps $I$
onto $I$.

*Proof.* $h$ is a composition of continuous functions. By Lemma 2.6, $h(0)=0$ and
$h(1)=1$; the intermediate value theorem then gives that $h(I) \supseteq [0,1] = I$,
and by Lemma 2.5, $h(I) \subseteq I$. Hence $h(I) = I$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Theorem 4.3 ($h$ is a homeomorphism of $I$).** $h$ restricts to a strictly
increasing continuous bijection of $I$ onto itself; its inverse
$h^{-1}(x) = \tfrac{2}{\pi}\arcsin\sqrt{x}$ is also continuous. Consequently $f|_I$
and $T|_I$ are topologically conjugate.

*Proof.* Strict monotonicity (Theorem 4.1) gives injectivity; Theorem 4.2 gives
continuity and surjectivity. A strictly monotone continuous bijection of a compact
interval has a continuous monotone inverse; solving $x = \sin^2(\pi t/2)$ for
$t \in I$ yields $t = \tfrac{2}{\pi}\arcsin\sqrt{x}$. With $h$ a homeomorphism and
$f \circ h = h \circ T$ (Theorem 3.1), the maps are conjugate by Definition 2.4.
$\qquad\blacksquare$

## 5. Transfer of fixed and periodic points

Because conjugacy preserves the orbit structure, every fixed or periodic point of
$T$ transfers to one of $f$ under $h$, and vice versa under $h^{-1}$.

**Theorem 5.1 (Tent fixed points).** $T(t) = t$ if and only if $t \in \{0, 2/3\}$.

*Proof.* On the near branch $T(t) = 2t$, so $2t = t \iff t = 0$ (and $0$ lies in
this branch). On the far branch $T(t) = 2 - 2t$, so $2 - 2t = t \iff t = 2/3$ (and
$2/3 > 1/2$ lies in this branch). $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Lemma 5.2.** $h(2/3) = 3/4$.

*Proof.* $h(2/3) = \sin^2(\pi/3) = (\sqrt3/2)^2 = 3/4$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Theorem 5.3 (Logistic fixed point by transport).** $f(3/4) = 3/4$.

*Proof.* By Theorem 5.1, $T(2/3) = 2/3$. Applying Theorem 3.1 at $t = 2/3$ and
Lemma 5.2, $f(3/4) = f(h(2/3)) = h(T(2/3)) = h(2/3) = 3/4$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

(One checks directly $f(3/4) = 4\cdot\tfrac34\cdot\tfrac14 = \tfrac34$; the point is
that transport *predicts* it from the elementary tent computation.)

**Theorem 5.4 (Transfer of periodic seeds).** If $T^n(t) = t$, then
$f^n(h(t)) = h(t)$.

*Proof.* Immediate from Theorem 3.2: $f^n(h(t)) = h(T^n(t)) = h(t)$.
$\qquad\blacksquare$

**Lemma 5.5 (A tent 2-cycle).** $T(2/5) = 4/5$ and $T^2(2/5) = 2/5$.

*Proof.* $T(2/5) = 1 - |4/5 - 1| = 1 - 1/5 = 4/5$; and
$T(4/5) = 1 - |8/5 - 1| = 1 - 3/5 = 2/5$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Theorem 5.6 (A genuine logistic period-two orbit).** There exists
$x \in [0,1]$ with $f^2(x) = x$ and $f(x) \ne x$; explicitly
$x = h(2/5) = \sin^2(\pi/5)$.

*Proof.* Set $x = h(2/5)$. By Lemma 5.5 and Theorem 3.2,
$f^2(x) = h(T^2(2/5)) = h(2/5) = x$. If $f(x) = x$, then by Theorem 3.1
$h(4/5) = h(T(2/5)) = f(h(2/5)) = f(x) = x = h(2/5)$, and injectivity of $h$
(Theorem 4.1) forces $2/5 = 4/5$, a contradiction. Hence $f(x) \ne x$, so $x$ has
*exact* period two. $\qquad\blacksquare$

Numerically $x = \sin^2(\pi/5) \approx 0.345492$ and
$f(x) = \sin^2(2\pi/5) \approx 0.904508$; these are the two points of the first
period-doubling orbit of the logistic family, appearing at $r = 4$.

## 6. Consequences for chaos-based cryptography

A chaos cipher fixes a secret seed $x_0 \in (0,1)$ and emits the keystream
$x_0, f(x_0), f^2(x_0), \dots$ (or bits extracted from it) to mask a message. The
design intuition is that the smooth, transcendental structure of $f$ — the $n$-th
iterate $f^n$ is a polynomial of degree $2^n$ — makes seed recovery and statistical
prediction hard.

The conjugacy of Section 3–4 refutes this intuition structurally. Setting
$t_0 = h^{-1}(x_0) = \tfrac{2}{\pi}\arcsin\sqrt{x_0}$, Theorem 3.2 gives
$$x_k = f^k(x_0) = h\big(T^k(t_0)\big), \qquad k = 0, 1, 2, \dots.$$
Thus the logistic keystream is, coordinate-for-coordinate, the tent keystream
$T^k(t_0)$ viewed through the fixed, publicly known bijection $h$. Because $h$ and
$h^{-1}$ are explicit and efficiently computable, an attacker converts freely
between the two representations. Any statistic an attacker might exploit — output
bias, autocorrelation, the algebraic degree of seed recovery, invariant measure —
has an exact tent-map counterpart obtained by applying $h^{-1}$. The tent map, in
turn, is (up to reflection) the binary shift: $T^k$ merely reads successive bits of
the binary expansion of $t_0$, so seed recovery from a run of outputs is trivial.

**Principle.** *Apparent analytic complexity is not cryptographic strength.* A
smooth chaotic keystream that is topologically conjugate, by a computable
homeomorphism, to a piecewise-linear model inherits exactly the security of that
model — here, essentially none.

## 7. Invariant measure: the arcsine law

The conjugacy also determines the statistical long-run behavior of $f$. The tent
map preserves Lebesgue (uniform) measure on $I$. Pushing this forward through $h$
via the change of variables $x = \sin^2(\pi t/2)$, with
$dx = \tfrac{\pi}{2}\sin(\pi t)\,dt$ and $\sin(\pi t) = 2\sqrt{x(1-x)}$, yields the
invariant density of the logistic map,
$$\rho(x) = \frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{x(1-x)}}, \qquad x \in (0,1),$$
the **arcsine law**. The concentration of logistic orbits near the endpoints
$0$ and $1$ is thus the image of the flat tent density under $h$; even the
statistical "personality" of the smooth map is inherited from its piecewise-linear
twin. (We record this as motivation; the formal development of this paper
establishes the exact conjugacy and its point-orbit consequences, from which the
measure statement follows by the standard change-of-variables argument sketched
here.)

## 8. Algorithms

We summarize the two computational routines that make the conjugacy operational.

**Algorithm A (Coordinate transport of an orbit).** Given a seed $t_0$, compute the
logistic orbit either directly (iterate $f$) or via the tent map and transport
(iterate $T$, apply $h$). Theorem 3.2 guarantees the two agree to numerical
precision, providing a self-checking cross-validation.

**Algorithm B (Seed recovery / cipher break).** Given a logistic keystream
$x_0, \dots, x_{m}$, apply $h^{-1}$ to obtain a tent orbit $t_0, \dots, t_m$, read
off the binary expansion of $t_0$ from the fold pattern, and thereby recover the
seed — demonstrating the fragility described in Section 6.

## 9. Discussion and future work

The conjugacy welds smooth interval dynamics to the combinatorics of the folding
map through a single monotone reparametrization. Several directions extend it:

- **Exponential periodic-point count.** The tent map's $n$-fold iterate is a
  sawtooth with $2^n$ ramps, each crossing the diagonal once; via the conjugacy,
  $f$ has exactly $2^n$ points of period dividing $n$, and points of exact period
  $n$ for every $n$.
- **Devaney chaos.** Density of periodic points and topological transitivity are
  transparent for the tent map (periodic seeds are the rationals $k/(2^n \pm 1)$)
  and transfer through the homeomorphism $h$, certifying Devaney chaos of $f$ on
  the whole interval.
- **Invariant density.** The arcsine law of Section 7 is the pushforward of
  Lebesgue measure, made rigorous by the explicit differentiable $h$.
- **Cryptographic fragility, generalized.** Every attacker-computable invariant of
  a logistic keystream has an exact tent counterpart via $h^{-1}$; the "chaos
  cipher" offers no security beyond its piecewise-linear shadow.

## 10. Conclusion

The smooth logistic map $f(x) = 4x(1-x)$ and the piecewise-linear tent map
$T(t) = 1 - |2t-1|$ are the same dynamical system in different coordinates, welded
by the strictly increasing homeomorphism $h(t) = \sin^2(\pi t/2)$ through the exact
identity $f \circ h = h \circ T$. Fixed points, periodic orbits, and all iterates
transfer verbatim. The curvature of the parabola is a coordinate illusion; the
engine of chaos is a single fold, worn openly by the tent and hidden by the
logistic map. And because the disguise is a computable homeomorphism, the smooth
map's cryptographic promise dissolves into the transparency of two straight lines.
