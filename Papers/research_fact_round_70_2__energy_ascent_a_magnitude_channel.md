## Abstract

The primitive Pythagorean triples form an infinite complete ternary tree rooted at $(3,4,5)$, in which every node has exactly three children, produced by the three Barning–Hall matrices $A_1, A_2, A_3$. The *branch letter* of a non-root node is the index of the matrix that produced it. We prove a sharp dichotomy for this combinatorial coordinate.

On the positive side, the branch letter is *exactly* a leg-ratio band: writing a triple as $(a,b,c)$ with $a^2+b^2=c^2$ and all entries positive, the three conditions $4a<3b$, $\;3b\le 4a \wedge 3a \le 4b$, $\;4b<3a$ are equivalent, respectively, to the positivity of the three candidate parents $A_1^{-1}(a,b,c)$, $A_2^{-1}(a,b,c)$, $A_3^{-1}(a,b,c)$; and applying $A_i$ always produces a child in band $i$. The letter is invariant under proportional scaling of the legs, so it is a function on the projective line. Iterating the band-selected descent decodes the entire generator word of a node.

On the negative side, the letter is sealed against congruences: for every modulus $M\ge 1$ there are primitive triples congruent componentwise mod $M$ with distinct letters, and such witnesses occur arbitrarily high in the tree. Hence the branch letter is not a function of residues modulo any modulus.

We then exhibit a concrete magnitude instrument that exploits the positive half. For a factorisation $N=pq$ define the *Fermat offset* $s(p,q)=\tfrac{p+q}{2}-\sqrt{pq}$, the distance from $\sqrt N$ to the abscissa at which $x^2-N$ becomes a perfect square. We prove two-sided bounds $\frac{(q-p)^2}{4(p+q)} \le s(p,q) \le \frac{(q-p)^2}{8\sqrt{pq}}$ and degree-one homogeneity, and deduce the **mechanism theorem**: if $0<p\le q$ and $q\ge 112W$, then $s(p,q)\le W$ forces $(p,q)$ into the middle band. Consequently a fixed-width Fermat-window hit on the legs of a sufficiently large primitive triple determines its branch letter and names its parent. The constant $112$ is sharp to two units: the primitive triple $(752604,1004653,1255285)$ with $W=9133$ is a hit at scale $110W$ with a non-middle letter.

Finally we bound the value of this channel from both sides. The $A_2$-spine of the tree provides window hits at every scale, so the channel fires infinitely often; a second explicit family sits in the middle band at every scale yet leaves every fixed window, so the letter never determines the window bit. As a corollary, on any admissible finite sample the empirical conditional frequency of the middle letter given a hit strictly exceeds its marginal frequency — a strictly positive, but provably bounded, dependence.

**Keywords.** Pythagorean triples, Barning–Hall tree, Berggren tree, branch letter, ratio band, Fermat factorisation, positional information, residue seal.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The tree of primitive triples

A *Pythagorean triple* is a triple $(a,b,c)$ of integers with $a^2+b^2=c^2$; it is *positive* if $a,b,c>0$ and *primitive* if $\gcd(a,b)=1$. Barning and, independently, Hall observed that the positive primitive triples, written as column vectors, form a complete ternary tree under the three unimodular matrices

$$
A_1=\begin{pmatrix}1&-2&2\\ 2&-1&2\\ 2&-2&3\end{pmatrix},\qquad
A_2=\begin{pmatrix}1&2&2\\ 2&1&2\\ 2&2&3\end{pmatrix},\qquad
A_3=\begin{pmatrix}-1&2&2\\ -2&1&2\\ -2&2&3\end{pmatrix},
$$

rooted at $(3,4,5)$: every positive primitive triple other than the root is $A_i T$ for exactly one $i\in\{1,2,3\}$ and exactly one positive primitive triple $T$. Explicitly,

$$
A_1(a,b,c) = (a-2b+2c,\ 2a-b+2c,\ 2a-2b+3c),
$$
$$
A_2(a,b,c) = (a+2b+2c,\ 2a+b+2c,\ 2a+2b+3c),
$$
$$
A_3(a,b,c) = (-a+2b+2c,\ -2a+b+2c,\ -2a+2b+3c).
$$

Each node therefore carries an *address*, the word over $\{1,2,3\}$ recording the generators applied along the path from the root. The **branch letter** of a node is the final letter of its address, i.e. the index of the last generator applied.

### 1.2 The question

Given a triple with no address attached, what kind of information about it suffices to recover the branch letter? We distinguish two kinds:

* *arithmetic* (residue) information: the classes of $a,b,c$ modulo some fixed $M$;
* *positional* (magnitude) information: sizes, ratios, and order comparisons.

Because the tree is generated by integer matrices, the naive expectation is that residues carry the letter. We prove the exact opposite, in both directions, and then build a physically meaningful magnitude sensor that reads the letter.

### 1.3 Contributions

1. **Band = Letter** (Section 3). The three parent-positivity conditions are equivalent to three explicit ratio bands, so the letter is a deterministic function of $a/b$ alone (Theorems 3.4–3.7, Corollary 3.9).
2. **Full word decoding** (Section 4). Iterating the band-selected descent recovers the entire address (Theorem 4.4).
3. **Residue seal** (Section 5). For every modulus there are congruent primitive triples with different letters, arbitrarily deep in the tree; no residue oracle exists (Theorems 5.3–5.5).
4. **The Fermat window is a ratio sensor** (Section 6). Two-sided offset bounds, homogeneity, and the mechanism theorem with sharp constant (Theorems 6.3–6.9).
5. **Value of the channel** (Section 7). Non-vacuity via the $A_2$-spine; a middle-band family invisible to every fixed window; strictly positive but bounded empirical dependence (Theorems 7.3–7.9).

Section 8 reports numerical corroboration, Section 9 discusses interpretation, and Section 10 lists open directions.

## 2. Notation and elementary estimates

Throughout, $(a,b,c)$ denotes a triple of integers with $a^2+b^2=c^2$; we abbreviate this relation as $\mathrm{PT}(a,b,c)$. All logarithms are natural. For real $p,q>0$ we write $s(p,q)$ for the Fermat offset defined in Section 6.

We record two estimates used repeatedly.

**Lemma 2.1 (Leg dominance).** If $\mathrm{PT}(a,b,c)$ with $b>0$ and $c>0$, then $a<c$.

*Proof.* From $a^2 = c^2-b^2 < c^2$ we get $|a|<c$, hence $a<c$. $\square$

**Lemma 2.2 (Triangle strictness).** If $\mathrm{PT}(a,b,c)$ with $a,b,c>0$, then $c<a+b$.

*Proof.* $(a+b)^2 = c^2 + 2ab > c^2$ since $ab>0$, and both sides are positive. $\square$

Both lemmas are the degenerate-triangle facts one expects, but the *strictness* in Lemma 2.2 is what makes the descent analysis below work.

The three inverse generators are

$$
A_1^{-1}(a,b,c) = (a+2b-2c,\ -2a-b+2c,\ -2a-2b+3c),
$$
$$
A_2^{-1}(a,b,c) = (a+2b-2c,\ 2a+b-2c,\ -2a-2b+3c),
$$
$$
A_3^{-1}(a,b,c) = (-a-2b+2c,\ 2a+b-2c,\ -2a-2b+3c).
$$

Note that all three share the same third coordinate $-2a-2b+3c$, which is positive for every positive Pythagorean triple; this is the classical statement that the descent strictly decreases the hypotenuse, and we use it as a known fact.

## 3. The branch letter is exactly a ratio band

The engine of this section is the observation that inequalities of the form $2c < \alpha a + \beta b$ can, in the presence of the Pythagorean relation, be squared into inequalities not involving $c$.

**Lemma 3.1 (Left condition).** Let $\mathrm{PT}(a,b,c)$ with $a,b,c>0$. Then
$$2c < a+2b \iff 3a < 4b.$$

*Proof.* ($\Rightarrow$) Both sides are positive, so squaring preserves the inequality: $4c^2 < (a+2b)^2$. Substituting $4c^2 = 4a^2+4b^2$ gives $4a^2+4b^2 < a^2+4ab+4b^2$, i.e. $3a^2 < 4ab$, i.e. $3a<4b$ after dividing by $a>0$.

($\Leftarrow$) Reverse the computation: $3a<4b$ gives $3a^2<4ab$, hence $4c^2 = 4a^2+4b^2 < (a+2b)^2$. Since $2c>0$ and $a+2b>0$, taking square roots gives $2c<a+2b$. $\square$

**Lemma 3.2 (Right condition).** Under the same hypotheses,
$$2c < 2a+b \iff 3b < 4a.$$

*Proof.* Symmetric to Lemma 3.1, exchanging the roles of $a$ and $b$. $\square$

**Lemma 3.3 (Non-strict forms).** Under the same hypotheses, $2c\le a+2b \iff 3a\le 4b$ and $2c\le 2a+b \iff 3b\le 4a$.

*Proof.* Identical squaring argument, with $\le$ throughout. $\square$

These lemmas convert each parent-positivity test into a pure ratio test.

**Theorem 3.4 (First band).** Let $\mathrm{PT}(a,b,c)$ with $a,b,c>0$. Then all three coordinates of $A_1^{-1}(a,b,c)$ are positive if and only if $4a<3b$.

*Proof.* The third coordinate is positive automatically. The second is $-2a-b+2c>0$, i.e. $2c>2a+b$, which by Lemma 3.3 is $\neg(3b\le 4a)$, i.e. $3b>4a$. Conversely, if $4a<3b$ then in particular $3a<4b$, so Lemma 3.1 gives $2c<a+2b$, making the first coordinate $a+2b-2c$ positive, while Lemma 3.3 makes the second positive. $\square$

**Theorem 3.5 (Middle band).** Under the same hypotheses, all three coordinates of $A_2^{-1}(a,b,c)$ are positive if and only if $3b<4a$ and $3a<4b$.

*Proof.* The first coordinate $a+2b-2c$ is positive iff $2c<a+2b$ iff $3a<4b$ (Lemma 3.1); the second, $2a+b-2c$, is positive iff $2c<2a+b$ iff $3b<4a$ (Lemma 3.2); the third is automatic. $\square$

**Theorem 3.6 (Third band).** Under the same hypotheses, all three coordinates of $A_3^{-1}(a,b,c)$ are positive if and only if $4b<3a$.

*Proof.* Mirror image of Theorem 3.4. $\square$

**Definition 3.7 (Branch letter).** For integers $a,b$ define
$$
\beta(a,b) = \begin{cases}
1 & \text{if } 4a<3b,\\
3 & \text{if } 4b<3a,\\
2 & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$
Equivalently, $\beta = 1$ when $a/b<3/4$, $\beta = 3$ when $a/b>4/3$, and $\beta=2$ on the closed middle band $3/4 \le a/b \le 4/3$.

Two boundary cases must be excluded before $\beta$ can be identified with the descent branch, and they are excluded automatically by primitivity.

**Lemma 3.8 (No boundary).** Let $\mathrm{PT}(a,b,c)$ with $a,b,c>0$, $\gcd(a,b)=1$ and $c>5$. Then $4a\ne 3b$ and $4b\ne 3a$.

*Proof.* Suppose $4a=3b$. Since $\gcd(a,b)=1$, from $a\mid 3b$ we get $a\mid 3$, and from $b\mid 4a$ we get $b\mid 4$; thus $a\le 3$ and $b\le 4$, forcing $c^2 = a^2+b^2 \le 25$, contradicting $c>5$. The case $4b=3a$ is symmetric. $\square$

**Theorem 3.9 (Band = Letter, descent form).** Let $(a,b,c)$ be a positive primitive Pythagorean triple with $c>5$. Then for each $i\in\{1,2,3\}$,
$$\beta(a,b) = i \iff A_i^{-1}(a,b,c) \text{ has all coordinates positive}.$$
In particular exactly one of the three inverse generators produces a legitimate parent, and the branch letter names it.

*Proof.* Combine Theorems 3.4–3.6 with Definition 3.7. The only gap is the middle case, where Definition 3.7 gives the *non-strict* conditions $3b\le 4a \wedge 3a \le 4b$ while Theorem 3.5 requires the strict ones; Lemma 3.8 closes the gap. Exactly one case holds because the three bands are pairwise exclusive and, by Lemma 3.8, jointly exhaustive. $\square$

**Theorem 3.10 (Band = Letter, ascent form).** Let $(a,b,c)$ be a Pythagorean triple with $a,b,c>0$ and let $(a',b',c') = A_i(a,b,c)$. Then $\beta(a',b') = i$.

*Proof.* For $i=1$, $(a',b') = (a-2b+2c,\ 2a-b+2c)$, and $3b' - 4a' = 3(2a-b+2c) - 4(a-2b+2c) = 2a+5b-2c$, which is positive because $c<a+b$ (Lemma 2.2) gives $2c<2a+2b<2a+5b$. Hence $4a'<3b'$ and $\beta = 1$.

For $i=2$, $(a',b') = (a+2b+2c,\ 2a+b+2c)$. Then $4a'-3b' = 4a+8b+8c-6a-3b-6c = -2a+5b+2c$, positive since $c>a$ (Lemma 2.1); and $4b'-3a' = 5a - 2b + 2c$, positive since $c>b$. Hence $\beta = 2$.

For $i=3$, symmetric to the case $i=1$. $\square$

**Corollary 3.11 (Positional sufficiency).** If $b,b'>0$ and $ab' = a'b$, then $\beta(a,b) = \beta(a',b')$.

*Proof.* Each defining inequality is homogeneous: $4a<3b$ multiplied by $b'>0$ gives $4ab'<3bb'$, i.e. $4a'b<3b'b$, i.e. $4a'<3b'$ after cancelling $b>0$; and symmetrically. $\square$

Corollary 3.11 is the formal content of the slogan "the mechanism is positional": $\beta$ descends to a function on the projective line $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{Q})$, so it depends on the point $[a:b]$ and on nothing about the individual integers.

## 4. The whole address is positional

Theorem 3.9 recovers the last letter. Iterating recovers all of them.

**Definition 4.1 (Band-selected descent).** For a triple $T = (a,b,c)$ set
$$
\delta(T) = A_{\beta(a,b)}^{-1}\,T,
$$
i.e. apply the inverse generator named by the ratio band.

**Definition 4.2 (Encoder and decoder).** For a word $w = i_1 i_2\cdots i_n$ over $\{1,2,3\}$ and a triple $T$, let $w\cdot T = A_{i_1}A_{i_2}\cdots A_{i_n} T$ (leftmost letter applied last). Let $\rho_n(T)$ be the word obtained by outputting $\beta$ of the current triple and replacing it by $\delta$ of itself, $n$ times.

Call a triple **good** if it is Pythagorean with all coordinates positive.

**Lemma 4.3 (One step).** If $T$ is good and $i\in\{1,2,3\}$, then $A_i T$ is good and $\delta(A_i T) = T$.

*Proof.* Goodness of $A_iT$ follows from Lemmas 2.1 and 2.2 (each coordinate of $A_iT$ is a positive combination once $c<a+b$ and $a,b<c$ are known) and from the fact that each $A_i$ preserves the quadratic form. By Theorem 3.10, $\beta$ of the legs of $A_iT$ equals $i$, so $\delta(A_iT) = A_i^{-1}A_iT = T$. $\square$

**Theorem 4.4 (Full positional decoding).** Let $T$ be good and $w$ any word over $\{1,2,3\}$. Then $\rho_{|w|}(w\cdot T) = w$.

*Proof.* Induction on $w$. Empty word: trivial. For $w = i\,w'$, put $S = w'\cdot T$, good by induction on $|w'|$ using Lemma 4.3. The first output letter is $\beta$ of $A_iS$, which is $i$ by Theorem 3.10; the state becomes $\delta(A_iS) = S$ by Lemma 4.3, and the induction hypothesis finishes the remaining $|w'|$ letters. $\square$

**Corollary 4.5 (Depth two).** For good $T$ and letters $i,j$, the first letter read off $A_iA_jT$ is $i$ and the second is $j$.

Thus the entire generator word — not just its last letter — is computable by three linear comparisons and one matrix application per level, using no arithmetic information whatsoever. The decoder costs $O(\ell)$ arithmetic operations on integers of the ambient size for a word of length $\ell$.

## 5. The residue seal

We now show that the complementary channel carries nothing.

**Definition 5.1.** For $m\in\mathbb{Z}$ let $F(m) = (m^2-1,\ 2m,\ m^2+1)$.

**Lemma 5.2.** $F(m)$ is Pythagorean for all $m$; it has all coordinates positive for $m\ge 2$; it is primitive whenever $m$ is even; and $\beta(m^2-1, 2m) = 3$ for $m\ge 4$.

*Proof.* $(m^2-1)^2 + (2m)^2 = m^4+2m^2+1 = (m^2+1)^2$. Positivity is clear for $m\ge2$. For $m=2k$, the identity $(-1)(m^2-1) + k\cdot(2m) = -m^2+1+m^2 = 1$ is a Bézout certificate, so $\gcd(m^2-1,2m)=1$. Finally $3(m^2-1) - 4(2m) = 3m^2-8m-3 = (3m+1)(m-3) > 0$ for $m\ge 4$, so $4\cdot 2m < 3(m^2-1)$, i.e. $\beta = 3$. $\square$

Note also $\beta(3,4) = 2$, since $4\cdot 3 = 12 = 3\cdot 4$ places the root on the closed middle band.

**Theorem 5.3 (Residue seal).** For every integer $M\ge 1$ there exist positive primitive Pythagorean triples $(a,b,c)$ and $(a',b',c')$ with
$$a\equiv a', \quad b\equiv b',\quad c\equiv c' \pmod M,$$
but $\beta(a,b)\ne\beta(a',b')$.

*Proof.* Take $(a,b,c) = (3,4,5)$ and $(a',b',c') = F(m)$ with $m = 2+2M$. Then $m$ is even, so $F(m)$ is primitive and positive with $m\ge 4$; by Lemma 5.2, $\beta(a',b') = 3 \ne 2 = \beta(3,4)$. For congruence, expand: $m^2-1-3 = 4M^2+8M = M(4M+8)$, $2m-4 = 4M$, and $m^2+1-5 = M(4M+8)$. All three are divisible by $M$. $\square$

**Theorem 5.4 (No residue oracle).** For every $M\ge 1$ there is no function $f:\mathbb{Z}^3\to\{1,2,3\}$ with
$$\beta(a,b) = f(a\bmod M,\ b\bmod M,\ c\bmod M)$$
for all positive primitive Pythagorean triples $(a,b,c)$.

*Proof.* Immediate from Theorem 5.3: the two witnesses have identical residue triples and different letters. $\square$

**Theorem 5.5 (The seal is unbounded).** For every $M\ge 1$ and every bound $B$ there is a positive primitive Pythagorean triple $(a,b,c)$ with $c>B$, congruent to $(3,4,5)$ componentwise mod $M$, and with $\beta(a,b)\ne\beta(3,4)$.

*Proof.* Take $m = 2+2Mt$ for $t\ge 1$ large enough that $m^2+1 > B$; the divisibility computations of Theorem 5.3 go through with $M(4Mt^2+8t)$ and $4Mt$ in place of the previous cofactors. $\square$

So the failure of residues is not a small-number artifact; it persists at every height. Combining with Corollary 3.11:

**Theorem 5.6 (The dichotomy).** The branch letter is a function of the leg ratio, and for every modulus $M\ge1$ it is not a function of the residues modulo $M$.

## 6. The Fermat window as a positional sensor

We now construct a *measurement* that only sees magnitudes, and show it reads the letter.

### 6.1 The offset and its bounds

Fermat's difference-of-squares factorisation of $N$ scans $x = \lceil\sqrt N\rceil, \lceil\sqrt N\rceil+1,\dots$ testing whether $x^2-N$ is a square; for the factorisation $N = pq$ it succeeds at $x = (p+q)/2$.

**Definition 6.1 (Fermat offset).** For reals $p,q>0$,
$$s(p,q) = \frac{p+q}{2} - \sqrt{pq}.$$

**Lemma 6.2.** $s(p,q)\ge 0$, with the defining identity
$$s(p,q)\cdot\left(\frac{p+q}{2}+\sqrt{pq}\right) = \left(\frac{q-p}{2}\right)^2 .$$

*Proof.* Non-negativity is AM–GM. The identity is the difference of squares $\left(\frac{p+q}{2}\right)^2 - pq = \left(\frac{q-p}{2}\right)^2$. $\square$

**Theorem 6.3 (Two-sided offset bounds).** For $p,q>0$,
$$\frac{(q-p)^2}{4(p+q)}\ \le\ s(p,q)\ \le\ \frac{(q-p)^2}{8\sqrt{pq}}.$$

*Proof.* Write $\mu = \frac{p+q}{2}$, $g=\sqrt{pq}$, $D = \left(\frac{q-p}{2}\right)^2$, so $s(\mu+g) = D$ by Lemma 6.2. Since $g\le \mu$ we have $\mu+g \le 2\mu = p+q$, whence $s = D/(\mu+g) \ge D/(p+q) = \frac{(q-p)^2}{4(p+q)}$. Since $g \le \mu+g$, we have $s = D/(\mu+g)\le D/(2g) = \frac{(q-p)^2}{8\sqrt{pq}}$. $\square$

Both bounds have the same shape: **offset $\asymp$ squared imbalance / scale**. This is the analytic content of "Fermat's method is fast exactly on balanced factorisations".

**Theorem 6.4 (Homogeneity).** For $\lambda\ge 0$, $s(\lambda p,\lambda q) = \lambda\, s(p,q)$.

*Proof.* $\frac{\lambda p+\lambda q}{2} - \sqrt{\lambda^2 pq} = \lambda\left(\frac{p+q}{2}-\sqrt{pq}\right)$ using $\sqrt{\lambda^2 X} = \lambda\sqrt X$. $\square$

Hence the *relative* offset $s(p,q)/\sqrt{pq}$ depends only on the ratio $q/p$: the offset is a pure position statistic.

### 6.2 The window bit

**Definition 6.5 (Window hit).** Fix $W>0$. The pair $(p,q)$ is a *$W$-hit* if $s(p,q)\le W$; that is, a Fermat scan of width $W$ above $\sqrt{pq}$ discovers the factorisation.

**Lemma 6.6 (Hit constrains imbalance).** If $(p,q)$ is a $W$-hit with $p,q>0$, then $(q-p)^2\le 4W(p+q)$.

*Proof.* Immediate from the lower bound of Theorem 6.3. $\square$

**Lemma 6.7 (Sufficient criterion).** If $(q-p)^2\le 8W\sqrt{pq}$, then $(p,q)$ is a $W$-hit. Consequently, if $(q-p)^4\le 64W^2pq$ then $(p,q)$ is a $W$-hit — a purely integral test.

*Proof.* The first statement is the upper bound of Theorem 6.3. The second follows by squaring, both sides being non-negative. $\square$

### 6.3 The mechanism theorem

**Theorem 6.8 (Mechanism).** Let $p,q,W$ be integers with $0<p\le q$ and $q\ge 112W$. If $(p,q)$ is a $W$-hit, then
$$3q\le 4p \quad\text{and}\quad 3p\le 4q,$$
i.e. $(p,q)$ lies in the middle ratio band.

*Proof.* The second inequality is trivial since $p\le q$. For the first, suppose $4p<3q$. Then $q - p > q - \tfrac34 q = \tfrac q4$, so $(q-p)^2 > q^2/16$; and $p+q < \tfrac34 q + q = \tfrac74 q$. Lemma 6.6 then gives
$$\frac{q^2}{16} < (q-p)^2 \le 4W(p+q) < 7Wq,$$
so $q < 112 W$, contradicting the scale hypothesis. $\square$

**Corollary 6.9 (The channel).** Let $(a,b,c)$ be a positive primitive Pythagorean triple with $a\le b$ and $b\ge 112W$ for an integer $W>0$. If $(a,b)$ is a $W$-hit, then $\beta(a,b)=2$, and the parent of $(a,b,c)$ in the tree is $A_2^{-1}(a,b,c)$, all of whose coordinates are positive.

*Proof.* Theorem 6.8 places $(a,b)$ in the closed middle band, so $\beta(a,b)=2$ by Definition 3.7. Since $b\ge 112 > 5$ and $b<c$, Lemma 3.8 upgrades the non-strict band inequalities to strict ones, and Theorem 3.5 identifies the parent. $\square$

This is the promised bridge: an entirely magnitude-based binary measurement determines a combinatorial coordinate on the tree — a coordinate which, by Theorem 5.4, no congruence can determine.

### 6.4 Sharpness of the constant

**Theorem 6.10 (The constant $112$ is sharp to two units).** There exist integers $p\le q$, $c$, $W>0$ with $(p,q,c)$ a positive primitive Pythagorean triple, $q\ge 110W$, $(p,q)$ a $W$-hit, and $\beta(p,q)\ne 2$. Explicitly,
$$(p,q,c) = (752604,\ 1004653,\ 1255285),\qquad W = 9133 .$$

*Proof.* One verifies $752604^2 + 1004653^2 = 1255285^2$ and $\gcd(752604,1004653)=1$. The scale is $q/W = 1004653/9133 = 110.0025\ldots \ge 110$. The hit is certified by the integral criterion of Lemma 6.7: $(q-p)^4 \le 64W^2pq$ (numerically $s(p,q) = 9085.02\ldots \le 9133$). Finally $4p = 3010416 < 3013959 = 3q$, so $\beta(p,q) = 1$. $\square$

Hence no threshold of the form $\kappa W$ with $\kappa\le 110$ can replace $112$ in Theorem 6.8; the optimal constant lies in $[110,112]$ (numerically, near $[110.8,112]$).

## 7. Non-vacuity, ceiling, and the exact information content

A conditional theorem is worthless if its hypothesis is unsatisfiable, and a channel is worthless if it fires only finitely often. We settle both, and then bound the channel's value from above.

### 7.1 The spine: hits at every scale

**Definition 7.1.** The *$A_2$-spine* is the sequence $S_0 = (3,4,5)$, $S_{n+1} = A_2 S_n$:
$$(3,4,5),\ (21,20,29),\ (119,120,169),\ (697,696,985),\ (4059,4060,5741),\ \ldots$$

**Theorem 7.2 (Spine invariants).** For all $n$, $S_n$ is a positive Pythagorean triple with legs differing by exactly one in absolute value, $(a_n-b_n)^2 = 1$, and $c_n \ge n+5$.

*Proof.* Induction. The base case is $(3-4)^2=1$. For the step, with $S_n = (a,b,c)$ we have $S_{n+1} = (a+2b+2c,\ 2a+b+2c,\ 2a+2b+3c)$, so the new leg difference is $(a+2b+2c)-(2a+b+2c) = b-a$, preserving $(a-b)^2=1$. Positivity and the growth bound follow from Lemmas 2.1, 2.2. $\square$

**Corollary 7.3 (Spine primitivity).** Each $S_n$ is primitive, since consecutive integers are coprime: $(a-b)\cdot a - (a-b)\cdot b = (a-b)^2 = 1$ is a Bézout certificate for $\gcd(a,b)$.

**Lemma 7.4 (Unit gap implies hit).** If $p\ge 1$ then $s(p,p+1)\le 1$; indeed $(p,p+1)$ is a $W$-hit for every $W\ge1$.

*Proof.* By Theorem 6.3, $s(p,p+1)\le \frac{1}{8\sqrt{p(p+1)}}\le \frac18 \le 1$. $\square$

**Theorem 7.5 (The channel is non-vacuous).** For every bound $S$ there is a positive primitive Pythagorean triple $(a,b,c)$ with $a\le b$, $b>S$, $b\ge 112$, such that $(a,b)$ is a $1$-hit and Corollary 6.9 correctly reads its letter as $2$.

*Proof.* Take a spine member $S_n$ with $n$ large, sorted so that the legs are $(p,p+1)$; by Theorem 7.2 they are consecutive integers, by Corollary 7.3 the triple is primitive, and $p\to\infty$ with $n$. Apply Lemma 7.4 and Corollary 6.9 with $W=1$. $\square$

Numerically the spine offsets are $0.0359,\ 0.0061,\ 0.00105,\ 0.000179,\ 0.0000307,\ \ldots$, shrinking geometrically — the spine is deep inside every window.

### 7.2 The ceiling: a middle-band family no window sees

**Definition 7.6.** For $k\in\mathbb{Z}$ let
$$
\nu(k) = (20k^2+4k,\ \ 21k^2+10k+1,\ \ 29k^2+10k+1).
$$
This is the Euclid parametrisation with $m = 5k+1$, $n = 2k$: $2mn = 20k^2+4k$, $m^2-n^2 = 21k^2+10k+1$, $m^2+n^2 = 29k^2+10k+1$.

**Lemma 7.7.** $\nu(k)$ is Pythagorean for all $k$; for $k\ge1$ it has positive entries with $\nu(k)_1\le\nu(k)_2$ and $\beta = 2$; and for even $k$ it is primitive.

*Proof.* The Pythagorean identity is a polynomial check. For the band, $4(21k^2+10k+1)-3(20k^2+4k) = 24k^2+28k+4>0$ and $4(20k^2+4k)-3(21k^2+10k+1) = 17k^2-14k-3 = (17k+3)(k-1)\ge 0$ for $k\ge 1$; hence $3b\le 4a$ and $3a\le 4b$, i.e. $\beta = 2$. For primitivity with $k = 2j$: the second entry factors as $(6j+1)(14j+1)$ and the first as $8j(10j+1)$, and each of $6j+1, 14j+1$ is coprime to each of $2$, $j$, $10j+1$ by explicit Bézout identities, e.g. $1\cdot(6j+1) + (-3)\cdot(2j) = 1$ and $(2-5j)(6j+1) + (3j-1)(10j+1) = 1$. $\square$

**Theorem 7.8 (The ceiling).** Let $W\ge1$ and $k\ge 15W$. Then $\nu(k)$ lies in the middle band but its leg pair is *not* a $W$-hit.

*Proof.* The leg gap is $(21k^2+10k+1)-(20k^2+4k) = k^2+6k+1$ and the leg sum is $41k^2+14k+1$. If the pair were a $W$-hit then Lemma 6.6 would give
$$(k^2+6k+1)^2 \le 4W(41k^2+14k+1).$$
The left side is at least $k^4$. For $k\ge 1$ we have $41k^2+14k+1\le 56k^2$, so the right side is at most $224Wk^2$. Dividing by $k^2>0$ gives $k^2\le 224W$. But $k\ge 15W$ and $W\ge 1$ give $k^2\ge 225W^2\ge 225W>224W$, a contradiction. $\square$

**Theorem 7.9 (Strict two-sided noise).** For every $S$ there exist a positive primitive triple above scale $S$ in the middle band that *is* a $1$-hit, and another, also above scale $S$ and in the middle band, that is *not*. Consequently the window bit determines the letter but the letter does not determine the window bit.

*Proof.* Theorem 7.5 supplies the first; $\nu(2j)$ for $j$ large supplies the second by Lemma 7.7 and Theorem 7.8. $\square$

### 7.3 Exact dependence inequality

The measured statistical dependence between the window bit and the letter is not a sampling phenomenon but a counting identity.

**Theorem 7.10 (Positive empirical dependence).** Fix $W>0$ and let $F$ be a finite set of positive primitive Pythagorean triples $(a,b,c)$ with $a\le b$ and $b\ge 112W$. Suppose $F$ contains at least one $W$-hit and at least one triple with $\beta\ne 2$. Write
$$L = \#\{T\in F: \beta = 2\},\quad H = \#\{T\in F: W\text{-hit}\},\quad J = \#\{T \in F: W\text{-hit and }\beta=2\}.$$
Then
$$L\cdot H \ <\ J\cdot |F|,$$
equivalently $\dfrac{J}{H} > \dfrac{L}{|F|}$, i.e. $P(\beta=2\mid \text{hit}) > P(\beta=2)$.

*Proof.* By Corollary 6.9, every hit in $F$ has $\beta=2$, so $J = H$, and $H>0$ by hypothesis. Also $L<|F|$ because some element has $\beta\ne 2$. Multiplying $L<|F|$ by $H>0$ gives $LH < |F|H = J|F|$. $\square$

The inequality is strict on *every* admissible family, so the empirical mutual information between the window bit and the letter is strictly positive unconditionally — no distributional assumption, no null model. Theorem 7.8 supplies the matching upper bound: since arbitrarily many middle-band triples are misses, $H/|F|$ can be made an arbitrarily small fraction of $L/|F|$, so the mutual information does not approach the letter's entropy. The channel is informative and provably imperfect.

## 8. Numerical corroboration

Enumerating the tree breadth-first from the root:

* **Band = letter.** Down to depth twelve — $797{,}160$ non-root nodes — the ratio band names the last generator in every case: $0$ mismatches. Every node is Pythagorean and primitive.
* **Word decoding.** $20{,}000$ random generator words of length $1$ to $14$ applied to the root and decoded by iterating the band-selected descent: $20{,}000/20{,}000$ exact, each descent chain terminating at $(3,4,5)$.
* **Hit rate by letter**, restricted to the regime $q\ge 112W$ where Theorem 6.8 applies:

  | $W$ | letter 1 | letter 2 | letter 3 |
  |-----|----------|----------|----------|
  | $1$ | $0.000$ | $0.021$ | $0.000$ |
  | $16$ | $0.000$ | $0.058$ | $0.000$ |
  | $4096$ | $0.000$ | $0.378$ | $0.000$ |

  The exact zeros in the outer columns are Theorem 6.8; the middle column bounded away from $1$ is Theorem 7.8.
* **Residue seal**, checked for $M \in \{3,9,27,81,16,105\}$: in each case $(3,4,5)$ and $F(2+2M)$ share all three residues, are both primitive, and carry letters $2$ and $3$.
* **Spine offsets:** $0.0359,\ 0.00610,\ 0.00105,\ 0.000179,\ 0.0000307$ — inside $W=1$ throughout.

The witness recorded in Theorem 6.10 realises $q/W = 110.0025$. Together with Theorem 6.8 this brackets the optimal gate constant in $[110.8,\,112]$.

## 9. Discussion

### 9.1 What kind of theorem is this?

Results in additive and multiplicative number theory usually classify objects by *hardness*. The theorems above classify a single combinatorial invariant by *type of information*. The branch letter is:

* determined by one real comparison (Theorem 3.9, Corollary 3.11);
* undetermined by the entire tower of congruences (Theorem 5.4);
* readable by a physical measurement instrument built from an unrelated algorithm (Corollary 6.9).

The third point is the one that makes the first two useful. In isolation, "the letter is a ratio band" is a pleasant reformulation. Combined with "residues say nothing", it becomes a statement about where the information lives; combined with a magnitude-based sensor drawn from factorisation practice, it becomes a bridge with an explicit, near-optimal constant.

### 9.2 Why the constant is $112$

The number $112 = 16\times 7$ is not mysterious. Outside the middle band the imbalance $q-p$ exceeds $q/4$, so the squared imbalance exceeds $q^2/16$; and the leg sum is below $\tfrac74 q$. The hit inequality $(q-p)^2 \le 4W(p+q)$ then reads $q^2/16 < 7Wq$. Both factors come from the band boundary $3/4$: change the band and the constant moves accordingly. The sharpness witness shows the loss from these two crude estimates is at most $2/112 \approx 1.8\%$.

### 9.3 Honest limits of the channel

Three limits deserve emphasis.

1. **One-way only.** A hit implies the letter; the letter does not imply a hit (Theorem 7.8).
2. **Scale-gated.** Below $112W$ the implication fails, and the sharpness witness shows the gate is real, not an artifact of the proof.
3. **Marginal-limited.** If the middle letter already has high marginal frequency in a population, an oracle that only fires on hits adds little raw predictive accuracy over always guessing the middle letter, even though its dependence with the letter is strictly positive. The correct measure of value is dependence and minority-structure recovery, not raw accuracy; Theorem 7.10 is the dependence statement, and its strictness is the honest claim.

### 9.4 Relation to Fermat factorisation

Theorem 6.3 is essentially the standard analysis of Fermat's method: the scan length to find $N=pq$ is $\Theta((q-p)^2/\sqrt N)$. What is new here is the *reading* of that scan length as an information channel about an unrelated combinatorial structure. The window bit is a one-bit quantisation of the relative offset $s/\sqrt{pq}$, which by Theorem 6.4 is a function of the ratio alone; the band is a two-threshold quantisation of the same ratio. Both are coarse-grainings of $\mathbb{P}^1$, and the mechanism theorem is precisely the statement that above a scale gate, the window's coarse-graining refines the band's on the hit side.

## 10. Future directions

1. **Optimising the window placement.** The scan here is anchored at $\sqrt{N}$ with fixed half-width $W$. Anchoring instead at a data-dependent point, or using a width growing like $N^{\alpha}$, should shift the hit-rate profile across bands; the resulting $(\alpha, \text{cost})$ trade-off is a natural next object.
2. **Closing the constant.** The optimal scale gate is in $[110,112]$. A finer analysis of the extremal families — those with $4p$ just below $3q$ and near-minimal offset — should pin it exactly.
3. **Deeper letters from a single measurement.** Theorem 4.4 shows the whole word is positional, but the window bit currently reads only the first letter. Does a *family* of windows at several widths read the first $k$ letters, and at what cost in scale?
4. **Other trees.** The Barning–Hall tree is one instance of a Vieta-jumping tree on a quadric. Does the band phenomenon generalise: for Markov triples, or Apollonian circle packings, is the branch letter a positional function and residue-sealed?
5. **Quantifying the ceiling.** Theorem 7.8 shows one family escapes every window. A density version — what fraction of middle-band triples up to height $X$ are $W$-hits, asymptotically in $X$ and $W$ — would convert the qualitative ceiling into an exact channel capacity.

---

## 11. Conclusion

The first branch letter of a primitive Pythagorean triple in the Barning–Hall tree is exactly a leg-ratio band; the whole address is exactly an iterated ratio computation; and no congruence, at any modulus, carries a single bit about it. A fixed-width Fermat window — a purely positional instrument built for a purpose entirely unrelated to the tree — therefore reads the letter whenever it fires, provided the scale exceeds $112$ times the window width, a threshold sharp to two units. The channel fires at every scale, its dependence with the letter is strictly positive on every admissible sample, and it can never be perfect. Sealed against arithmetic, open to position.
