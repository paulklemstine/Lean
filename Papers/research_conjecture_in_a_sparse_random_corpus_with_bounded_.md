## Abstract

We develop the extremal and structural theory of the higher-order co-citation complex of a finite corpus, the simplicial complex obtained by declaring every subset of a document's bibliography to be a face. Three questions of the "topology of theorem networks" programme are settled in deterministic form.

First, we identify the correct extremal parameter. Writing $n$ for the number of theorems and $d$ for a bound on document size, we show that a $d$-bounded corpus has no faces, and hence no homology, in dimension $d$ or above — a bound that is entirely independent of $n$. Among $d$-bounded corpora the maximal number of $q$-element faces is exactly $\binom{n}{q}$ for $q \le d$, attained by the *complete $d$-uniform design* in which every $d$-set of theorems is a document. We compute its Euler characteristic in closed form, $\chi = 1 - (-1)^d\binom{n-1}{d}$, by way of the partial alternating binomial identity $\sum_{j\le d}(-1)^j\binom{m+1}{j} = (-1)^d\binom{m}{d}$, and use the Euler–Poincaré relation as a pigeonhole to convert this counting statement into a topological lower bound: some Betti number in a dimension below $d$ is of order $n^d$, and — because all lower dimensions are capped at $O(n^{d-1})$ by their chain dimensions — the mass is concentrated in the single dimension $d-1$. Explicitly, $(n-d)^d \le d!\,(\beta_{d-1} + (d-1)n^{d-1} + 1)$. All homological statements are proved for an abstract *homology profile*: any sequence satisfying domination by chain dimensions and the Euler–Poincaré relation, hence simultaneously for every coefficient field. The class is nonempty: the classical Betti sequence of the skeleton realises it.

Second, we show that faithfulness of the pairwise projection is a strict hierarchy rather than a single exception. For every level $m \ge 2$ there is a corpus in which every clique of at most $m$ theorems has a common witnessing document while some clique of $m+1$ theorems does not; the familiar three-theorem hollow triangle is the case $m = 2$. For a $d$-bounded corpus, conformality (equality of the co-citation complex with the flag complex of its two-section) splits exactly into a clique-number condition and local conformality at level $d$; for the design family it is an all-or-nothing event at $d = n$.

Third, we prove a non-identifiability theorem: on a vertex-transitive corpus, every renaming-equivariant recovery rule is constant, so it fails to recover any labelling separating two theorems. The witnesses are the designs, which are simultaneously maximally symmetric and homologically extremal ($\beta_1 = \binom{n-1}{2} > 0$ already for the pairwise design), so the obstruction is symmetry rather than a shortage of topology.

Finally, we prove a *document budget*: in a $d$-bounded corpus with $N$ documents the number of $q$-faces is at most $N\binom{d}{q}$, a bound independent of $n$. Sparse corpora therefore never approach the binomial ceiling; concretely, a pairwise corpus on $n \ge 4$ theorems with at most $n$ documents has $\beta_1 < \binom{n}{2}$.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Motivation

A bibliography is a set, not a list of arrows. When an article cites results $A$, $B$ and $C$ together, it asserts a *simultaneous relevance* among them that no pairwise relation records. The natural object attached to a body of literature is therefore a hypergraph — one hyperedge per document — and the natural geometric object is the simplicial complex generated by that hypergraph.

Applied topology has taken this seriously, and a folklore has grown up around it: that Betti numbers of citation complexes obey power laws in the corpus size, that persistent homology detects the birth of research fields, that first-homology cycles correspond to research communities. These assertions are rarely stated precisely enough to be true or false. The present work supplies the missing precision, and in doing so replaces one folklore claim by a theorem, refines a second, and refutes a third.

### 1.2 Contributions

1. **The support of the topology is governed by document size, not corpus size** (Theorems 3.1, 3.2).
2. **The extremal $f$-vector is attained by the complete design** (Theorem 4.3), with Euler characteristic computed in closed form (Theorem 5.2).
3. **Euler characteristic forces and localises polynomial homology** (Theorems 6.5, 6.7, 6.8): the extremal Betti number has order $n^d$ and sits in dimension $d-1$.
4. **Conformality is a strict hierarchy** (Theorem 7.4), with a structural decomposition for bounded corpora (Theorem 7.7) and a sharp threshold for the design (Theorem 7.5).
5. **Semantic labels are not recoverable from incidence** (Theorem 8.3), witnessed by homologically rich corpora (Proposition 8.6).
6. **The document budget** (Theorem 9.1) and the resulting strict-sparsity separations (Theorems 9.3, 9.5).

### 1.3 Method

Homology is used axiomatically. Rather than fixing a coefficient field and constructing chain complexes, we isolate the two properties of field-coefficient simplicial homology that the arguments consume — that $\beta_k$ is at most the number of $k$-dimensional cells, and that the alternating sum of the $\beta_k$ equals the alternating sum of the cell counts — and prove everything from those. Every theorem below therefore holds for all coefficient fields at once, and the class of objects it speaks about is demonstrably nonempty (Proposition 6.9).

## 2. The model

Throughout, $V$ is a finite set of *theorems* with $n = |V|$, and all sets are finite.

**Definition 2.1 (Corpus).** A *corpus* $\mathcal{C}$ on $V$ is a finite family of subsets of $V$. Each member $W \in \mathcal{C}$ is a *document*, understood as the set of theorems it cites.

**Definition 2.2 (Co-citation complex).** The *co-citation complex* of $\mathcal{C}$ is the downward closure
$$X(\mathcal{C}) \;=\; \{\, S \subseteq V \;:\; S \subseteq W \text{ for some } W \in \mathcal{C} \,\}.$$
It is an abstract simplicial complex (closed under subsets). Its members are *faces*; a face with $q$ vertices has dimension $q - 1$.

**Definition 2.3 ($f$-vector).** For $q \ge 0$ let
$$f_q(\mathcal{C}) \;=\; \#\{\, S \in X(\mathcal{C}) : |S| = q \,\}$$
be the number of faces with exactly $q$ vertices, so $f_{k+1}(\mathcal{C})$ is the dimension of the $k$-th chain group over any field.

**Definition 2.4 (Two-section and flag complex).** The *co-citation graph* $G(\mathcal{C})$ has vertex set $V$ and an edge $\{x,y\}$ whenever $x \ne y$ and some document contains both. Its *flag complex* $\mathrm{Fl}(G(\mathcal{C}))$ consists of all cliques of $G(\mathcal{C})$.

Every face of $X(\mathcal{C})$ is a clique of $G(\mathcal{C})$, so $X(\mathcal{C}) \subseteq \mathrm{Fl}(G(\mathcal{C}))$ always. Equality is a genuine restriction.

**Definition 2.5 (Conformality).** $\mathcal{C}$ is *conformal* if every clique of $G(\mathcal{C})$ is contained in a single document; equivalently, $X(\mathcal{C}) = \mathrm{Fl}(G(\mathcal{C}))$.

**Definition 2.6 (Document-size bound).** For $d \in \mathbb{N}$, the corpus $\mathcal{C}$ is *$d$-bounded* if $|W| \le d$ for every $W \in \mathcal{C}$.

The following is the ambient counting bound from which the programme starts; it is immediate, since faces of size $q$ are $q$-subsets of $V$.

**Proposition 2.7 (Binomial ceiling).** $f_q(\mathcal{C}) \le \binom{n}{q}$ for every corpus $\mathcal{C}$ and every $q$.

Proposition 2.7 mentions only $n$. Everything that follows argues that this is the wrong parameter.

## 3. Document size bounds the support of the topology

**Theorem 3.1 (Vanishing of faces).** *If $\mathcal{C}$ is $d$-bounded and $q > d$, then $f_q(\mathcal{C}) = 0$; that is, $X(\mathcal{C})$ has no face with more than $d$ vertices.*

*Proof.* A face $S$ satisfies $S \subseteq W$ for some document $W$, whence $|S| \le |W| \le d < q$. $\square$

Consequently $X(\mathcal{C})$ is a complex of dimension at most $d - 1$: it embeds in the $(d-1)$-skeleton of the simplex on $V$. In particular, no chain group is nonzero in degree $\ge d$, and so:

**Theorem 3.2 (Dimension obstruction).** *For a $d$-bounded corpus, every Betti number vanishes in dimensions $k \ge d$, for every coefficient field:*
$$\beta_k(X(\mathcal{C})) = 0 \qquad (k \ge d).$$

*Proof.* $\beta_k$ is bounded by the dimension $f_{k+1}$ of the $k$-th chain group, which is $0$ by Theorem 3.1 since $k + 1 > d$. $\square$

This is a strict sharpening of the naive obstruction "no homology above dimension $n$": here $d$ may be a small constant while $n \to \infty$. **Every claim about the existence of $k$-dimensional structure in a citation corpus is a claim about bibliography lengths exceeding $k$.**

## 4. The extremal corpus: the complete $d$-uniform design

**Definition 4.1 (Design corpus).** For $0 \le d \le n$, the *complete $d$-uniform design* $\mathcal{D}_{n,d}$ is the corpus consisting of **all** $d$-element subsets of $V$.

$\mathcal{D}_{n,d}$ is $d$-bounded, and it is maximally symmetric: it is invariant under every permutation of $V$.

**Proposition 4.2 (The design generates a skeleton).** *For $d \le n$ and $S \subseteq V$ we have $S \in X(\mathcal{D}_{n,d})$ if and only if $|S| \le d$. Hence $X(\mathcal{D}_{n,d})$ is the full $(d-1)$-skeleton of the simplex on $V$, and $f_q(\mathcal{D}_{n,d}) = \binom{n}{q}$ for $q \le d$ (and $0$ for $q > d$).*

*Proof.* Necessity is Theorem 3.1. For sufficiency, if $|S| \le d \le n$ then $S$ extends to some $W \subseteq V$ with $|W| = d$, and $W$ is a document. $\square$

**Theorem 4.3 (Sharp extremal face count).** *Fix $q \le d \le n$. Then*
$$\max\{\, f_q(\mathcal{C}) \;:\; \mathcal{C} \text{ a } d\text{-bounded corpus on } V \,\} \;=\; \binom{n}{q},$$
*and the maximum is attained by $\mathcal{D}_{n,d}$.*

*Proof.* The upper bound is Proposition 2.7; attainment is Proposition 4.2. $\square$

The extremal $f$-vector entry is genuinely of polynomial order $n^q$: since $q!\binom nq = n^{\underline q} \ge (n-q)^q$,
$$(n-q)^q \;\le\; q!\, f_q(\mathcal{D}_{n,d}) \;\le\; n^q \qquad (q \le d).$$

## 5. Euler characteristic of the design

**Definition 5.1.** The *Euler characteristic* of a corpus is the alternating sum of face counts by dimension,
$$\chi(\mathcal{C}) \;=\; \sum_{q \ge 0} (-1)^q f_{q+1}(\mathcal{C}) \;=\; f_1 - f_2 + f_3 - \cdots.$$
(The empty face is not counted, so $\chi$ is the unreduced Euler characteristic.)

**Lemma 5.2 (Partial alternating binomial identity).** *For all $m, d \ge 0$,*
$$\sum_{j=0}^{d} (-1)^j \binom{m+1}{j} \;=\; (-1)^d \binom{m}{d}.$$

*Proof.* Induction on $d$. For $d = 0$ both sides are $1$. For the step, Pascal's rule gives $\binom{m+1}{d+1} = \binom{m}{d} + \binom{m}{d+1}$, so adding $(-1)^{d+1}\binom{m+1}{d+1}$ to $(-1)^d\binom md$ yields $(-1)^{d+1}\binom{m}{d+1}$. $\square$

**Theorem 5.3 (Euler characteristic of the design).** *For $1 \le d \le n$, writing $m = n - 1$,*
$$\chi(\mathcal{D}_{n,d}) \;=\; 1 - (-1)^d \binom{n-1}{d}.$$

*Proof.* By Proposition 4.2 the sum truncates at dimension $d-1$:
$$\chi(\mathcal{D}_{n,d}) = \sum_{q=0}^{d-1} (-1)^q \binom{n}{q+1} = -\sum_{j=1}^{d} (-1)^{j}\binom{m+1}{j}.$$
Adding and subtracting the $j = 0$ term and applying Lemma 5.2 with the same $m$ and $d$ gives $-\left((-1)^d\binom md - 1\right)$. $\square$

**Examples.** $(n,d) = (3,2)$: hollow triangle, $\chi = 1 - \binom22 = 0 = 3 - 3$. $(n,d) = (4,3)$: $2$-skeleton of the tetrahedron, $\chi = 1 + \binom33 = 2 = 4 - 6 + 4$. $(n,d) = (5,3)$: $2$-skeleton of the $4$-simplex, $\chi = 1 + \binom43 = 5 = 5 - 10 + 10$. $(n,d)=(n,2)$: a complete graph, $\chi = 1 - \binom{n-1}{2} = n - \binom n2$.

Since $\binom{n-1}{d} = \Theta(n^d)$ for fixed $d$, the design has Euler characteristic of magnitude $\Theta(n^d)$ — but supported on only $d$ possibly-nonzero Betti numbers. This tension is the engine of the next section.

## 6. Homology profiles and the extremal lower bound

### 6.1 The axiomatisation

**Definition 6.1 (Homology profile).** A *homology profile* of a corpus $\mathcal{C}$ is a sequence $(\beta_k)_{k\ge 0}$ of nonnegative integers satisfying

* **(P1) Chain domination.** $\beta_k \le f_{k+1}(\mathcal{C})$ for all $k$;
* **(P2) Euler–Poincaré.** $\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1} (-1)^k \beta_k = \chi(\mathcal{C})$.

For any field $F$, the simplicial homology of $X(\mathcal{C})$ with coefficients in $F$ furnishes such a sequence: (P1) is rank–nullity applied to the boundary maps, and (P2) is the classical Euler–Poincaré theorem. Since nothing below uses more, every result applies to every coefficient field simultaneously — and to any other invariant satisfying (P1)–(P2).

### 6.2 Immediate consequences

**Proposition 6.2.** *If $\mathcal{C}$ is $d$-bounded then $\beta_k = 0$ for $k \ge d$ (by (P1) and Theorem 3.1); and always $\beta_k \le \binom{n}{k+1}$ (by (P1) and Proposition 2.7).*

**Lemma 6.3 (Euler mass is carried by surviving dimensions).** *If $\mathcal{C}$ is $d$-bounded with $d \le n$, then*
$$|\chi(\mathcal{C})| \;\le\; \sum_{k=0}^{d-1} \beta_k.$$

*Proof.* By Proposition 6.2 the Euler–Poincaré sum truncates to $k < d$; the triangle inequality then removes the signs. $\square$

### 6.3 Concentration

**Theorem 6.4 (Euler characteristic forces a large Betti number).** *Let $\mathcal{C}$ be $d$-bounded with $1 \le d \le n$ and let $(\beta_k)$ be a homology profile. Then there is $k < d$ with*
$$|\chi(\mathcal{C})| \;\le\; d\,\beta_k.$$

*Proof.* Choose $k < d$ maximising $\beta_k$; then $\sum_{j<d}\beta_j \le d\beta_k$, and apply Lemma 6.3. $\square$

**Theorem 6.5 (Localisation at the top dimension).** *Under the hypotheses of Theorem 6.4,*
$$|\chi(\mathcal{C})| \;\le\; \beta_{d-1} \;+\; \sum_{k=0}^{d-2} f_{k+1}(\mathcal{C}).$$

*Proof.* In Lemma 6.3, keep the term $k = d-1$ and bound every other $\beta_k$ by $f_{k+1}$ using (P1). $\square$

The point of Theorem 6.5 is a difference of orders: the correction $\sum_{k<d-1} f_{k+1}$ is a sum of $d-1$ binomial coefficients each at most $\binom{n}{d-1} = O(n^{d-1})$, whereas for the design $|\chi|$ is of order $n^d$. Hence for fixed $d$ and large $n$, $\beta_{d-1}$ must itself be of order $n^d$.

### 6.4 The extremal bounds for the design

**Lemma 6.6.** *For $1 \le d \le n$, $\;\binom{n-1}{d} \le |\chi(\mathcal{D}_{n,d})| + 1$.*

*Proof.* By Theorem 5.3, $\chi = 1 - (-1)^d\binom{n-1}{d}$. If $d$ is odd, $\chi = 1 + \binom{n-1}{d} > \binom{n-1}{d}$. If $d$ is even, $\chi = 1 - \binom{n-1}{d}$, so $|\chi| \ge \binom{n-1}{d} - 1$. $\square$

**Theorem 6.7 (Polynomial extremal growth).** *Let $(\beta_k)$ be a homology profile of $\mathcal{D}_{n,d}$ with $1 \le d \le n$. Then there is $k < d$ with*
$$\binom{n-1}{d} \;\le\; d\,\beta_k + 1, \qquad\text{and hence}\qquad (n-d)^d \;\le\; d!\,d\,\beta_k \;+\; d!.$$

*Proof.* Combine Theorem 6.4 with Lemma 6.6 for the first inequality. For the second, $d!\binom{n-1}{d} = (n-1)^{\underline d} \ge (n-d)^d$, since each of the $d$ descending factors $n-1, n-2, \dots, n-d$ is at least $n-d$. $\square$

**Theorem 6.8 (Localised extremal growth).** *Under the same hypotheses,*
$$\binom{n-1}{d} \;\le\; \beta_{d-1} \;+\; \sum_{k=0}^{d-2}\binom{n}{k+1} \;+\; 1 \;\le\; \beta_{d-1} + (d-1)\,n^{d-1} + 1,$$
*and therefore*
$$(n-d)^d \;\le\; d!\left(\beta_{d-1} + (d-1)n^{d-1} + 1\right).$$

*Proof.* Apply Theorem 6.5 to $\mathcal{D}_{n,d}$, use $f_{k+1} = \binom{n}{k+1}$ (Proposition 4.2), Lemma 6.6, and $\binom{n}{k+1} \le n^{d-1}$ for $k \le d-2$. $\square$

**Interpretation.** For fixed $d$ and $n \to \infty$ the right-hand correction is $O(n^{d-1})$ while the left side is $\Theta(n^d)$, so $\beta_{d-1} = \Theta(n^d)$; meanwhile Proposition 6.2 caps every $\beta_k$ with $k \le d-2$ at $\binom{n}{k+1} = O(n^{d-1})$. **The extremal homology is not distributed across dimensions: it is concentrated in the single dimension $d-1$, at polynomial order $n^d$, and it is exactly zero from dimension $d$ upwards.** This is the precise, and only correct, form of the folklore "power law": the exponent is not free, it is pinned to the top surviving dimension by the document-size bound.

### 6.5 Non-vacuity

**Proposition 6.9 (Realisation).** *For $1 \le d \le n$ the sequence*
$$\beta_k \;=\; [\,k = 0\,] \;+\; [\,k = d-1\,]\binom{n-1}{d}$$
*is a homology profile of $\mathcal{D}_{n,d}$. (For $d = 1$ the two indicators coincide and the formula returns $\beta_0 = n$, correctly describing $n$ isolated theorems.)*

*Proof.* This is the classical Betti sequence of the $(d-1)$-skeleton of an $(n-1)$-simplex: it is connected, has no homology in intermediate dimensions, and its top homology is free of rank $\binom{n-1}{d}$. Property (P1) holds because $1 \le \binom n1$, $\binom{n-1}{d} \le \binom{n}{d} = f_d$, and all other terms vanish; property (P2) reduces, via Theorem 5.3 and $(-1)^{d-1} = -(-1)^d$, to the identity $1 - (-1)^d\binom{n-1}{d} = 1 + (-1)^{d-1}\binom{n-1}{d}$. $\square$

So the class of profiles is nonempty, all bounds above are attained up to their stated correction terms, and Theorems 6.7–6.8 are not vacuous statements about an empty class.

## 7. Conformality is a strict hierarchy

Real pipelines rarely see documents; they see the co-citation *graph*, and reconstruct the complex as its flag complex. The classical warning example is the corpus $\{\{A,B\},\{A,C\},\{B,C\}\}$: the graph is a triangle, so the flag complex has a $2$-cell, while the true complex is a hollow triangle with $\beta_1 = 1$. The pairwise shadow has invented a face. We show this is the bottom rung of an infinite ladder.

**Definition 7.1 (Local conformality).** $\mathcal{C}$ is *locally conformal at level $m$* if every clique $S$ of $G(\mathcal{C})$ with $|S| \le m$ is contained in a single document.

Conformality is local conformality at level $n$, and local conformality is monotone decreasing in $m$.

**Proposition 7.2 (The pairwise projection of a design is blind).** *For $2 \le d \le n$, $G(\mathcal{D}_{n,d})$ is the complete graph $K_n$.*

*Proof.* Any pair $\{x,y\}$ has size $2 \le d$, so it extends to a $d$-set, which is a document. $\square$

**Proposition 7.3.** *For $2 \le d$, the design $\mathcal{D}_{n,d}$ is locally conformal at level $d$ (indeed every set of $\le d$ theorems, clique or not, is inside a document), and it is not locally conformal at level $d+1$ whenever $d + 1 \le n$.*

*Proof.* The first claim is set extension as in Proposition 4.2. For the second, take any $S$ with $|S| = d+1$; it is a clique by Proposition 7.2, but no document has $d+1$ elements. $\square$

**Theorem 7.4 (Hierarchy theorem).** *For every $m \ge 2$ there is a corpus (on $m+1$ theorems) that is locally conformal at level $m$, is not locally conformal at level $m+1$, and is therefore not conformal.*

*Proof.* Take $\mathcal{D}_{m+1,m}$ and apply Proposition 7.3 with $n = m+1$, $d = m$. $\square$

Thus no fixed level of local witness-checking certifies conformality: for every $m$, passing all checks up to size $m$ is consistent with failure at size $m+1$. The three-theorem hollow triangle is exactly the case $m = 2$, i.e. $\mathcal{D}_{3,2}$; it is *a* minimal obstruction at its level, not *the* obstruction.

**Theorem 7.5 (Sharp threshold for the design).** *For $2 \le d \le n$, $\mathcal{D}_{n,d}$ is conformal if and only if $d = n$.*

*Proof.* If $d < n$ then Proposition 7.3 exhibits a clique of size $d+1 \le n$ with no witness. If $d = n$ the unique document is all of $V$ and every subset is a face. $\square$

**Corollary 7.6 (Strict loss of the pairwise projection).** *For $2 \le d < n$, $X(\mathcal{D}_{n,d}) \subsetneq \mathrm{Fl}(G(\mathcal{D}_{n,d}))$. Quantitatively, the flag complex has all $2^{\,n}$ subsets as faces while the true complex has only $\sum_{q \le d}\binom nq$: the projection invents $2^n - \sum_{q\le d}\binom nq$ faces.*

**Theorem 7.7 (Structure of conformality for bounded corpora).** *If $\mathcal{C}$ is $d$-bounded, then $\mathcal{C}$ is conformal if and only if*
1. *(clique-number condition) every clique of $G(\mathcal{C})$ has at most $d$ vertices, and*
2. *(local condition) $\mathcal{C}$ is locally conformal at level $d$.*

*Proof.* ($\Rightarrow$) A clique lies in a document, which has $\le d$ elements; local conformality is a weakening. ($\Leftarrow$) Any clique has $\le d$ elements by (1), hence a witness by (2). $\square$

Condition (2) is verifiable by bounded local search; condition (1) is global, and by Theorem 7.4 no amount of bounded local search implies it. Any probabilistic "conformality threshold" must therefore be indexed by a witness level, or equivalently stated in terms of the clique number of the two-section relative to the maximal document size.

## 8. Semantic labels are not recoverable from incidence

Write $L$ for a finite set of community labels. A *recovery rule* is a map $R$ assigning to each corpus $\mathcal{C}$ on $V$ a labelling $R(\mathcal{C}) : V \to L$. The relevant stability notion is invariance under renaming of theorems.

**Definition 8.1 (Relabelling; uniformity).** For a permutation $\sigma$ of $V$, let $\sigma\mathcal{C} = \{\sigma(W) : W \in \mathcal{C}\}$. A rule $R$ is *uniform* if for all $\sigma$, $\mathcal{C}$ and $v$,
$$R(\sigma\mathcal{C})(\sigma v) \;=\; R(\mathcal{C})(v).$$

Uniformity says exactly that the rule reads the incidence pattern and nothing else — no identity, no metadata. Every rule constructed from homology, persistence, spectra, clique structure or any other isomorphism-invariant of the corpus is uniform.

**Definition 8.2 (Vertex transitivity).** $\mathcal{C}$ is *vertex-transitive* if for all $x, y \in V$ there is a permutation $\sigma$ with $\sigma\mathcal{C} = \mathcal{C}$ and $\sigma x = y$.

**Theorem 8.3 (Non-identifiability).** *Let $R$ be uniform and $\mathcal{C}$ vertex-transitive. Then $R(\mathcal{C})$ is constant. Consequently, for any ground-truth labelling $\mathrm{lab} : V \to L$ with $\mathrm{lab}(x) \ne \mathrm{lab}(y)$ for some $x,y$, we have $R(\mathcal{C}) \ne \mathrm{lab}$.*

*Proof.* Given $x,y$, pick $\sigma$ with $\sigma\mathcal{C} = \mathcal{C}$, $\sigma x = y$. Uniformity gives $R(\mathcal{C})(y) = R(\sigma\mathcal{C})(\sigma x) = R(\mathcal{C})(x)$. $\square$

**Proposition 8.4 (Designs are vertex-transitive).** *$\mathcal{D}_{n,d}$ is invariant under every permutation of $V$ — the image of the family of all $d$-sets under a bijection is again the family of all $d$-sets — hence transpositions witness vertex transitivity.*

**Corollary 8.5.** *No uniform rule recovers any non-constant labelling of the theorems of a complete design, for any document size $d$.*

One might object that the counterexample could be topologically degenerate. It is the opposite.

**Proposition 8.6 (The witness is homologically rich).** *For the pairwise design $\mathcal{D}_{n,2}$ with $n \ge 3$, the realised profile of Proposition 6.9 has $\beta_1 = \binom{n-1}{2} > 0$.* Indeed by Theorem 6.8 the designs carry the maximum homology any $d$-bounded corpus can carry.

**Proposition 8.7 (Metadata is genuine information).** *If $R$ recovers a labelling $\mathrm{lab}$ separating two theorems on a vertex-transitive corpus — for instance the trivial rule $R(\mathcal{C}) = \mathrm{lab}$ — then $R$ is not uniform.*

This is the converse reading of Theorem 8.3, and the useful one: vertex metadata cannot be a repackaging of the incidence data, because incidence-only rules provably cannot see it. The theorem concerns deterministic exact recovery on the symmetric extremes; it leaves open approximate or probabilistic recovery under a generative assortativity assumption, which is precisely where the identifiability assumptions must be made explicit.

## 9. Sparse corpora and the document budget

The binomial ceiling $\binom nq$ counts what the *ambient* vertex set permits. Real corpora are limited by what has been written.

**Theorem 9.1 (Document budget).** *If $\mathcal{C}$ is $d$-bounded with $N = |\mathcal{C}|$ documents, then for every $q$,*
$$f_q(\mathcal{C}) \;\le\; N\binom{d}{q}.$$
*Consequently $\beta_k \le N\binom{d}{k+1}$ for every homology profile. Neither bound involves $n$.*

*Proof.* Every $q$-face is a $q$-subset of some document, so $X(\mathcal{C})$'s $q$-faces are contained in $\bigcup_{W\in\mathcal{C}}\binom{W}{q}$, of size at most $\sum_{W}\binom{|W|}{q} \le N\binom dq$. The homological statement follows from (P1). $\square$

**Corollary 9.2 (Tightness).** *A corpus consisting of one document of size $d$ has exactly $\binom dq$ faces of size $q$, so Theorem 9.1 is attained.*

**Theorem 9.3 (Sparse corpora miss the ceiling).** *If $\mathcal{C}$ is $d$-bounded and $N\binom{d}{k+1} < \binom{n}{k+1}$, then $\beta_k < \binom{n}{k+1}$ strictly.*

In asymptotic language: if the number of documents grows more slowly than $n^{k+1}$, the normalised Betti number $\beta_k/\binom{n}{k+1}$ tends to $0$. This is the deterministic skeleton that any random null model must inherit, and it is what makes the folklore power law falsifiable: reaching order $n^{k+1}$ requires a comparable number of documents.

**Theorem 9.4 (Attaining the ceiling requires many documents).** *If a $d$-bounded corpus satisfies $f_q(\mathcal{C}) = \binom nq$, then $N \ge \binom nq / \binom dq$.*

**Theorem 9.5 (Explicit small case).** *Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a $2$-bounded corpus (pure pairwise co-citation) on $n \ge 4$ theorems with at most $n$ documents. Then*
$$\beta_1 \;<\; \binom n2,$$
*even though dimension $1$ is the only dimension such a corpus can support.*

*Proof.* By Theorem 9.1, $\beta_1 \le N\binom22 = N \le n$, and $n < \binom n2$ for $n \ge 4$ (induction: $\binom{p+1}{2} = p + \binom p2$, with base $4 < 6$). $\square$

**Proposition 9.6 (Non-vacuity).** *For $n \ge 4$ the hypotheses of Theorem 9.5 are satisfiable by an object with an honest homology profile: a single document of size $2$ is $2$-bounded, has one document, has $\chi = 1$, and admits the profile $\beta_0 = 1$, $\beta_k = 0$ otherwise.*

The gap between the two ceilings is the whole point. The ambient ceiling grows like $n^{k+1}$ and is attained only by the design, which needs $\binom nd$ documents. The document budget grows like $N$ and is what governs any corpus produced by finitely many authors.

## 10. Algorithms

Three computational primitives follow directly from the theory.

**(A) Design profile evaluation.** Given $n, d$, output the $f$-vector $\left(\binom n1,\dots,\binom nd\right)$, the Euler characteristic via the closed form $1 - (-1)^d\binom{n-1}{d}$ (verified against the direct alternating sum), the realised Betti profile of Proposition 6.9, and the certified bounds of Theorems 6.7–6.8. Cost: $O(d)$ arithmetic operations after $O(d)$ binomial evaluations, versus $O(nd)$ for naive summation.

**(B) Conformality audit.** Given a corpus, build the two-section, enumerate maximal cliques, and report the largest $m$ such that every clique of size $\le m$ has a witnessing document, together with the minimal witness-free cliques. By Theorem 7.7 the audit terminates with a definitive answer once $m$ exceeds the clique number; by Theorem 7.4 no earlier stopping rule is sound. Cost is dominated by clique enumeration, $O(3^{n/3})$ in the worst case but linear in the number of maximal cliques in practice.

**(C) Modulo-2 Betti computation for corpus complexes.** Enumerate the faces of $X(\mathcal{C})$ by dimension, build boundary matrices over $\mathbb{F}_2$, and compute $\beta_k = f_{k+1} - \mathrm{rank}\,\partial_k - \mathrm{rank}\,\partial_{k+1}$ by Gaussian elimination with bitset rows. Cost $O(\sum_k f_{k+1} f_k \min(f_{k+1},f_k)/w)$ with word size $w$; feasible for the small corpora on which the phenomena above are illustrated, and by Theorem 3.1 only $d$ dimensions ever need to be processed.

## 11. Discussion

**What was refuted.** The reading of the three-theorem hollow triangle as *the* conformality obstruction, and the reading of first homology as a semantic object. The first fails by the hierarchy theorem; the second by non-identifiability on maximally symmetric, maximally homological corpora.

**What was refined.** The power-law folklore. Polynomial growth of Betti numbers is genuinely possible, but only in the single dimension $d-1$, only at exponent $d$, and only for corpora with $\Theta(\binom nd)$ documents. In dimensions $\ge d$ the Betti numbers are exactly zero for every corpus and every field.

**What was added.** The document budget, which replaces an ambient combinatorial ceiling by an authorship-limited one and makes normalised Betti asymptotics computable from corpus statistics alone.

**Limitations.** (i) The homology profile is an axiomatisation, not a construction; it captures exactly the two properties used, both theorems for field-coefficient homology of a finite complex, but a claim depending on more (torsion, cup products, homotopy type) is out of scope. (ii) Theorem 6.4 bounds a maximum over dimensions, not a specific dimension; only the comparison of orders in Theorem 6.8 localises it, and that comparison is asymptotic in $n$ for fixed $d$. (iii) The bound $(n-d)^d$ degenerates when $d$ is comparable to $n$ — correctly, since $\mathcal{D}_{n,n}$ is a simplex and contractible. (iv) "Sparse" here is a hypothesis on the document count, not a probability statement.

## 12. Future work

1. **A null model for higher-dimensional citation topology.** For a sparse random corpus with bounded expected document size and theorem degrees converging to a nondegenerate distribution, is each fixed-dimensional normalised Betti number convergent to a deterministic limit? The document budget shows the limit is $0$ whenever the document count is subpolynomial of the right order; the interesting regime is the matched one, where boundary ranks, not face counts, decide.

2. **Conformality thresholds in temporal co-citation hypergraphs.** In a growing random corpus, is there a sharp threshold at which every clique of the two-section acquires a common witness? Theorem 7.7 identifies the two events to track; Theorem 7.4 shows the threshold must be indexed by witness level.

3. **Persistent detection of localised structural change.** Since the co-citation complex of a growing corpus is monotone, it defines a filtration. Does an abrupt rise and decay in persistent second homology detect sustained failure of local conformality more reliably than raw triangle or tetrahedron counts? Corollary 7.6 supplies the elementary mechanism: pairwise projection fills spurious cells, while the genuine hypergraph filtration records whether the cavity survives.

4. **Uniqueness of the extremal corpus.** Theorem 9.4 shows that attaining the binomial ceiling requires $\Omega(\binom nq/\binom dq)$ documents. Is the design the unique $d$-bounded corpus attaining $f_q = \binom nq$ for all $q \le d$, up to redundant documents?

5. **Extremal profiles for structured corpora.** Multipartite constructions, block designs, and bounded-degree corpora each restrict the achievable $f$-vector further. What is the extremal $\beta_{d-1}$ under a bound on how many documents a single theorem may appear in?

6. **Identifiability under assortativity.** Theorem 8.3 rules out exact uniform recovery. Under a generative model in which documents preferentially cite within a latent community, at what signal strength does approximate recovery become possible, and does homology contribute information beyond graph connectivity?

## 13. Summary of results

| Vanishing of faces | $d$-bounded $\Rightarrow f_q = 0$ for $q > d$ |

| Euler characteristic of the design | $\chi = 1 - (-1)^d\binom{n-1}{d}$ |
| Concentration | $\exists k < d$ with $|\chi| \le d\beta_k$ |
| Localisation | $|\chi| \le \beta_{d-1} + \sum_{k<d-1} f_{k+1}$ |
| Extremal growth | $(n-d)^d \le d!\left(\beta_{d-1} + (d-1)n^{d-1} + 1\right)$ |
| Realisation | $\beta_0 = 1$, $\beta_{d-1} = \binom{n-1}{d}$ is a valid profile |
