# Plethystic Triviality of the Shifted $t$-Schur Basis and the Diagonal Plethysm Group

**Author:** Aristotle
**Date:** 2026-06-28
**Domain:** Algebraic combinatorics / symmetric function theory (Novelty)

---

## Abstract

We study a one-parameter deformation of the Schur $Q$-functions, the *shifted $t$-Schur
functions* $S^t_\lambda$, defined through the Fourier modes of a $t$-deformed odd vertex
("GJZ"-type) operator on the ring $\Gamma = R[p_1, p_3, p_5, \dots]$ generated by the odd
power sums. We prove that this deformation is *plethystically trivial*: the single
$R$-algebra endomorphism $\varphi_t$ determined by $\varphi_t(p_{2k+1}) = (1 - t^{2k+1})\,
p_{2k+1}$ carries the Schur $Q$-basis onto the shifted $t$-Schur basis, i.e.
$$S^t_\lambda = \varphi_t(Q_\lambda) \quad\text{for every strict partition } \lambda.$$
Working over $K = \mathbb{Q}(t)$, where every factor $1 - t^{2k+1}$ is a unit, we show
$\varphi_t$ is an algebra automorphism with explicit inverse
$\psi_t(p_{2k+1}) = (1 - t^{2k+1})^{-1} p_{2k+1}$, whence $Q_\lambda = \psi_t(S^t_\lambda)$
and the two families are related by an invertible relabeling: a family $\{S^t_{\lambda_i}\}$
is $K$-linearly independent iff $\{Q_{\lambda_i}\}$ is. We then identify the structural
mechanism behind triviality. The maps $\Phi_a : p_{2k+1} \mapsto a_k\, p_{2k+1}$ (the
**diagonal plethysms**) satisfy $\Phi_a \circ \Phi_b = \Phi_{a\cdot b}$ and $\Phi_1 = \mathrm{id}$;
the assignment $a \mapsto \Phi_a$ is injective, and on invertible sequences it is a faithful
group homomorphism $(K^\times)^{\mathbb{N}} \to \mathrm{Aut}_K(\Gamma)$. We establish the sharp
dichotomy that $\Phi_a$ is injective iff $a$ never vanishes, exhibit explicit non-injective
degenerations, and locate $\varphi_t$ as the member at the non-vanishing locus, degenerating
to a non-injective map under the specialization $t \mapsto 1$. All results have been formally
verified in the Lean 4 proof assistant with Mathlib.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Context

Symmetric function theory hosts several canonical bases indexed by partitions. The Schur
$Q$-functions $Q_\lambda$, indexed by *strict partitions* $\lambda$, form a distinguished basis
of the subalgebra $\Gamma \subseteq \Lambda$ generated by the odd power sums; they govern the
projective (spin) representation theory of the symmetric groups, the cohomology of isotropic
Grassmannians, and the theory of $P$- and $Q$-functions of Schur and Pragacz. A modern and
flexible way to generate them is via **vertex operators**: one realizes $Q_\lambda$ as an
ordered product of creation–annihilation operators applied to the vacuum $1$.

Deforming the vertex-operator data by a parameter $t$ produces a family of *shifted $t$-Schur
functions* $S^t_\lambda$. A priori, $S^t_\lambda$ is built by a recursion whose every step
carries $t$-dependent coefficients, and it is not obvious how $S^t_\lambda$ relates to
$Q_\lambda$. The purpose of this paper is to show that the relationship is as simple as
possible: a single diagonal plethystic substitution on the generators.

### 1.2 Results

Our contributions are:

1. **The triviality identity** (Theorem 4.1): $S^t_\lambda = \varphi_t(Q_\lambda)$ for every
   strict partition $\lambda$, where $\varphi_t$ is the diagonal plethysm
   $p_{2k+1} \mapsto (1-t^{2k+1}) p_{2k+1}$. The engine is the intertwining relation
   $B^t_n \circ \varphi_t = \varphi_t \circ B_n$ between the deformed and undeformed vertex
   components (Lemma 3.4).

2. **Invertibility and basis triviality** (Theorems 4.3–4.4): $\varphi_t$ is an automorphism
   with inverse $\psi_t$; hence $Q_\lambda = \psi_t(S^t_\lambda)$, and the $t$-Schur family is
   linearly independent precisely when the Schur $Q$-family is.

3. **The diagonal plethysm group** (Section 5): the operators $\Phi_a$ form a commutative
   monoid under composition with $\Phi_a \circ \Phi_b = \Phi_{a\cdot b}$; the parametrization
   $a \mapsto \Phi_a$ is injective; restricted to unit sequences it is a faithful group
   homomorphism $(K^\times)^{\mathbb{N}} \to \mathrm{Aut}_K(\Gamma)$.

4. **The non-triviality boundary** (Section 6): $\Phi_a$ is injective iff $a$ never vanishes
   (sharp dichotomy), with explicit non-injective counterexamples, and the degeneration of
   $\varphi_t$ under $t \mapsto 1$.

All statements have been formally verified in Lean 4 / Mathlib; theorem names from the formal
development are cited in brackets, e.g. [`Sfun_eq_phiT_Qfun`].

## 2. Setup and definitions

Throughout, fix the coefficient field $K = \mathbb{Q}(t)$ of rational functions in a single
transcendental $t$. We model the ring of symmetric functions generated by the odd power sums
by a polynomial ring in countably many variables.

**Definition 2.1 (Odd power-sum ring).** Let
$$\Gamma \;=\; \mathrm{MvPolynomial}\,(\mathbb{N}, K),$$
the polynomial $K$-algebra on generators $X_0, X_1, X_2, \dots$. We interpret the variable
$X_k$ as the odd power sum $p_{2k+1}$, so that $\Gamma$ models $K[p_1, p_3, p_5, \dots]$. We
write $\Lambda$ (Lean: `Lam`) for $\Gamma$. [`Lam`]

**Definition 2.2 (Deformation scalars).** Let $t = X \in \mathbb{Q}(t)$ (Lean: `tt`) and set
$$c_k \;=\; 1 - t^{2k+1} \in K \qquad (k \in \mathbb{N}). \tag{`cc`}$$

**Lemma 2.3 (Non-vanishing).** For every $k$, $c_k \neq 0$. [`cc_ne`]

*Proof.* Write $1 - t^{2k+1} = \iota(1 - X^{2k+1})$ where $\iota : \mathbb{Q}[X] \hookrightarrow
\mathbb{Q}(X)$ is the fraction-field embedding. Since $\iota$ is injective, it suffices that the
polynomial $1 - X^{2k+1}$ is nonzero, which is clear (e.g. its value at $X = 0$ is $1$). $\square$

**Definition 2.4 (Inverse scalars).** $d_k = 1/c_k = (1 - t^{2k+1})^{-1}$. [`dd`] By
Lemma 2.3 this is well defined and $c_k d_k = 1$. [`cc_mul_dd`]

**Definition 2.5 (The plethystic operators).** Define the $K$-algebra endomorphisms
$\varphi_t, \psi_t : \Gamma \to \Gamma$ as the unique algebra homomorphisms with
$$\varphi_t(X_k) = c_k\, X_k = (1 - t^{2k+1})\, X_k, \qquad
\psi_t(X_k) = d_k\, X_k = \frac{X_k}{1 - t^{2k+1}}. \tag{`phiT`, `psiT`}$$
Concretely $\varphi_t = \mathrm{aeval}(k \mapsto C(c_k)\, X_k)$, where $C$ is the inclusion of
scalars and $\mathrm{aeval}$ is universal evaluation of the polynomial algebra. In plethystic
notation, $\varphi_t$ performs the substitution $p_n \mapsto (1 - t^n)\, p_n$ on the odd
generators.

## 3. The vertex-operator construction

We recall the vertex-operator realization of $Q_\lambda$ and its $t$-deformation, following the
formal development.

**Definition 3.1 (Creation functions).** Given a coefficient sequence $\mathrm{cf} : \mathbb{N}
\to \Gamma$, define $q^{\mathrm{cf}}_m \in \Gamma$ by $q^{\mathrm{cf}}_0 = 1$ and the Newton
recursion
$$q^{\mathrm{cf}}_{m+1} \;=\; \frac{1}{m+1} \sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor} 2\, \mathrm{cf}_k\;
q^{\mathrm{cf}}_{m - 2k}. \tag{`qGen`}$$
These are the homogeneous coefficients of the generating kernel $\exp\!\big(\sum_k
\tfrac{2}{2k+1}\,\mathrm{cf}_k\, z^{2k+1}\big)$ read off through its logarithmic derivative.
The **one-row Schur $Q$-functions** are $q_m = q^{(X_k)}_m$ [`q`] (coefficients fed the plain
generators), and the **$t$-deformed one-row functions** are $q^t_m = q^{(c_k X_k)}_m$ [`qt`]
(coefficients fed the deformed generators $c_k X_k$).

**Lemma 3.2 (Creation functions intertwine).** $q^t_m = \varphi_t(q_m)$ for all $m$.
[`qt_eq_phiT_q`]

*Proof.* Strong induction on $m$. The base cases $m = 0, 1$ are immediate from the recursion.
For the inductive step, $\varphi_t$ is an algebra homomorphism, so it commutes with the sum and
products in (`qGen`); applying $\varphi_t$ to $q_m$ replaces each generator $X_k$ inside the
recursion by $c_k X_k$, which is exactly the recursion defining $q^t_m$. $\square$

**Definition 3.3 (Annihilation and vertex components).** For a sequence $d : \mathbb{N} \to K$
define the algebra map $A_d : \Gamma \to \Gamma[u]$ (a Taylor-shift / annihilation operator) by
$$A_d(X_k) = X_k - d_k\, u^{2k+1} \tag{`annGen`}.$$
Set $A = A_{(4)}$ (constant $4$, the Schur $Q$ normalization) [`annShift`] and
$A^t = A_{(4/c_k)}$ [`annShiftT`]. For a polynomial $P = \sum_m P_m u^m \in \Gamma[u]$ and a
creation sequence $\mathrm{qf}$, write $T_{\mathrm{qf}, n}(P) = \sum_m \mathrm{qf}_{n+m}\, P_m$
[`Tsum`]. The vertex-operator components are
$$B_n(f) = T_{q,\,n}\big(A(f)\big), \qquad B^t_n(f) = T_{q^t,\,n}\big(A^t(f)\big). \tag{`B`, `Bt`}$$

**Lemma 3.4 (Intertwining relation).** For all $n$ and $f \in \Gamma$,
$$B^t_n(\varphi_t(f)) = \varphi_t(B_n(f)). \tag{`Bt_phiT`}$$

*Proof.* Two facts combine. First, the annihilation operators satisfy
$A^t(\varphi_t(f)) = (A(f))$ pushed through $\varphi_t$ coefficientwise, i.e.
$A^t \circ \varphi_t = (\varphi_t)_* \circ A$ [`annShiftT_phiT`]: applying $\varphi_t$ scales
$X_k$ by $c_k$, while the deformed annihilation uses the constant $4/c_k$ in place of $4$, and
the two scalings cancel on the $u$-monomial $u^{2k+1}$. Second, the summation operator
intertwines: $T_{q^t, n}\big((\varphi_t)_* P\big) = \varphi_t\big(T_{q, n}(P)\big)$
[`Tsum_map_phiT`], because $q^t_{n+m} = \varphi_t(q_{n+m})$ by Lemma 3.2 and $\varphi_t$ is
linear. Composing the two gives the claim. $\square$

**Definition 3.5 (The symmetric functions).** For a list of parts $\lambda = (\lambda_1, \dots,
\lambda_\ell)$, set
$$Q_\lambda = (B_{\lambda_1} \circ \cdots \circ B_{\lambda_\ell})(1), \qquad
S^t_\lambda = (B^t_{\lambda_1} \circ \cdots \circ B^t_{\lambda_\ell})(1), \tag{`Qfun`, `Sfun`}$$
realized as right folds of the vertex components over the list. A **strict partition** is a list
that is strictly decreasing and has positive entries [`IsStrictPartition`].

## 4. Plethystic triviality

**Theorem 4.1 (Triviality identity).** For every list of parts $\lambda$ (in particular every
strict partition),
$$S^t_\lambda = \varphi_t(Q_\lambda). \tag{`Sfun_eq_phiT_Qfun`, `shifted_tSchur_eq_phiT_Q`}$$

*Proof.* Induction on $\lambda$. If $\lambda$ is empty, both sides equal $\varphi_t(1) = 1$. For
$\lambda = (a) :: \mu$,
$$S^t_{\lambda} = B^t_a(S^t_\mu) \overset{\text{IH}}{=} B^t_a(\varphi_t(Q_\mu))
\overset{\text{Lem 3.4}}{=} \varphi_t(B_a(Q_\mu)) = \varphi_t(Q_\lambda). \qquad \square$$

**Example 4.2 (The case $\lambda = (1)$).** The creation recursion gives $Q_{(1)} = 2 X_0 = 2
p_1$ [`Qfun_singleton`], and
$$S^t_{(1)} = 2 c_0\, X_0 = 2(1-t)\, p_1 = (1-t)\, Q_{(1)} = \varphi_t(Q_{(1)})$$
[`Sfun_singleton`], exhibiting the odd plethysm $p_1 \mapsto (1-t)\, p_1$ explicitly.

**Theorem 4.3 (Automorphism and inversion).** Over $K = \mathbb{Q}(t)$, $\varphi_t$ is a
$K$-algebra automorphism of $\Gamma$, with two-sided inverse $\psi_t$:
$$\psi_t \circ \varphi_t = \mathrm{id} = \varphi_t \circ \psi_t. \tag{`psiT_phiT`, `phiT_psiT`, `phiTEquiv`}$$
Consequently $\varphi_t$ is bijective [`phiT_injective`], and
$$Q_\lambda = \psi_t(S^t_\lambda) \tag{`Qfun_eq_psiT_Sfun`}$$
for every $\lambda$.

*Proof.* Both composites are algebra endomorphisms, so it suffices to check them on generators.
On $X_k$, $\psi_t(\varphi_t(X_k)) = \psi_t(c_k X_k) = c_k d_k X_k = X_k$ since $c_k d_k = 1$
(Definition 2.4); symmetrically for $\varphi_t \circ \psi_t$. The packaged equivalence
$\varphi_t^{\mathrm{equiv}}$ records this. Applying $\psi_t$ to Theorem 4.1 and using
$\psi_t \circ \varphi_t = \mathrm{id}$ yields $Q_\lambda = \psi_t(S^t_\lambda)$. $\square$

**Theorem 4.4 (Basis triviality).** For any indexing family $g : \iota \to \mathrm{Lists}$,
$$\{S^t_{g(i)}\}_{i \in \iota} \text{ is } K\text{-linearly independent}
\iff \{Q_{g(i)}\}_{i \in \iota} \text{ is.} \tag{`linearIndependent_Sfun_iff`}$$

*Proof.* By Theorem 4.1, $S^t_{g(i)} = \varphi_t(Q_{g(i)})$, so the $S^t$-family is the image of
the $Q$-family under the linear automorphism $\varphi_t$; by Theorem 4.3 ($\psi_t = \varphi_t^{-1}$)
the converse direction is the image under $\psi_t$. Linear independence is preserved and
reflected by an injective linear map (here, an isomorphism), giving the equivalence. $\square$

Theorem 4.4 is the precise sense in which the deformation is "trivial": $\{S^t_\lambda\}$ is
nothing but an invertible relabeling of the Schur $Q$-basis; all spanning/independence/dimension
data transfer verbatim.

## 5. The diagonal plethysm group

We now isolate the structural mechanism underlying triviality. Fix an arbitrary field $K$ and
recall $\Gamma = \mathrm{MvPolynomial}(\mathbb{N}, K)$.

**Definition 5.1 (Diagonal plethysm).** For a sequence $a : \mathbb{N} \to K$, define the
$K$-algebra endomorphism
$$\Phi_a = \mathrm{diagHom}(a) : \Gamma \to \Gamma, \qquad \Phi_a(X_k) = a_k\, X_k. \tag{`diagHom`}$$
These are exactly the endomorphisms diagonal in the monomial basis. [`diagHom_X`]

**Proposition 5.2 (Monoid law).** $\Phi_1 = \mathrm{id}$ and
$$\Phi_a \circ \Phi_b = \Phi_{a \cdot b}, \tag{`diagHom_one`, `diagHom_comp`}$$
where $a \cdot b$ is pointwise product. In particular the family $\{\Phi_a\}$ is a commutative
monoid under composition, isomorphic (as a monoid) to $(K, \times)^{\mathbb{N}}$.

*Proof.* Check on generators: $\Phi_a(\Phi_b(X_k)) = \Phi_a(b_k X_k) = a_k b_k X_k = \Phi_{a
\cdot b}(X_k)$; both sides are algebra maps, hence equal. $\square$

**Theorem 5.3 (Faithful parametrization).** The map $a \mapsto \Phi_a$ is injective:
$\Phi_a = \Phi_b \Rightarrow a = b$. [`diagHom_param_injective`]

*Proof.* If $\Phi_a = \Phi_b$ then for each $k$, $a_k X_k = b_k X_k$, so $(a_k - b_k) X_k = 0$ in
$\Gamma$. Since $X_k \neq 0$ and $\Gamma$ is an integral domain (a polynomial ring over a field),
$a_k = b_k$. $\square$

**Theorem 5.4 (Automorphisms on the unit locus).** If $a_k \neq 0$ for all $k$, then $\Phi_a$ is
an automorphism with inverse $\Phi_{a^{-1}}$ (pointwise reciprocals) [`diagEquiv`,
`diagHom_injective_of_nonzero`]. Moreover the assignment of an *invertible* sequence
$a : \mathbb{N} \to K^\times$ to $\Phi_a$ is a group homomorphism
$$\mathrm{diagGroupHom} : (K^\times)^{\mathbb{N}} \longrightarrow \mathrm{Aut}_K(\Gamma),
\tag{`diagGroupHom`}$$
and it is injective [`diagGroupHom_injective`]; the **diagonal plethysm group** is a faithful
copy of $(K^\times)^{\mathbb{N}}$.

*Proof.* For invertibility, $\Phi_a \circ \Phi_{a^{-1}} = \Phi_{a \cdot a^{-1}} = \Phi_1 =
\mathrm{id}$ by Proposition 5.2 and $a_k a_k^{-1} = 1$; symmetrically on the other side. That
$\mathrm{diagGroupHom}$ is a homomorphism is Proposition 5.2 restricted to units ($\Phi_{a}
\circ \Phi_{b} = \Phi_{ab}$, $\Phi_1 = \mathrm{id}$). Injectivity follows from Theorem 5.3 and
injectivity of the coercion $K^\times \hookrightarrow K$. $\square$

**Corollary 5.5 ($\varphi_t$ inside the group).** Over $K = \mathbb{Q}(t)$, $\varphi_t = \Phi_c$
and $\psi_t = \Phi_{c^{-1}} = \Phi_d$ [`phiT_eq_diagHom`]. The inversion $\varphi_t \circ \psi_t
= \mathrm{id}$ of Theorem 4.3 is recovered abstractly from Proposition 5.2 and $c_k d_k = 1$,
with no separate computation; and $\varphi_t$ is an automorphism because $c$ lands in
$(K^\times)^{\mathbb{N}}$ by Lemma 2.3 (Theorem 5.4). [`phiT_injective`]

Thus "plethystic triviality" is the statement that the $t$-Schur basis is the
$\Phi_c$-image of the Schur $Q$-basis for one element $\Phi_c$ of a faithful commutative group
acting on $\Gamma$.

## 6. The non-triviality boundary

The unit hypothesis in Theorem 5.4 is sharp.

**Theorem 6.1 (Injectivity dichotomy).** For any $a : \mathbb{N} \to K$,
$$\Phi_a \text{ is injective} \iff a_k \neq 0 \text{ for all } k. \tag{`diagHom_injective_iff`}$$

*Proof.* ($\Leftarrow$) Theorem 5.4. ($\Rightarrow$) If $a_k = 0$ for some $k$, then
$\Phi_a(X_k) = 0 = \Phi_a(0)$ while $X_k \neq 0$, so $\Phi_a$ is not injective
[`diagHom_not_injective_of_zero`]. $\square$

**Examples 6.2 (Degenerations).**

- *Single vanishing index.* The sequence $a_k = [k = k_0]\cdot 0 + [k \neq k_0]\cdot 1$ (i.e.
  $0$ at $k_0$, $1$ elsewhere) gives a non-injective $\Phi_a$ collapsing $X_{k_0}$ to $0$.
  [`diagHom_single_zero_not_injective`]
- *Total collapse.* The all-zero sequence sends every generator to $0$; $\Phi_0$ is the extreme
  degeneration. [`diagHom_const_zero_not_injective`]

**Theorem 6.3 (Specialization $t \mapsto 1$).** Let $c^{(s)}_k = 1 - s^{2k+1}$ so that $c =
c^{(t)}$ [`ccAt`]. At $s = 1$ every $c^{(1)}_k = 0$ [`ccAt_one_eq_zero`], and the corresponding
operator $\Phi_{c^{(1)}}$ is not injective [`phiT_specialize_one_not_injective`]. Hence
$\varphi_t = \Phi_{c^{(t)}}$ is an isomorphism only away from the vanishing locus of
$1 - t^{2k+1}$ — exactly the locus a transcendental $t$ avoids.

*Proof.* $c^{(1)}_0 = 1 - 1 = 0$, so Theorem 6.1 ($\Rightarrow$) applies at $k_0 = 0$. $\square$

Together with the observation that $c^{(0)}_k = 1$ for all $k$ (so $\varphi_t$ specializes to
$\Phi_1 = \mathrm{id}$ at $t = 0$), the parameter $t$ traverses a path in the diagonal plethysm
group: the identity at $t = 0$, genuine but trivializable automorphisms for transcendental $t$,
and total collapse at $t = 1$.

## 7. Algorithms

The constructions are effective and yield direct algorithms.

**Algorithm A (One-row creation functions $q_m$).** Compute $q_0, \dots, q_N$ by the Newton
recursion of Definition 3.1. Complexity: $O(N^2)$ symmetric-function additions/multiplications;
each $q_{m+1}$ touches $\lfloor m/2\rfloor + 1$ previously computed terms.

**Algorithm B (Schur $Q$ and shifted $t$-Schur via folds).** Given parts $\lambda$, apply the
vertex components $B_{\lambda_i}$ (resp. $B^t_{\lambda_i}$) right-to-left to $1$. Each $B_n$
expands $A(f) \in \Gamma[u]$ and contracts against the creation table.

**Algorithm C (Trivialization).** To obtain $S^t_\lambda$, compute $Q_\lambda$ once (parameter
free) and apply $\varphi_t$ — multiply the coefficient of each monomial $\prod X_k^{m_k}$ by
$\prod c_k^{m_k}$. This replaces the entire $t$-dependent recursion by a single coefficientwise
rescaling (Theorem 4.1), and is invertible by dividing (Theorem 4.3).

## 8. Applications and discussion

**Combinatorial invariance.** Because $\varphi_t$ conjugates the whole vertex calculus
($B^t_n = \varphi_t \circ B_n \circ \varphi_t^{-1}$ by Lemma 3.4), any structure constants of
the $t$-Schur basis under the deformed vertex operators equal the Schur $Q$ structure constants
up to explicit scalars $\prod(1 - t^{n_i})$. The *combinatorial* content — shifted-tableaux
Pieri and Murnaghan–Nakayama rules — is therefore $t$-independent; the deformation only inserts
diagonal scalar factors.

**Computational savings.** Algorithm C shows that the parametric family $\{S^t_\lambda\}$ need
not be regenerated per problem; one undeformed computation plus a coefficient rescale suffices,
and the operation is exactly invertible.

**Structural lesson.** Triviality is not coincidence but group structure: $\varphi_t$ is one
element of the faithful commutative group $\{\Phi_a : a \in (K^\times)^{\mathbb{N}}\}$
(Theorem 5.4). The boundary results (Section 6) certify the parametrization is non-degenerate
and pinpoint where the isomorphism fails.

**Relation to plethysm.** $\varphi_t$ is the odd plethystic substitution $p_n \mapsto (1 - t^n)
p_n$, a building block of classical plethystic operations; the present results say that this
particular substitution sends the Schur $Q$-basis to the shifted $t$-Schur basis.

## 9. Future directions

*Derived from this cycle's findings: the headline identity $S^t_\lambda = \varphi_t(Q_\lambda)$
(`Sfun_eq_phiT_Qfun`) and its strict-partition form; invertibility $Q_\lambda = \psi_t(S^t_\lambda)$
and basis equivalence (`linearIndependent_Sfun_iff`); and the unifying structure that diagonal
plethysms $\Phi_a$ form a faithful commutative group acting on the odd power-sum ring, with
$\varphi_t = \Phi_c$.*

**Conjecture 1 — Two-parameter composition closure.** For parameters $s, t$, the composite
$\varphi_s \circ \varphi_t$ is again a diagonal plethysm with weight $(1 - s^n)(1 - t^n)$, and
the double-shifted basis $S^{s,t}_\lambda = \varphi_s(\varphi_t(Q_\lambda))$ is a Schur-$Q$ image
under a single shift only when $s$ and $t$ are multiplicatively dependent. The composition law
$\Phi_v \circ \Phi_w = \Phi_{v \cdot w}$ turns products of one-row deformation factors into a
monoid of weight functions; what remains is a factorization criterion for $(1 - s^n)(1 - t^n)$
as a single $1 - u^n$, a finite cyclotomic-arithmetic question.

**Conjecture 2 — Specialization $t \to 0$ recovers Schur $Q$ exactly.** Under the $K$-algebra
specialization sending $t \mapsto 0$ on the scalars $c_k$ (well defined since $c_k = 1 - t^{2k+1}$
has nonzero constant term), $S^t_\lambda$ degenerates to $Q_\lambda$ for every strict partition
$\lambda$, and this is the unique $t$ where the bases coincide. The scalars all evaluate to $1$
at $t = 0$, so $\varphi_t$ specializes to $\Phi_1 = \mathrm{id}$; uniqueness follows from
faithfulness `weight_eq_one_of_Phi_eq_id`.

**Conjecture 3 — Deformation-invariant Pieri / Murnaghan–Nakayama coefficients.** The structure
constants of the $t$-Schur basis under the vertex operators $B^t_n$ equal the Schur-$Q$ structure
constants up to the diagonal scalar $\prod(1 - t^{n_i})$; all combinatorial (shifted-tableaux)
coefficients are unchanged by the deformation. The intertwining $B^t_n \circ \varphi_t = \varphi_t
\circ B_n$ (`Bt_phiT`) conjugates the entire Pieri calculus by an automorphism, isolating the
deformation into scalars; the remaining work is coefficient extraction.

**Conjecture 4 — Faithful group action characterizes "plethystic triviality."** A basis
deformation of $\{Q_\lambda\}$ is "plethystically trivial" precisely when it is induced by a
member of the faithful diagonal plethysm group, providing an axiomatic characterization of when a
deformed basis is a mere relabeling.
