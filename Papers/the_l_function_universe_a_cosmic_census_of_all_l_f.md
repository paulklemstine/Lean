# Computational Evidence Justification

A finite computational search is not probative for the principal result. The counterexample is uniform in the cutoff: for every natural number `N`, two bounded normalized coefficient sequences can be placed at positions beyond `N`, where they agree on every observed coefficient but define distinct analytic Dirichlet series. This symbolic construction covers all finite cutoffs at once and is stronger than a table of sampled cases.

Likewise, no numerical list of the first hundred Selberg-class elements is supplied. Such a list would presuppose unresolved classification, finite-fibre, equality-testing, and tie-breaking results. Presenting experimental entries as a canonical census would therefore overstate the available mathematics.
