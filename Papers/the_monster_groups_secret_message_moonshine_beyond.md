# Computational Evidence Justification

A numerical coefficient experiment was not undertaken because the project contains neither a certified Monster character table nor the 194 McKay–Thompson coefficient tables required to check the requested first 100 coefficients. Reconstructing those datasets from memory would not constitute reliable evidence.

The investigation instead targets claims that can be settled from their structural form without external tables. Finite products of normalized Laurent expansions have a rigorously determined aggregate principal exponent; finite products of invariant functions remain invariant; and coefficient data recover multiplicities exactly under an injective character-evaluation map. These statements expose two decisive boundary cases before large-scale computation: a class-indexed product and an element-indexed product have different pole orders, and a positive modular weight cannot arise from multiplying weight-zero invariant functions alone at a nonzero point.

The concrete small cases are included directly with the mathematical development: a three-index normalized family and a conjugacy-class family indexed by the three classes of the symmetric group on three letters. For the full Monster experiment, the next prerequisite is a citable, machine-readable source for all series coefficients and character values, together with conventions identifying classes and coefficient indices.
