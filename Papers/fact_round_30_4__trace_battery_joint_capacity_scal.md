# Computational evidence — TRACE-BATTERY (round-30 #4, paper 108)

Small, targeted checks made before and while formalising.  Everything reported
here as *verified* is additionally backed by a sorry-free Lean theorem in
`Catalog/Combinatorics/TraceBattery*.lean`; the float values below are
exploratory only and are labelled as such.

## 1. The reported table against the CRT ceilings

| sub-battery | moduli | M = ∏ mᵢ | measured I(joint) | ceiling log₂ M (float) | integer certificate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S₃a + S₃b | 31, 23 | 713 | 7.9455 | 9.477758 | `2^8 ≤ 713` ⟹ ceiling ≥ 8 |
| + A₄@9 | 31, 23, 9 | 6 417 | 10.4462 | 12.647683 | `2^12 ≤ 6417` ⟹ ceiling ≥ 12 |
| + D₄@8 | 31, 23, 9, 8 | 51 336 | 12.1080 | 15.647683 | `2^15 ≤ 51336` ⟹ ceiling ≥ 15 |

The integer certificates are the ones actually used in Lean
(`TraceBattery.logb_713_gt`, `logb_6417_gt`, `logb_51336_gt`): each measured
value is *strictly* below the corresponding CRT ceiling, so the reported table
is consistent with `TraceBattery.capacity_le_logb_prod`.  No counterexample to
the ceiling was found; a violation would have falsified the theorem.

Monotonicity of the measured chain `7.9455 < 10.4462 < 12.1080` matches
`TraceBattery.capacity_mono` (and, given a separating dial, the strict form
`capacity_strict_mono_of_separates`).

## 2. Per-dial trace information vs per-dial ceilings

Float values, `log₂ m`:

* `C₅@11`: ceiling `log₂ 11 = 3.459432`, measured `3.46` — the dial is at its
  ceiling to within rounding.  Formalised as `TraceBattery.dial_at_11_below_346`
  (`Hb < 3.46`, certificate `11^50 < 2^173`, verified `true` by evaluation and
  by `norm_num` in the proof).  Note that the *literal* reported value `3.46`
  is therefore not attainable: it is a rounded `≤ 3.4595`.
* `S₃a@31`: ceiling `log₂ 31 = 4.954196`, measured `0.04` — a genuine ≈124×
  shortfall, not a ceiling effect (`TraceBattery.dial_at_31_ceiling_high`).

So the reported 80× spread of per-dial informations is compatible with the
theory: one dial saturates, the other is nearly uninformative.

## 3. The which-factor wall 0.4677

Binary statistics have capacity `binEntropy(p)` where `p` is the minority
fraction (`TraceBattery.H_two_values`).  Inverting numerically (bisection,
exploratory float computation, not a Lean artifact):

```
h(0.0995917) = 0.4677  bits
h(0.1)       = 0.468996 bits
```

so a wall at `0.4677` bits corresponds to a which-factor split of about
`9.96% / 90.04%`.  Since `binEntropy` is strictly monotone on `[0, 1/2]`
(`TraceBattery.wall_determines_imbalance`), that fraction is *uniquely*
determined by the reported value: the wall is an imbalance meter, and its
being below `1` bit (`binary_capacity_le_one_bit`) carries no further
information.

## 4. Sanity/counterexample hunt on the structural claims

* Monotone scaling: no counterexample is possible — proved
  (`capacity_mono`).  Attempting the reverse inequality fails already on the
  toy battery of §5.
* Strictness is *not* automatic: adding a dial that is a function of the
  existing readings leaves the capacity unchanged
  (`H_comp_eq_of_injective` gives the equality case), which is why the strict
  statement is guarded by a separation hypothesis.
* Sample ceiling: with `N` individuals the capacity cannot exceed `log₂ N`
  (`capacity_le_logb_pop`).  For the reported `12.1080` bits this forces the
  fresh population to have had at least `2^12.108 ≈ 4413` members — a
  falsifiable prediction about the experiment's table size.

## 5. Toy battery (Lean-verified, `Fin 4`)

Population `Fin 4`; dial 0 = parity, dial 1 = halving.  Dial 0 alone confuses
`0` and `2`; dial 1 separates them.  `TraceBattery.strict_scaling_witness`
proves `capacity {0} < capacity {0,1}` for this battery, showing the strict
scaling criterion is non-vacuous.  (By hand: capacities are exactly 1 and 2
bits.)

## 6. OEIS

No integer sequence is generated by this experiment: the objects are moduli
products `713, 6417, 51336` (a single CRT chain), so an OEIS lookup is not
informative here.
