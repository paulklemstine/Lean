# Computational Evidence: Two-Branch Quantum-Surreal Measurement

## Small-case calculations

For two distinct labels `s` and `t` and a common nonzero amplitude `a`, direct finite-support expansion gives

| amplitude `a` | squared norm | weight at `s` | weight at `t` | observed pair |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `1` | `2` | `1/2` | `1/2` | `(1/2, 1/2)` |
| `2` | `8` | `4/8 = 1/2` | `4/8 = 1/2` | `(1/2, 1/2)` |
| `1/√2` | `1` | `1/2` | `1/2` | `(1/2, 1/2)` |
| positive infinitesimal `ε` | `2ε²` | `ε²/(2ε²) = 1/2` | `ε²/(2ε²) = 1/2` | `(1/2, 1/2)` |

Thus neither the numerical value nor the infinitesimal status of a label enters the calculation. Even a common infinitesimal amplitude cancels during normalization, provided it is nonzero.

For the corrected asymmetric state `|0⟩ + ε|1⟩`, the exact weights are

| branch | exact hyperreal weight | standard part |
|---|---:|---:|
| `0` | `1/(1+ε²)` | `1` |
| `1` | `ε²/(1+ε²)` | `0` |

This identifies amplitude asymmetry, rather than an infinitesimal ket label, as the source of observational suppression.

## Counterexample hunt

The proposed equal-amplitude test predicts probability zero for the infinitesimal-labelled branch. Every nonzero common amplitude is a counterexample: the general cancellation law gives probability `1/2` for both branches. The result is independent of label order and applies to arbitrary distinct surreal labels.

The hypotheses were also tested at their boundaries. If `s = t`, the two coordinates merge and the norm is that of the single amplitude `2a`, so the two-branch formula is inapplicable. If `a = 0`, the normalized Born ratio has zero denominator. These are genuine excluded cases rather than counterexamples.

The evidence rejects label-dependent suppression and supports the corrected rule: standard part erases branches only when their normalized Born weights are infinitesimal. The accompanying theorem establishes the cancellation law for every pair of distinct surreal labels and every nonzero hyperreal common amplitude.
