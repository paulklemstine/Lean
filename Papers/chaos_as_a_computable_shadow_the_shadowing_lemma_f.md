Computational evidence was not used because the principal universal claim conflates a classical hyperbolic shadowing theorem with unrestricted programs computing arbitrary chaotic maps, and the proposed million-step logistic-map test would depend on an unspecified floating-point format, initial value, binary-search objective, and representation of the “true” orbit. A numerical run under arbitrary choices could not validate that universal statement. The research instead isolates a parameterized theorem: any execution with a certified local defect and invariant-region certificate satisfies an explicit finite-horizon error bound. This result identifies the exact quantities that a later reproducible experiment must report.
