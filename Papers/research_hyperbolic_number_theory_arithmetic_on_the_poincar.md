## Abstract

We develop an intrinsic arithmetic model for discrete hyperbolic dynamics generated by determinant-one integral Möbius transformations. If $A\in \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$ has trace $t$ and $u_n=\operatorname{tr}(A^n)$, then the Cayley–Hamilton identity gives the recurrence $u_0=2$, $u_1=t$, and $u_{n+2}=tu_{n+1}-u_n$. We prove that the quadratic form $Q_t(x,y)=x^2-txy+y^2$ is invariant under the recurrence step and hence that every adjacent trace pair lies on the integral Pell conic

$$
x^2-txy+y^2=4-t^2=-(t^2-4).
$$

The parameter is unchanged by conjugation, so the construction is independent of the matrix representative used to describe a modular transformation. We present iterative and logarithmic-time algorithms for trace generation, invariant checking, modular reduction, and visualization. For trace $t=3$, the sequence begins $2,3,7,18,47,123$, with every adjacent pair on $x^2-3xy+y^2=-5$. We explain why trace orbits provide sound arithmetic coordinates while arbitrary tessellation vertices do not possess canonical addition, multiplication, or primality. The resulting bridge among hyperbolic dynamics, binary quadratic forms, and real quadratic fields suggests precise programs involving primitive conjugacy classes, modular periods, Pell-orbit primitivity, and geodesic zeta functions.

## 1. Introduction

The modular group acts on the upper half-plane by fractional linear transformations and, after a change of model, on the Poincaré disk. It is therefore a basic laboratory for arithmetic hyperbolic geometry. A recurring ambition is to transfer notions such as integers, primes, factorization, and zeta functions to this curved setting. Such a transfer requires care. A vertex in a tessellation is a geometric point, but it has no canonical product with another vertex. Without a well-defined multiplicative structure, irreducibility and unique factorization are not yet meaningful.

This paper takes a different route. Rather than assigning arithmetic to points by decree, we extract it from a canonical invariant of a transformation: its trace. Iterating a determinant-one matrix produces powers $A^n$, and their traces form an integer sequence controlled by the original trace. The sequence has a quadratic first integral. Thus a geometric orbit under repeated hyperbolic motion determines an orbit of integral points on a Pell-type conic.

The construction has four advantages. First, it is explicit: only a second-order recurrence is needed. Second, it is intrinsic under conjugation because trace is conjugacy-invariant. Third, it is Diophantine: every consecutive pair satisfies one integral quadratic equation. Fourth, it remains meaningful modulo every integer, where the recurrence becomes a reversible finite-state system.

Our principal result states that if $t\in\mathbb Z$ and

$$
u_0=2,\qquad u_1=t,\qquad u_{n+2}=tu_{n+1}-u_n,
$$

then

$$
u_n^2-tu_nu_{n+1}+u_{n+1}^2=4-t^2
$$

for every $n\ge 0$. When $u_n$ is the trace of $A^n$, this is the scalar shadow of the Cayley–Hamilton theorem and the determinant-one condition. The right side is the negative of the trace discriminant $D=t^2-4$, which also determines the eigenvalue field of $A$.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 defines the modular action, trace recurrence, discriminant, and trace conic. Section 3 establishes the one-step conservation law and global invariant. Section 4 specializes the result to matrix powers and proves conjugacy invariance. Section 5 discusses hyperbolic and quadratic-field interpretations. Section 6 develops algorithms. Section 7 gives examples and modular experiments. Section 8 states the precise scope of the results. Sections 9–11 discuss applications and future research.

## 2. Definitions and setup

### 2.1 Integral Möbius transformations

Let

$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}
$$

be an integral matrix with determinant $ad-bc=1$. It acts on the upper half-plane by

$$
z\longmapsto A\cdot z=\frac{az+b}{cz+d}.
$$

The matrices $A$ and $-A$ induce the same Möbius transformation, so the modular group is naturally the projective group $\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb Z)$. For trace calculations it is convenient to retain a chosen lift to $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$. Its trace is

$$
t=\operatorname{tr}(A)=a+d.
$$

A matrix with $|t|>2$ is hyperbolic. It has two distinct real fixed points on the ideal boundary and translates along a geodesic axis. The borderline cases $|t|=2$ are parabolic, while $|t|<2$ corresponds to elliptic behavior where available in the relevant group.

### 2.2 The trace recurrence

For an integer $t$, define the trace sequence $(u_n)_{n\ge0}$ by

$$
u_0=2,\qquad u_1=t,\qquad u_{n+2}=tu_{n+1}-u_n.
$$

The recurrence is defined for every integer $t$, independently of a matrix. When $t$ is the trace of a determinant-one matrix, it equals the sequence of traces of matrix powers, as proved below.

The adjacent-pair state is

$$
v_n=(u_n,u_{n+1})\in\mathbb Z^2.
$$

Its update map is

$$
T_t(x,y)=(y,ty-x).
$$

Equivalently,

$$
\begin{pmatrix}u_{n+1}\\u_{n+2}\end{pmatrix}
=M_t\begin{pmatrix}u_n\\u_{n+1}\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
M_t=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&t\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Since $\det M_t=1$, this update is invertible over $\mathbb Z$ and modulo every modulus.

### 2.3 Trace discriminant and Pell form

Define the trace discriminant by

$$
D=t^2-4.
$$

Define the binary quadratic form

$$
Q_t(x,y)=x^2-txy+y^2.
$$

For fixed $t$, an equation $Q_t(x,y)=N$ is a Pell-type conic. In the present setting the distinguished level is $N=4-t^2=-D$.

The terminology is justified by diagonalization. Multiplying by $4$ gives

$$
4Q_t(x,y)=(2x-ty)^2-(t^2-4)y^2.
$$

Thus $Q_t(x,y)=4-t^2$ is equivalent to

$$
(2x-ty)^2-Dy^2=-4D.
$$

When $D>0$ is nonsquare, this is an indefinite Pell-type equation in the real quadratic field $\mathbb Q(\sqrt D)$.

## 3. The quadratic conservation law

We begin with the local algebraic identity from which all global statements follow.

### Lemma 3.1 (One-step preservation)

For all integers $t,x,y$,

$$
Q_t\bigl(T_t(x,y)\bigr)=Q_t(x,y).
$$

In expanded form,

$$
Q_t(y,ty-x)=Q_t(x,y).
$$

#### Proof sketch

Substitute the update into the quadratic form:

$$
Q_t(y,ty-x)=y^2-ty(ty-x)+(ty-x)^2.
$$

Expanding the final square and collecting terms yields

$$
y^2-t^2y^2+txy+t^2y^2-2txy+x^2=x^2-txy+y^2.
$$

The cancellation is exact over the integers and therefore remains valid in every commutative ring. $\square$

### Theorem 3.2 (Trace-sequence Pell invariant)

Let $t\in\mathbb Z$, and define $(u_n)$ by $u_0=2$, $u_1=t$, and $u_{n+2}=tu_{n+1}-u_n$. Then for every $n\ge0$,

$$
Q_t(u_n,u_{n+1})=4-t^2.
$$

Equivalently,

$$
u_n^2-tu_nu_{n+1}+u_{n+1}^2=4-t^2.
$$

#### Proof sketch

The initial value is

$$
Q_t(u_0,u_1)=Q_t(2,t)=4-2t^2+t^2=4-t^2.
$$

The recurrence identifies the next state with

$$
(u_{n+1},u_{n+2})=T_t(u_n,u_{n+1}).
$$

Lemma 3.1 shows that applying $T_t$ preserves $Q_t$. Induction on $n$ therefore proves that every state has the initial value $4-t^2$. $\square$

### Corollary 3.3 (Discriminant bridge)

Under the hypotheses of Theorem 3.2, every adjacent pair satisfies

$$
Q_t(u_n,u_{n+1})=-D,
$$

where $D=t^2-4$ is the trace discriminant.

#### Proof sketch

The assertion follows from $4-t^2=-(t^2-4)=-D$. $\square$

This elementary reformulation is conceptually important. The same discriminant controls both the conic containing the integer orbit and the quadratic equation for the transformation’s eigenvalues.

## 4. Matrix powers and intrinsicity

### Theorem 4.1 (Power traces satisfy the recurrence)

Let $A\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$ and $t=\operatorname{tr}(A)$. Then

$$
\operatorname{tr}(A^0)=2,\qquad \operatorname{tr}(A)=t,
$$

and for every $n\ge0$,

$$
\operatorname{tr}(A^{n+2})=t\operatorname{tr}(A^{n+1})-\operatorname{tr}(A^n).
$$

Consequently, $\operatorname{tr}(A^n)=u_n$ for the trace sequence with parameter $t$.

#### Proof sketch

The characteristic polynomial of a determinant-one $2\times2$ matrix is $X^2-tX+1$. Cayley–Hamilton gives

$$
A^2-tA+I=0.
$$

Multiply by $A^n$ to obtain

$$
A^{n+2}-tA^{n+1}+A^n=0.
$$

Taking traces yields the recurrence. The initial values follow from $A^0=I$ and $\operatorname{tr}(I)=2$. Uniqueness of a sequence generated by a second-order recurrence completes the identification. $\square$

### Theorem 4.2 (Pell conic for consecutive power traces)

Let $A\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$ have trace $t$. For every $n\ge0$,

$$
\operatorname{tr}(A^n)^2
-t\operatorname{tr}(A^n)\operatorname{tr}(A^{n+1})
+\operatorname{tr}(A^{n+1})^2
=4-t^2.
$$

#### Proof sketch

By Theorem 4.1, the traces of powers are exactly the recurrence terms $u_n$. Apply Theorem 3.2. $\square$

### Theorem 4.3 (Conjugacy-invariant conic parameter)

Let $A,B\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$. The conjugate $ABA^{-1}$ has the same trace as $B$. Therefore

$$
4-\operatorname{tr}(ABA^{-1})^2=4-\operatorname{tr}(B)^2.
$$

In particular, conjugate transformations determine the same discriminant and the same Pell-conic level.

#### Proof sketch

Cyclicity of trace gives

$$
\operatorname{tr}(ABA^{-1})=\operatorname{tr}(BA^{-1}A)=\operatorname{tr}(B).
$$

Squaring and subtracting from $4$ proves the stated identity. $\square$

This theorem is essential for geometric interpretation. A change of coordinates acts by conjugation. Although entries and visible axes may change, the trace parameter and its associated arithmetic conic do not.

## 5. Hyperbolic and quadratic-field interpretations

Suppose $|t|>2$. The eigenvalues of $A$ solve

$$
\lambda^2-t\lambda+1=0,
$$

so

$$
\lambda_{\pm}=\frac{t\pm\sqrt{t^2-4}}{2}.
$$

They satisfy $\lambda_+\lambda_-=1$. The trace sequence has the closed form

$$
u_n=\lambda_+^n+\lambda_-^n.
$$

This follows either by diagonalizing $A$ over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt D)$ or by checking that the expression has the correct initial values and recurrence. If $t>2$, then $\lambda_+>1$ and $0<\lambda_-<1$, so $u_n$ grows asymptotically as $\lambda_+^n$.

For a hyperbolic isometry, the translation length $\ell$ is related to trace by

$$
2\cosh(\ell/2)=|t|.
$$

Thus exponential arithmetic growth records linear travel in hyperbolic length. Powers multiply translation length by $n$, while their traces grow approximately like $e^{n\ell/2}$.

The Pell conic reflects the norm form in the same quadratic field. If $\lambda=\lambda_+$, then $\lambda^{-1}=t-\lambda$. Multiplication by the unit $\lambda$ acts linearly on suitable integral coordinates, while the field norm remains fixed. The matrix $M_t$ and form $Q_t$ are a coordinate realization of this norm-preserving action. Accordingly, three descriptions coincide:

1. repeated hyperbolic translation by a modular transformation;
2. iteration of the recurrence $u_{n+2}=tu_{n+1}-u_n$;
3. motion among integral points on $Q_t(x,y)=4-t^2$.

## 6. Algorithms

### 6.1 Linear-time orbit generation

Given $t$ and a nonnegative bound $N$, initialize $(x,y)=(2,t)$ and repeatedly replace $(x,y)$ by $(y,ty-x)$. At step $n$, the pair equals $(u_n,u_{n+1})$. Each step uses one multiplication and one subtraction of integers.

In an arithmetic-operation model, generating the first $N+1$ pairs costs $O(N)$ operations and $O(1)$ auxiliary state beyond the output. For hyperbolic $t$, integer bit lengths grow linearly with $N$, so bit complexity must also account for increasingly large operands.

The invariant can be checked at every step by testing

$$
x^2-txy+y^2=4-t^2.
$$

This adds constant many arithmetic operations per state and serves as a robust diagnostic.

### 6.2 Logarithmic-time random access

To compute $(u_n,u_{n+1})$ without producing all earlier terms, evaluate

$$
M_t^n\begin{pmatrix}2\\t\end{pmatrix}
$$

by binary exponentiation. This takes $O(\log n)$ matrix multiplications and $O(1)$ matrices of storage. Exact integer entries may be large; modulo $m$, all arithmetic remains bounded.

### 6.3 Period detection modulo $m$

Reduce states modulo $m>1$. Since $\det M_t=1$, the update is a bijection on $(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^2$. A finite bijective orbit is periodic from its first state: there is no transient tail. Starting from $(2,t)\bmod m$, iterate until the initial pair returns. The period is at most $m^2$, though the invariant confines the orbit to the smaller set

$$
\{(x,y):Q_t(x,y)\equiv4-t^2\pmod m\}.
$$

A dictionary of visited states gives explicit cycle information in $O(P)$ time and $O(P)$ memory for period $P$. If only the period is required, Floyd’s cycle algorithm uses $O(1)$ memory, though the known absence of a transient permits an even simpler return-to-start loop.

## 7. Numerical examples

### 7.1 Trace three

Take $t=3$. The recurrence gives

$$
u_0=2,\quad u_1=3,
$$

followed by

$$
u_2=3\cdot3-2=7,
$$

$$
u_3=3\cdot7-3=18,
$$

$$
u_4=3\cdot18-7=47,
$$

$$
u_5=3\cdot47-18=123.
$$

Hence the initial segment is

$$
2,3,7,18,47,123.
$$

The discriminant is $D=3^2-4=5$, and the invariant conic is

$$
x^2-3xy+y^2=-5.
$$

For the pair $(47,123)$,

$$
47^2-3\cdot47\cdot123+123^2
=2209-17343+15129=-5.
$$

### 7.2 A generating matrix

The matrix

$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}
$$

has determinant $1$ and trace $3$. Its powers therefore have the trace sequence above. Its eigenvalues are $(3\pm\sqrt5)/2$, connecting this orbit to the real quadratic field $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$.

### 7.3 Reduction modulo five

For $t=3$ modulo $5$, the adjacent states begin

$$
(2,3),(3,2),(2,3),\ldots
$$

because $u_2=7\equiv2\pmod5$. Thus the state period is $2$. At each state,

$$
x^2-3xy+y^2\equiv-5\equiv0\pmod5.
$$

This small example illustrates how the discriminant affects modular behavior: here $D=5$ is ramified modulo $5$, and the conic level becomes zero.

## 8. Scope and necessary distinctions

The established arithmetic concerns traces of iterated transformations. It does not endow every vertex of a hyperbolic tessellation with canonical addition or multiplication. Consequently, a declaration that tessellation vertices are “primes” does not by itself define primality, and no unique-factorization statement follows from the trace invariant alone.

A geometrically natural replacement is a primitive hyperbolic conjugacy class, equivalently a primitive closed geodesic on the modular orbifold. “Primitive” then means not obtained as a proper positive power of another class. This notion is intrinsic and is directly connected to iteration, the structure studied here.

Similarly, a hyperbolic zeta function intended to mirror prime factorization should be built from primitive closed geodesics with their lengths or norms and, where appropriate, scattering data. An unweighted Dirichlet sum over unspecified orbit points has no canonical multiplicity or norm and should not be identified automatically with a geodesic zeta function.

Finally, counting laws depend critically on the size variable. The area of a hyperbolic disk of radius $R$ grows exponentially in $R$, not quadratically. A proposed asymptotic of the form $R^2/(2\log R)$ is therefore not naturally compatible with $R$ as genuine hyperbolic radius. Prime-geodesic asymptotics are generally expressed using an exponential norm $X$ associated with length, leading to a scale analogous to $X/\log X$.

These distinctions do not weaken the trace-conic result. They identify exactly what has been established and supply sound definitions for extending it.

## 9. Applications

The recurrence provides efficient exact coordinates for powers of integral hyperbolic transformations. It can replace repeated matrix multiplication when only traces are required. The quadratic invariant catches implementation errors and can restrict searches for possible orbit states.

Modulo $m$, the recurrence is a reversible finite-state system. Its period is related to the order of $M_t$ in $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)$ and to the splitting behavior of $D=t^2-4$. This connects orbit computation to modular matrix groups, Lucas-type sequences, and quadratic residue theory.

The conic also offers a Diophantine search space for primitiveness. Rather than scanning all matrices with large entries, one may study integral points $(x,y)$ on

$$
x^2-txy+y^2=4-t^2
$$

and ask which correspond to consecutive traces of primitive iterates. Not every integral point need belong to the distinguished orbit, so orbit membership remains a substantive arithmetic condition.

For visualization, plotting recurrence pairs on the conic reveals a useful contrast. Euclidean coordinates grow exponentially, but all points remain on a fixed algebraic curve. Mapping points through logarithmic scales makes the stable asymptotic directions visible; these directions correspond to eigenvectors of $M_t$ and slopes determined by the roots of $r^2-tr+1=0$.

## 10. Discussion

The central conservation law is elementary, but its role is structural. It gives an exact interface among group dynamics, recurrences, and quadratic arithmetic. The determinant-one hypothesis appears twice: it fixes the constant term of the characteristic polynomial and makes the pair update unimodular. If the determinant were another integer $\delta$, the recurrence and invariant would change, and modular evolution might cease to be invertible when $\delta$ is not a unit.

Trace is particularly well suited to geometric arithmetic because it survives conjugation. Individual matrix entries depend on coordinates, as do individual points in a chosen fundamental domain. The trace discriminant does not. This is why a conic indexed by $t$ is a more intrinsic arithmetic object than a labeling of tessellation vertices.

The results also suggest a hierarchy of structures. The trace $t$ fixes a quadratic order through $D=t^2-4$. A conjugacy class supplies a geometric orbit. Its powers select a cyclic subset of integral points on a norm conic. Primitivity asks whether a class is a proper power. Spectral and prime-geodesic questions then aggregate primitive classes by length. Each layer requires additional definitions; none should be collapsed into the previous one.

## 11. Future work

A first direction is to classify primitive hyperbolic conjugacy classes using the trace conics. For fixed $t\ge3$, one may ask which integral points on $Q_t(x,y)=4-t^2$ occur in the distinguished trace orbit and which correspond to proper iterates. Counting primitive points up to height would connect recurrence divisibility with Pell equations.

A second direction is an exact theory of periods modulo composite moduli. The Chinese remainder theorem reduces much of the problem to prime powers, while the splitting, inertness, or ramification of $D$ should govern local orbit structure. Determining exact periods would sharpen the elementary finite-state argument.

A third direction concerns class groups. For nonsquare positive $D$, primitive matrix conjugacy classes of fixed trace are expected to interact with narrow ideal classes of the quadratic order of discriminant $D$. The trace conic supplies explicit coordinates for investigating these fibers.

A fourth direction replaces vertex-based primality with primitive closed geodesics and studies their counting function using exponential norm. This is the appropriate setting for a prime-geodesic theorem and an effective error term related to spectral gaps.

A fifth direction is spectral. A completed geodesic zeta function should use primitive closed geodesics and the scattering contribution for finite-area noncompact surfaces. Its zeros must be interpreted through Laplace eigenvalues, topology, and scattering poles, with exceptional real zeros classified separately.

## 12. Reproducibility protocol for numerical exploration

Numerical investigation of the theory requires only exact integer arithmetic. Choose an integer $t$, initialize $(x,y)=(2,t)$, and store the target $c=4-t^2$. Before and after each update $(x,y)\leftarrow(y,ty-x)$, evaluate $Q_t(x,y)$ and compare it with $c$. Any discrepancy indicates an arithmetic or implementation error. Because the identities are integral, floating-point approximation is unnecessary and should be avoided.

For modular exploration, reduce every operation modulo $m$. The target check becomes $Q_t(x,y)\equiv c\pmod m$. To measure a period, record the first positive step at which the initial state $(2,t)mod m$ returns. The determinant-one update guarantees return within at most $m^2$ steps. For large indices, binary exponentiation of $M_t$ should be compared against ordinary iteration at small indices before deployment. These independent computation paths provide a useful consistency check.

A conic plot should distinguish the complete real curve from the distinguished integer orbit. Plotting only recurrence points can misleadingly suggest that all integral points belong to one orbit. Conversely, plotting only the continuous curve hides the discrete and exponentially spaced nature of the trace states. A faithful visualization overlays both and labels iteration indices. For hyperbolic parameters, logarithmic coordinate scaling may be used, but signs and branches must be retained.

## 13. Limitations

The invariant is a necessary description of trace-orbit states, not a classification of all integral points on each conic. It does not decide when two matrices with equal trace are integrally conjugate, nor does it classify primitive conjugacy classes. Those tasks involve additional arithmetic, including quadratic orders and ideal classes.

The finite-state argument proves periodicity modulo $m$ but does not supply an exact period formula. Exact periods may vary with prime-power factorization and discriminant splitting. Finally, no spectral zero theorem follows solely from the recurrence. Spectral claims require a separately defined geodesic zeta function and analytic information about the Laplacian and scattering operator. Keeping these boundaries explicit prevents a correct elementary invariant from being asked to bear conclusions that require substantially richer theory.

## 14. Conclusion

For every integer parameter $t$, the recurrence $u_{n+2}=tu_{n+1}-u_n$ preserves the quadratic form $Q_t(x,y)=x^2-txy+y^2$ along adjacent states starting at $(2,t)$. The conserved value is $4-t^2$, the negative trace discriminant. When $t$ is the trace of an integral determinant-one Möbius transformation, the sequence is precisely the trace sequence of its powers. Conjugation preserves the parameter, making the resulting Pell conic an intrinsic arithmetic signature of the transformation.

This framework does not manufacture arithmetic by naming geometric points. It derives arithmetic from symmetry, iteration, and invariance. Hyperbolic motion becomes an integer recurrence; the recurrence becomes a Pell orbit; and the discriminant links both to a real quadratic field. That bridge provides a precise starting point for future theories of primitive geodesics, modular periods, class-group organization, and spectral zeta functions.
