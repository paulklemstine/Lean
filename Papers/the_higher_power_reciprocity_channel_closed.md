# Computational evidence — KPOWER (#374), higher-power residue channel

All numbers below were produced by exhaustive integer computation (modular
exponentiation and sieving) *before* formalisation, and every fact that a
theorem depends on was afterwards re-verified inside Lean's kernel (see
`Catalog/Combinatorics/PowerResidue*.lean`).  Items marked **[Lean]** are backed
by a sorry-free Lean proof; items marked *(exploratory)* are not, and are
reported only as motivation.

## 1. Escape witnesses: cubic residuacity of 2 is not a congruence condition

Search over all primes `p < 6·10⁶` with `p ≡ 1 (mod 3)`, bucketed by
`p mod 720720` (`720720 = lcm(1,…,16)`).  Smallest bucket containing both a
cubic residue and a cubic non-residue of `2`:

| p | p mod 720720 | (p−1)/3 | 2^((p−1)/3) mod p | 2 a cube mod p? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 43 | 43 | 14 | 1 | yes, `20³ = 8000 = 186·43 + 2` |
| 720763 | 43 | 240254 | 632375 | no |

**[Lean]** `PowerResidueEscape.cubic_residue_two_43`,
`PowerResidueEscape.not_cubic_residue_two_720763`, and hence
`PowerResidueEscape.cubic_two_not_dial`: no dial of conductor dividing `720720`
(in particular no modulus `M ≤ 16`) decides the cubic bit.

## 2. Escape witnesses: quartic residuacity of 2

Same search with `p ≡ 1 (mod 4)`:

| p | p mod 720720 | (p−1)/4 | 2^((p−1)/4) mod p | 2 a fourth power mod p? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 137 | 137 | 34 | 136 | no |
| 720857 | 137 | 180214 | 1 | yes, `96769⁴ ≡ 2` |

**[Lean]** `PowerResidueEscape.quartic_two_not_dial`.

## 3. Leakage saturation: cubic vs quadratic fingerprints

Bases `2, 3, 5, 7, 11`; the 68 primes `p ∈ [1000, 2000]` with `p ≡ 1 (mod 3)`
(there are 135 primes in the interval).

| fingerprint | distinct values on the 68 primes |
|---|---|
| full quadratic symbols `a^((p−1)/2) mod p` | 68 |
| full cubic symbols `a^((p−1)/3) mod p` | 68 |
| quadratic residuacity **bits** | 31 |
| cubic residuacity **bits** | 23 |

The `68/68` of the original experiment is an artefact: the *value* of a symbol
lives in `ZMod p`, so it encodes `p` itself — this is the circularity.  The
modulus-independent read-out (the residuacity bit) saturates: `2⁵ = 32` is the
hard ceiling, and both channels sit under it, the cubic one *lower* because its
bit is biased `1/3` rather than `1/2`.

**[Lean]** ceiling: `PowerResidue.card_le_two_pow_of_injOn` (`≤ 2^K` for every
exponent `k`); bias: `PowerResidueCount.card_powerResidue_units`
(`(p−1)/k` of the `p−1` units are `k`-th powers) and
`PowerResidueCount.cubic_sparser_than_quadratic`.

## 4. Sharpness of the `2^K` ceiling (K = 2, bases 2 and 3, cubic)

| p | 2 a cube mod p | 3 a cube mod p |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | no | no |
| 31 | yes (`4³ = 64 = 2·31 + 2`) | no |
| 61 | no | yes (`4³ = 64 = 61 + 3`) |
| 307 | yes (`52³ ≡ 2`) | yes (`192³ ≡ 3`) |

All four patterns occur, so two cubic symbols separate exactly `2² = 4`
candidates: the capacity bound is attained, not merely an upper bound.

**[Lean]** `PowerResidueSharp.capacity_two_bits_sharp`.

## 5. Transversality of the two channels (base 2)

| p | 2 a square mod p | 2 a cube mod p |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | yes (`3² = 9 ≡ 2`) | no |
| 13 | no | no |
| 31 | yes (`8² = 64 ≡ 2`) | yes |
| 43 | no (`43 ≡ 3 mod 8`) | yes |

**[Lean]** `PowerResidueSharp.quadratic_cubic_all_patterns`,
`PowerResidueSharp.cubic_not_determined_by_quadratic`,
`PowerResidueSharp.quadratic_not_determined_by_cubic`.

## 6. OEIS

The primes with `2` a cubic residue — `31, 43, 109, 127, 157, 223, 229, 277, 283, …`
— are the primes represented by `x² + 27y²`; the fine-arithmetic (non-congruence)
character of this set is precisely why no dial can read the cubic bit.  No new
sequence was generated by this work, so no OEIS submission applies.
