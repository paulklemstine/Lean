## Abstract

We present a finite logical account of interventional-query transformation based on the three rule schemata of do-calculus. For a fixed finite set of causal variables, a query consists of pairwise disjoint target, observation, and intervention sets. A graphical oracle supplies the side-condition tests for observation insertion or deletion, action–observation exchange, and action insertion or deletion. Derivability is defined as the least equivalence relation containing every oracle-approved primitive transformation. Semantics is intentionally non-parametric and relational: a model is any equivalence relation on queries that validates all primitive transformations, and a query equality is semantically identifiable when it holds in every such model. We prove soundness and completeness by structural induction and a canonical-model construction. We then define a finite exhaustive decision procedure that accepts a pair precisely when every Boolean equivalence relation containing the primitive rules relates that pair. The procedure is proved sound and complete for the relational semantics and therefore equivalent to derivability. We give direct algorithms based on exhaustive model enumeration and equivalence-class reachability, discuss complexity and certificates, and separate the completed logical result from the additional graphical and structural-equation bridges needed for a full implementation of causal effect identification.

## 1. Introduction

Causal inference distinguishes conditioning from intervention. An observational expression $P(Y\mid X=x)$ describes the distribution of $Y$ in the subpopulation where $X=x$. An interventional expression $P(Y\mid \operatorname{do}(X=x))$ describes the distribution produced by externally setting $X$ to $x$. Causal identification asks whether an interventional quantity is uniquely determined by observational or otherwise available distributions under stated causal assumptions.

Do-calculus addresses this question through three graphical transformation rules. Their applications are certified by d-separation statements in suitably modified causal graphs. In ordinary derivations, one repeatedly inserts or deletes observations, exchanges observations with actions, and inserts or deletes actions. The resulting chains can be difficult to audit unless the syntax, side conditions, semantics, and closure principles are clearly separated.

This paper isolates the finite logical core. We fix $n$ variables and represent an interventional query by three disjoint subsets. A graphical oracle records which pairs of queries satisfy each rule’s side condition. The oracle is abstract: it may ultimately be implemented by d-separation, but the logical development assumes only its Boolean outputs. This makes explicit what follows from the rule approvals alone.

Our semantics is correspondingly precise. A model is not, in this paper, a probability space or a system of structural equations. It is an arbitrary equivalence relation on query expressions that contains all primitive rule pairs. Semantic identifiability means truth in every such relation. Within this relational semantics, we prove that the three-rule calculus is sound and complete. The canonical model is the derivability relation itself, so completeness follows by applying universal validity to that model.

The finiteness of the query language yields decidability. We define a Boolean theory to be a Boolean-valued equivalence relation containing every primitive pair. An exhaustive procedure accepts $(q,r)$ exactly when every Boolean theory relates $q$ and $r$. We prove that acceptance, universal relational validity, and finite derivability are equivalent.

The scope is important. The result is a completeness theorem for the non-parametric relational theory generated by the oracle-approved rules. It does not by itself establish that a concrete oracle computes the standard mutilated-graph d-separation conditions, nor that those conditions preserve kernels in every intended structural-equation model, nor the broader completeness of a recursive identification algorithm for semi-Markovian models. Those are explicit bridge theorems for future work. The benefit of the present separation is modularity: graphical correctness, causal-model soundness, and logical closure become independent proof obligations.

## 2. Finite query syntax

Let

$$
V_n=\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
$$

be a finite set of variable indices.

**Definition 2.1 (Well-formed causal query).** A well-formed query over $V_n$ is a triple

$$
q=(T_q,O_q,I_q)
$$

of subsets of $V_n$ satisfying

$$
T_q\cap O_q=\varnothing,\qquad
T_q\cap I_q=\varnothing,\qquad
O_q\cap I_q=\varnothing.
$$

The intended expression is

$$
P(T_q\mid O_q,\operatorname{do}(I_q)).
$$

Here $T_q$ is the target set, $O_q$ is the ordinary observation set, and $I_q$ is the intervention set.

Each variable has exactly four possible roles: target, observed, intervened, or absent. Consequently, the set $Q_n$ of all well-formed queries has cardinality

$$
|Q_n|=4^n.
$$

This count is useful both conceptually and computationally. It shows that all quantification over queries and all tables of binary relations on queries are finite.

**Definition 2.2 (Graphical oracle).** A graphical oracle consists of a causal directed acyclic graph on $n$ vertices together with three Boolean predicates on ordered pairs of queries:

$$
G_1,G_2,G_3:Q_n\times Q_n\to\{0,1\}.
$$

Their intended meanings are:

1. $G_1(q,r)=1$ certifies an instance of observation insertion or deletion;
2. $G_2(q,r)=1$ certifies an instance of action–observation exchange;
3. $G_3(q,r)=1$ certifies an instance of action insertion or deletion.

The directed graph records the causal structure associated with the tests. The logical results below depend only on the predicates $G_1,G_2,G_3$, not on how they are computed.

**Definition 2.3 (Primitive rule step).** A primitive step $q\leadsto r$ holds when at least one graphical test accepts:

$$
q\leadsto r
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
G_1(q,r)=1\ \lor\ G_2(q,r)=1\ \lor\ G_3(q,r)=1.
$$

This definition treats the three rules as distinct constructors even when more than one rule approves the same pair. Distinguishing the labels is useful for derivation certificates.

## 3. Derivations as equivalence closure

**Definition 3.1 (Derivability).** The derivability relation $\vdash$ on $Q_n$ is generated by the following clauses:

- **Reflexivity:** $q\vdash q$ for every $q$;
- **Primitive inclusion:** if $q\leadsto r$, then $q\vdash r$;
- **Symmetry:** if $q\vdash r$, then $r\vdash q$;
- **Transitivity:** if $q\vdash r$ and $r\vdash s$, then $q\vdash s$.

Equivalently, $\vdash$ is the least equivalence relation containing $\leadsto$.

A derivation is finite because it is built from finitely many applications of these constructors. Although a primitive test is presented on ordered pairs, the symmetry clause makes the final closure undirected.

**Lemma 3.2 (Minimality of derivability).** Let $R$ be any equivalence relation on $Q_n$ such that $q\leadsto r$ implies $R(q,r)$. Then

$$
q\vdash r\quad\Longrightarrow\quad R(q,r).
$$

**Proof sketch.** Induct on the construction of $q\vdash r$. Reflexive derivations use reflexivity of $R$; primitive derivations use the containment assumption; reversed derivations use symmetry of $R$; and composed derivations use transitivity of $R$. Thus every equivalence relation containing the primitive steps also contains all derivable pairs. $\square$

The lemma already suggests the semantics: admissible interpretations are exactly relations of the kind quantified over in the minimality statement.

## 4. Non-parametric relational semantics

**Definition 4.1 (Relational model of the rule theory).** A non-parametric relational model $M$ assigns a proposition

$$
q\equiv_M r
$$

to each pair $q,r\in Q_n$ and satisfies:

1. $q\equiv_M q$ for every $q$;
2. $q\equiv_M r$ implies $r\equiv_M q$;
3. $q\equiv_M r$ and $r\equiv_M s$ imply $q\equiv_M s$;
4. $q\leadsto r$ implies $q\equiv_M r$.

Thus $\equiv_M$ is an equivalence relation validating all instances of the three primitive rules.

The adjective “non-parametric” indicates that no linear, Gaussian, polynomial, or other parametric form is assumed. More strongly, the present semantics abstracts away from numerical kernels entirely. It captures the universal equality theory generated by the rules. Accordingly, any application to structural causal models must separately establish that equality of interventional kernels supplies a model satisfying Definition 4.1.

**Definition 4.2 (Semantic identifiability).** A pair of queries $(q,r)$ is semantically identifiable, written

$$
q\models r,
$$

when

$$
q\equiv_M r
$$

for every relational model $M$ of the rule theory.

This is universal validity. A failure of semantic identifiability is witnessed by a countermodel: an admissible equivalence relation that separates $q$ and $r$.

## 5. Soundness and completeness

**Theorem 5.1 (Soundness).** For all $q,r\in Q_n$,

$$
q\vdash r\quad\Longrightarrow\quad q\models r.
$$

**Proof sketch.** Fix an arbitrary relational model $M$ and induct on the derivation of $q\vdash r$. The reflexive case follows from reflexivity of $\equiv_M$. A primitive step is valid by axiom 4 of Definition 4.1. If a shorter derivation establishes $q\equiv_M r$, symmetry gives $r\equiv_M q$. If two derivations establish $q\equiv_M r$ and $r\equiv_M s$, transitivity gives $q\equiv_M s$. Hence every derivation is valid in $M$. Since $M$ was arbitrary, the equality is semantically identifiable. $\square$

To prove the converse, we construct a distinguished model.

**Definition 5.2 (Canonical model).** Define a relation $\equiv_C$ by

$$
q\equiv_C r
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
q\vdash r.
$$

**Lemma 5.3 (Canonical-model admissibility).** The canonical relation $\equiv_C$ is a relational model of the rule theory.

**Proof sketch.** Reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity are constructors of derivability. Primitive inclusion ensures that every $q\leadsto r$ satisfies $q\vdash r$. Hence all four model conditions hold. $\square$

**Theorem 5.4 (Completeness).** For all $q,r\in Q_n$,

$$
q\models r\quad\Longrightarrow\quad q\vdash r.
$$

**Proof sketch.** Assume $q\models r$. Universal validity applies in particular to the canonical model, so $q\equiv_C r$. By the definition of the canonical model, this is exactly $q\vdash r$. $\square$

**Theorem 5.5 (Soundness and completeness).** For every finite graphical oracle and all well-formed queries $q,r$,

$$
q\models r
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
q\vdash r.
$$

**Proof sketch.** The forward implication is Theorem 5.4 and the reverse implication is Theorem 5.1. $\square$

Theorem 5.5 is a canonical-model completeness result. It is exact for Definition 4.1: derivability captures all and only the equalities common to every equivalence relation validating the primitive rules.

## 6. Boolean theories and finite decision

Because $Q_n$ is finite, a binary relation can be represented by a Boolean matrix.

**Definition 6.1 (Boolean theory).** A function

$$
R:Q_n\times Q_n\to\{0,1\}
$$

is a Boolean theory when it satisfies all six conditions below:

$$
R(q,q)=1
$$

for every $q$;

$$
R(q,r)=1\Longrightarrow R(r,q)=1;
$$

$$
R(q,r)=1\land R(r,s)=1\Longrightarrow R(q,s)=1;
$$

and, for each $i\in\{1,2,3\}$,

$$
G_i(q,r)=1\Longrightarrow R(q,r)=1.
$$

The first three conditions assert equivalence; the last three assert inclusion of each primitive rule relation.

**Lemma 6.2 (Boolean-theory specification).** A Boolean relation passes the theory checker exactly when it satisfies reflexivity, symmetry, transitivity, and closure under $G_1$, $G_2$, and $G_3$ as stated in Definition 6.1.

**Proof sketch.** The checker is the finite conjunction of precisely these six decidable conditions. Evaluating the conjunction to true is equivalent to each conjunct holding. $\square$

**Definition 6.3 (Exhaustive identification procedure).** Define $\operatorname{Identify}(q,r)$ to return true exactly when

$$
\forall R:Q_n\times Q_n\to\{0,1\},
\quad
R\text{ is a Boolean theory}\Longrightarrow R(q,r)=1.
$$

This procedure terminates because $Q_n$ is finite. If $N=|Q_n|=4^n$, then a relation has $N^2$ entries and there are $2^{N^2}$ relation tables.

**Lemma 6.4 (Boolean theories induce relational models).** Every Boolean theory $R$ defines a relational model by

$$
q\equiv_R r
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
R(q,r)=1.
$$

**Proof sketch.** The first three Boolean-theory conditions become reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity of $\equiv_R$. The final three conditions validate every primitive rule step. $\square$

**Theorem 6.5 (Decision soundness).** If $\operatorname{Identify}(q,r)$ returns true, then $q\models r$.

**Proof sketch.** Let $M$ be any relational model. Form its characteristic Boolean relation

$$
R_M(a,b)=1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
a\equiv_M b.
$$

The model axioms imply that $R_M$ is a Boolean theory. Since the procedure accepts $(q,r)$, every Boolean theory relates $q$ and $r$; in particular, $R_M(q,r)=1$. Thus $q\equiv_M r$. As $M$ was arbitrary, $q\models r$. $\square$

**Theorem 6.6 (Decision completeness).** If $q\models r$, then $\operatorname{Identify}(q,r)$ returns true.

**Proof sketch.** Let $R$ be any Boolean theory. By Lemma 6.4 it induces a relational model. Universal validity gives $q\equiv_R r$, hence $R(q,r)=1$. Since this holds for every Boolean theory, the exhaustive procedure accepts. $\square$

**Theorem 6.7 (Correctness of exhaustive identification).** For every graphical oracle and every $q,r\in Q_n$,

$$
\operatorname{Identify}(q,r)=\mathrm{true}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
q\models r.
$$

**Proof sketch.** Combine Theorems 6.5 and 6.6. $\square$

**Corollary 6.8 (Decision–derivation equivalence).** For every graphical oracle and every $q,r\in Q_n$,

$$
\operatorname{Identify}(q,r)=\mathrm{true}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
q\vdash r.
$$

**Proof sketch.** Combine Theorem 6.7 with Theorem 5.5. $\square$

The corollary unifies model-theoretic and proof-theoretic computation. Universal quantification over all admissible relations and reachability through primitive transformations return the same answer.

## 7. Algorithms

### 7.1 Exhaustive model enumeration

The specification-level algorithm follows Definition 6.3 literally.

**Algorithm 1 (Universal Boolean-Theory Enumeration).**

1. Enumerate the $N=4^n$ well-formed queries.
2. Enumerate every $N\times N$ Boolean matrix $R$.
3. Test whether $R$ is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive.
4. Test whether $R$ contains every pair approved by $G_1$, $G_2$, or $G_3$.
5. If a valid $R$ has $R(q,r)=0$, return false and retain $R$ as a countermodel.
6. If no such $R$ exists, return true.

A straightforward theory check costs $O(N^3)$ time because transitivity examines triples; the other tests cost $O(N^2)$. Enumerating all relations gives worst-case time

$$
O(2^{N^2}N^3),
$$

with $O(N^2)$ working space when matrices are streamed. This is not a practical large-scale algorithm. Its virtue is semantic transparency and immediate termination.

### 7.2 Primitive-edge connectivity

Corollary 6.8 suggests a much better decision method. Build an undirected graph $H$ whose vertices are queries and where $\{q,r\}$ is an edge whenever $q\leadsto r$ or $r\leadsto q$. Then derivability is connectedness in $H$.

**Algorithm 2 (Query Equivalence-Class Reachability).**

1. Initialize a queue with the source query $q$ and mark $q$ visited.
2. Remove a query $u$ from the queue.
3. For every query $v$, evaluate the six directed oracle possibilities $G_i(u,v)$ and $G_i(v,u)$.
4. Whenever any test accepts and $v$ is unvisited, record $u$ as its predecessor, label the approving rule, and enqueue $v$.
5. Return true when $r$ is reached; return false if the queue empties.

With explicit all-pairs neighbor generation, the algorithm takes $O(N^2C_G)$ time and $O(N)$ auxiliary space, where $C_G$ bounds the cost of the three oracle evaluations. If primitive neighbors can be generated directly, the cost becomes $O(N+E)$ oracle-independent graph work for $E$ primitive edges.

The predecessor map provides a certificate: a path from $q$ to $r$. Every edge becomes a primitive derivation, and transitivity composes the path. If a traversed edge arose in the reverse oracle direction, symmetry reverses its primitive derivation.

### 7.3 Equivalence closure by disjoint sets

When many pair queries must be answered for one fixed oracle, a disjoint-set union structure is natural.

**Algorithm 3 (Batch Equivalence Closure).**

1. Enumerate all queries and create one disjoint-set component per query.
2. For every ordered pair $(q,r)$ and each rule $i$, test $G_i(q,r)$.
3. If a test succeeds, union the components of $q$ and $r$.
4. After all tests, answer whether $q\vdash r$ by comparing component representatives.

The algorithm uses $O(N)$ disjoint-set storage. With all-pairs oracle evaluation it takes $O(N^2C_G+N^2\alpha(N))$ time, where $\alpha$ is the inverse Ackermann function. Subsequent pair queries are answered in nearly constant amortized time. Unlike breadth-first search, ordinary disjoint sets do not automatically retain a readable derivation; a union forest augmented with edge witnesses can recover certificates.

## 8. Worked finite example

Let the query universe be

$$
Q=\{q_0,q_1,q_2,q_3,q_4\}.
$$

Suppose the oracle approves the primitive pairs

$$
G_1(q_0,q_1)=1,\qquad
G_2(q_1,q_2)=1,\qquad
G_3(q_3,q_4)=1,
$$

and no others. Derivability partitions $Q$ into two equivalence classes:

$$
\{q_0,q_1,q_2\},\qquad \{q_3,q_4\}.
$$

The pair $(q_0,q_2)$ is derivable: primitive inclusion gives $q_0\vdash q_1$ and $q_1\vdash q_2$, then transitivity gives $q_0\vdash q_2$. Soundness implies that every admissible model equates them. The exhaustive decision procedure therefore accepts.

By contrast, $(q_0,q_4)$ is not derivable. Define $R$ to equate precisely the members within each displayed class. This is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive, and it contains every primitive pair. Yet $R(q_0,q_4)=0$. Thus $R$ is a countermodel, semantic identifiability fails, and the exhaustive procedure rejects. The example illustrates a general negative certificate: connected components themselves supply the coarsest admissible partition, while any admissible partition that separates two queries refutes universal validity.

## 9. Applications and interpretation

### 9.1 Auditable causal-expression rewriting

A practical causal system can store each oracle-approved rewrite with its rule label and graphical evidence. The logical layer then composes those local equalities without reinterpreting their causal meaning. Positive answers can be accompanied by a path certificate, allowing an analyst to inspect every transformation.

### 9.2 Countermodels for failed identification

When two queries are not connected, the component partition is an admissible Boolean theory that separates them. This is a logical countermodel: it demonstrates that the desired equality does not follow from the supplied primitive approvals. It does not necessarily prove that no richer causal argument can identify the effect, because additional probability rules or a stronger oracle may enlarge the theory. It precisely diagnoses insufficiency of the present rule relation.

### 9.3 Modular validation

The architecture separates three concerns:

1. **Graphical layer:** computes side conditions, ideally via d-separation in modified graphs;
2. **Logical layer:** closes approved transformations under equality reasoning;
3. **Causal-semantic layer:** interprets query equality as equality of interventional kernels in a structural model class.

Theorems 5.5 and 6.7 settle the logical layer. A complete end-to-end causal implementation should establish interfaces between the first and second layers and between the second and third.

### 9.4 Finite experimentation

For small $n$, the entire query universe has size $4^n$. This permits exhaustive regression tests of oracle implementations, optimized solvers, and certificate bounds. For instance, an optimized work-list can be compared pointwise with universal Boolean-theory enumeration on tiny universes, providing a powerful check before scaling.

## 10. Scope and limitations

The phrase “complete for identifiability” can refer to several different statements. The exact theorem established here is:

> An equality of finite query expressions holds in every equivalence-relation interpretation validating the oracle-approved instances of the three rule schemata if and only if it lies in their equivalence closure.

This theorem is mathematically complete for the stated relational semantics. It should not be conflated with the stronger claim that a specific implementation is complete for all causal effects in all non-parametric structural equation models. Establishing that stronger claim requires additional objects and theorems: explicit mutilated graphs, d-separation, induced interventional kernels, latent-variable structures, probability-calculus normalization, and an identification algorithm capable of producing expressions rather than only equating pre-existing query triples.

A second limitation concerns efficiency. Universal enumeration of all relations is enormous: substituting $N=4^n$ gives $2^{16^n}$ candidate tables. Reachability or disjoint-set closure is the appropriate operational realization of the same result.

A third limitation is expressiveness. The syntax stores sets of target, observed, and intervened variables, but not values, summations, products, kernel factorization, or marginalization. Those operations are central to realistic identification formulas. The present language is therefore best regarded as a finite equality core around which a richer expression calculus can be constructed.

## 11. Future work

Five directions follow naturally.

First, **graphical-oracle adequacy** should be established: for every finite acyclic causal graph and disjoint query components, Boolean tests implemented using moralized ancestral graphs should agree exactly with the relevant d-separation statements in mutilated graphs. Exhaustive comparison over all directed acyclic graphs with at most five vertices offers a falsifiable first milestone.

Second, **polynomial-space reachability** should replace exhaustive model enumeration while preserving its Boolean output. The finite connected-component characterization makes a work-list algorithm the natural candidate.

Third, a **structural-equation soundness bridge** should show that, in finite-valued acyclic non-parametric structural equation models with mutually independent exogenous variables, every graphically certified primitive step preserves the induced interventional kernel.

Fourth, the language should be connected to a **concrete recursive identification algorithm** for finite semi-Markovian graphs. Probability-calculus normalization rules will be needed, and success of the recursive algorithm should coincide with derivability in the enriched calculus.

Fifth, **certificate extraction** should accompany optimized positive decisions. A predecessor map can produce a primitive-step derivation whose length is bounded by the number of discovered query states.

## 12. Conclusion

A finite system of causal-query transformations admits a particularly clean logical analysis. Queries form a finite language; the three rule schemata generate primitive equalities; derivability is their equivalence closure; and models are equivalence relations that validate those equalities. Structural induction proves soundness, while the derivability relation itself supplies the canonical model needed for completeness.

The same structure yields a decision theorem. Exhaustive quantification over all Boolean theories accepts exactly the semantically valid pairs, and therefore exactly the derivable pairs. Although that specification-level procedure is expensive, its result is computable efficiently as graph reachability or equivalence-class closure once primitive edges are available.

The contribution is thus both conceptual and algorithmic: universal relational validity, finite proof, and query-graph connectedness are three descriptions of the same phenomenon. This equivalence provides a rigorous logical core for auditable causal-expression reasoning and a clear foundation for future graphical and structural-equation extensions.
