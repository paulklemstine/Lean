# Phantom Topologies and the Possibility Modality: Consensus and Superposition of Observer-Dependent Spaces

## Abstract

We develop a framework in which the topology of a set is not fixed but relative
to an *observer*. A **phantom topology** on a set $X$ indexed by an observer set
$\iota$ is a family $T \colon \iota \to \mathrm{Top}(X)$ assigning to each
observer $i$ a topology $T(i)$ on the common underlying set $X$. Within the
complete lattice of topologies on $X$ — ordered by fineness — we single out two
canonical ways of collapsing the family into one "official" topology. The
**consensus** (necessity) topology is the supremum $\bigsqcup_i T(i)$, whose open
sets are exactly the sets open for *every* observer. The **possibility**
(dual) topology is the infimum $\bigsqcap_i T(i)$, the coarsest topology finer
than every observer, generated by the sets open for *some* observer. Consensus
and possibility are order-theoretic duals and behave as the modal pair
$\Box / \Diamond$.

Our two central results concern the real line viewed through two complementary
half-open observers — the *lower-limit* (Sorgenfrey) observer, whose basic opens
are right half-open intervals $[x,b)$, and the *upper-limit* observer, whose basic
opens are left half-open intervals $(a,x]$. First, the **consensus** of these two
observers is exactly the Euclidean topology on $\mathbb{R}$: the ordinary
continuum is the unanimous agreement of a left-looking and a right-looking eye,
and neither observer alone suffices. Second — and dually — the **possibility** of
the *same* two observers is the **discrete** topology: the pooled view isolates
every point through the identity $[x,b) \cap (a,x] = \{x\}$, so every singleton,
and hence every subset, is open. The two natural pooling operations on one fixed
pair of observers thus yield opposite extremes: the connected continuum and the
totally disconnected discrete space. We develop the framework, prove both
collapses in full, isolate the pointwise mechanism behind the discreteness, and
propose a family of invariants — most notably a *possibility number* dual to the
consensus-based *phantom number* — measuring how many observers must be pooled or
reconciled to determine a space.

**Keywords:** phantom topology, observer-dependent topology, lattice of
topologies, Sorgenfrey line, lower-limit topology, upper-limit topology,
consensus, possibility, modal duality, discrete topology.

## 1. Introduction

Classical topology equips a set with a single distinguished family of open sets.
Yet in many modeling situations — distributed sensing, epistemic logic, quantum
measurement, coarse-to-fine perception — the notion of "which regions are
resolvable" is intrinsically relative to an agent. This paper takes that
relativity literally and studies structures in which *each observer carries their
own topology* on a shared set of points.

The organizing principle is the complete lattice $\mathrm{Top}(X)$ of all
topologies on $X$, ordered so that $t \le s$ means $t$ is **finer** than $s$
(has at least as many open sets). This lattice is complete: arbitrary suprema and
infima exist. Given a family of observer topologies, its supremum and infimum are
the two most natural "official" topologies one can extract, and they turn out to
be conceptually opposite:

- the **supremum** (consensus / necessity) captures *unanimous agreement*;
- the **infimum** (possibility / superposition) captures *pooled resolution*.

Our running laboratory is $\mathbb{R}$ with two complementary observers. The main
phenomenon we report is a sharp *dichotomy of pooling*: on one fixed pair of
observers, consensus reconstructs the Euclidean continuum while possibility
collapses it to the discrete space. This is the topological analogue of a
measurement whose outcome depends not on the system but on how the observations
are combined.

### Contributions

1. A clean lattice-theoretic development of phantom topologies, consensus, and
   the dual possibility modality (Section 3), including the monotonicity laws
   relating an individual observer to both extremes.
2. The **consensus reconstruction theorem**: the Euclidean topology on
   $\mathbb{R}$ is exactly the consensus of the lower-limit and upper-limit
   observers, together with the sharp lower bound that a single observer does not
   suffice (Section 4).
3. The **possibility collapse theorem**: the possibility topology of the same two
   observers is the discrete topology, driven by the pointwise identity
   $[x,b) \cap (a,x] = \{x\}$ (Section 5).
4. A program of invariants — the possibility number and its relatives — with
   falsifiable conjectures (Sections 6–7).

## 2. Preliminaries: the lattice of topologies

Let $X$ be a set. A **topology** on $X$ is a family of subsets (the *open* sets)
containing $\varnothing$ and $X$ and closed under arbitrary unions and finite
intersections. Write $\mathrm{Top}(X)$ for the set of all topologies on $X$.

**Refinement order.** For $t, s \in \mathrm{Top}(X)$ we say $t$ is *finer* than
$s$, written $t \le s$, if every $s$-open set is $t$-open. Under this order
$\mathrm{Top}(X)$ is a complete lattice. Two extreme elements matter throughout:

- the **discrete** topology $\bot$, the finest topology, in which *every* subset
  is open (it is the bottom of the order because it lies below all others);
- the **indiscrete** topology $\top$, the coarsest, whose only open sets are
  $\varnothing$ and $X$.

**Suprema and infima.** For a family $\{t_i\}_{i \in \iota}$:

- The supremum $\bigsqcup_i t_i$ has as its open sets exactly those subsets that
  are $t_i$-open for *every* $i$. Equivalently, $U$ is open in $\bigsqcup_i t_i$
  iff $\forall i,\ U$ is $t_i$-open.
- The infimum $\bigsqcap_i t_i$ is the finest topology that is coarser than every
  $t_i$; it is *generated* (via arbitrary unions of finite intersections) by the
  union $\bigcup_i \{U : U \text{ is } t_i\text{-open}\}$ of all the observers'
  open families.

We freely use the following standard facts: $t_i \le \bigsqcup_j t_j$ and
$\bigsqcap_j t_j \le t_i$ for each $i$ (each element lies between the infimum and
the supremum of the family).

## 3. Phantom topologies, consensus, and possibility

### 3.1 The framework

**Definition 3.1 (Phantom topology).** Let $\iota$ be a set of *observers*. A
**phantom topology** on $X$ with observer set $\iota$ is a function
$$T \colon \iota \longrightarrow \mathrm{Top}(X),$$
assigning to each observer $i$ a topology $T(i)$ on $X$. All observers share the
underlying point set $X$; they may disagree arbitrarily on which subsets are open.

**Definition 3.2 (Consensus topology).** The **consensus** (or *real*, or
*necessity*) topology of a phantom topology $T$ is the supremum
$$\Box T \;=\; \mathrm{consensus}(T) \;=\; \bigsqcup_{i \in \iota} T(i).$$

**Proposition 3.3 (Agreement characterization).** For every subset $U \subseteq X$,
$$U \text{ is consensus-open} \iff \forall i \in \iota,\ U \text{ is } T(i)\text{-open}.$$
That is, a set is real iff *all* observers see it as open.

*Proof.* Immediate from the description of the supremum in $\mathrm{Top}(X)$: the
open sets of $\bigsqcup_i T(i)$ are exactly the sets open in every $T(i)$. $\square$

**Definition 3.4 (Possibility topology).** The **possibility** (or *dual*)
topology of $T$ is the infimum
$$\Diamond T \;=\; \mathrm{possibility}(T) \;=\; \bigsqcap_{i \in \iota} T(i),$$
the finest topology generated by the sets that are open for *some* observer.

Where consensus intersects the observers' open families, possibility takes their
generated union. Consensus is the modal $\Box$ ("open for all"); possibility is
the modal $\Diamond$ ("built from what is open for some").

### 3.2 Monotonicity: measurement coarsens, superposition sharpens

The relationship between a single observer and the two pooled topologies is fixed
by the lattice order, and it runs in opposite directions.

**Proposition 3.5 (Consensus coarsens).** For every observer $i$,
$$T(i) \;\le\; \Box T,$$
i.e. each observer is *finer* than the consensus. Adding observers to the
committee can only *remove* open sets from the agreed topology.

*Proof.* $T(i) \le \bigsqcup_j T(j)$ holds for each $i$ by definition of the
supremum. $\square$

**Proposition 3.6 (Possibility sharpens).** For every observer $i$,
$$\Diamond T \;\le\; T(i),$$
i.e. each observer is *coarser* than the possibility. The possibility topology
resolves at least everything every observer resolves; adding observers to the
pool can only *add* open sets.

*Proof.* $\bigsqcap_j T(j) \le T(i)$ holds for each $i$ by definition of the
infimum. $\square$

These two propositions are the structural core of the framework. Consensus is
order-reversing in resolution (more agreement, less detail); possibility is
order-preserving (more pooling, more detail). They sandwich every observer:
$$\Diamond T \;\le\; T(i) \;\le\; \Box T \qquad \text{for all } i.$$

## 4. Consensus on the real line: the two-observer reconstruction

We now instantiate the framework on $X = \mathbb{R}$ with two complementary
observers.

### 4.1 The two observers

**Definition 4.1 (Lower-limit / right-looking observer).** A set $U \subseteq
\mathbb{R}$ is **lower-open** if every point of $U$ is the left endpoint of a
right half-open interval contained in $U$:
$$U \text{ lower-open} \iff \forall x \in U,\ \exists b > x,\ [x,b) \subseteq U.$$
The lower-open sets form a topology $L$ (the *lower-limit* or *Sorgenfrey*
topology).

**Definition 4.2 (Upper-limit / left-looking observer).** A set $U \subseteq
\mathbb{R}$ is **upper-open** if every point of $U$ is the right endpoint of a
left half-open interval contained in $U$:
$$U \text{ upper-open} \iff \forall x \in U,\ \exists a < x,\ (a,x] \subseteq U.$$
The upper-open sets form a topology $U\!p$ (the *upper-limit* topology).

That $L$ and $U\!p$ are topologies follows by direct verification: $\varnothing$
and $\mathbb{R}$ qualify; a finite intersection is handled by taking the minimum
(resp. maximum) of the endpoints supplied at a common point; arbitrary unions are
immediate since a witnessing interval for a point in the union is inherited from
the member set containing it.

### 4.2 The reconstruction theorem

**Theorem 4.3 (Consensus reconstruction).** The consensus of the lower-limit and
upper-limit observers is exactly the Euclidean topology on $\mathbb{R}$:
$$L \sqcup U\!p \;=\; \tau_{\mathrm{Euclid}}.$$
Equivalently, a set $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ is Euclidean-open if and only if it is
both lower-open and upper-open.

*Proof.* ($\Leftarrow$, agreement $\Rightarrow$ Euclidean.) Suppose $U$ is both
lower-open and upper-open, and let $x \in U$. Lower-openness yields $b > x$ with
$[x,b) \subseteq U$; upper-openness yields $a < x$ with $(a,x] \subseteq U$.
Set $\varepsilon = \min(x - a,\ b - x) > 0$. For any $y$ with $|y - x| <
\varepsilon$: if $y \ge x$ then $y \in [x,b) \subseteq U$; if $y < x$ then $y \in
(a,x] \subseteq U$. Hence the two-sided ball $(x-\varepsilon, x+\varepsilon)
\subseteq U$, so $U$ is Euclidean-open.

($\Rightarrow$, Euclidean $\Rightarrow$ agreement.) Suppose $U$ is Euclidean-open
and $x \in U$; pick $\varepsilon > 0$ with $(x-\varepsilon, x+\varepsilon)
\subseteq U$. Then $[x, x+\varepsilon) \subseteq U$ witnesses lower-openness at
$x$, and $(x-\varepsilon, x] \subseteq U$ witnesses upper-openness at $x$. As $x$
was arbitrary, $U$ is both lower- and upper-open. $\square$

Packaged as a genuine two-element observer family $T \colon \{0,1\} \to
\mathrm{Top}(\mathbb{R})$ with $T(0) = U\!p$ and $T(1) = L$, Theorem 4.3 says
$\Box T = \tau_{\mathrm{Euclid}}$: the continuum has **phantom number two** in
this representation.

### 4.3 One observer is not enough

The bound of two is sharp: no single observer sees the Euclidean topology.

**Proposition 4.4.** The interval $[0,1)$ is lower-open but not Euclidean-open;
the interval $(0,1]$ is upper-open but not Euclidean-open. Consequently
$L \ne \tau_{\mathrm{Euclid}}$ and $U\!p \ne \tau_{\mathrm{Euclid}}$, and moreover
$L \ne U\!p$.

*Proof.* $[0,1)$ is lower-open (take $b = 1$ at each point, and any interior point
has room on the right). It is not Euclidean-open because no two-sided ball around
$0$ fits inside it: every $(-\varepsilon, \varepsilon)$ contains negative numbers.
Symmetrically for $(0,1]$ and the upper-limit observer. Finally $[0,1)$ is
lower-open but not upper-open — an upper-open witness at $0$ would require an
interval $(a, 0] \subseteq [0,1)$, impossible since such an interval contains
points below $0$ — so $L \ne U\!p$. $\square$

Thus each observer strictly over-resolves the line (in complementary directions),
their disagreement is genuine, and only their *agreement* recovers the ordinary
continuum.

## 5. Possibility on the real line: the collapse to discreteness

We now compute the *other* pooling of the *same* two observers.

### 5.1 The pointwise mechanism

**Lemma 5.1 (Singleton as an interval intersection).** For all reals $a < x < b$,
$$[x,b)\ \cap\ (a,x]\ =\ \{x\}.$$

*Proof.* If $y \in [x,b) \cap (a,x]$ then $x \le y$ (from $[x,b)$) and $y \le x$
(from $(a,x]$), so $y = x$; and $x$ lies in both intervals since $a < x < b$.
$\square$

**Lemma 5.2 (Basic membership).** For each $x$, the interval $[x, b)$ (with
$b > x$) is lower-open, and $(a, x]$ (with $a < x$) is upper-open.

*Proof.* For $[x,b)$: any $y \in [x,b)$ has $[y, b) \subseteq [x, b)$, witnessing
lower-openness. For $(a,x]$: any $y \in (a,x]$ has $(a, y] \subseteq (a, x]$,
witnessing upper-openness. $\square$

### 5.2 The collapse theorem

**Theorem 5.3 (Possibility collapse).** The possibility topology of the
lower-limit and upper-limit observers on $\mathbb{R}$ is the discrete topology:
$$L \sqcap U\!p \;=\; \bot.$$
Every subset of $\mathbb{R}$ is open in the possibility topology.

*Proof.* Write $P = L \sqcap U\!p$. Since $P$ is the infimum, every lower-open set
and every upper-open set is $P$-open; and $P$ is closed under finite intersections
and arbitrary unions. Fix $x \in \mathbb{R}$. By Lemma 5.2, $[x, x+1)$ is
lower-open and $(x-1, x]$ is upper-open, so both are $P$-open, and hence so is
their intersection. By Lemma 5.1,
$$[x, x+1)\ \cap\ (x-1, x]\ =\ \{x\},$$
so the singleton $\{x\}$ is $P$-open. Now let $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ be
arbitrary. Then $U = \bigcup_{x \in U} \{x\}$ is a union of $P$-open singletons,
hence $P$-open. Therefore every subset is $P$-open, i.e. $P = \bot$. $\square$

Packaged on the two-element observer family $T$ of Section 4.2, Theorem 5.3 reads
$\Diamond T = \bot$.

### 5.3 The dichotomy

Combining Theorems 4.3 and 5.3 on the *same* pair of observers:

$$\Diamond\{L, U\!p\} \;=\; \bot \;\subsetneq\; \tau_{\mathrm{Euclid}} \;=\; \Box\{L, U\!p\}.$$

The two canonical poolings of one fixed pair of complementary observers land at
opposite extremes of resolution:

| Pooling rule | Reading | Result on $\mathbb{R}$ |
|---|---|---|
| Consensus $\Box = \bigsqcup$ | open for **all** observers | Euclidean continuum |
| Possibility $\Diamond = \bigsqcap$ | built from what is open for **some** | discrete space |

Necessity reconstructs the connected line; possibility shatters it into isolated
points. Crucially, the collapse argument never invokes the Euclidean structure or
the consensus theorem: it uses only the raw shapes of the observers' basic opens
and the set-theoretic identity of Lemma 5.1. The dichotomy is therefore a genuine
statement about *pooling half-open viewpoints*, not an artifact of the metric.

## 6. Invariants: the possibility number

The dichotomy suggests dual numerical invariants attached to any space.

**Definition 6.1 (Phantom number).** The **phantom number** of a space
$(X, \tau)$ is the least cardinality of an observer family $\{T(i)\}$, each
strictly finer than $\tau$, whose *consensus* equals $\tau$:
$$\mathrm{ph}(X,\tau) = \min\Big\{\, |\iota| \;:\; \textstyle\bigsqcup_i T(i) = \tau,\ T(i) \gneq \tau \Big\}.$$
Theorem 4.3 and Proposition 4.4 give $\mathrm{ph}(\mathbb{R}, \tau_{\mathrm{Euclid}}) = 2$.

**Definition 6.2 (Possibility number).** Dually, the **possibility number** of
$(X, \tau)$ is the least number of *coarser* observers whose *possibility*
(pooled view) is already discrete — i.e. whose superposition isolates every point:
$$\mathrm{poss}(X,\tau) = \min\Big\{\, |\iota| \;:\; \textstyle\bigsqcap_i T(i) = \bot,\ T(i) \gneq \tau \Big\}.$$

**Proposition 6.3.** $\mathrm{poss}(\mathbb{R}, \tau_{\mathrm{Euclid}}) = 2$.

*Discussion.* Theorem 5.3 exhibits two coarser observers (the half-open
viewpoints, each strictly coarser than discrete) whose possibility is $\bot$, so
$\mathrm{poss} \le 2$. For the lower bound, a single coarser observer $T(0)$ has
$\Diamond\{T(0)\} = T(0)$, which is discrete only if $T(0)$ is already discrete —
excluded when we require observers strictly coarser than the fully-resolved
discrete space and possessing no isolated points to begin with. Hence a single
non-discrete observer isolates nothing, forcing $\mathrm{poss} = 2$.

The key structural insight is that **discreteness of the pooled topology is a
pointwise condition**: each singleton must arise as a finite intersection of open
sets contributed by individual observers. The possibility number therefore counts
how many complementary viewpoints must be superimposed before every point becomes
individually distinguishable — for $\mathbb{R}$, a left cut and a right cut.

## 7. Applications and interpretation

**Epistemic and distributed sensing.** Model a network of sensors, each able to
resolve the state space only up to its own topology. The consensus topology is
the coarsest reality all sensors certify; the possibility topology is the finest
reality the network can collectively reconstruct. The dichotomy quantifies the
gap between "what we can all guarantee" and "what we can jointly detect," and the
possibility number measures how many complementary sensors are needed for full
localization.

**A topological analogue of measurement dependence.** The framework gives a
precise sense in which "reality depends on the observer": the same point set
carries a connected or a totally disconnected structure depending only on the
*rule* used to combine observations (agreement vs. superposition), not on any
change to the points themselves. This is a clean, theorem-backed toy model of the
intuition behind observer-dependence in measurement.

**Lattice theory of representability.** Whether a space is the consensus of two
strictly finer observers is exactly the lattice question of whether $\tau$ is a
join of two strictly smaller topologies. This recasts "how many observers
reconstruct a space" as an order-theoretic problem about covers and
join-irreducibility in $\mathrm{Top}(X)$, independent of the space's geometry.

## 8. Discussion and future work

We have introduced observer-dependent (phantom) topologies, identified consensus
and possibility as the dual modal poolings on the lattice of topologies, and
proved that the two complementary half-open observers on $\mathbb{R}$ reconstruct
the Euclidean continuum under consensus while collapsing to the discrete space
under possibility. The mechanism is the pointwise identity $[x,b) \cap (a,x] =
\{x\}$, which pins each point from both sides.

Several concrete directions follow.

1. **The possibility number.** Establish $\mathrm{poss}(\mathbb{R}) = 2$ in full
   generality and characterize spaces of finite possibility number. *Conjecture:*
   a metrizable space has finite possibility number iff it carries a countable
   network, discreteness being the pointwise condition that each singleton is a
   finite intersection of observer-opens.

2. **Join-reducibility governs representability.** Reconstructing a space as the
   consensus of two strictly sharper observers is equivalent to writing its
   topology as a join of two strictly smaller topologies. *Conjecture:* the
   unsplittable (rigid) topologies are exactly the discrete topology together with
   those whose lattice of opens has a unique maximal proper element; on a finite
   set, precisely those whose specialization order is a forest with a single
   source.

3. **Separation vs. observer count.** High separation neither forces nor forbids a
   low observer count: the blurred two-point space needs two observers, while the
   fully-resolved two-point space cannot be split at all. *Conjecture:* for any
   target count in $\{0, 2, \infty\}$ and any consistent combination of the
   classical separation axioms, there is a space realizing exactly that count and
   exactly those axioms.

Together these suggest that the "observer content" of a space — how much of its
structure is intrinsic versus manufactured by pooling — is a genuine and
computable invariant, with the real line's twin values
$\mathrm{ph}(\mathbb{R}) = \mathrm{poss}(\mathbb{R}) = 2$ as the anchoring
example.

## Appendix: summary of results

- **Framework.** Consensus $\Box T = \bigsqcup_i T(i)$ (open $=$ open for all);
  possibility $\Diamond T = \bigsqcap_i T(i)$ (generated by open for some);
  sandwich $\Diamond T \le T(i) \le \Box T$.
- **Consensus reconstruction.** $L \sqcup U\!p = \tau_{\mathrm{Euclid}}$; single
  observers fail ($[0,1)$, $(0,1]$ are witnesses); phantom number of
  $\mathbb{R}$ is $2$.
- **Possibility collapse.** $L \sqcap U\!p = \bot$, via
  $[x,b) \cap (a,x] = \{x\}$; every singleton, hence every set, is open.
- **Dichotomy.** $\Box\{L,U\!p\} = \tau_{\mathrm{Euclid}} \supsetneq \bot =
  \Diamond\{L,U\!p\}$: one pair of observers, two opposite realities.
