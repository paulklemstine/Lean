# Green-Order Incomparability in the Berggren Free Semigroup: Formally Verified Anti-Lattice Structure for Cryptographic Non-Compressibility

## Abstract

We formally verify in Lean 4 a suite of theorems establishing the two-sided divisibility geometry of the Berggren semigroup — the free semigroup of rank 3 generated by the classical Pythagorean-triple matrices A, B, C inside SL(2, ℤ). The central result is a **Green-order incomparability theorem**: for any two words in the semigroup that share neither a suffix overlap nor a prefix overlap, there exists no common left multiple and no common right multiple. As a corollary, we extract explicit LCM-free pairs from every finite ball of radius ≥ 1, proving that the Berggren semigroup exhibits **anti-lattice** behavior. These results go beyond plain freeness (no collisions) to rule out a whole class of "merge attacks" in semigroup-based cryptographic protocols, providing the first formally verified structural security guarantee of this kind.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background

The Berggren tree, discovered by B. Berggren in 1934 and independently by A. Hall in 1970, organizes all primitive Pythagorean triples into a ternary tree rooted at (3, 4, 5). The three branches are generated by the matrices:

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad B = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad C = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$$

acting on pairs (m, n) with m > n > 0 via (m, n) ↦ (am + bn, cm + dn). These matrices lie in SL(2, ℤ), and it has long been known that they generate a **free semigroup** — distinct words in A, B, C yield distinct matrices.

### 1.2 Beyond Freeness

Freeness tells us that the evaluation map `evalBergWord : List BergGen → Mat₂(ℤ)` is injective. But from a cryptographic and algebraic perspective, much more is true. In semigroup theory, the **Green preorders** ≤_L and ≤_R capture divisibility: X ≤_L Y iff Y = ZX for some Z, and X ≤_R Y iff Y = XZ. In a group, all elements are comparable under both preorders. In a free semigroup, the structure is strikingly different:

**Two elements are left-comparable iff one is a suffix-extension of the other, and right-comparable iff one is a prefix-extension.**

This means that "most" pairs of elements are incomparable in both the left and right Green preorders simultaneously. We call such pairs **LCM-free**: they admit neither a common left multiple nor a common right multiple.

### 1.3 Contributions

We prove the following chain of results, all formally verified in Lean 4 with Mathlib:

1. **List Overlap Decomposition** (`list_eq_append_overlap`): A pure combinatorial lemma: if x ++ u = y ++ v, then one of x, y extends the other.

2. **Left/Right Overlap Rigidity** (`berggren_word_left_overlap`, `berggren_word_right_overlap`): Transported through the injective evaluation map, equal products force structural overlap.

3. **Green Incomparability** (`berggren_green_incomparable_of_no_overlap`): The two-sided incomparability theorem.

4. **LCM-Free Pair Extraction** (`exists_lcm_free_pair_in_ball`): Every ball of radius ≥ 1 contains an explicit pair — the generators [A] and [B] — with no common left or right multiple.

## 2. Mathematical Framework

### 2.1 The Free Semigroup Structure

**Definition.** Let `BergGen = {A, B, C}` and `BergWord = List BergGen`. Define `evalBergWord` recursively:
- `evalBergWord [] = I` (identity matrix)
- `evalBergWord (g :: w) = bergMat g * evalBergWord w`

**Theorem (Injectivity).** The map `evalBergWord` is injective. 

*Proof sketch.* We define an auxiliary "pair evaluation" `evalPair : BergWord → ℤ × ℤ` that acts on pairs (m, n), and show:
1. The root pair (2, 1) satisfies 0 < n < m ("valid").
2. Each generator preserves validity.
3. Each generator produces m ≥ 3, so the result is never the root — distinguishing empty from nonempty words.
4. Different generators applied to valid pairs yield different first/second coordinate parities — distinguishing generators.
5. Each generator is injective on pairs — distinguishing suffixes by induction.

The matrix evaluation `evalBergWord` factors through `evalPair` via a bridge function `pairOfMat`, transferring injectivity. □

**Key property.** Evaluation respects concatenation:

`evalBergWord (u ++ v) = evalBergWord u * evalBergWord v`

This is the multiplicative homomorphism property of the free semigroup embedding.

### 2.2 The Overlap Lemma

**Lemma (List Overlap).** For any lists x, y, u, v: if x ++ u = y ++ v, then there exists a list w such that either:
- x = y ++ w and v = w ++ u (y is a prefix of x), or
- y = x ++ w and u = w ++ v (x is a prefix of y).

This is the free-monoid version of the familiar fact that if two intervals overlap, one contains the endpoint of the other.

### 2.3 Overlap Rigidity

By composing the overlap lemma with `evalBergWord_injective` and `evalBergWord_append`, we obtain:

**Theorem (Left Overlap Rigidity).** If `evalBergWord (x ++ u) = evalBergWord (y ++ v)`, then there exists w such that either (u = w ++ v and y = x ++ w) or (v = w ++ u and x = y ++ w).

**Theorem (Right Overlap Rigidity).** If `evalBergWord (u ++ x) = evalBergWord (v ++ y)`, then there exists w such that either (u = v ++ w and y = w ++ x) or (v = u ++ w and x = w ++ y).

These are equivalently stated at the matrix level: if `evalBergWord x * evalBergWord u = evalBergWord y * evalBergWord v`, then the same structural overlap holds, with the connecting word w satisfying the corresponding matrix identity.

### 2.4 Green-Order Incomparability

**Theorem (No Common Left Multiple).** If there is no w with u = w ++ v or v = w ++ u (i.e., neither is a suffix-extension of the other), then there exist no words a, b with `evalBergWord a * evalBergWord u = evalBergWord b * evalBergWord v`.

*Proof.* Suppose such a, b existed. By left overlap rigidity applied to `evalBergWord (a ++ u) = evalBergWord (b ++ v)`, there exists w with u = w ++ v or v = w ++ u. Contradiction. □

**Theorem (No Common Right Multiple).** Analogously, if there is no w with u = v ++ w or v = u ++ w, then there exist no a, b with `evalBergWord u * evalBergWord a = evalBergWord v * evalBergWord b`.

**Corollary (Full Two-Sided Incomparability).** If u ≠ v have neither suffix overlap nor prefix overlap, then they are incomparable in both Green preorders: no common left multiple and no common right multiple exist.

### 2.5 LCM-Free Pair Extraction

**Theorem.** For every R ≥ 1, there exist words u, v of length ≤ R with:
- u ≠ v
- No common left multiple in the Berggren semigroup
- No common right multiple in the Berggren semigroup

*Proof.* Take u = [A] and v = [B]. These are distinct singleton words of length 1 ≤ R. Neither [A] = w ++ [B] nor [B] = w ++ [A] for any w (since |[A]| = |[B]| = 1, w must be empty, forcing A = B, contradiction). Similarly for prefix overlap. The result follows from the incomparability theorem. □

## 3. Formal Verification

### 3.1 Lean 4 Implementation

The entire development is formalized in a single file `BerggrenGreenIncomparability.lean` (~360 lines) using Lean 4.28.0 with Mathlib. The key design decisions:

1. **Self-contained foundation.** All definitions (BergGen, evalBergWord, etc.) and the injectivity proof are included directly, avoiding dependency on external catalog files.

2. **Pair-based injectivity proof.** Rather than working directly with matrices (which involves messy 2×2 arithmetic), injectivity is proved via the pair evaluation `evalPair`, then transferred to matrices.

3. **Clean theorem chain.** Each theorem builds on the previous one with minimal proof:
   - `list_eq_append_overlap` (pure list theory)
   - `berggren_word_left_overlap` (2 lines: apply injectivity, then overlap)
   - `no_common_left_multiple_of_no_suffix_overlap` (2 lines: contrapositive)
   - `berggren_green_incomparable_of_no_overlap` (1 line: pair the two directions)
   - `exists_lcm_free_pair_in_ball` (explicit witness [A], [B])

### 3.2 Axiom Audit

All theorems depend only on the standard Lean/Mathlib axioms:
- `propext` (propositional extensionality)
- `Classical.choice` (classical choice)
- `Quot.sound` (quotient soundness)

No `sorry`, `native_decide`, or custom axioms are used.

## 4. Computational Verification

### 4.1 Exhaustive Search

We independently verified the incomparability results computationally:
- **Injectivity**: All 120 words of length ≤ 4 map to distinct matrices.
- **No common left multiples**: For all 3 pairs of distinct generators, no common left multiple exists with multipliers up to length 4 (14,400+ matrix comparisons).
- **No common right multiples**: Same exhaustive check.

### 4.2 Growth of Anti-Lattice Structure

The fraction of LCM-free pairs in the ball of radius R grows rapidly:

| R | Words | Total pairs | LCM-free pairs | Ratio |
|---|-------|-------------|----------------|-------|
| 1 | 3     | 3           | 3              | 100%  |
| 2 | 12    | 66          | 51             | 77.3% |
| 3 | 39    | 741         | 630            | 85.0% |
| 4 | 120   | 7,140       | 6,582          | 92.2% |

The ratio converges to 100% because in a ternary tree of depth d, two random words of length d share a prefix or suffix of length ≥ 1 with probability roughly O(d · 3^{-d}), which vanishes exponentially.

## 5. Applications

### 5.1 Cryptographic Non-Compressibility

In semigroup-based cryptographic protocols (e.g., matrix-based key exchange, hash chains, or one-way functions built from Berggren generators), a **merge attack** attempts to find a, b such that a · u = b · v for two target elements u, v. Our theorem proves this is impossible when u and v lack structural overlap — a condition that holds for the vast majority of pairs in any reasonably sized key space.

This is strictly stronger than collision resistance (which says u ≠ v ⟹ eval(u) ≠ eval(v)). Collision resistance prevents forgery; Green incomparability prevents **transcript merging**, where an attacker forces two independent protocol runs to converge.

### 5.2 One-Way Semigroup Actions

The Berggren semigroup acts faithfully on Pythagorean triples. The anti-lattice structure means:
- Given a Pythagorean triple T and two paths u, v to descendants T·u and T·v, there is no "shortcut" through a common multiple that collapses both paths.
- This makes the Berggren tree a natural candidate for constructing **verifiable computation** schemes where the computation path is uniquely determined and cannot be compressed.

### 5.3 Semigroup-Based Signatures

In a signature scheme based on the Berggren semigroup:
- The signing key is a word w.
- The verification key is evalBergWord(w).
- A signature on message m is evalBergWord(w · m).

The Green incomparability theorem ensures that two different signing keys cannot produce signatures that are "left-compatible" (sharing a common left multiple), preventing a class of key-substitution attacks.

## 6. Discussion: An Anti-Lattice Inside SL(2, ℤ)

*For a general audience.*

### The Map Analogy

Imagine a map of all possible hiking trails on a mountain, where every trail forks into exactly three sub-trails at each junction. This is the Berggren tree. Each trail is described by a sequence of turns — "left" (A), "middle" (B), or "right" (C) — and no two different sequences lead to the same point on the mountain. That's freeness: the map has no secret shortcuts.

But we proved something much stronger. Take any two distinct trails that don't literally share a starting or ending segment. Then there's no way to extend both trails (by hiking further from their endpoints) so that they ever meet at the same destination. Not just "they haven't met yet" — they **provably never will**, no matter how far you extend them.

This is surprising because in most algebraic structures (groups, lattices, rings), you can always find common multiples. In the integers, any two numbers have a least common multiple. In a group, every pair of elements has infinitely many common multiples. The Berggren semigroup is fundamentally different: it's an **anti-lattice** where common multiples are the exception, not the rule.

### Why This Matters for Security

Modern cryptography often relies on "hard" mathematical problems — factoring large numbers, computing discrete logarithms. But hardness is a quantitative statement: the problem is hard because the best known algorithms are slow.

Anti-lattice structure gives a **qualitative** guarantee. It doesn't say "finding a merge is slow." It says "a merge doesn't exist." This is the difference between a lock that's hard to pick and a wall with no door. For certain classes of attacks (transcript merging, key substitution), the Berggren semigroup provides provable, unconditional security — not dependent on computational assumptions, but on the mathematical structure itself.

### Historical Context

The connection between free semigroups and cryptography has been explored since the 1990s, particularly in the work of Anshel, Anshel, and Goldfeld on non-abelian key exchange. However, most of this work focuses on the *word problem* (deciding equality) and the *conjugacy problem* (deciding conjugacy). Our contribution is orthogonal: we study the *divisibility problem* (deciding if one element divides another) and show that in the Berggren semigroup, most pairs are not just hard to compare — they are provably incomparable.

The formal verification aspect is also novel. While free semigroup properties are "well known," the specific application to the Berggren generators and the machine-checked proof chain from matrix arithmetic to cryptographic security statements appears to be new.

## 7. Future Directions

1. **Quantitative incomparability.** Prove tight asymptotic bounds on the fraction of LCM-free pairs in the ball of radius R. We conjecture it is 1 - O(R · 3^{-R}).

2. **Higher-dimensional Berggren semigroups.** The Berggren construction generalizes to higher dimensions (Pythagorean n-tuples). Do the Green incomparability results extend?

3. **Residual finiteness applications.** The Berggren semigroup is residually finite (every element can be separated from the identity by a finite quotient). Can this be combined with Green incomparability to give stronger separation results?

4. **Concrete cryptographic protocols.** Design and analyze specific key exchange or signature protocols that exploit the anti-lattice structure, with formal security proofs.

5. **Tropical and p-adic extensions.** The Berggren action has natural tropicalizations and p-adic lifts. Do these preserve the anti-lattice property?

## 8. Conclusion

We have formally verified that the Berggren semigroup — the classical free semigroup of rank 3 acting on Pythagorean triples — exhibits strong anti-lattice behavior: most pairs of elements have neither a common left multiple nor a common right multiple. This goes beyond freeness (no collisions) to establish a structural divisibility geometry that rules out an entire class of merge attacks. The proofs are fully machine-checked in Lean 4, depending only on standard axioms, and are complemented by computational verification and visualizations. The results position the Berggren semigroup as a mathematically rigorous foundation for semigroup-based cryptographic constructions with provable, unconditional security guarantees against transcript merging.
