# Computational evidence — higher-dimensional Pythagorean trees

All numbers below were produced by exhaustive enumeration before the Lean formalisation, and
every qualitative statement they suggested is now a machine-checked theorem in
`Catalog/Physics/HigherPythagoreanTrees/`.  The tables themselves are exploratory (they were
produced by a scratch enumeration, not by a Lean proof) and are recorded only as motivation.

## 1. Branching of the quadruple graph (`n = 3`)

Reflection moves are indexed by sign patterns `ε ∈ {±1}³`; the move sends the height `d` to
`2d − ε·x`, so it *descends* iff `ε·x > d`, is *neutral* iff `ε·x = d`, and is a *child*
otherwise.

Enumerating all 135 primitive quadruples `(a,b,c,d)` with `0 ≤ a ≤ b ≤ c`, `d ≤ 60`:

| # descending patterns | # nodes |
|---|---|
| 0 | 1  (only `(0,0,1,1)`) |
| 1 | 48 |
| 2 | 86 |
| ≥ 3 | 0 |

Never more than two descents — matching the catalog bound and our exact count.  Examples with
two descents: `(0,3,4,5)`, `(1,4,8,9)`, `(2,6,9,11)`, `(0,5,12,13)`.

Counting *children* (strictly ascending patterns) for nodes with all coordinates positive gave
only the values **6 and 7**, never 5 or 8.  Formalised as
`HigherPythagoreanBranching.quad_children_card`: the count is `6` iff the weak harmonic defect
`a(b+c) ≤ bc ∨ b(a+c) ≤ ac ∨ c(a+b) ≤ ab` holds, and `7` otherwise.

Seven-children nodes found: `(4,4,7,9)`, `(6,6,7,11)`, `(8,9,12,17)`, `(6,6,17,19)`,
`(8,11,16,21)`, `(9,12,20,25)`, … .  The sub-pattern `(2m, 2m, 2m²−1, 2m²+1)` (`m = 2, 3, …`)
reproduces `(4,4,7,9)`, `(6,6,17,19)`, `(8,8,31,33)`, and is the family formalised in
`quad_children_seven_family`.  The catalog family `(1, 2m, 2m², 2m²+1)` supplies the
six-children case.

## 2. Two-element descent faces in dimension four

For `n = 3` no pair of coordinates can be flipped simultaneously and still descend.  Searching
primitive quintuples `(x₁,x₂,x₃,x₄; d)` with `d ≤ 60` produced many two-element descents, the
first ones with all coordinates positive being

`(1,1,7,7; 10)`, `(1,2,8,10; 13)`, `(1,1,5,13; 14)`, … .

The family `(1,1,t,t; d)` with `d² = 2t² + 2` is Pell: `(t,d) = (1,2), (7,10), (41,58),
(239,338), …`, generated by `(t,d) ↦ (3t+2d, 4t+3d)`.  Formalised in
`HigherPythagoreanPell.dim_four_descent_bound_sharp`, which shows the general bound
`#S ≤ n − 2` is attained for `n = 4`.

## 3. Mirror (neutral) nodes and the divisor law

Neutral moves exist only from dimension three on.  Enumeration of neutral nodes sorted by
smallest coordinate `a` gave

| `a` | neutral nodes `(a, b, c, d)` | count | `τ(a²)` |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `(1,2,2,3)` | 1 | 1 |
| 2 | `(2,3,6,7)`, `(2,4,4,6)`, `(2,6,3,7)` | 3 | 3 |
| 3 | `(3,4,12,13)`, `(3,6,6,9)`, `(3,12,4,13)` | 3 | 3 |
| 4 | 5 nodes | 5 | 5 |
| 6 | 9 nodes | 9 | 9 |

The counts are exactly `τ(a²)`, the number of divisors of `a²` — the observation formalised as
`HigherPythagoreanDivisor.mirror_pairs_card`, via the bijection `a² = p·q ↦ (a, a+p, a+q,
a+p+q)`.

## 4. Growth constants

`ρₙ = (√n+1)/(√n−1)`: `ρ₂ = 3+2√2 ≈ 5.8284`, `ρ₃ = 2+√3 ≈ 3.7321`, `ρ₄ = 3`, `ρ₉ = 2`,
`ρₙ → 1`.  Critical exponents `δ = log(branching)/log ρ`:

| dimension | branching | `ρ` | `δ` |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 3 | 5.8284 | 0.6233 |
| 3 | 6 | 3.7321 | 1.3603 |
| 3 | 7 | 3.7321 | 1.4771 |

The strict separation `δ₂ < 1 < δ₃` is proved in
`HigherPythagoreanGrowth.criticalExponent_three_gt_two`.
