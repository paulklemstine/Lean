# The Möbius Discriminant: A Scalar Invariant Governing Log-Convexity, Turán Inequalities, and Hankel Dynamics of Recurrence Sequences

## Abstract

We study the family of **first-order multiplicative recurrences**
$$
(\alpha n + \beta)\,a(n+1) = (\gamma n + \delta)\,a(n), \qquad n = 0, 1, 2, \dots,
$$
whose step multiplier $(\gamma n + \delta)/(\alpha n + \beta)$ is a Möbius (linear-fractional) function of the index. We isolate a single scalar invariant, the **Möbius discriminant** $\Delta = \gamma\beta - \alpha\delta$, and show that its sign controls the global qualitative behaviour of every positive solution. Concretely, we prove: (i) an exact forward-difference identity expressing the change in the consecutive-ratio sequence as $\Delta$ over a product of denominators; (ii) strict monotonicity of the ratio sequence $n \mapsto a(n+1)/a(n)$ determined by $\operatorname{sign}(\Delta)$; (iii) **all-order Turán inequalities** $a(i+1)a(j) < a(i)a(j+1)$ for all $i < j$ when $\Delta > 0$ (reversed when $\Delta < 0$), i.e. global log-convexity/concavity across arbitrary index gaps; and (iv) the exact telescoping-product closed form $a(n) = a(0)\prod_{k<n}(\gamma k + \delta)/(\alpha k + \beta)$. We then turn to the second-order constant-coefficient recurrence $p\,a(n+2) = q\,a(n+1) + r\,a(n)$ and prove that the pointwise Hankel determinant $D(n) = a(n)a(n+2) - a(n+1)^2$ obeys the exact first-order law $p\,D(n+1) = -r\,D(n)$, with geometric closed form $p^n D(n) = (-r)^n D(0)$. The middle coefficient $q$ cancels, isolating $r/p$ as the curvature multiplier: $r/p > 0$ forces perpetual sign alternation (never eventually one-signed), $r/p < 0$ eventual sign constancy. Cassini's identity for Fibonacci and the resulting sign oscillation emerge as the special case $p = r = 1$. Together these results demonstrate that one scalar per recurrence order compresses the log-behaviour of the entire sequence.

**Keywords:** first-order multiplicative recurrence, Möbius discriminant, log-convexity, Turán inequality, Hankel determinant, Cassini identity, telescoping product, Catalan numbers.

## 1. Introduction

A vast number of sequences arising in combinatorics, special-function theory, and analysis are generated by a rule in which each term is a rational multiple of its predecessor, and the multiplier is a ratio of two affine functions of the index. Factorials, binomial coefficients along a row, Catalan numbers, central binomial coefficients, and general hypergeometric term sequences all satisfy a recurrence of the form
$$
(\alpha n + \beta)\,a(n+1) = (\gamma n + \delta)\,a(n). \tag{1}
$$
The multiplier $m(n) = (\gamma n + \delta)/(\alpha n + \beta)$ is a linear-fractional (Möbius) function of $n$; we accordingly attach to (1) the scalar
$$
\boxed{\;\Delta = \gamma\beta - \alpha\delta\;}
$$
which we call the **Möbius discriminant**. It is precisely the $2\times 2$ determinant
$$
\Delta = \det\begin{pmatrix} \gamma & \delta \\ \alpha & \beta \end{pmatrix},
$$
the cross-difference of the numerator and denominator coefficients. When $\Delta = 0$ the numerator and denominator lines are proportional, $m$ is constant, and (1) generates a geometric sequence; the interesting regime is $\Delta \neq 0$.

The purpose of this paper is to show that $\Delta$ is not a bookkeeping convenience but a **complete qualitative invariant** for the log-behaviour of solutions of (1): its sign alone determines whether the sequence is log-convex or log-concave, uniformly and at every scale. We further show that the natural second-order analogue — the sign dynamics of the Hankel determinant of a constant-coefficient linear recurrence — is governed by an equally simple first-order law in which a single coefficient ratio $r/p$ plays the role of $\Delta$.

Throughout, we work with real sequences and assume positivity where indicated. All denominators appearing in a recurrence are assumed positive on the relevant range, which holds automatically for the classical examples.

## 2. The forward-difference identity

We begin with the local engine of the entire theory.

**Standing hypotheses.** Fix real coefficients $\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \delta$ and a sequence $a : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfying, for all $n$,
$$
a(n) > 0, \qquad \alpha n + \beta > 0, \qquad (\alpha n + \beta)\,a(n+1) = (\gamma n + \delta)\,a(n). \tag{H}
$$

**Lemma 2.1 (Ratio equals Möbius datum).** *Under (H), for every $n$,*
$$
\frac{a(n+1)}{a(n)} = \frac{\gamma n + \delta}{\alpha n + \beta}.
$$

*Proof.* Divide the recurrence by $(\alpha n + \beta)\,a(n) > 0$. $\square$

**Theorem 2.2 (Forward-difference identity).** *Under (H), for every $n$,*
$$
\frac{a(n+2)}{a(n+1)} - \frac{a(n+1)}{a(n)} = \frac{\Delta}{(\alpha n + \beta)\,\big(\alpha(n+1) + \beta\big)}, \qquad \Delta = \gamma\beta - \alpha\delta.
$$

*Proof sketch.* By Lemma 2.1 the left side equals
$$
\frac{\gamma(n+1) + \delta}{\alpha(n+1) + \beta} - \frac{\gamma n + \delta}{\alpha n + \beta}.
$$
Placing over the common denominator $(\alpha n + \beta)(\alpha(n+1)+\beta)$, the numerator is
$$
(\gamma(n+1)+\delta)(\alpha n + \beta) - (\gamma n + \delta)(\alpha(n+1)+\beta).
$$
Expanding, the terms in $n^2$ and $n$ cancel, leaving exactly $\gamma\beta - \alpha\delta = \Delta$. $\square$

The denominator is a product of two positive quantities. Hence the sign of the forward difference is exactly the sign of $\Delta$ — a fact we now promote to global structure.

## 3. Monotone ratios

**Theorem 3.1 (Monotone growth factor).** *Assume (H).*
1. *If $\Delta > 0$, then $n \mapsto a(n+1)/a(n)$ is strictly increasing on all of $\mathbb{N}$.*
2. *If $\Delta < 0$, then $n \mapsto a(n+1)/a(n)$ is strictly decreasing on all of $\mathbb{N}$.*

*Proof sketch.* It suffices to compare consecutive terms of the ratio sequence, since a real sequence that increases (resp. decreases) at every step is strictly monotone. By Theorem 2.2, the step difference $\frac{a(n+2)}{a(n+1)} - \frac{a(n+1)}{a(n)}$ equals $\Delta$ divided by a strictly positive product of denominators. Thus it is strictly positive when $\Delta > 0$ and strictly negative when $\Delta < 0$, giving strict monotonicity in each case. $\square$

The sequence $r(n) = a(n+1)/a(n)$ is the discrete logarithmic derivative of $a$. Its monotonicity is exactly the statement that $\log a$ is (discretely) convex or concave; the next section makes the "all scales" content of this precise.

---

## 4. All-order Turán inequalities

Local log-convexity is the three-term inequality $a(n+1)^2 < a(n)a(n+2)$. We prove the substantially stronger cross-index (all-gap) form.

**Theorem 4.1 (All-order Turán inequalities).** *Assume (H) and let $i < j$ be natural numbers.*
1. *If $\Delta > 0$, then $\;a(i+1)\,a(j) < a(i)\,a(j+1).$*
2. *If $\Delta < 0$, then $\;a(i)\,a(j+1) < a(i+1)\,a(j).$*

*Proof sketch.* Consider case (1). By Theorem 3.1 the ratio sequence $r(\cdot)$ is strictly increasing, so $i < j$ gives $r(i) < r(j)$, i.e.
$$
\frac{a(i+1)}{a(i)} < \frac{a(j+1)}{a(j)}.
$$
Both denominators are positive, so clearing them (multiplying through by $a(i)a(j) > 0$) yields $a(i+1)\,a(j) < a(i)\,a(j+1)$. Case (2) is identical with the inequality reversed, using strict decrease. $\square$

Specializing to $j = i + 1$ recovers the pointwise log-convexity law $a(i+1)^2 < a(i)\,a(i+2)$; the general statement, valid across arbitrary gaps $j - i$, expresses that the sequence is log-convex uniformly at every scale. It is exactly the family of Turán-type determinantal inequalities familiar from orthogonal-polynomial and special-function theory, here obtained for the entire class (1) from the single sign of $\Delta$.

## 5. The telescoping product

**Theorem 5.1 (Closed form).** *Assume (H). For every $n$,*
$$
a(n) = a(0)\,\prod_{k=0}^{n-1} \frac{\gamma k + \delta}{\alpha k + \beta}.
$$

*Proof sketch.* Induction on $n$. The base case $n = 0$ is the empty product $a(0) = a(0)$. For the step, by Lemma 2.1, $a(m+1) = a(m)\cdot \frac{\gamma m + \delta}{\alpha m + \beta}$; substituting the inductive hypothesis for $a(m)$ and appending the new factor gives the product up to $k = m$. $\square$

This makes transparent the logical chain of the first-order theory: the ratios *are* the Möbius data, monotone Möbius numerator $\Delta$ forces monotone ratios (Theorem 3.1), which force log-convex totals (Theorem 4.1). The product also opens the door to asymptotics: when $\alpha, \gamma > 0$, standard estimates for products of linear factors give $a(n) \sim C\,(\gamma/\alpha)^n\, n^{\,\delta/\gamma - \beta/\alpha}$, so the exponential rate is $\gamma/\alpha$ and the polynomial correction is set by the *secondary* Möbius datum $\delta/\gamma - \beta/\alpha$.

**Worked example (Catalan numbers).** The Catalan recurrence is $(n+2)\,C(n+1) = (4n+2)\,C(n)$, so $\alpha = 1, \beta = 2, \gamma = 4, \delta = 2$ and $\Delta = \gamma\beta - \alpha\delta = 8 - 2 = 6 > 0$. Theorem 3.1 predicts strictly increasing growth factors; indeed $C(n+1)/C(n) = 2(2n+1)/(n+2)$ gives $1, 2, 2.5, 2.8, 3, 3.1\overline{6}, \dots \nearrow 4$. Theorem 4.1 gives global log-convexity, and Theorem 5.1 gives $C(n) = \prod_{k<n}(4k+2)/(k+2)$.

## 6. Second order: the Hankel law

We now turn to constant-coefficient second-order recurrences and the natural curvature quantity, the pointwise Hankel determinant.

**Definition 6.1.** For a sequence $a : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{R}$ the **Hankel determinant** is
$$
D(n) = a(n)\,a(n+2) - a(n+1)^2.
$$

A coefficient-only discriminant cannot govern the sign of $D$ for second-order recurrences: Fibonacci has constant coefficients yet $D(n) = (-1)^{n+1}$ takes both signs infinitely often. The following results explain this structurally.

**Theorem 6.2 (Hankel recurrence).** *Let $p, q, r \in \mathbb{R}$ and suppose $a$ satisfies $p\,a(n+2) = q\,a(n+1) + r\,a(n)$ for all $n$. Then for all $n$,*
$$
p\,D(n+1) = -r\,D(n).
$$
*The middle coefficient $q$ does not appear.*

*Proof sketch.* Write out $p\,D(n+1) = p\,a(n+1)a(n+3) - p\,a(n+2)^2$. Substitute $p\,a(n+3) = q\,a(n+2) + r\,a(n+1)$ (the recurrence at index $n+1$) and $p\,a(n+2) = q\,a(n+1) + r\,a(n)$ (at index $n$) into the two terms. The $q$-contributions cancel against each other, and what remains is $-r\big(a(n)a(n+2) - a(n+1)^2\big) = -r\,D(n)$. Concretely this is the linear combination $a(n+1)\cdot[\text{rec}(n+1)] - a(n+2)\cdot[\text{rec}(n)]$. $\square$

**Theorem 6.3 (Geometric closed form).** *Under the hypotheses of Theorem 6.2, for all $n$,*
$$
p^n\,D(n) = (-r)^n\,D(0).
$$

*Proof sketch.* Induction on $n$ using Theorem 6.2: multiply the inductive identity $p^k D(k) = (-r)^k D(0)$ by the ratio implicit in $p\,D(k+1) = -r\,D(k)$; algebraically, $p^{k+1}D(k+1) = p^k\cdot(p\,D(k+1)) = p^k\cdot(-r\,D(k)) = -r\cdot(-r)^k D(0) = (-r)^{k+1}D(0)$. $\square$

Thus $D$ is a geometric sequence with ratio $-r/p$. Two consequences on signs follow immediately.

**Theorem 6.4 (Nonvanishing).** *If $r \neq 0$ and $D(0) \neq 0$, then $D(n) \neq 0$ for all $n$.*

*Proof sketch.* If $D(n) = 0$ then the closed form forces $(-r)^n D(0) = 0$, impossible since $r \neq 0$ and $D(0) \neq 0$. $\square$

**Theorem 6.5 (Sign alternation).** *If $p > 0$, $r > 0$, and $D(0) \neq 0$, then $D(n+1)\,D(n) < 0$ for all $n$; consequently $D$ is strictly positive at infinitely many indices and strictly negative at infinitely many indices, and can never be eventually one-signed.*

*Proof sketch.* By Theorem 6.4, $D(n) \neq 0$. From Theorem 6.2, $D(n+1) = (-r/p)\,D(n)$, so $D(n+1)D(n) = (-r/p)\,D(n)^2$. Here $D(n)^2 > 0$ and $-r/p < 0$, giving a strictly negative product: consecutive determinants have opposite signs. Alternation of sign at every step means that starting from any index $N$ one finds both a positive and a negative value within one step, so neither sign is eventually excluded. $\square$

**Corollary 6.6 (Curvature dichotomy for second order).** *For a constant-coefficient recurrence $p\,a(n+2) = q\,a(n+1) + r\,a(n)$ with $D(0) \neq 0$: if $r/p < 0$ the sign of $D(n)$ is eventually constant; if $r/p > 0$ the sign of $D(n)$ alternates forever.*

This is the structural resolution of the second-order "obstruction": the Hankel determinant always obeys a clean first-order law $p\,D(n+1) = -r\,D(n)$; the only question is the *sign* of the multiplier $-r/p$.

**Corollary 6.7 (Cassini's identity).** *For the Fibonacci sequence ($p = q = r = 1$, $D(0) = -1$), Theorem 6.3 gives $D(n) = (-1)^{n+1}$, i.e.*
$$
F(n)\,F(n+2) - F(n+1)^2 = (-1)^{n+1}.
$$
*By Theorem 6.5 (with $p = r = 1 > 0$) the sign of $D(n)$ alternates forever — recovering the classical oscillation of Cassini's identity as the special case $r/p = 1 > 0$.*

## 7. Algorithms

We record two elementary algorithms used to compute and verify the objects above.

**Algorithm A (Möbius discriminant classifier).** Given coefficients $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \delta)$: compute $\Delta = \gamma\beta - \alpha\delta$; return `log-convex` if $\Delta > 0$, `log-concave` if $\Delta < 0$, `geometric` if $\Delta = 0$. Constant time, $O(1)$. Its correctness is Theorems 3.1 and 4.1: the classification is exact for any positive solution with positive denominators.

**Algorithm B (Hankel sign tracer).** Given $(p, q, r)$ and two initial terms $a(0), a(1)$ (and $a(2)$ from the recurrence): compute $D(0)$, then generate $D(n) = (-r/p)\,D(n-1)$ for $n = 1, \dots, N$ and report the sign pattern. Linear time $O(N)$. Correctness is Theorem 6.2; equivalently one may use the closed form $D(n) = (-r/p)^n D(0)$ in $O(\log n)$ per query by fast exponentiation.

## 8. Applications

- **Special-function inequalities.** Any positive hypergeometric term sequence fits (1); its log-convexity/concavity and full Turán inequalities are read off from $\operatorname{sign}(\Delta)$ without term-by-term analysis.
- **Combinatorial enumeration.** For counting sequences of type (1) (Catalan, central binomial, factorial-type), $\Delta > 0$ certifies log-convexity, which underpins unimodality arguments, ratio-limit results, and generating-function regularity.
- **Asymptotics.** The telescoping product yields the two-term growth law $C\,(\gamma/\alpha)^n n^{\delta/\gamma - \beta/\alpha}$, separating exponential rate from polynomial drift via the primary and secondary Möbius data.
- **Sign dynamics of linear recurrences.** The Hankel law gives an $O(1)$-per-step, closed-form description of the curvature sign of any constant-coefficient second-order sequence, with Cassini oscillation as the canonical $r/p > 0$ instance.

---

## 9. Discussion

The two halves of this paper share a philosophy: the qualitative log-behaviour of a recurrence sequence is controlled by one scalar per order. In first order, that scalar is the Möbius discriminant $\Delta = \gamma\beta - \alpha\delta$, whose sign is equivalent (for positive solutions with positive denominators) to global log-convexity, expressed both as monotone growth factors and as all-order Turán inequalities. In second order, the analogous scalar is the coefficient ratio $r/p$, whose sign determines whether the Hankel determinant is eventually one-signed or perpetually alternating — the middle coefficient $q$ being irrelevant to curvature dynamics. The apparent chaos of Cassini's identity is thereby demystified: it is a geometric sequence with a negative ratio.

A unifying viewpoint is that in both cases a determinantal curvature quantity — the forward difference of ratios in first order, the Hankel determinant in second order — satisfies an exact multiplicative law whose multiplier's sign is the whole story.

## 10. Future work

- **A curvature dichotomy across recurrence order.** Conjecture: for an order-$k$ constant-coefficient recurrence with leading/trailing coefficients $p, r$, the order-$k$ Hankel (Casoratian) determinant is geometric with ratio $(-1)^{k-1} r/p$; its sign is eventually constant iff $(-1)^{k-1} r/p > 0$ and alternating otherwise. The second-order computation already predicts the parity factor $(-1)^{k-1}$.
- **$\Delta$ as a complete invariant.** Conjecture: for positive solutions of (1) with positive denominators, $\Delta > 0$, strict increase of the ratio, and the full Turán family are mutually equivalent — no strictly weaker hypothesis than $\operatorname{sign}(\Delta)$ distinguishes log-convex from log-concave. The forward-difference identity makes the converse a short algebraic step.
- **Sharp asymptotics.** Make precise, with explicit error bounds, the two-term law $a(n) \sim C\,(\gamma/\alpha)^n n^{\delta/\gamma - \beta/\alpha}$ from the telescoping product, identifying $C$ and the subexponential drift governed by the secondary Möbius datum.
