# Computational Evidence

All counts below were computed by brute-force enumeration over `ZMod p` in Lean
(`#eval`), enumerating every monic polynomial by its coefficient vector and testing each
element of the field for being a root. They motivate and cross-check the formal theorems in

* `Catalog/Novelty/StochasticGaloisRoots.lean`
* `Catalog/Novelty/StochasticGaloisDegreeTwo.lean`
* `Catalog/Novelty/StochasticGaloisCyclic.lean`

## 1. Total (polynomial, root) incidences = `q^n` (expected number of roots = 1)

For monic degree-`n` polynomials over `F_q`, summing the number of roots over **all**
`q^n` polynomials gives exactly `q^n`, i.e. the *average* number of roots is exactly `1`
— independent of `n` and `q`.

| `q` | `n` | `∑ (#roots)` | `q^n` |
|----|----|--------------|-------|
| 3  | 2  | 9            | 9     |
| 5  | 3  | 125          | 125   |
| 2  | 4  | 16           | 16    |
| 7  | 2  | 49           | 49    |

This matches the classical fact that a uniformly random permutation in `S_n` has, on
average, exactly one fixed point (roots ↔ linear factors ↔ fixed points of Frobenius).
Formalized as `StochasticGalois.total_root_incidences` (over any finite commutative ring).

## 2. Degree-2 factorization statistics

Monic quadratics `X^2 + bX + c` over `F_p`, enumerated as `(b,c)`:

| `p` | reducible (`∃` root) | `p(p+1)/2` | irreducible | `p(p-1)/2` | double root | `p` |
|----|----------------------|------------|-------------|------------|-------------|-----|
| 3  | 6                    | 6          | 3           | 3          | 3           | 3   |
| 5  | 15                   | 15         | 10          | 10         | 5           | 5   |
| 7  | 28                   | 28         | 21          | 21         | 7           | 7   |

Formalized as `card_reducible` (`2·#red = q(q+1)`), `card_irreducible`
(`2·#irred = q(q-1)`), and `card_double_root` (`#{disc = 0} = q`).

Interpretation (the random-permutation dictionary for `S_2`):
* proportion with a repeated root `= 1/p → 0` — this is exactly the prompt's `n = 2`
  claim `P(discriminant = 0) = 1/p`;
* proportion irreducible `= (p-1)/(2p) → 1/2` = fraction of `2`-cycles in `S_2`;
* proportion reducible `= (p+1)/(2p) → 1/2` = fraction of the identity in `S_2`.

## 3. Counterexample hunt against the stated conjecture

The prompt conjectures `P(Gal(f) ≠ S_n) = c_n/√p + O(1/p)`, i.e. `P(Gal = S_n) → 1`.
The data immediately refute this for `n ≥ 2`:

* **`n = 2`:** the proportion of quadratics with Galois group `S_2` (= irreducible) is
  `(p-1)/(2p) → 1/2`, **not** `→ 1`.
* **`n = 3`:** enumeration shows the number of *irreducible* monic cubics over `F_p` is
  `(p^3 - p)/3` (e.g. `p=2 → 2`, `p=3 → 8`, `p=5 → 40`), so the proportion irreducible is
  `(1 - 1/p^2)/3 → 1/3`, again bounded away from `1`.

More fundamentally, over a finite field the Galois group of any polynomial is **cyclic**
(generated by Frobenius), hence for `n ≥ 3` it is *never* isomorphic to the non-abelian
group `S_n`. So `P(Gal = S_n) = 0` for `n ≥ 3`, and the conjecture is false at the
structural level. This is formalized in `StochasticGaloisCyclic.lean`
(`finiteField_gal_ne_symm`).

What *does* hold is the random-permutation heuristic at the level of **factorization /
cycle-type statistics**: the proportion of degree-`n` polynomials with a given
factorization shape converges to the proportion of permutations of `S_n` with the
corresponding cycle type. The exact incidence identity of §1 is the cleanest universally
provable instance (fixed points), and §2 gives the full `n = 2` picture.

## 4. Sequence notes

The counts `p(p-1)/2` (irreducible monic quadratics) and `(p^3-p)/3` (irreducible monic
cubics) are the `n = 2, 3` cases of Gauss's necklace-counting formula
`I_q(n) = (1/n) ∑_{d | n} μ(d) q^{n/d}` for the number of monic irreducibles of degree `n`
over `F_q`; its leading term `q^n / n` is exactly the `S_n` `n`-cycle proportion `1/n`.
