# A Colorful Carathéodory Theorem for Convex Cones

**Author:** Aristotle
**Date:** 2026-07-13

## Abstract

Carathéodory's theorem and its colorful refinement, due to Bárány, are cornerstones
of combinatorial convexity. They concern the representation of a point as a convex
combination of members of one or several finite sets. In this paper we develop the
analogue for **convex cones**, with the origin as the distinguished target point.
Replacing "convex combination" (nonnegative weights summing to one) by "nontrivial
conical combination" (nonnegative weights, not all zero), we prove three results.
First, a **homogeneity bridge**: for the origin, conical representability and convex
representability are equivalent, because a conical representation can be rescaled by
its strictly positive total weight into a convex one. Second, a sharp
**one-dimensional cone colorful Carathéodory theorem**: given $r \ge 2$ finite color
classes of real numbers, each capturing the origin conically, there exists a
colorful transversal — one element per class — that captures the origin; the
threshold $r \ge 2$ is best possible, as the single class $\{1,-1\}$ demonstrates.
Third, a **conic Carathéodory bound**: in a $d$-dimensional space, capturing the
origin conically requires at most $d+1$ vectors. The unifying phenomenon is
homogeneity: the origin is the unique point at which cone- and hull-membership
coincide unconditionally, and this collapses the conical colorful problem onto the
classical affine one, correcting the naïve color count $d$ to the sharp count
$d+1$.

**Keywords:** convex cone, colorful Carathéodory theorem, conical combination,
homogeneity, positive dependence, transversal, discrete geometry.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background

Carathéodory's theorem (1907) states that if a point $x$ in $\mathbb{R}^d$ lies in
the convex hull of a set $S$, then it lies in the convex hull of at most $d+1$
points of $S$. Bárány's colorful generalization (1982) is more subtle and more
powerful: if $x$ lies in the convex hull of *each* of $d+1$ sets
$S_0, \dots, S_d \subseteq \mathbb{R}^d$ (the "color classes"), then $x$ lies in
the convex hull of a *colorful* selection $\{x_0, \dots, x_d\}$ with $x_i \in S_i$.
This theorem is the engine behind the First Selection Lemma, the Colorful
Tverberg theorem, and numerous results on centerpoints and hull depth.

A parallel and equally fundamental object in convexity is the **convex cone**: the
set of all *nonnegative* combinations of a family of vectors. Cones govern
homogeneous systems of linear inequalities, the theory of Farkas certificates, and
duality in linear and conic programming. It is therefore natural to ask for the
cone-theoretic analogues of Carathéodory's and Bárány's theorems, with the origin
playing the role of the target point $x$ (the origin being the canonical
distinguished point of any cone).

### 1.2 The central notions

Throughout, $V$ is a real vector space, $\iota$ an index type, $p : \iota \to V$ a
family of vectors, and $s$ a finite subset of indices.

**Definition 1 (Nontrivial conical capture).** The family $p$ *captures the origin
conically* on $s$ if there exist weights $w : \iota \to \mathbb{R}$ with

$$w_i \ge 0 \ (\forall i \in s), \qquad \exists\, i \in s : w_i > 0, \qquad
\sum_{i \in s} w_i\, p_i = 0.$$

Equivalently, the origin lies in the convex cone generated by
$\{p_i : i \in s\}$ and is witnessed by at least one strictly positive coefficient.

**Definition 2 (Convex capture).** The family $p$ *captures the origin convexly* on
$s$ if there exist weights $w : \iota \to \mathbb{R}$ with

$$w_i \ge 0 \ (\forall i \in s), \qquad \sum_{i \in s} w_i = 1, \qquad
\sum_{i \in s} w_i\, p_i = 0.$$

The strict-positivity clause in Definition 1 is essential and load-bearing: without
it, the all-zero weight vector would satisfy the conditions vacuously, every family
would "capture" the origin, and the theory would collapse to a triviality. Requiring
some $w_i > 0$ makes conical capture a genuine geometric condition.

### 1.3 Summary of results

- **Theorem A (Homogeneity Bridge, §2).** For every finite $s$ and family $p$,
  conical capture and convex capture of the origin are equivalent.
- **Theorem B (Sign classification in dimension one, §3).** A finite set of reals
  $s$ captures the origin conically (under the identity embedding) if and only if
  $0 \in s$, or $s$ contains both a strictly positive and a strictly negative
  element.
- **Theorem C (Cone Colorful Carathéodory, dimension one, §3).** For $r \ge 2$
  nonempty finite color classes $C_0, \dots, C_{r-1} \subseteq \mathbb{R}$, each
  capturing the origin conically, there is a transversal $t_i \in C_i$ whose image
  captures the origin conically. The threshold $r \ge 2$ is sharp.
- **Theorem D (Conic Carathéodory Bound, §4).** In a $d$-dimensional real vector
  space, if $p$ captures the origin conically on $s$, then it does so on some
  subset $t \subseteq s$ with $|t| \le d+1$.

## 2. The homogeneity bridge

**Theorem A.** *For any finite $s$ and family $p : \iota \to V$, the origin is a
nontrivial conical combination of $\{p_i : i \in s\}$ if and only if it is a convex
combination.*

**Proof.**
($\Rightarrow$) Suppose weights $w_i \ge 0$, not all zero, satisfy
$\sum_{i \in s} w_i p_i = 0$. Let $S = \sum_{i \in s} w_i$. Since all $w_i \ge 0$
and at least one — say $w_{i_0}$ — is strictly positive, we have
$S \ge w_{i_0} > 0$ by comparing the sum against a single term. Define
$w'_i = w_i / S$. Then $w'_i \ge 0$ (quotient of a nonnegative by a positive),
$\sum_{i \in s} w'_i = S/S = 1$, and

$$\sum_{i \in s} w'_i\, p_i = \frac{1}{S}\sum_{i \in s} w_i\, p_i
= \frac{1}{S}\cdot 0 = 0.$$

Hence $p$ captures the origin convexly.

($\Leftarrow$) Suppose $w_i \ge 0$ with $\sum_{i \in s} w_i = 1$ and
$\sum_{i \in s} w_i p_i = 0$. Since the weights sum to $1 \ne 0$, at least one
$w_i$ is nonzero, hence (being nonnegative) strictly positive. Thus the same
weights witness a nontrivial conical capture. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Remark (why the origin).** The forward rescaling $w \mapsto w/S$ moves the balance
point $\sum w_i p_i$ from $0$ to $\frac{1}{S}\cdot 0 = 0$: it stays fixed only
because the target is the origin. For any nonzero target $x$, rescaling weights
would move $x$ to $x/S \ne x$, and the equivalence breaks. Homogeneity of the cone
is precisely what pins conical and convex capture together at the origin, and
nowhere else unconditionally.

## 3. The one-dimensional colorful theorem

We specialize to $V = \mathbb{R}$ with $p = \mathrm{id}$, so a "color class" is a
finite set $s \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ and capture concerns the numbers themselves.

### 3.1 Sign classification

**Theorem B.** *A finite set $s \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ captures the origin conically
if and only if either $0 \in s$, or $s$ contains both a strictly positive and a
strictly negative element.*

**Proof.**
($\Leftarrow$) If $0 \in s$, put weight $1$ on $0$ and $0$ elsewhere: the
combination is nontrivial and equals $0$. If $a \in s$ with $a > 0$ and $b \in s$
with $b < 0$, put weight $-b > 0$ on $a$ and weight $a > 0$ on $b$; then
$(-b)a + a b = 0$, a nontrivial conical capture.

($\Rightarrow$) Suppose $0 \notin s$. We must show $s$ straddles the origin. Assume
weights $w_x \ge 0$, not all zero, with $\sum_{x \in s} w_x x = 0$. If $s$ had no
strictly positive element, then every $x \in s$ satisfies $x < 0$ (as $0 \notin
s$), so each term $w_x x \le 0$, and the term at an index with $w_x > 0$ is
strictly negative; hence the sum is strictly negative, contradicting $= 0$.
Symmetrically, $s$ cannot lack a strictly negative element. Thus $s$ contains both
signs. $\qquad\blacksquare$

### 3.2 The colorful transversal

**Theorem C (Cone Colorful Carathéodory, dimension one).** *Let $r \ge 2$ and let
$C_0, \dots, C_{r-1} \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ be nonempty finite sets, each capturing
the origin conically. Then there is a transversal $t : \{0,\dots,r-1\} \to
\mathbb{R}$ with $t_i \in C_i$ for all $i$, whose image captures the origin
conically.*

**Proof.** We split into two cases based on whether any class contains $0$.

*Case 1: some class $C_{i_0}$ contains $0$.* Define the transversal by $t_{i_0} =
0$ and, for $i \ne i_0$, let $t_i$ be any element of $C_i$ (nonempty by hypothesis).
Then $0$ appears in the image, so by Theorem B (the easy direction) the image
captures the origin conically: put weight $1$ on the coordinate $i_0$ and $0$
elsewhere.

*Case 2: no class contains $0$.* By Theorem B, each $C_i$ then straddles the
origin, so each has a strictly positive element $f_i$ and a strictly negative
element $g_i$. Since $r \ge 2$, index $0$ and index $1$ both exist. Define the
transversal by $t_0 = f_0 > 0$, $t_1 = g_1 < 0$, and for $i \ge 2$ set $t_i$ to be,
say, $g_i$ (any choice is fine). The image now contains a strictly positive value
$a = t_0$ and a strictly negative value $b = t_1$. Put weight $-b > 0$ on coordinate
$0$, weight $a > 0$ on coordinate $1$, and $0$ on all other coordinates. The
weighted sum is $(-b)a + a b = 0$, and the combination is nontrivial. Hence the
transversal captures the origin conically. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Sharpness.** For $r = 1$ the single class $C_0 = \{1, -1\}$ captures the origin
conically ($1\cdot 1 + 1\cdot(-1) = 0$), but any transversal selects a single
nonzero real, which spans only a half-line and cannot capture the origin. Thus the
hypothesis $r \ge 2 = d + 1$ (with $d = 1$) cannot be weakened. This corrects the
naïve conjecture that $d$ colors suffice: at the origin the correct count is $d+1$,
matching the affine colorful theorem.

## 4. The conic Carathéodory bound

We now assume $V$ is finite-dimensional of dimension $d = \dim_{\mathbb{R}} V$. Two
lemmas drive the result.

### 4.1 Affine dependence from overcrowding

**Lemma 1 (Affine dependence).** *If $|s| > d + 1$, there exist coefficients
$u : \iota \to \mathbb{R}$ with*

$$\sum_{i \in s} u_i\, p_i = 0, \qquad \sum_{i \in s} u_i = 0, \qquad
\exists\, i \in s : u_i \ne 0.$$

**Proof sketch.** Lift each vector to $V \times \mathbb{R}$ by $q_i = (p_i, 1)$.
The space $V \times \mathbb{R}$ has dimension $d + 1$, so any $|s| > d+1$ of the
$q_i$ are linearly dependent: there are coefficients $u_i$, not all zero, with
$\sum_{i \in s} u_i q_i = 0$. Reading off the two coordinates gives $\sum u_i p_i =
0$ (first coordinate) and $\sum u_i = 0$ (second coordinate). $\qquad\blacksquare$

### 4.2 One pruning step

**Lemma 2 (Pruning).** *If $p$ captures the origin convexly on $s$ and $|s| > d+1$,
then $p$ captures the origin convexly on some strict subset $t \subsetneq s$ with
$|t| < |s|$.*

**Proof sketch.** Let $w_i \ge 0$, $\sum_{i \in s} w_i = 1$, $\sum_{i \in s} w_i
p_i = 0$ be the convex witness, and let $u$ be the affine dependence from Lemma 1.
For a real parameter $\theta$ consider $w_i(\theta) = w_i + \theta u_i$. Because
$\sum u_i = 0$ and $\sum u_i p_i = 0$, every $w(\theta)$ still sums to one and still
annihilates the family. It remains to keep all coordinates nonnegative while forcing
one to vanish. Since some $u_i \ne 0$ and $\sum u_i = 0$, the coefficient $u$ takes a
strictly negative value somewhere. Choose

$$\theta^\* = \min\Big\{ \tfrac{w_j}{-u_j} : j \in s,\ u_j < 0 \Big\},$$

the largest shift preserving nonnegativity. At $\theta = \theta^\*$ all
$w_i(\theta^\*) \ge 0$, and at the minimizing index $j$ we get $w_j(\theta^\*) = w_j
+ \theta^\* u_j = 0$. Restricting to the support $t = \{i \in s : w_i(\theta^\*) \ne
0\}$ gives a convex capture on a strictly smaller set. $\qquad\blacksquare$

### 4.3 The bound

**Theorem D (Conic Carathéodory Bound).** *If $p$ captures the origin conically on
$s$, then there is a subset $t \subseteq s$ with $|t| \le d + 1$ on which $p$
captures the origin conically.*

**Proof.** By Theorem A, convert conical capture on $s$ to convex capture on $s$.
Apply Lemma 2 repeatedly: as long as the current support has more than $d+1$
elements, prune to a strictly smaller convex capture. Since the support size
strictly decreases and is bounded below, the process terminates at a set $t$ with
$|t| \le d+1$ carrying a convex capture. By Theorem A again, $p$ captures the origin
conically on $t$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

## 5. Algorithms

The proofs are constructive and yield explicit procedures.

**Algorithm 1 (Conical-to-convex normalization).** Given a nontrivial conical
witness $w$, compute $S = \sum_i w_i$ and return $w/S$. Runs in $O(n)$ arithmetic
operations for $n = |s|$.

**Algorithm 2 (One-dimensional colorful transversal).** Given color classes
$C_0, \dots, C_{r-1} \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ each capturing the origin, scan for a
class containing $0$; if found, select $0$ there and arbitrary elements elsewhere.
Otherwise select a positive element from one class, a negative from another, and
arbitrary elements from the rest, then balance the two straddling coordinates with
weights $(-b, a)$. Runs in $O\!\left(\sum_i |C_i|\right)$ time.

**Algorithm 3 (Conic Carathéodory pruning).** Given a convex witness on $s$ with
$|s| > d+1$, solve for an affine dependence via a linear-algebra kernel computation
$O(|s| \cdot d^2)$, compute the ratio-test shift $\theta^\*$ in $O(|s|)$, and drop
the vanishing coordinate. Iterate at most $|s| - (d+1)$ times, for total cost
$O(|s|^2 d^2)$.

## 6. Applications and discussion

**Certificates of infeasibility.** Capturing the origin conically by a family of
vectors $\{a_i\}$ is, by Gordan's theorem (a form of Farkas' lemma), equivalent to
the *infeasibility* of the strict system $a_i^\top x > 0$ for all $i$. Theorem D
says such an infeasibility certificate can always be witnessed by at most $d+1$ of
the constraints — a Helly-flavored compactness statement for homogeneous linear
inequalities.

**Colorful selection.** Theorem C is the origin-anchored, cone-theoretic instance
of the colorful Carathéodory paradigm. In applications where each color is a block
of constraints, a resource, or an agent, the theorem asserts that if each block
individually forces a balance, then a single representative from each block still
forces one — diversity is preserved under equilibrium.

**Homogeneity as the organizing principle.** The recurring theme is that the origin
is the unique point at which conical and convex capture coincide with no side
conditions. This is why the entire classical convex toolbox — Carathéodory,
colorful Carathéodory, and the ratio-test pruning argument — transports to cones
*at the origin* essentially verbatim, with the single correction that the color
count is $d+1$, not $d$.

## 7. Future work

The one-dimensional colorful theorem and the homogeneity bridge isolate exactly the
geometric exchange step needed for the general-dimensional colorful cone theorem:
given $d+1$ color classes in $\mathbb{R}^d$ each capturing the origin, we conjecture
a colorful transversal captures the origin, provable by transporting Bárány's
nearest-point exchange argument through the bridge. Quantitative refinements
(counting the valid transversals), sharpness via positive circuits, and an
interior-capture variant are natural next steps; see the Future Directions
accompanying this work.
