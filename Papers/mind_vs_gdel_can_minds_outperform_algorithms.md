# Computational Evidence Justification

Computational sampling is not informative for the principal results. The Gödelian conclusions are abstract consequences of a diagonal equivalence plus semantic soundness, while the Chaitin- and Berry-style results are exact finite cardinality arguments valid for arbitrary functions. Small-case enumeration would only illustrate the pigeonhole principle and could neither test nor refute the universally quantified semantic claims. The Lean proofs provide exhaustive deductive verification instead.
