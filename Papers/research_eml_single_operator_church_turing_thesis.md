**Aristotle**

## Abstract

We study finite real expressions generated by constants, variables, field operations, exponential, and a total real logarithm. Two candidate two-input primitives are considered:

$$
D(a,b)=e^a-\log b,
\qquad
P(a,b)=e^a\log b.
$$

For the difference primitive $D$, we define a structural compiler that replaces exponential by $D(a,1)$ and logarithm by $1-D(0,b)$. We prove by induction on expression trees that compilation preserves denotation at every real input. Expansion of each $D$ node into its defining exp–log expression yields the converse, so the finite exp–log–field language and the finite $D$-field language represent exactly the same functions. For the product primitive $P$, the identities $P(0,b)=\log b$ and $P(a,e)=e^a$ give a second exact compiler. We also give explicit recursive representations of monomials and coefficient-list polynomials through Horner evaluation. The results are syntactic and exact, not approximation claims. In particular, they do not establish that sine, cosine, or every computable real function has a finite expression in these languages. We discuss algorithmic complexity, numerical considerations, applications to compact nonlinear instruction sets, and precise directions for studying the boundary between exact representation and approximation.

## 1. Introduction

A recurring question in computation is whether a rich language can be reduced to a small instruction set. In real-variable symbolic computation, a natural source language consists of constants and variables together with addition, multiplication, negation, reciprocal, exponential, and logarithm. Exponential and logarithm are conventionally treated as distinct transcendental primitives. The purpose of this paper is to show that, for finite expressions, they can be fused into a single two-input transcendental operation without loss of expressive power.

We analyze two fusions. The first is the difference primitive

$$
D(a,b)=e^a-\log b.
$$

The second is the product primitive

$$
P(a,b)=e^a\log b.
$$

In either case, constants and field operations remain available. The main results are exact compilation theorems. Every finite source expression has a target expression with the same value for every environment. The proof is constructive: it gives recursive algorithms for producing the target trees.

For $D$, the essential identities are

$$
e^a=D(a,1),
\qquad
\log b=1-D(0,b).
$$

For $P$, they are

$$
\log b=P(0,b),
\qquad
e^a=P(a,e).
$$

The argument is elementary but its scope must be stated carefully. It proves equivalence of specified finite grammars. It does not prove a Church–Turing thesis for real computation, nor does it show that all elementary functions in every conventional sense belong to the language. In particular, sine and cosine are not source primitives and no exact finite exp–log representation of them is obtained.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 defines total semantics and the three expression languages. Sections 3 and 4 establish the difference-primitive compiler, its inverse expansion, and expressive equivalence. Section 5 treats the product primitive. Section 6 develops monomials and Horner polynomials. Section 7 describes the algorithms and complexity. Sections 8–10 discuss examples, applications, limitations, and future directions.

## 2. Expression languages and semantics

### 2.1 Total real operations

To give every expression a value on every real input, we use total reciprocal and logarithm. Reciprocal has the field convention

$$
0^{-1}=0.
$$

The total logarithm is specified by

$$
\log 0=0,
\qquad
\log x=\log |x| \quad \text{for }x\ne 0,
$$

where the logarithm on the right is the ordinary logarithm of a positive real number. In particular, all familiar positive-domain identities used below remain valid:

$$
\log 1=0,
\qquad
\log(e^x)=x.
$$

This totalization avoids partial denotations. The compilation arguments themselves depend only on identities at $0$, $1$, and positive exponential values, so they preserve the selected exceptional-input conventions exactly.

An **environment** is a sequence $\rho:\mathbb N\to\mathbb R$ assigning a real value to each variable $x_k$. A finite-arity function may be handled by using only the first $n$ coordinates.

### 2.2 The source language

A **finite exp–log–field expression** is generated recursively by the grammar

$$
E ::= c\mid x_k\mid E+E\mid E\cdot E\mid -E\mid E^{-1}\mid e^E\mid \log E,
$$

where $c\in\mathbb R$ and $k\in\mathbb N$. Its denotation $\llbracket E\rrbracket_\rho$ is defined recursively:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\llbracket c\rrbracket_\rho&=c,
&\llbracket x_k\rrbracket_\rho&=\rho(k),\\
\llbracket A+B\rrbracket_\rho&=\llbracket A\rrbracket_\rho+\llbracket B\rrbracket_\rho,
&\llbracket AB\rrbracket_\rho&=\llbracket A\rrbracket_\rho\llbracket B\rrbracket_\rho,\\
\llbracket-A\rrbracket_\rho&=-\llbracket A\rrbracket_\rho,
&\llbracket A^{-1}\rrbracket_\rho&=\llbracket A\rrbracket_\rho^{-1},\\
\llbracket e^A\rrbracket_\rho&=e^{\llbracket A\rrbracket_\rho},
&\llbracket\log A\rrbracket_\rho&=\log\llbracket A\rrbracket_\rho.
\end{aligned}
$$

A function $f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R$ is **finitely exp–log representable** if some source expression using variables among $x_0,\ldots,x_{n-1}$ evaluates to $f$ for every input.

### 2.3 The difference-primitive language

A **finite difference-primitive expression** is generated by

$$
T ::= c\mid x_k\mid T+T\mid T\cdot T\mid -T\mid T^{-1}\mid D(T,T),
$$

with

$$
D(a,b)=e^a-\log b.
$$

Its semantics is recursive, with the field nodes interpreted as above and

$$
\llbracket D(A,B)\rrbracket_\rho
=e^{\llbracket A\rrbracket_\rho}-\log\llbracket B\rrbracket_\rho.
$$

A function is **finitely difference-primitive representable** when a target expression denotes it on every input.

### 2.4 The product-primitive language

A **finite product-primitive expression** uses the same constants, variables, and field nodes, but its sole transcendental constructor is

$$
P(a,b)=e^a\log b.
$$

Thus

$$
\llbracket P(A,B)\rrbracket_\rho
=e^{\llbracket A\rrbracket_\rho}\log\llbracket B\rrbracket_\rho.
$$

Finite product-primitive representability is defined analogously.

## 3. Compilation to the difference primitive

### 3.1 Compiler definition

Define a translation $C_D$ from source expressions to difference-primitive expressions by structural recursion. Constants and variables are unchanged. The compiler commutes with each field operation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_D(A+B)&=C_D(A)+C_D(B),\\
C_D(AB)&=C_D(A)C_D(B),\\
C_D(-A)&=-C_D(A),\\
C_D(A^{-1})&=C_D(A)^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The transcendental clauses are

$$
C_D(e^A)=D(C_D(A),1)
$$

and

$$
C_D(\log A)=1-D(0,C_D(A)).
$$

These clauses are forced by the elementary identities

$$
D(a,1)=e^a-\log 1=e^a
$$

and

$$
1-D(0,b)=1-(e^0-\log b)=\log b.
$$

### 3.2 Semantic preservation

**Theorem 1 (Exact Difference-Primitive Compilation).** For every finite exp–log–field expression $E$ and every environment $\rho$,

$$
\llbracket C_D(E)\rrbracket_\rho=\llbracket E\rrbracket_\rho.
$$

**Proof sketch.** Proceed by structural induction on $E$. Constants and variables are immediate. For addition, multiplication, negation, and reciprocal, apply the induction hypotheses to the immediate subexpressions and then use congruence of the corresponding real operation. If $E=e^A$, then the induction hypothesis gives equal values for $C_D(A)$ and $A$, while

$$
\llbracket D(C_D(A),1)\rrbracket_\rho
=e^{\llbracket C_D(A)\rrbracket_\rho}-\log 1
=e^{\llbracket A\rrbracket_\rho}.
$$

If $E=\log A$, then

$$
\begin{aligned}
\llbracket 1-D(0,C_D(A))\rrbracket_\rho
&=1-\left(e^0-\log\llbracket C_D(A)\rrbracket_\rho\right)\\
&=\log\llbracket A\rrbracket_\rho.
\end{aligned}
$$

These cases exhaust the grammar. $\square$

**Corollary 2 (Forward Representability).** Every finitely exp–log representable function is finitely difference-primitive representable.

**Proof sketch.** Choose a source expression representing the function and compile it. Theorem 1 gives equality at every input. $\square$

The theorem applies to arbitrary nesting. For example, the source expression

$$
E(x,y)=e^{x+y}+\frac{1}{3-\log y}
$$

compiles to

$$
C_D(E)(x,y)=D(x+y,1)+\frac{1}{3-\bigl(1-D(0,y)\bigr)}.
$$

The equality is exact for every pair $(x,y)$ under the total semantics.

## 4. Expansion and expressive equivalence

### 4.1 Expansion definition

Define an expansion $X_D$ from difference-primitive expressions to source expressions. Again, constants, variables, and field nodes are handled recursively and unchanged in kind. At the transcendental node, set

$$
X_D(D(A,B))=e^{X_D(A)}-\log X_D(B).
$$

### 4.2 Preservation under expansion

**Theorem 3 (Exact Expansion).** For every finite difference-primitive expression $T$ and every environment $\rho$,

$$
\llbracket X_D(T)\rrbracket_\rho=\llbracket T\rrbracket_\rho.
$$

**Proof sketch.** Use structural induction on $T$. All field cases follow from the induction hypotheses. At a $D$ node, the expanded expression is its defining denotation, so

$$
\llbracket e^{X_D(A)}-\log X_D(B)\rrbracket_\rho
=e^{\llbracket A\rrbracket_\rho}-\log\llbracket B\rrbracket_\rho.
$$

This is exactly the target semantics. $\square$

**Theorem 4 (Expressive Equivalence).** For every finite arity $n$ and every function $f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R$, the following are equivalent:

1. $f$ is finitely exp–log representable;
2. $f$ is finitely difference-primitive representable.

**Proof sketch.** The forward implication is Corollary 2. For the reverse implication, choose a difference-primitive expression representing $f$ and expand it using $X_D$. Theorem 3 preserves its value at every input. $\square$

**Corollary 5 (Extensional Retraction).** For every difference-primitive expression $T$ and environment $\rho$,

$$
\llbracket C_D(X_D(T))\rrbracket_\rho=\llbracket T\rrbracket_\rho.
$$

**Proof sketch.** Apply Theorem 1 to $X_D(T)$ and then Theorem 3 to $T$. $\square$

The adjective “extensional” is essential. The tree $C_D(X_D(T))$ need not be syntactically identical to $T$. Compilation can introduce constants and arithmetic nodes. The claim is that no environment can distinguish their denotations.

## 5. Compilation to the product primitive

The product primitive supports a parallel but not identical construction. Define $C_P$ recursively, preserving constants, variables, and all field operations. Its transcendental clauses are

$$
C_P(\log A)=P(0,C_P(A))
$$

and

$$
C_P(e^A)=P(C_P(A),e).
$$

The first clause follows from

$$
P(0,b)=e^0\log b=\log b.
$$

For the second, the constant in the second slot is $e=e^1$, so

$$
P(a,e)=e^a\log(e)=e^a.
$$

**Theorem 6 (Exact Product-Primitive Compilation).** For every finite exp–log–field expression $E$ and every environment $\rho$,

$$
\llbracket C_P(E)\rrbracket_\rho=\llbracket E\rrbracket_\rho.
$$

**Proof sketch.** Induct structurally on $E$. The field cases are homomorphic. In the logarithm case, use $e^0=1$. In the exponential case, use $\log(e)=1$. The induction hypothesis identifies each recursively compiled argument with its source denotation. $\square$

**Corollary 7 (Product-Primitive Representability).** Every finitely exp–log representable function is finitely product-primitive representable.

The result stated here is one-way because the required direction is compilation from the source language. A converse can also be obtained directly by replacing $P(A,B)$ with $e^A\log B$, but that additional expansion is not needed for the principal construction.

## 6. Monomials and coefficient-list polynomials

### 6.1 Monomials

Fix a variable $x_k$. Define monomial expressions recursively by

$$
M_{k,0}=1,
\qquad
M_{k,m+1}=x_kM_{k,m}.
$$

**Lemma 8 (Monomial Evaluation).** For every $k,m\in\mathbb N$ and every environment $\rho$,

$$
\llbracket M_{k,m}\rrbracket_\rho=\rho(k)^m.
$$

**Proof sketch.** Induct on $m$. The base case is $1=\rho(k)^0$. For the successor,

$$
\llbracket M_{k,m+1}\rrbracket_\rho
=\rho(k)\llbracket M_{k,m}\rrbracket_\rho
=\rho(k)\rho(k)^m
=\rho(k)^{m+1}.
$$

$\square$

### 6.2 Horner expressions

Let $A=[a_0,a_1,\ldots,a_m]$ be a finite list of real coefficients in ascending degree order. Define the numerical Horner value recursively by

$$
H(x,[])=0,
$$

and

$$
H(x,a::A)=a+xH(x,A).
$$

Define the corresponding expression tree by

$$
Q_k([])=0,
$$

and

$$
Q_k(a::A)=a+x_kQ_k(A).
$$

**Lemma 9 (Horner Expression Evaluation).** For every coefficient list $A$, variable index $k$, and environment $\rho$,

$$
\llbracket Q_k(A)\rrbracket_\rho=H(\rho(k),A).
$$

**Proof sketch.** Induct on the list. The empty case gives $0$. For $a::A$, apply the induction hypothesis inside $a+\rho(k)(\cdot)$. $\square$

Unfolding the recursion shows that

$$
H(x,[a_0,a_1,\ldots,a_m])
=a_0+x\bigl(a_1+x(\cdots+x a_m)\cdots\bigr)
=\sum_{j=0}^m a_jx^j.
$$

**Theorem 10 (Polynomial Difference-Primitive Representation).** For every finite real coefficient list $A$, the univariate polynomial $x\mapsto H(x,A)$ has an explicit finite difference-primitive representation, namely $C_D(Q_0(A))$.

**Proof sketch.** Lemma 9 identifies the source expression with the Horner polynomial. Theorem 1 shows that compiling the source expression preserves its value. $\square$

**Theorem 11 (Polynomial Product-Primitive Representation).** For every finite real coefficient list $A$, the univariate polynomial $x\mapsto H(x,A)$ has an explicit finite product-primitive representation, namely $C_P(Q_0(A))$.

**Proof sketch.** Combine Lemma 9 with Theorem 6. $\square$

Since a polynomial tree contains no exponential or logarithm node, these particular compiled trees need no transcendental gate. Their importance is constructive: they supply a canonical family of exact source expressions and establish compatibility of the compiler with a standard numerical evaluation scheme.

## 7. Algorithms and complexity

### 7.1 Structural compilation

Both compilers are single-pass tree transformations. A depth-first implementation recursively compiles each child and then emits one target fragment for the current node.

For $C_D$, constants, variables, addition, multiplication, negation, and reciprocal contribute constant overhead. A source exponential node becomes one $D$ node with a constant-$1$ child. A source logarithm node becomes a subtraction, representable as addition plus negation, involving a constant $1$, a constant $0$, and one $D$ node.

Let $|E|$ denote the number of nodes in a source tree, counting every constructor and constant occurrence. If target subtrees are constructed without copying already-built descendants, compilation takes

$$
O(|E|)
$$

time and produces a tree of size

$$
O(|E|).
$$

The recursion stack has depth $O(h)$, where $h$ is the height of the source tree. The product compiler has the same asymptotic bounds; each source transcendental node becomes one $P$ node and one constant child.

No common-subexpression elimination is assumed. If source expressions are represented as directed acyclic graphs and sharing is preserved by memoization, the same translation can operate in time linear in the number of distinct nodes.

### 7.2 Horner evaluation

A list of $m+1$ coefficients is evaluated from the highest-degree tail outward. Horner evaluation uses exactly $m$ multiplications and $m$ additions. Its time complexity is $O(m)$ and its iterative implementation uses $O(1)$ auxiliary storage. The recursively defined expression has $O(m)$ nodes.

### 7.3 Denotational testing

A numerical demonstration can evaluate a source tree and its compiled target tree on a grid of environments and compare the values. Such testing illustrates the theorem and can reveal implementation mistakes, but finite sampling is not itself a proof of equality on all real inputs. Floating-point comparisons also require tolerances and domain-aware handling of overflow.

## 8. Worked examples

### 8.1 A nested difference-primitive compilation

Consider

$$
F(x,y)=e^{x+y}+\frac{1}{3-\log y}.
$$

Apply the compiler recursively. The exponential becomes

$$
D(x+y,1),
$$

and the logarithm becomes

$$
1-D(0,y).
$$

Hence

$$
F_D(x,y)
=D(x+y,1)+\frac{1}{3-\bigl(1-D(0,y)\bigr)}.
$$

Direct simplification gives

$$
D(x+y,1)=e^{x+y}
$$

and

$$
1-D(0,y)=\log y,
$$

so $F_D(x,y)=F(x,y)$ wherever the displayed reciprocal is evaluated, including the totalized zero case.

### 8.2 Product-primitive compilation

For

$$
G(x)=e^{x^2}+\log(1+x^2),
$$

the product compiler yields

$$
G_P(x)=P(x^2,e)+P(0,1+x^2).
$$

Since $P(x^2,e)=e^{x^2}$ and $P(0,1+x^2)=\log(1+x^2)$, the equality is immediate.

### 8.3 A polynomial

Take the coefficient list

$$
[2,-3,0,5].
$$

Its Horner value is

$$
2+x\bigl(-3+x(0+5x)\bigr)=2-3x+5x^3.
$$

The recursive expression and ordinary polynomial therefore agree at every real $x$. It is accepted by both target languages because constants and field operations are shared.

## 9. Applications and interpretation

### 9.1 Compact nonlinear instruction sets

A symbolic or hardware language may wish to expose one transcendental gate type while retaining arithmetic. Theorems 1 and 6 show that either $D$ or $P$ is semantically complete for the finite exp–log source language. A front end may allow separate exponential and logarithm notation, while a back end lowers both to a uniform gate.

### 9.2 Symbolic regression and expression search

Expression-search systems face a combinatorial choice of primitives. Replacing two transcendental constructors by one can simplify the operator vocabulary, though it may increase individual tree sizes. Expressive equivalence means that the search space has not lost any function already expressible in the finite exp–log language. Search efficiency, regularization, and discoverability remain empirical questions.

### 9.3 Neural and differentiable architectures

Repeated instances of a common nonlinear gate can be composed with arithmetic layers. The results identify exact algebraic encodings of exponential and logarithm through a single gate type. They do not by themselves address training, parameter efficiency, gradients near exceptional points, or universal approximation, but they isolate the representational component of such questions.

### 9.4 Exact semantics versus floating-point behavior

The theorems assert equality over the real numbers. Floating-point realizations may differ. For example,

$$
\log b=1-D(0,b)
$$

computes a subtraction of quantities that may be close, and $D(a,1)$ still requires evaluating an exponential internally. Overflow, underflow, cancellation, and logarithmic sensitivity are not removed by syntactic compilation. A practical compiler should combine semantic lowering with range reduction, stability analysis, and possibly algebraic simplification.

## 10. Scope and limitations

The phrase “single operator” means a single *kind* of transcendental node. A target expression can contain arbitrarily many finite occurrences of that node, together with constants and field operations. It does not mean that one application of $D$ or $P$ computes every source expression.

The results concern finite expression definability, not general computability. A computable real function may be given by an algorithm, limit process, differential equation, infinite series, or oracle-based representation without possessing a finite closed form in this grammar.

Sine and cosine are deliberately outside the source language. The complex identity

$$
\sin x=\frac{e^{ix}-e^{-ix}}{2i}
$$

does not furnish a real exp–log expression because it uses complex constants and complex exponential. Consequently, the present work cannot be summarized as a proof that all conventional elementary functions reduce exactly to one real gate.

There is a plausible obstruction to global trigonometric representation: sine has infinitely many isolated real zeros, while one-variable functions definable in tame expansions of the real field are expected to have zero sets consisting of finitely many points and intervals. Establishing the relevant tameness theorem for the exact grammar would require mathematical machinery beyond the structural inductions used here.

Approximation should also be distinguished from equality. On compact intervals, polynomial approximation can provide single-gate expressions arbitrarily close to sine or cosine because polynomials already belong to the target languages. The approximating expression generally depends on the interval and requested tolerance, and no one finite approximant equals sine on all real inputs.

## 11. Future work

Several concrete questions sharpen the frontier.

1. **Trigonometric non-representability.** Prove that no finite real exp–log–field expression denotes sine on all of $\mathbb R$, and similarly for cosine. A possible route is a one-variable tameness theorem: an exp–log term should have a zero set that is a finite union of points and intervals, whereas sine has the infinite discrete zero set $\{k\pi:k\in\mathbb Z\}$.

2. **Restricted-interval non-representability.** Determine whether a finite real exp–log–field expression can agree exactly with sine on a nonempty open interval. Analytic continuation or differential-algebraic invariants may be required, because the global zero-set argument alone does not settle local agreement.

3. **Quantitative approximation.** For a compact interval $[a,b]$ and $\varepsilon>0$, construct difference-primitive expressions uniformly approximating sine and cosine within $\varepsilon$. Bounds relating expression size to $b-a$ and $\varepsilon$ would turn existence into an algorithmic theorem.

4. **Sharp compiler-size bounds.** Assign explicit node costs and derive constants $A,B$ such that target size is at most $A|E|+B$. Determine the optimal leading constant under a fixed syntax and cost model, and study how sharing or simplification changes it.

5. **Computability-thesis boundary.** Under a standard representation of computable real functions that includes sine, decide the stronger claim that every computable real function has an exact finite single-operator expression. A trigonometric non-representability theorem would refute that claim immediately.

6. **Numerical stability.** Develop equivalent lowering rules chosen according to input ranges, and quantify forward and backward error for implementations of the compiled trees.

7. **Multivariate polynomial and rational compilation.** Extend the explicit coefficient-list construction to sparse and dense multivariate formats, preserving sharing and establishing operation-count guarantees.

## 12. Conclusion

Finite expressions generated by real constants, variables, field operations, exponential, and total logarithm admit exact compilation to a language with one transcendental gate. For the difference primitive

$$
D(a,b)=e^a-\log b,
$$

the identities $e^a=D(a,1)$ and $\log b=1-D(0,b)$ yield a semantics-preserving structural compiler. Expansion of $D$ back into exponential and logarithm proves exact expressive equivalence. For the product primitive

$$
P(a,b)=e^a\log b,
$$

the identities $\log b=P(0,b)$ and $e^a=P(a,e)$ yield a second exact compiler. Monomials and Horner polynomials provide explicit constructive examples.

The contribution is therefore a precise instruction-set theorem for a finite exp–log language. Its strength is exactness and generality over all finite expression trees; its boundary is equally precise. It neither supplies exact trigonometric expressions nor proves universality for all computable real functions. Those questions remain a distinct and mathematically substantive frontier.
