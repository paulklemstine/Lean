# Why computational evidence was skipped

The central claim is an order-theoretic structural theorem valid for every finite vertex type and every pair of ordered simple graphs. Its proof is not based on discovering a numerical pattern: the identity homomorphism from an induced subgraph of the smaller graph to the corresponding induced subgraph of the larger graph induces a surjection on connected components. Cardinality monotonicity then gives the result uniformly.

Small graph enumeration would only confirm finitely many instances of this general quotient-map argument and would not materially guide or strengthen the proof. The development instead includes machine-checked consequences and boundary-oriented witness theorems: toughness is upward closed, failure is downward closed, and a violating deletion set for a connected supergraph remains a violation in every subgraph.
