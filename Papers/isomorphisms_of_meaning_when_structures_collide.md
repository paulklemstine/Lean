# Why computational evidence is not the primary test

The principal claims are parametric invariance theorems: they range over arbitrary types of worlds and observations, arbitrary transition relations, arbitrary valuations, and every formula generated by a modal grammar. Small-case enumeration cannot substantively test that level of generality; the decisive evidence is the structural induction, especially the inverse transport required by the modal case.

The semantic-separation result already contains the smallest possible concrete example: one world, one atom, and two distinct Boolean interpretations. There are no smaller nonempty structures to enumerate. Its role is not to suggest a numerical pattern but to mark a definability boundary: observations restricted to the model signature cannot recover an annotation deliberately placed outside that signature.
