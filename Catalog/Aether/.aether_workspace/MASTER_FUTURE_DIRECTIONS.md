## 5. Algorithmic Extraction from Density Proofs

**Statement**: The constructive content of the density proof can be extracted into an explicit approximation algorithm: given f ∈ C(X × Y, ℝ) and ε > 0, compute a max-plus combination of pure tensors within ε of f.

**Approach**: The proof via the lattice Stone–Weierstrass theorem is inherently constructive — it builds approximants via two-point interpolation and finite coverings. Making this extraction explicit requires:
1. Bounding the number of terms in the tropical sum (related to covering numbers of X × Y).

3. Implementing the construction as a certified algorithm in Lean with `#eval` support.

**Significance**: Bridges the gap between existence theorems and practical approximation, enabling verified numerical tropical computation.

## 3. Min-Plus / Max-Plus Duality for Tensor-Generated EML Algebras

**Statement**: The max-plus density theorem dualizes to a min-plus density theorem: the family generated by inf and addition (tropical "min-plus" operations) of pure tensors is also dense in C(X × Y, ℝ).

**Approach**: Use the negation isomorphism max(a, b) = −min(−a, −b) to transport the max-plus result to the min-plus setting. The formal proof is a one-line corollary.

**Extensions**: Prove that the max-plus and min-plus representations are dual in the sense of tropical convexity: every continuous function is both the supremum of max-plus linear functions and the infimum of min-plus linear functions.

## 2. Tropical Low-Rank Complexity and Approximation Bounds

**Definition**: The *tropical rank* of f ∈ C(X × Y, ℝ) relative to families A, B is the minimum n such that f can be uniformly approximated to within ε by max₁≤ᵢ≤ₙ (cᵢ + aᵢ(x) + bᵢ(y)) with aᵢ ∈ A, bᵢ ∈ B.

**Conjectured bound**: For Lipschitz functions on compact metric spaces, the tropical rank grows polynomially in 1/ε, in contrast to the exponential growth typical of classical polynomial approximation in high dimensions.

**Significance**: Would give quantitative tropical Barron-type theorems, bounding the complexity of max-plus neural network approximations.

## 1. Multivariate / n-fold Tensor-Product Extension

**Statement**: For compact Hausdorff spaces X₁, …, Xₙ with point-separating families Aᵢ ⊆ C(Xᵢ, ℝ), the max-plus family generated by all lifted functions from each Aᵢ is dense in C(X₁ × ⋯ × Xₙ, ℝ).

**Approach**: Induction on n. The base case n = 2 is the theorem proved in this file. The inductive step identifies X₁ × ⋯ × Xₙ₊₁ with (X₁ × ⋯ × Xₙ) × Xₙ₊₁ and applies the bivariate result.

**Significance**: Enables compositional multivariate tropical approximation from univariate building blocks, analogous to iterated tensor products in functional analysis.

## 4. Retract-Valued and Manifold-Valued Extension

**Statement**: For compact Hausdorff spaces X, Y and a suitable target space S (e.g., a compact ANR or a tropical module), the tensor-product density theorem extends to C(X × Y, S).
