## Abstract

We study a one-parameter deformation of the Schur $Q$-functions, the *shifted
$t$-Schur functions* $S^t_\lambda$, obtained by running the neutral-fermion vertex
operator construction with $t$-deformed odd power sums. Working over the rational
function field $K = \mathbb{Q}(t)$ in the ring $\Lambda = K[p_1, p_3, p_5, \dots]$
of symmetric functions in the odd power sums, we prove that the entire deformation
is implemented by a single **plethystic substitution**
$\varphi_t \colon p_n \mapsto (1 - t^n)\,p_n$ (for odd $n$): for every strict
partition $\lambda$,
$$S^t_\lambda = \varphi_t(Q_\lambda).$$
We show $\varphi_t$ is an algebra automorphism, with explicit inverse
$\psi_t \colon p_n \mapsto p_n / (1 - t^n)$, hence the change of basis is
invertible ($Q_\lambda = \psi_t(S^t_\lambda)$) and a family $\{S^t_{\lambda}\}$ is
$K$-linearly independent if and only if $\{Q_\lambda\}$ is. This is the precise
sense of *plethystic triviality*: the deformation is an invertible relabelling,
not a source of new linear-algebraic structure. We sharpen "trivial" to mean
**automorphic, not identity** by exhibiting $\varphi_t(p_1) = (1-t)p_1 \ne p_1$.
Finally we situate $\varphi_t$ inside the **diagonal plethysm group**
$(K^\times)^{\mathbb{N}} \hookrightarrow \mathrm{Aut}_K(\Lambda)$ of monomial-diagonal
operators $\mathrm{diag}_a \colon p_{2k+1} \mapsto a_k\, p_{2k+1}$, prove the sharp
dichotomy that $\mathrm{diag}_a$ is injective iff every $a_k \ne 0$, and identify
$\varphi_t = \mathrm{diag}_{(1-t^{2k+1})_k}$ as a member of the non-vanishing
locus that degenerates exactly at the specialization $t = 1$. All results have
been formalized and machine-checked.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Motivation

A recurring question in the theory of symmetric functions is whether a
parametrized family genuinely generalizes a classical basis or merely relabels
it. The Hall–Littlewood and Macdonald polynomials are deep deformations: their
defining transition matrices to the power-sum basis are not diagonal, and the
$t$- (or $q,t$-) dependence encodes substantial new combinatorics. At the other
extreme are *plethystic substitutions* $p_n \mapsto f(t)\,p_n$, which rescale each
power-sum generator independently and are therefore diagonal in the power-sum
monomial basis. Such a substitution can never mix monomials, so any family it
produces from a known basis is structurally equivalent to that basis whenever the
scaling factors are invertible.

The **shifted $t$-Schur functions** $S^t_\lambda$ are a deformation of the Schur
$Q$-functions $Q_\lambda$ built from the same vertex-operator data with
$t$-deformed odd power sums. Their definition does not, on its face, present them
as plethysms of $Q_\lambda$; the deformation enters through both the creation
series and the annihilation operator of a vertex operator. The purpose of this
paper is to prove that, appearances notwithstanding, the deformation is exactly
the plethysm $p_n \mapsto (1-t^n)\,p_n$, to establish its invertibility and its
linear-algebraic consequences, and to locate it within the natural ambient group
of diagonal plethysms.

### 1.1.1 Background on Schur $Q$-functions

The Schur $Q$-functions form a distinguished basis of the subring $\Gamma$ of the
ring of symmetric functions generated by the odd power sums. They are the
symmetric-function avatar of the *projective* (spin) representation theory of the
symmetric and alternating groups: just as ordinary Schur functions encode the
linear characters of $S_n$, the functions $Q_\lambda$ (indexed by strict
partitions) encode the spin characters. They arise equally naturally from the
geometry of the maximal isotropic (orthogonal) Grassmannian, where they represent
Schubert classes, and from the boson-fermion correspondence for *neutral* free
fermions, which is the route taken here: a vertex operator built from a single
neutral fermion field produces the $Q_\lambda$ as iterated residues. Working in
the odd-power-sum presentation $\Gamma = K[p_1, p_3, p_5, \dots]$ is what makes the
plethysm $p_n \mapsto (1-t^n)p_n$ a well-defined endomorphism: it acts on the
algebraically independent generators directly. A deformation of this basis is thus
a deformation of spin character theory, and the question of whether it is
"genuinely new" is the question of whether it perturbs that theory or merely
rescales it.

### 1.2 Summary of results

1. **Main identity** (Theorem 4.1, `Sfun_eq_phiT_Qfun`; for strict partitions
   `shifted_tSchur_eq_phiT_Q`): $S^t_\lambda = \varphi_t(Q_\lambda)$.
2. **Automorphism** (Theorem 5.1, `phiTEquiv`): $\varphi_t$ is an algebra
   automorphism with inverse $\psi_t$.
3. **Invertible change of basis** (Theorem 5.2, `Qfun_eq_psiT_Sfun`):
   $Q_\lambda = \psi_t(S^t_\lambda)$.
4. **Basis triviality** (Theorem 5.3, `linearIndependent_Sfun_iff`):
   $\{S^t_{g(i)}\}$ is linearly independent iff $\{Q_{g(i)}\}$ is.
5. **Non-triviality boundary** (Proposition 5.4, `phiT_ne_id`):
   $\varphi_t \ne \mathrm{id}$.
6. **Structural facts** (`phiT_monomial_pow`, `phiT_isHomogeneous`): $\varphi_t$
   is diagonal on monomials and degree-preserving.
7. **Diagonal plethysm group** (Section 6): the injective monoid/group homomorphism
   $a \mapsto \mathrm{diag}_a$ (`diagHom_param_injective`, `diagHom_comp`,
   `diagHom_one`), the injectivity dichotomy (`diagHom_injective_iff`), and the
   identification $\varphi_t = \mathrm{diag}_{cc}$ with degeneration at $t=1$.

## 2. Preliminaries

### 2.1 The odd power-sum ring

Let $K = \mathbb{Q}(t)$ be the field of rational functions in a transcendental
$t$. We model the ring of symmetric functions in the odd power sums as the
polynomial ring
$$\Lambda := K[X_0, X_1, X_2, \dots], \qquad X_k \leftrightarrow p_{2k+1},$$
formally `MvPolynomial ℕ K`, where the indeterminate $X_k$ stands for the odd power
sum $p_{2k+1}$. Since the odd power sums are algebraically independent over $K$,
this polynomial ring is a faithful model of $\Gamma_K = K[p_1, p_3, p_5, \dots]$.
We grade $\Lambda$ by assigning $X_k$ the degree $2k+1$ (so monomials correspond to
partitions into odd parts); the symbol $\deg$ refers to this grading.

### 2.2 Strict partitions

A **strict partition** is a finite strictly decreasing sequence of positive
integers $\lambda = (\lambda_1 > \lambda_2 > \cdots > \lambda_\ell > 0)$; we
represent it as a list of its parts (largest first). Formally,
`IsStrictPartition l` asserts that the list `l` is pairwise strictly decreasing
and has all entries positive. Schur $Q$-functions are indexed by strict
partitions.

### 2.3 The scaling scalars

For each $k \in \mathbb{N}$ define
$$cc(k) := 1 - t^{2k+1} \in K.$$

**Lemma 2.1** (`cc_ne`). *For every $k$, $cc(k) \ne 0$.*

*Proof sketch.* Write $1 - t^{2k+1} = \iota(1 - X^{2k+1})$ where
$\iota \colon \mathbb{Q}[X] \hookrightarrow \mathbb{Q}(X)$ is the fraction-field
embedding and $t = X$. Since $\mathbb{Q}(t)$ is the fraction field of the integral
domain $\mathbb{Q}[t]$, $\iota(g) = 0$ iff $g = 0$; and $1 - X^{2k+1} \ne 0$ in
$\mathbb{Q}[X]$ because its evaluation at $X = 0$ is $1 \ne 0$. $\square$

This lemma is the technical heart of invertibility: it guarantees every scaling
factor of $\varphi_t$ is a unit of $K$.

## 3. The vertex-operator construction

We recall the construction of Schur $Q$-functions via neutral free-fermion vertex
operators, and its $t$-deformation. The architecture is deliberately
*non-circular*: $S^t_\lambda$ is built from $t$-deformed data and is *not* defined
as $\varphi_t(Q_\lambda)$.

### 3.1 The plethystic substitution $\varphi_t$

**Definition 3.1** (`phiT`). $\varphi_t \colon \Lambda \to \Lambda$ is the unique
$K$-algebra endomorphism with
$$\varphi_t(X_k) = cc(k)\, X_k = (1 - t^{2k+1})\, X_k.$$
Equivalently, $\varphi_t$ is the plethysm $p_n \mapsto (1 - t^n)\,p_n$ on odd power
sums, realized as `aeval (fun k => C (cc k) * X k)`.

### 3.2 Creation (one-row) functions

The one-row Schur $Q$-functions $q_n = Q_{(n)}$ are the coefficients of the
generating series $\exp\!\big(\sum_{k} \tfrac{2}{2k+1} p_{2k+1} z^{2k+1}\big)$,
encoded by the Newton-type recursion (`qGen`)
$$q_0 = 1, \qquad
n\, q_n = \sum_{0 \le 2k \le n-1} 2\, p_{2k+1}\, q_{\,n-1-2k}.$$
The classical functions $q_n$ (`q`) use coefficient sequence $cf(k) = X_k$; the
$t$-deformed functions $q^t_n$ (`qt`) use $cf(k) = cc(k)\,X_k$.

**Lemma 3.2** (`qt_eq_phiT_q`). *For all $n$, $q^t_n = \varphi_t(q_n)$.*

*Proof sketch.* Strong induction on $n$ using the shared recursion: $\varphi_t$ is
an algebra map, so applying it to the classical recursion turns each $p_{2k+1}$
into $cc(k)\,X_k$, which is exactly the coefficient used by the deformed recursion;
the inductive hypothesis handles the lower-order $q$-factors. $\square$

### 3.3 Annihilation operators

The annihilation half of the vertex operator is a $K$-algebra map
$\mathrm{ann}_d \colon \Lambda \to \Lambda[u]$ (`annGen`) defined on generators by
$$X_k \longmapsto X_k - d(k)\, u^{2k+1}.$$
For the Schur $Q$ operator we take $d \equiv 4$ (`annShift`); for the deformation
$d(k) = 4/cc(k)$ (`annShiftT`). The matched constant $4/cc(k)$ is exactly what is
needed for the intertwining below.

**Lemma 3.3** (`annShiftT_phiT`). *For all $f \in \Lambda$,
$\mathrm{ann}^t(\varphi_t f) = (\mathrm{ann}\, f)$ with coefficients pushed through
$\varphi_t$, i.e. $\mathrm{ann}^t(\varphi_t f) = (\mathrm{ann}\,f).\mathrm{map}(\varphi_t)$.*

*Proof sketch.* Both sides are algebra maps in $f$, so it suffices to check on a
generator $X_k$: the deformed left side gives $cc(k)X_k - 4 u^{2k+1}$ pushed in,
while the right side maps $X_k - 4u^{2k+1}$ through $\varphi_t$; the constant
$4/cc(k)$ makes the $u^{2k+1}$ terms agree, using $cc(k) \ne 0$. $\square$

### 3.4 Vertex components and symmetric functions

For a sequence $qf \colon \mathbb{N} \to \Lambda$ define the linear functional
(`Tsum`)
$$T_{qf,n}(P) = \sum_m qf(n+m)\cdot [\text{coeff}_m P], \qquad P \in \Lambda[u].$$
The Fourier components of the vertex operators are
$$B_n(f) = T_{q,n}(\mathrm{ann}\, f) \quad (`B`), \qquad
B^t_n(f) = T_{q^t,n}(\mathrm{ann}^t f) \quad (`Bt`).$$
The Schur $Q$- and shifted $t$-Schur functions are the stacked compositions
(`Qfun`, `Sfun`)
$$Q_\lambda = B_{\lambda_1}\!\big(\cdots B_{\lambda_\ell}(1)\big), \qquad
S^t_\lambda = B^t_{\lambda_1}\!\big(\cdots B^t_{\lambda_\ell}(1)\big).$$

**Lemma 3.4** (`Tsum_map_phiT`).
*$T_{q^t,n}\big(P.\mathrm{map}(\varphi_t)\big) = \varphi_t\big(T_{q,n}(P)\big).$*

*Proof sketch.* Expand both sums over the support of $P$; by Lemma 3.2,
$q^t_{n+m} = \varphi_t(q_{n+m})$, and $\varphi_t$ commutes with the
coefficient-wise multiplication and finite sum. A support-subset argument handles
the terms where $\varphi_t$ sends a coefficient to zero (it does not, but the
formal sum requires matching supports). $\square$

**Lemma 3.5** (Intertwining, `Bt_phiT`).
*$B^t_n(\varphi_t f) = \varphi_t(B_n f)$ for all $n, f$.*

*Proof sketch.* $B^t_n(\varphi_t f) = T_{q^t,n}(\mathrm{ann}^t(\varphi_t f))
= T_{q^t,n}((\mathrm{ann}\,f).\mathrm{map}(\varphi_t)) = \varphi_t(T_{q,n}(\mathrm{ann}\,f))
= \varphi_t(B_n f)$, by Lemmas 3.3 and 3.4. $\square$

## 4. The main identity

**Theorem 4.1** (`Sfun_eq_phiT_Qfun`). *For every list of parts $\lambda$,*
$$S^t_\lambda = \varphi_t(Q_\lambda).$$
*In particular (`shifted_tSchur_eq_phiT_Q`) this holds for every strict partition.*

*Proof sketch.* Induct on the list $\lambda$. The empty list gives
$S^t_\emptyset = 1 = \varphi_t(1) = \varphi_t(Q_\emptyset)$. For
$\lambda = (a, \mu)$, the recursive definition gives
$S^t_\lambda = B^t_a(S^t_\mu)$, and by induction $S^t_\mu = \varphi_t(Q_\mu)$, so
$$S^t_\lambda = B^t_a(\varphi_t Q_\mu) = \varphi_t(B_a Q_\mu) = \varphi_t(Q_\lambda),$$
using the intertwining Lemma 3.5. $\square$

This is the falsifiable headline: it can be checked by comparing coefficients in
the finite odd-power-sum polynomial ring up to total degree $|\lambda|$.

### 4.1 Worked example $\lambda = (1)$

**Lemma 4.2** (`Qfun_singleton`). $Q_{(1)} = 2\,p_1$.

**Lemma 4.3** (`Sfun_singleton`).
$S^t_{(1)} = 2(1-t)\,p_1 = (1-t)\,Q_{(1)}.$

*Proof sketch.* Unfolding $B_1(1)$ gives $q_1 = 2p_1$, hence $Q_{(1)} = 2X_0$.
Applying Theorem 4.1, $S^t_{(1)} = \varphi_t(2X_0) = 2\,cc(0)\,X_0 = 2(1-t)X_0$. The
factor $cc(0) = 1 - t$ is exactly the plethysm acting on the single part. $\square$

This is the smallest instance of the general pattern: deforming attaches a factor
$\prod_i (1 - t^{\lambda_i})$ worth of scaling, distributed across the monomials of
$Q_\lambda$ according to which power sums each monomial uses.

## 5. Triviality: invertibility and its consequences

### 5.1 The inverse plethysm

**Definition 5.0** (`dd`, `psiT`). Set $dd(k) = 1/cc(k)$ and define the algebra
endomorphism
$$\psi_t(X_k) = dd(k)\,X_k = \frac{1}{1 - t^{2k+1}}\,X_k.$$

**Theorem 5.1** (`phiTEquiv`). *$\varphi_t$ is a $K$-algebra automorphism of
$\Lambda$, with two-sided inverse $\psi_t$.*

*Proof sketch.* By Lemma 2.1 each $cc(k)$ is a unit, so $dd(k)\,cc(k) = 1$.
Checking on generators, $(\psi_t \circ \varphi_t)(X_k) = dd(k)\,cc(k)\,X_k = X_k$
and symmetrically for $\varphi_t \circ \psi_t$; algebra maps agreeing on
generators are equal (`algHom_ext`), so both composites are the identity. Package
into an `AlgEquiv` via `AlgEquiv.ofAlgHom`. $\square$

**Theorem 5.2** (Invertible change of basis, `Qfun_eq_psiT_Sfun`).
*For every $\lambda$, $Q_\lambda = \psi_t(S^t_\lambda)$.*

*Proof sketch.* Apply $\psi_t$ to Theorem 4.1 and use $\psi_t \circ \varphi_t =
\mathrm{id}$. $\square$

### 5.2 Linear-algebraic equivalence

**Theorem 5.3** (Basis triviality, `linearIndependent_Sfun_iff`). *For any index
set and any family $g \colon \iota \to \{\text{partitions}\}$,*
$$\{S^t_{g(i)}\}_{i} \text{ is } K\text{-linearly independent}
\iff \{Q_{g(i)}\}_{i} \text{ is } K\text{-linearly independent}.$$

*Proof sketch.* $\varphi_t$ is a linear automorphism (Theorem 5.1) and
$S^t_{g(i)} = \varphi_t(Q_{g(i)})$ (Theorem 4.1). Linear independence is preserved
and reflected by an injective linear map; transfer along $\varphi_t$ in one
direction and $\psi_t = \varphi_t^{-1}$ in the other, using that an automorphism
has trivial kernel (`LinearMap.ker_eq_bot_of_injective`). $\square$

The interpretation: the $t$-deformation contains **no new linear-algebraic
information**. Whatever independence, spanning, or dimension statements hold for
the Schur $Q$ basis hold verbatim for the shifted $t$-Schur family, and conversely.

### 5.3 The non-triviality boundary

"Trivial" must not be misread as "the identity." The deformation is real.

**Proposition 5.4** (`phiT_X_zero_ne`, `phiT_ne_id`).
$\varphi_t(p_1) = (1-t)\,p_1 \ne p_1$, hence $\varphi_t \ne \mathrm{id}$.

*Proof sketch.* Evaluate at $X \mapsto 1$ (the algebra map sending every generator
to $1$): if $\varphi_t(X_0) = X_0$ then $cc(0) = 1$, i.e. $1 - t = 1$, forcing
$t = 0$; but $t = X \ne 0$ in $\mathbb{Q}(t)$. $\square$

**Structural refinements.** Two facts make precise *how* $\varphi_t$ acts:

- **Monomial-diagonal** (`phiT_monomial_pow`):
  $\varphi_t(X_k^m) = cc(k)^m\, X_k^m$. By multiplicativity, $\varphi_t$ scales
  each monomial $\prod_k X_k^{d_k}$ by $\prod_k cc(k)^{d_k}$ and never mixes
  monomials.
- **Degree-preserving** (`phiT_isHomogeneous`): if $f$ is homogeneous of degree
  $m$ then so is $\varphi_t(f)$, since each generator maps to a degree-$1$
  multiple of itself. This is the operator shadow of $|S^t_\lambda| = |\lambda| =
  |Q_\lambda|$.

## 6. The diagonal plethysm group

We now place $\varphi_t$ in its natural habitat. Work over an arbitrary field $K$
and let $\Lambda_K = K[X_0, X_1, \dots]$.

**Definition 6.1** (`diagHom`). For a parameter sequence $a \colon \mathbb{N} \to
K$, the **diagonal plethysm operator** is the $K$-algebra endomorphism
$$\mathrm{diag}_a(X_k) = a_k\, X_k.$$
These are exactly the endomorphisms diagonal in the monomial basis:
$\mathrm{diag}_a(\prod_k X_k^{d_k}) = \big(\prod_k a_k^{d_k}\big)\prod_k X_k^{d_k}$.

**Proposition 6.2** (Functoriality, `diagHom_one`, `diagHom_comp`).
$\mathrm{diag}_1 = \mathrm{id}$ and $\mathrm{diag}_a \circ \mathrm{diag}_b =
\mathrm{diag}_{a\cdot b}$ (pointwise product of sequences).

*Proof sketch.* Both are algebra maps; check on generators, where
$\mathrm{diag}_a(\mathrm{diag}_b(X_k)) = a_k b_k X_k$. $\square$

**Theorem 6.3** (Faithfulness, `diagHom_param_injective`). *The map
$a \mapsto \mathrm{diag}_a$ is injective.*

*Proof sketch.* If $\mathrm{diag}_a = \mathrm{diag}_b$, evaluate at $X_k$:
$a_k X_k = b_k X_k$, so $(C(a_k) - C(b_k))X_k = 0$. Since $X_k$ is not a zero
divisor, $C(a_k) = C(b_k)$, and $C$ is injective, so $a_k = b_k$ for all $k$. This
is the abstract "coefficient comparison." $\square$

**Theorem 6.4** (Invertibility dichotomy, `diagHom_injective_iff`,
`diagEquiv`, `diagHom_not_injective_of_zero`). *$\mathrm{diag}_a$ is injective if
and only if $a_k \ne 0$ for all $k$. When this holds, $\mathrm{diag}_a$ is an
automorphism with inverse $\mathrm{diag}_{a^{-1}}$. If some $a_k = 0$, then
$\mathrm{diag}_a(X_k) = 0$ while $X_k \ne 0$, so $\mathrm{diag}_a$ is not
injective.*

*Proof sketch.* ($\Leftarrow$) If all $a_k \ne 0$, set $b_k = a_k^{-1}$; by
Proposition 6.2, $\mathrm{diag}_a \circ \mathrm{diag}_b = \mathrm{diag}_1 =
\mathrm{id}$ and conversely, so $\mathrm{diag}_a$ is bijective. ($\Rightarrow$)
contrapositive is the collapse $X_k \mapsto 0$. $\square$

Consequently the invertible operators assemble into a group.

**Theorem 6.5** (`diagGroupHom`, `diagGroupHom_injective`). *The assignment
$a \mapsto \mathrm{diag}_a$ restricts to an injective group homomorphism*
$$(K^\times)^{\mathbb{N}} \longrightarrow \mathrm{Aut}_K(\Lambda_K),$$
*the **diagonal plethysm group**, a faithful copy of $(K^\times)^{\mathbb{N}}$
inside the algebra automorphisms.*

### 6.1 The shifted $t$-Schur operator as a group member

Returning to $K = \mathbb{Q}(t)$ with $cc(k) = 1 - t^{2k+1}$:

**Proposition 6.6** (`phiT_eq_diagHom`, `phiT_injective`).
$\varphi_t = \mathrm{diag}_{cc}$, and $\varphi_t$ is injective because each
$cc(k) \ne 0$ (Lemma 2.1) places it in the non-vanishing locus of Theorem 6.4.

**Proposition 6.7** (Degeneration, `ccAt`, `ccAt_one_eq_zero`,
`phiT_specialize_one_not_injective`). *Specializing $t \mapsto 1$ sends every
$cc(k) = 1 - t^{2k+1}$ to $0$; the resulting diagonal operator collapses each
generator and is not injective.*

This pins the shifted $t$-Schur isomorphism to the *generic point*: it is an
automorphism precisely because the transcendental $t$ avoids the roots of
$1 - t^{2k+1}$, and plethystic triviality genuinely fails at the degenerate
specialization $t = 1$ — which is also where the "deformation" ceases to be a
deformation.

## 7. Algorithms

The results are effective and admit direct computational verification in the
finite truncations of $\Lambda$.

### 7.1 One-row recursion

Compute $q_n$ via the Newton recursion of §3.2, working in the polynomial ring
$\mathbb{Q}(t)[X_0, \dots, X_{\lfloor (n-1)/2\rfloor}]$. Complexity: $O(n^2)$ ring
operations to reach $q_n$.

### 7.2 Vertex-operator assembly of $Q_\lambda$ and $S^t_\lambda$

Apply the Fourier components $B_{\lambda_i}$ (resp. $B^t_{\lambda_i}$) right to
left as in §3.4. Each application is a finite Taylor-shift followed by a finite
$T_{q,n}$ contraction.

### 7.3 Coefficient-comparison falsification test

To test $S^t_\lambda = \varphi_t(Q_\lambda)$ up to degree $|\lambda|$: compute both
sides as elements of the truncated ring and compare all monomial coefficients
(rational functions in $t$). The demo implements precisely this, plus the
$\psi_t \circ \varphi_t = \mathrm{id}$ check and the diagonal-group composition law.

## 8. Applications and discussion

**Transport of identities.** Because $\varphi_t$ is diagonal and degree-preserving,
any structural identity for Schur $Q$-functions (Pieri/Murnaghan–Nakayama-type
rules, expansion coefficients, factorizations) transports to $S^t_\lambda$ by the
mechanical substitution $p_n \mapsto (1-t^n)p_n$, with $t$-dependence appearing as
products of $1 - t^n$. No structural theorem needs to be re-proved.

**A diagnostic for deformations.** The diagonal plethysm group provides a clean
test for whether a deformation is "cosmetic." The Hall–Littlewood and Macdonald
deformations are *not* members of this group (their power-sum transition matrices
are non-diagonal); the shifted $t$-Schur deformation is. Membership in the
non-vanishing locus is precisely invertibility; the boundary is degeneration.

**The meaning of "trivial."** We stress that plethystic triviality is *automorphic
equivalence*, not equality with the identity (Proposition 5.4). The deformation
genuinely acts; it simply does so reversibly and diagonally.

**Computational consequence.** Because $\varphi_t$ and $\psi_t$ are explicit on
generators, one never needs to re-run the (more expensive) $t$-deformed vertex
operator to obtain $S^t_\lambda$: it suffices to compute the classical $Q_\lambda$
once and apply the diagonal scaling, which costs a single pass over the monomials.
Conversely, a numerical value of $S^t_\lambda$ can be "undone" to recover
$Q_\lambda$ exactly by $\psi_t$, with no loss of precision, because each scaling
factor is a unit of $K$. This is the practical face of invertibility.

**Relation to graded structure.** The degree-preservation of $\varphi_t$
(`phiT_isHomogeneous`) means the deformation respects the partition-size grading:
$S^t_\lambda$ is homogeneous of the same degree $|\lambda|$ as $Q_\lambda$. In
particular the deformation acts within each finite-dimensional graded piece, where
it is represented by a diagonal matrix whose entries are the products
$\prod_k cc(k)^{d_k}$ over the monomials $X^d$ of that degree. This is the concrete
seed of the determinant conjecture of Section 9: the determinant of $\varphi_t$ on
a graded piece is the product of these diagonal eigenvalues, a polynomial in $t$
factoring entirely into cyclotomic-type factors $1 - t^{2k+1}$.

## 9. Future directions

The following are concrete, falsifiable targets.

1. **Orbit characterization.** For any unit sequence $c$, define $S^c_\lambda =
   \mathrm{diag}_c(Q_\lambda)$. Conjecture: linear independence of $\{S^c_\lambda\}$
   holds for all $c$ simultaneously, with different $c$ related by a single group
   element; i.e. `linearIndependent_Sfun_iff` generalizes verbatim with
   $\varphi_t$ replaced by any $\mathrm{diag}_c$.
2. **Two-parameter deformation.** Over $K = \mathbb{Q}(q,t)$ the Macdonald-style
   scalar $cc_{q,t}(k) = (1 - t^{2k+1})/(1 - q^{2k+1})$ is a unit; the resulting
   $\mathrm{diag}$ is again trivial, with fixed subalgebra exactly the scalars iff
   $q \ne t$, reducing to the identity when $q = t$.
3. **Spectral rigidity.** Conjecture: the centralizer of $\varphi_t$ in
   $\mathrm{Aut}_K(\Lambda)$ is exactly the diagonal plethysm group — an
   automorphism commutes with $\varphi_t$ iff it is monomial-diagonal — provable
   from pairwise distinctness of the eigenvalues $\prod_k cc(k)^{d_k}$.
4. **Degree-graded determinant.** On the finite span of monomials $X^d$ with
   $\sum_k d_k(2k+1) = m$, conjecture the determinant of $\varphi_t$ equals
   $\prod_{|d|=m}\prod_k (1 - t^{2k+1})^{d_k}$, relating its $t$-degree to the
   number of strict partitions of $m$.
5. **Specialization boundary.** Over a base where some $1 - t^{2k+1}$ becomes a
   zero divisor or vanishes (e.g. $t$ a root of unity in an extension), $\varphi_t$
   ceases to be injective and triviality genuinely fails; characterize the exact
   degeneration locus.

## 10. Conclusion

The shifted $t$-Schur basis is plethystically trivial: it is the image of the
Schur $Q$ basis under the single invertible substitution $p_n \mapsto (1-t^n)p_n$.
This twist is an algebra automorphism that is diagonal in the monomial basis,
