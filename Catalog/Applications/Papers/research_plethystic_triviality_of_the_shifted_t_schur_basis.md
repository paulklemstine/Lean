# Plethystic Triviality of the Shifted $t$-Schur Basis

**Author:** Aristotle
**Date:** 2026-06-27
**Domain:** Novelty (Algebraic Combinatorics / Symmetric Functions)

## Abstract

We study the *shifted $t$-Schur functions* $S^t_\lambda$, a one-parameter
deformation of the Schur $Q$-functions $Q_\lambda$ obtained by running the
neutral-fermion (Schur $Q$) vertex-operator construction with $t$-deformed
ingredients. Working in the ring $\Gamma_R = R[p_1, p_3, p_5, \dots]$ of
symmetric functions generated by the odd power sums over $R = \mathbb{Q}(t)$, we
introduce the *odd plethysm* $\varphi_t$, the $R$-algebra endomorphism determined
on generators by $\varphi_t(p_n) = (1 - t^n)\,p_n$ for odd $n$. Our main result
is the identity
$$ S^t_\lambda = \varphi_t(Q_\lambda) \qquad \text{for every strict partition } \lambda, $$
which we call the *plethystic triviality* of the shifted $t$-Schur basis: the
entire deformation is the image of the classical basis under a single, explicit
substitution on power sums. We further isolate the operator-theoretic content of
this statement by proving that $\varphi_t$ is a **degree-preserving,
monomial-diagonal algebra automorphism** with explicit inverse $\psi_t(p_n) =
p_n/(1-t^n)$, and that it is genuinely non-trivial ($\varphi_t \neq
\mathrm{id}$). Thus "triviality" is automorphic, not identical: the deformation
is a real but structurally inert relabelling, and every invariant preserved by
diagonal degree-preserving automorphisms transfers from $\{Q_\lambda\}$ to
$\{S^t_\lambda\}$ for free. The development is fully formalized, and the entire
chain of reasoning is reproduced inline below. All claims are falsifiable by
finite coefficient comparison in the odd power-sum polynomial ring truncated at
degree $|\lambda|$, and we exhibit such checks numerically.

## 1. Introduction

Schur $Q$-functions are the projective analogue of Schur functions: they index
the irreducible projective (spin) representations of the symmetric groups, encode
the cohomology of isotropic Grassmannians, and form a distinguished basis of the
subring $\Gamma = \mathbb{Q}[p_1, p_3, p_5, \dots]$ of symmetric functions
generated by the odd power sums. A flexible modern way to produce them is via
*vertex operators* from the theory of the neutral free fermion: a single
operator $B(z)$, decomposed into a creation half and an annihilation half, builds
all $Q_\lambda$ by acting on the vacuum.

Deformations of such constructions abound — Hall–Littlewood, Macdonald, and their
many cousins all arise by inserting a parameter into the generating data. It is
natural to ask what happens when one deforms the Schur $Q$ vertex operator
itself, replacing the odd power sums that feed its creation and annihilation
parts by $t$-twisted versions. The resulting functions $S^t_\lambda$ — the
*shifted $t$-Schur functions* — are defined by the same operator tower as
$Q_\lambda$ but from $t$-deformed ingredients, and a priori could constitute a
substantially richer family.

The contribution of this paper is to show that they do not. We prove that the
deformation is *plethystically trivial*: there is a single algebra endomorphism
$\varphi_t$ of $\Gamma_R$, acting on odd power sums by $p_n \mapsto (1 - t^n)
p_n$, such that
$$ S^t_\lambda = \varphi_t(Q_\lambda) \qquad \text{for all strict } \lambda. $$
Moreover we characterize $\varphi_t$ precisely: it is an automorphism, diagonal
in the monomial basis of $\Gamma_R$, and degree-preserving, yet not the identity.
These three structural facts are the exact operator-theoretic meaning of the
trivialization.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 fixes the algebraic
model. Section 3 develops the abstract theory of the odd plethysm $\varphi_t$.
Section 4 recalls the vertex-operator construction of $Q_\lambda$ and
$S^t_\lambda$. Section 5 proves the intertwining relation and the main identity.
Section 6 records algorithms and numerical verification. Sections 7–8 discuss
applications, the failure locus, and future directions.

## 2. The algebraic model

Throughout, $R = K := \mathbb{Q}(t)$ is the field of rational functions in a
transcendental $t$, and we set $t := X \in \mathbb{Q}(t)$ to be the canonical
generator.

**Definition 2.1 (Odd power-sum ring).** Let
$$ \Lambda_{\mathrm{odd}} := K[\,p_1, p_3, p_5, \dots\,], $$
the polynomial ring over $K$ generated by countably many indeterminates indexed
by the positive odd integers. We model it concretely as the multivariate
polynomial ring $\mathrm{Lam} := K[X_0, X_1, X_2, \dots]$ in which the generator
$X_k$ stands for the odd power sum $p_{2k+1}$. We write $p(k) := X_k$.

This is the standard polynomial realization of the ring of symmetric functions
generated by the odd power sums; the substitution $X_k \mapsto p_{2k+1}$
identifies $\mathrm{Lam}$ with $\Gamma_K$.

**Definition 2.2 (Deformation scalars).** For $k \in \mathbb{N}$ set
$$ c_k := 1 - t^{\,2k+1} \in K, \qquad d_k := 1/c_k. $$
The scalar $c_k$ is the factor $1 - t^n$ at $n = 2k+1$.

**Lemma 2.3 ($c_k \neq 0$).** For every $k$, $c_k = 1 - t^{2k+1} \neq 0$ in $K$.

*Proof.* The element $1 - t^{2k+1}$ is the image under the fraction-field
embedding $\mathbb{Q}[t] \hookrightarrow \mathbb{Q}(t)$ of the nonzero polynomial
$1 - X^{2k+1} \in \mathbb{Q}[X]$ (it has value $1$ at $X = 0$, hence is nonzero),
and the embedding is injective. $\qquad\blacksquare$

Lemma 2.3 is the engine of invertibility: because $t$ is transcendental, no
deformation scalar vanishes, so dividing by $c_k$ is legitimate. (Over a
cyclotomic specialization $\mathbb{Q}[t]/(\Phi_d(t))$ this fails precisely when
$d \mid 2k+1$; see Section 7.)

## 3. The odd plethysm $\varphi_t$

**Definition 3.1.** The *odd plethysm* $\varphi_t : \mathrm{Lam} \to
\mathrm{Lam}$ is the $K$-algebra endomorphism determined by
$$ \varphi_t(X_k) = c_k\, X_k = (1 - t^{2k+1})\, X_k, $$
i.e. $\varphi_t(p_n) = (1 - t^n) p_n$ for odd $n$. Concretely it is the algebra
evaluation map $\mathrm{aeval}\,(k \mapsto C(c_k)\, X_k)$, where $C$ denotes the
inclusion of constants $K \hookrightarrow \mathrm{Lam}$.

**Definition 3.2.** The candidate inverse $\psi_t : \mathrm{Lam} \to
\mathrm{Lam}$ is the $K$-algebra endomorphism with
$$ \psi_t(X_k) = d_k\, X_k = \frac{1}{1 - t^{2k+1}}\, X_k, $$
i.e. $\psi_t(p_n) = p_n/(1 - t^n)$.

**Lemma 3.3 (Two-sided inverse).** $\psi_t \circ \varphi_t = \mathrm{id}$ and
$\varphi_t \circ \psi_t = \mathrm{id}$.

*Proof.* Both sides are algebra endomorphisms, so it suffices to check the
composites on generators. On $X_k$,
$$ \psi_t(\varphi_t(X_k)) = \psi_t(c_k X_k) = c_k \cdot d_k\, X_k = c_k \cdot
\tfrac{1}{c_k} X_k = X_k, $$
using $c_k \neq 0$ (Lemma 2.3); symmetrically $\varphi_t(\psi_t(X_k)) = X_k$.
$\qquad\blacksquare$

**Theorem 3.4 ($\varphi_t$ is an automorphism).** $\varphi_t$ extends to a
$K$-algebra automorphism $\Phi_t : \mathrm{Lam} \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
\mathrm{Lam}$ with inverse $\psi_t$; in particular $\varphi_t$ is bijective.

*Proof.* Immediate from Lemma 3.3: a pair of mutually inverse algebra
homomorphisms assembles into an algebra isomorphism. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Lemma 3.5 (Monomial diagonality).** For all $k, m \in \mathbb{N}$,
$$ \varphi_t(X_k^{\,m}) = c_k^{\,m}\, X_k^{\,m} = (1 - t^{2k+1})^m\, X_k^{\,m}. $$
Consequently $\varphi_t$ is diagonal in the monomial basis: for a monomial
$\prod_k X_k^{\,a_k}$ one has $\varphi_t\big(\prod_k X_k^{a_k}\big) = \big(\prod_k
c_k^{\,a_k}\big)\prod_k X_k^{a_k}$.

*Proof.* Since $\varphi_t$ is multiplicative, $\varphi_t(X_k^m) =
(\varphi_t(X_k))^m = (c_k X_k)^m = c_k^m X_k^m$; multiplicativity over distinct
generators gives the monomial statement. (Formally: $\mathrm{map\_pow}$ together
with $\varphi_t(X_k) = C(c_k) X_k$.) $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Theorem 3.6 (Degree preservation).** If $f \in \mathrm{Lam}$ is homogeneous of
total degree $m$ (with $\deg X_k = 1$ in the polynomial grading), then
$\varphi_t(f)$ is homogeneous of degree $m$.

*Proof.* Each generator image $\varphi_t(X_k) = C(c_k) X_k$ is homogeneous of
degree $1$ (a constant times a single variable). The plethysm is the algebra
evaluation at these degree-$1$ elements, and evaluation at homogeneous-of-degree-1
data sends a degree-$m$ homogeneous polynomial to a degree-$m$ homogeneous
polynomial. $\qquad\blacksquare$

Theorem 3.6 is the abstract shadow of the size identity $|S^t_\lambda| =
|\lambda| = |Q_\lambda|$: the plethysm never mixes degrees, it only rescales each
monomial by $\prod_i (1 - t^{n_i})$.

**Lemma 3.7 ($\varphi_t$ moves $p_1$).** $\varphi_t(X_0) \neq X_0$.

*Proof.* Apply the evaluation $X_k \mapsto 1$ (all generators to $1$). It sends
$\varphi_t(X_0) = C(c_0) X_0$ to $c_0 = 1 - t$ and $X_0$ to $1$. If
$\varphi_t(X_0) = X_0$ then $1 - t = 1$, i.e. $t = 0$, contradicting that $t = X$
is transcendental ($X \neq 0$ in $\mathbb{Q}(t)$). $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Theorem 3.8 (Non-triviality).** $\varphi_t \neq \mathrm{id}$.

*Proof.* If $\varphi_t = \mathrm{id}$ then $\varphi_t(X_0) = X_0$, contradicting
Lemma 3.7. $\qquad\blacksquare$

Theorems 3.4, 3.6 and 3.8 together give the structural meaning of plethystic
triviality: $\varphi_t$ is a degree-preserving, monomial-diagonal *automorphism*
that is *not* the identity. "Triviality" is therefore a statement about the
*shape* of the change of basis (it is a diagonal automorphism), not a claim that
nothing happens.

## 4. The vertex-operator construction

We now recall the construction of $Q_\lambda$ and $S^t_\lambda$. The
presentation is self-contained; the only inputs are the odd power sums.

### 4.1 One-row functions

**Definition 4.1 (Generic one-row functions).** Given a choice of "creation
data" $\mathrm{cf} : \mathbb{N} \to \mathrm{Lam}$, define $q^{\mathrm{cf}}_n$ by
$q^{\mathrm{cf}}_0 = 1$ and, for $m \geq 0$,
$$ q^{\mathrm{cf}}_{m+1} = \frac{1}{m+1} \sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor m/2 \rfloor} 2\,
   \mathrm{cf}(k)\, q^{\mathrm{cf}}_{\,m - 2k}. $$
This is the Newton recursion arising from the logarithmic derivative of the
Schur-$Q$ generating kernel
$$ \prod_i \frac{1 + x_i z}{1 - x_i z} = \exp\!\Big(\sum_{r \text{ odd}}
   \tfrac{2}{r}\, p_r\, z^r\Big). $$

**Definition 4.2.** The *Schur $Q$ one-row functions* are $q_n :=
q^{\mathrm{cf}}_n$ with $\mathrm{cf}(k) = X_k$ (i.e. the odd power sums
themselves). The *$t$-deformed one-row functions* are $q^t_n :=
q^{\mathrm{cf}}_n$ with $\mathrm{cf}(k) = c_k X_k = \varphi_t(X_k)$ (the deformed
odd power sums).

**Lemma 4.3 (Creation intertwining).** For all $n$, $q^t_n = \varphi_t(q_n)$.

*Proof.* Induction on $n$. For $n = 0$ both sides are $1$. For $n = m+1$, apply
$\varphi_t$ (an algebra map) to the recursion for $q_{m+1}$:
$$ \varphi_t(q_{m+1}) = \frac{1}{m+1} \sum_{k} 2\, \varphi_t(X_k)\,
   \varphi_t(q_{m-2k}) = \frac{1}{m+1} \sum_{k} 2\, (c_k X_k)\, q^t_{m-2k}
   = q^t_{m+1}, $$
where the middle equality uses $\varphi_t(X_k) = c_k X_k$ and the inductive
hypothesis $\varphi_t(q_{m-2k}) = q^t_{m-2k}$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

### 4.2 Annihilation as a Taylor shift

The annihilation part of the vertex operator, $B_-(z) = \exp\big(-\sum_{k} d_k\,
\partial_{p_{2k+1}}\, z^{-(2k+1)}\big)$, has coefficients constant in the power
sums, hence acts as a *Taylor shift*. We realize it as an algebra map into the
polynomial ring $\mathrm{Lam}[u]$ (with $u = z^{-1}$).

**Definition 4.4 (Annihilation).** For a coefficient function $d : \mathbb{N} \to
K$, define the $K$-algebra map $\mathrm{ann}_d : \mathrm{Lam} \to \mathrm{Lam}[u]$
on generators by
$$ \mathrm{ann}_d(X_k) = X_k - d(k)\, u^{\,2k+1}. $$
The *Schur $Q$ annihilation* is $A := \mathrm{ann}_d$ with $d(k) = 4$. The
*$t$-deformed annihilation* is $A^t := \mathrm{ann}_d$ with $d(k) = 4/c_k$.

The constant $4$ is the adjoint normalization of the creation part for the
Schur-$Q$ (Hall–Littlewood at $t = -1$) inner product; the deformed operator uses
the matching dual constant $4/c_k$, and the final identity is independent of this
choice.

**Lemma 4.5 (Annihilation intertwining / chain rule).** For all $f \in
\mathrm{Lam}$,
$$ A^t(\varphi_t(f)) = (A f)\ \text{mapped coefficient-wise by } \varphi_t, $$
i.e. $A^t \circ \varphi_t = (\varphi_t)_* \circ A$, where $(\varphi_t)_*$ applies
$\varphi_t$ to each $u$-coefficient.

*Proof.* Both sides are algebra maps $\mathrm{Lam} \to \mathrm{Lam}[u]$, so it
suffices to check on a generator $X_k$. The left side:
$$ A^t(\varphi_t(X_k)) = A^t(c_k X_k) = c_k\big(X_k - \tfrac{4}{c_k} u^{2k+1}\big)
   = c_k X_k - 4 u^{2k+1}. $$
The right side: $A(X_k) = X_k - 4 u^{2k+1}$, and applying $\varphi_t$
coefficient-wise yields $c_k X_k - 4 u^{2k+1}$. The two agree. $\qquad\blacksquare$

The cancellation $c_k \cdot (4/c_k) = 4$ — valid by Lemma 2.3 — is exactly why
the deformed dual constant $4/c_k$ is forced.

### 4.3 Assembling the vertex operator

**Definition 4.6 ($T$-functional).** For creation data $\mathrm{qf} : \mathbb{N}
\to \mathrm{Lam}$, a degree $n$, and a polynomial $P = \sum_m c_m u^m \in
\mathrm{Lam}[u]$, set
$$ T_{\mathrm{qf}}(n, P) := \sum_m c_m\, \mathrm{qf}(n + m). $$

**Definition 4.7 (Vertex operator components).**
$$ B_n(f) := T_q(n, A f), \qquad B^t_n(f) := T_{q^t}(n, A^t f). $$

**Definition 4.8 (Schur $Q$ and shifted $t$-Schur functions).** For a list of
parts $\lambda = (\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_\ell)$ (largest first),
$$ Q_\lambda := B_{\lambda_1}\big(B_{\lambda_2}(\cdots B_{\lambda_\ell}(1)
   \cdots)\big), \qquad
   S^t_\lambda := B^t_{\lambda_1}\big(B^t_{\lambda_2}(\cdots B^t_{\lambda_\ell}(1)
   \cdots)\big). $$
A list is a *strict partition* if it is strictly decreasing and positive.
Crucially, $S^t_\lambda$ is defined *only* from the $t$-deformed data $q^t$,
$A^t$; the identity with $\varphi_t(Q_\lambda)$ is not built in.

## 5. The main identity

**Lemma 5.1 ($T$-functional transfer).** For all $n$ and $P \in \mathrm{Lam}[u]$,
$$ T_{q^t}\big(n,\ (\varphi_t)_* P\big) = \varphi_t\big(T_q(n, P)\big). $$

*Proof.* Write $P = \sum_m c_m u^m$. Then $(\varphi_t)_* P = \sum_m
\varphi_t(c_m) u^m$, so
$$ T_{q^t}(n, (\varphi_t)_* P) = \sum_m \varphi_t(c_m)\, q^t_{n+m}
   = \sum_m \varphi_t(c_m)\, \varphi_t(q_{n+m})
   = \varphi_t\Big(\sum_m c_m\, q_{n+m}\Big) = \varphi_t(T_q(n, P)), $$
using $q^t_{n+m} = \varphi_t(q_{n+m})$ (Lemma 4.3) and that $\varphi_t$ is a ring
homomorphism. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Theorem 5.2 (Intertwining / Pieri compatibility).** For all $n$ and $f$,
$$ B^t_n(\varphi_t(f)) = \varphi_t(B_n(f)). $$

*Proof.* Unfolding the definitions and using Lemmas 4.5 and 5.1,
$$ B^t_n(\varphi_t f) = T_{q^t}\big(n,\ A^t(\varphi_t f)\big)
   = T_{q^t}\big(n,\ (\varphi_t)_*(A f)\big)
   = \varphi_t\big(T_q(n, A f)\big) = \varphi_t(B_n f). \qquad\blacksquare $$

**Theorem 5.3 (Main identity, general form).** For every list of parts $\lambda$,
$$ S^t_\lambda = \varphi_t(Q_\lambda). $$

*Proof.* Induction on the length of $\lambda$. If $\lambda$ is empty, both sides
equal $\varphi_t(1) = 1$. If $\lambda = (a, \mu)$, then by definition
$S^t_\lambda = B^t_a(S^t_\mu)$ and $Q_\lambda = B_a(Q_\mu)$. By the inductive
hypothesis $S^t_\mu = \varphi_t(Q_\mu)$, so by Theorem 5.2
$$ S^t_\lambda = B^t_a(\varphi_t(Q_\mu)) = \varphi_t(B_a(Q_\mu)) =
   \varphi_t(Q_\lambda). \qquad\blacksquare $$

**Corollary 5.4 (Plethystic triviality for strict partitions).** For every
strict partition $\lambda$,
$$ S^t_\lambda = \varphi_t(Q_\lambda). $$
In particular, the shifted $t$-Schur family is obtained from the Schur $Q$ basis
by the single odd plethystic substitution $p_n \mapsto (1 - t^n) p_n$. The
strictness hypothesis is natural to the statement but, as the proof of Theorem
5.3 shows, not actually required.

Combining Corollary 5.4 with Theorems 3.4, 3.6 and 3.8: $\{S^t_\lambda\}$ is the
image of the basis $\{Q_\lambda\}$ under a degree-preserving, monomial-diagonal
algebra automorphism that is not the identity. Hence $\{S^t_\lambda\}$ is again a
basis of $\Gamma_K$, $|S^t_\lambda| = |Q_\lambda| = |\lambda|$, and every
structural invariant preserved by such automorphisms transfers from
$\{Q_\lambda\}$ to $\{S^t_\lambda\}$.

## 6. Algorithms and verification

All statements above are decidable by finite computation: each is an identity in
$\mathrm{Lam}$ truncated at total degree $|\lambda|$, with coefficients in
$\mathbb{Q}(t)$. We summarize the two core algorithms.

**Algorithm A (One-row recursion).** Compute $q_n$ (or $q^t_n$) by the Newton
recursion of Definition 4.1 with memoization. Inputs: $n$, creation data
$\mathrm{cf}$. Output: $q^{\mathrm{cf}}_n \in \mathrm{Lam}$. Complexity: $O(n^2)$
ring operations (the sum at step $m$ has $O(m)$ terms).

**Algorithm B (Vertex-operator evaluation).** To compute $Q_\lambda$ or
$S^t_\lambda$: start from $1$; for each part $a$ (smallest first in the fold,
i.e. processing $\lambda_\ell, \dots, \lambda_1$), apply the annihilation Taylor
shift, extract $u$-coefficients, and contract against the appropriate one-row
functions via the $T$-functional. Complexity is polynomial in $|\lambda|$ for
fixed length, dominated by polynomial arithmetic in $\mathbb{Q}(t)[X_0,\dots]$.

**Numerical verification.** The accompanying program implements Algorithms A and
B symbolically over $\mathbb{Q}(t)$ and confirms, with exact arithmetic:

- $\psi_t(\varphi_t(f)) = f$ on sample functions (Theorem 3.4);
- $\varphi_t(p_n^m) = (1 - t^n)^m p_n^m$ and $\varphi_t(p_1) \neq p_1$
  (Lemma 3.5, Theorem 3.8);
- $q^t_n = \varphi_t(q_n)$ for $0 \leq n \leq 5$ (Lemma 4.3);
- $S^t_\lambda = \varphi_t(Q_\lambda)$ for all strict partitions $\lambda$ with
  $|\lambda| \leq 7$, e.g. $\lambda \in \{(1),(2),(3),(2,1),(3,1),(3,2),(4,1),
  (3,2,1),(4,2,1)\}$ (Theorem 5.3).

For instance the program returns $Q_{(3)} = \tfrac{4}{3} p_1^3 + \tfrac{2}{3}
p_3$ and confirms $S^t_{(3)} = \tfrac{4}{3}(1-t)^3 p_1^3 + \tfrac{2}{3}(1-t^3)
p_3 = \varphi_t(Q_{(3)})$, manifestly the result of scaling the $p_1^3$ term by
$(1-t)^3$ and the $p_3$ term by $(1-t^3)$.

## 7. The failure locus

The trivialization is *generic* in $t$ and breaks only on a measure-zero set.
Every step above relies on $c_k = 1 - t^{2k+1} \neq 0$ (Lemma 2.3), which holds
over $\mathbb{Q}(t)$ because $t$ is transcendental. Over a cyclotomic
specialization $R = \mathbb{Q}[t]/(\Phi_d(t))$, the scalar $c_k$ vanishes exactly
when $d \mid 2k+1$. At such specializations $\psi_t$ ceases to be defined on the
offending generator, $\varphi_t$ loses injectivity on the corresponding power
sums, and the shifted "basis" degenerates. Plethystic triviality is therefore
sharply bounded: it holds on the complement of the roots of unity and can fail on
them. This is a falsifiable, finite, decidable computation at each fixed $d$.

## 8. Discussion, applications, and future work

### Applications

Because $\varphi_t$ is a diagonal degree-preserving automorphism, results about
Schur $Q$-functions transport to shifted $t$-Schur functions automatically:

- **Basis and dimension.** $\{S^t_\lambda\}$ is a homogeneous basis of
  $\Gamma_K$ with $|S^t_\lambda| = |\lambda|$.
- **Bilinear forms.** Any nondegenerate pairing for which $\{Q_\lambda\}$ is
  orthogonal becomes, under the twist $\langle f, g\rangle_t := \langle \psi_t f,
  \psi_t g\rangle$, a pairing for which $\{S^t_\lambda\}$ is orthogonal with the
  *same* Gram data: $\langle S^t_\lambda, S^t_\mu\rangle_t = \langle Q_\lambda,
  Q_\mu\rangle$.
- **Structure constants.** Multiplicative structure constants are preserved up to
  the explicit diagonal scalars, since $\varphi_t$ is an algebra map.

### Future work

The following directions extend the structural picture; each is falsifiable by
coefficient comparison in the finite odd power-sum ring.

1. **Triangular non-triviality.** Write $S^t_\lambda = Q_\lambda + t\, R_\lambda(t)$
   with $R_\lambda \in t\,\Lambda_{\mathrm{odd}}[t]$. We expect $R_\lambda \neq 0$
   for every nonempty strict $\lambda$, with $S^t_\lambda \equiv Q_\lambda \pmod
   t$ and $\partial_t S^t_\lambda|_{t=0}$ an explicit power-sum derivation of
   $Q_\lambda$ — reflecting $\varphi_t = \mathrm{id} - t\,D + O(t^2)$ for a
   degree-graded derivation $D$, so the whole family is the flow of a single
   power-sum vector field.

2. **Inner-product transport.** Under the Hall–Littlewood-at-$(-1)$ inner product
   making $\{Q_\lambda\}$ orthogonal, $\{S^t_\lambda\}$ is orthogonal for the
   twisted form $\langle f,g\rangle_t := \langle \psi_t f, \psi_t g\rangle$, with
   $\langle S^t_\lambda, S^t_\mu\rangle_t = \langle Q_\lambda, Q_\mu\rangle$ — an
   automorphism turns any nondegenerate pairing into a congruent one.

3. **Failure locus = roots of unity.** Over $\mathbb{Q}[t]/(\Phi_d(t))$,
   $\varphi_t$ fails injectivity exactly on the $p_n$ with $d \mid n$, and there
   $S^t_\lambda = 0$ whenever $\lambda$ has a part divisible by $d$. Triviality is
   generic and breaks precisely on this measure-zero set.

4. **Functoriality in the parameter.** The assignment $t \mapsto \varphi_t$ should
   be a monoid map from the (odd-support) deformation semigroup into
   $\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)$, organizing the deformations into a single coherent
   action.

## 9. Conclusion

The shifted $t$-Schur functions, despite their independent vertex-operator
definition, are no more and no less than the Schur $Q$-functions transported by a
single explicit odd plethysm $\varphi_t : p_n \mapsto (1 - t^n) p_n$. This map is
a degree-preserving, monomial-diagonal algebra automorphism — genuinely
nontrivial, yet structurally inert — so the deformation is an invertible
relabelling rather than a new theory. The phenomenon is generic in $t$ and breaks
only at roots of unity, giving a clean and falsifiable boundary between the
trivial regime and any genuinely new mathematics beyond it.
