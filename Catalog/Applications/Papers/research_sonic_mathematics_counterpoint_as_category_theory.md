# Sonic Mathematics: First-Species Counterpoint as a Failed Category

## Abstract

We formalize the voice-leading structure of Fux's first-species counterpoint in Lean 4, treating consonant interval classes as objects and valid voice leadings as morphisms in a proposed category over ℤ/12ℤ. Our main results are: (1) the set of consonant intervals {0, 3, 4, 7, 8, 9} is not closed under interval inversion, with the asymmetry localized to the perfect fifth/fourth pair; (2) the transition graph on consonant intervals is the complete graph K₆ despite the parallel-perfects restriction; (3) exactly 410 of 432 possible voice leadings survive the parallel-perfects rule, with a deficit of 22 concentrated at perfect-consonance self-loops; (4) valid voice leadings do **not** compose to valid voice leadings, disproving the conjecture that first-species counterpoint forms a category; (5) the restriction to imperfect consonances yields a genuine subcategory. All results are machine-verified in Lean 4 with Mathlib.

**Keywords**: counterpoint, category theory, voice leading, interval class, formal verification, music theory

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background

Musical counterpoint—the art of combining independent melodic lines—has been a subject of formal study since the Renaissance. The rules codified by Fux (1725) in *Gradus ad Parnassum* remain the foundation of compositional pedagogy. First-species counterpoint, the simplest type, requires two voices to produce note-against-note consonances from a restricted set of intervals.

Recent work has sought to formalize these rules in mathematical frameworks: Tymoczko (2006, 2011) models voice leading geometrically as paths in orbifolds; Mazzola (2002) applies topos theory to music; Cohn (1998) uses group actions to study neo-Riemannian transformations. The lattice-theoretic approach of the companion formalization (`Catalog/Algebra/MusicalCounterpoint.lean`) treats voice leading cost as an L¹ seminorm on ℤ-modules.

### 1.2 The Categorical Conjecture

The motivating conjecture states: *The category of first-species counterpoint over a diatonic scale is equivalent to the thin category generated by a specific poset of 12 elements.* We test this conjecture by formalizing the voice-leading structure and analyzing its categorical properties.

### 1.3 Summary of Results

We disprove the conjecture in two independent ways:
1. The voice-leading structure has multiple morphisms between each pair of objects (up to 12), so it cannot be thin.
2. Composition of valid voice leadings is not closed, so the structure is not a category.

Along the way, we establish several results about the structure of consonance and voice leading that appear to be new in the formal verification literature.

## 2. Definitions

### 2.1 Interval Classes

We work in ℤ/12ℤ, the integers modulo 12, representing the 12 chromatic pitch classes.

**Definition 2.1** (Consonant Set). The set of *consonant interval classes* in first-species counterpoint is:
$$C = \{0, 3, 4, 7, 8, 9\} \subset \mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$$

These correspond to: unison (0), minor third (3), major third (4), perfect fifth (7), minor sixth (8), major sixth (9).

**Definition 2.2** (Perfect/Imperfect Partition). The consonant set decomposes as $C = P \cup I$ where:
- $P = \{0, 7\}$ (perfect consonances: unison, fifth)
- $I = \{3, 4, 8, 9\}$ (imperfect consonances: thirds, sixths)

**Definition 2.3** (Interval Inversion). The *inversion* map $\iota: \mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$ is defined by $\iota(i) = -i$.

### 2.2 Voice Leadings

**Definition 2.4** (Voice Leading). A *voice leading* is a pair $(b, t) \in (\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z})^2$ where $b$ is the bass step and $t$ is the treble step. The *interval change* is $t - b$.

**Definition 2.5** (Parallel Motion). A voice leading $(b, t)$ exhibits *parallel motion* if $b = t$ and $b \neq 0$.

**Definition 2.6** (Validity). A voice leading $(b, t)$ from source interval $s$ to target interval $t'$ is *valid* if:
1. $s, t' \in C$ (both intervals are consonant)
2. $t - b = t' - s$ (interval change matches)
3. If $t' \in P$, then the motion is not parallel

### 2.3 Transitions and Composition

**Definition 2.7** (Transition). $\text{canTransition}(s, t)$ holds iff there exists a valid voice leading from $s$ to $t$.

**Definition 2.8** (Composition). The composition of $(b_1, t_1)$ and $(b_2, t_2)$ is $(b_1 + b_2, t_1 + t_2)$.

## 3. Main Results

### 3.1 Consonance Asymmetry

**Theorem 3.1** (Fourth Not Consonant). $(5 : \mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}) \notin C$.

**Theorem 3.2** (Inversion of Fifth). $\iota(7) = 5$.

**Theorem 3.3** (Consonance Not Inversion-Closed). $\neg(\forall i \in C,\, \iota(i) \in C)$.

*Proof sketch*. The perfect fifth $7 \in C$ but $\iota(7) = 5 \notin C$. ∎

**Theorem 3.4** (Imperfect Inversion-Closed). $\forall i \in I,\, \iota(i) \in I$.

*Proof*. Exhaustive: $\iota(3) = 9 \in I$, $\iota(4) = 8 \in I$, $\iota(8) = 4 \in I$, $\iota(9) = 3 \in I$. ∎

**Theorem 3.5** (Inversion Swaps Consonant/Dissonant). The interval 7 is consonant with dissonant inversion, while 5 is dissonant with consonant inversion. Formally:
$$7 \in C \wedge \iota(7) \in D \wedge 5 \in D \wedge \iota(5) \in C$$
where $D = \{1, 2, 5, 6, 10, 11\}$ is the dissonant set.

**Remark 3.6**. Neither $C$ nor $D$ is closed under inversion. The inversion map creates exactly one "bridge" between the two sets, at the fifth/fourth pair. This reflects the physical asymmetry of the overtone series.

### 3.2 Transition Completeness

**Theorem 3.7** (Self-Loop Validity). For all $s \in C$, the identity voice leading $(0, 0)$ is valid from $s$ to $s$.

*Proof*. The interval change is $0 = s - s$, and $(0, 0)$ is not parallel since $b = 0$. ∎

**Theorem 3.8** (Transition Completeness). For all $s, t \in C$, there exists a valid voice leading from $s$ to $t$. The transition graph is $K_6$.

*Proof*. Verified computationally for all 36 ordered pairs. For $s = t \in P$, the identity voice leading works. For $s \neq t$, any voice leading with the correct interval change works (non-parallel since interval change is nonzero). ∎

### 3.3 Morphism Counting

**Theorem 3.9** (Perfect Self-Loop Count). $(|\text{validVLsFor}(0, 0)| = 1) \wedge (|\text{validVLsFor}(7, 7)| = 1)$.

*Proof*. For $s = t \in P$, the interval change is 0, so $b = t$. The non-parallel condition requires $b = 0$, leaving only $(0, 0)$. ∎

**Theorem 3.10** (Imperfect Self-Loop Count). For all $i \in I$, $|\text{validVLsFor}(i, i)| = 12$.

*Proof*. The non-parallel condition is vacuous since $i \notin P$. All 12 bass steps are valid. ∎

**Theorem 3.11** (Non-Self-Loop Count). For all $s, t \in C$ with $s \neq t$, $|\text{validVLsFor}(s, t)| = 12$.

*Proof*. When $s \neq t$, the interval change $d = t - s \neq 0$, so $t' = b + d \neq b$ for all $b$. Thus no voice leading is parallel, and all 12 are valid. ∎

**Theorem 3.12** (Total Count). $|\text{totalValidVLs}| = 410$.

**Theorem 3.13** (Unrestricted Count). Without the parallel-perfects rule, there would be 432 valid voice leadings.

**Corollary 3.14** (Deficit). The parallel-perfects rule removes exactly $432 - 410 = 22$ voice leadings.

### 3.4 Composition Failure

**Theorem 3.15** (Composition Not Closed). There exist $s, m, t \in C$ and valid voice leadings $\text{vl}_1: s \to m$ and $\text{vl}_2: m \to t$ such that $\text{vl}_1 \circ \text{vl}_2$ is not valid.

*Proof*. Take $s = 0, m = 3, t = 0$, $\text{vl}_1 = (0, 3)$, $\text{vl}_2 = (3, 0)$.
- $\text{vl}_1$ is valid: source 0 ∈ C, target 3 ∈ I (imperfect, no restriction), interval change 3 = 3 - 0. ✓
- $\text{vl}_2$ is valid: source 3 ∈ C, target 0 ∈ P, interval change -3 = 0 - 3, bass = 3 ≠ treble = 0, not parallel. ✓
- Composition $(3, 3)$: target 0 ∈ P, bass = treble = 3 ≠ 0, parallel. ✗ ∎

**Corollary 3.16**. First-species counterpoint does NOT form a category. The motivating conjecture is false.

### 3.5 The Imperfect Subcategory

**Theorem 3.17** (Imperfect Composition Closed). For all $s, m, t \in I$ and valid voice leadings $\text{vl}_1: s \to m$, $\text{vl}_2: m \to t$, the composition $\text{vl}_1 \circ \text{vl}_2$ is valid from $s$ to $t$.

*Proof*. The target $t \in I$, so $t \notin P$ (by disjointness of $P$ and $I$). Therefore the parallel-perfects condition is vacuous for the composition. The interval change of the composition is the sum of the individual changes: $(t_1 - b_1) + (t_2 - b_2) = (m - s) + (t - m) = t - s$. ✓ ∎

**Corollary 3.18**. The imperfect consonances form a subcategory with:
- 4 objects: {m3, M3, m6, M6}
- 192 morphisms (4² × 12)
- Closed composition
- Identity morphisms at each object

### 3.6 Order-Theoretic Properties

**Theorem 3.19** (Transition Reflexive). For all $s \in C$, $\text{canTransition}(s, s)$.

**Theorem 3.20** (Transition Symmetric). For all $s, t \in C$, $\text{canTransition}(s, t) \Rightarrow \text{canTransition}(t, s)$.

**Remark 3.21**. Since the transition relation is the complete relation on $C$ (Theorem 3.8), it is trivially reflexive, symmetric, and transitive. The resulting preorder is the indiscrete preorder, not a nontrivial poset. This further disproves the original 12-element poset conjecture.

### 3.7 Consonant-Dissonant Partition

**Theorem 3.22** (Partition). $C \cup D = \mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$ and $C \cap D = \emptyset$, where $D = \{1, 2, 5, 6, 10, 11\}$.

**Theorem 3.23** (Neither Closed). Neither $C$ nor $D$ is closed under interval inversion.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 Why Counterpoint Fails to Be a Category

The composition failure (Theorem 3.15) has a clear musical interpretation: the parallel-perfects rule is a *global* constraint that cannot be enforced locally. Checking each step individually is insufficient—one must track the cumulative effect of voice motions. This is precisely why counterpoint pedagogy emphasizes "looking ahead" rather than simply checking each transition in isolation.

Mathematically, this means the right framework for counterpoint is not a category but something weaker. Candidates include:
- **Partial categories** (composition defined only when the result is valid)
- **Constrained composition systems** (composition carries a predicate)
- **Multicategories** (validity depends on the entire sequence)

### 4.2 The Imperfect Subcategory

The imperfect subcategory (Theorem 3.17) is the largest "well-behaved" piece of the counterpoint system. Its existence reflects a deep musical fact: voice leading between thirds and sixths is inherently more flexible than voice leading involving fifths and unisons. Practically, this is why composers have more freedom in passages dominated by imperfect consonances.

### 4.3 Connection to the Voice Leading Lattice

The companion formalization (`Catalog/Algebra/MusicalCounterpoint.lean`) establishes that voice leading cost is an L¹ seminorm with a lattice identity: $\text{cost}(m_1 \sqcap m_2) + \text{cost}(m_1 \sqcup m_2) = \text{cost}(m_1) + \text{cost}(m_2)$. Our categorical analysis complements this by showing that the *qualitative* structure (which transitions are possible) is trivial (K₆), while the *quantitative* structure (how many ways, at what cost) carries all the musical information.

### 4.4 Connection to Harmonic Music Theory

The Pythagorean music theory formalization (`Catalog/Pythagorean/HarmonicMusicTheory.lean`) derives consonance from the complexity of frequency ratios of Pythagorean triples. Our consonance asymmetry result (Theorem 3.3) provides a complementary perspective: the set of consonances defined by Fux's rules is not the same as the set one would obtain from a pure symmetry principle. The physics of the overtone series breaks the mathematical symmetry.

## 5. Algorithms

### 5.1 Voice Leading Enumeration

**Algorithm 1**: Enumerate valid voice leadings

```
Input: Consonant set C, Perfect set P, modulus n
Output: Dictionary mapping (s, t) to list of valid VLs

for s in C:
  for t in C:
    for b in 0..n-1:
      treble = (b + (t - s)) mod n
      if t in P and b == treble and b != 0:
        skip  // parallel to perfect
      else:
        emit (s, t, VL(b, treble))
```

Complexity: O(|C|² · n)

### 5.2 Composition Failure Search

**Algorithm 2**: Find all composition failures

```
Input: Valid VL dictionary
Output: List of (s, m, t, vl1, vl2) failures

for (s, m) in valid VLs:
  for (m, t) in valid VLs:
    for vl1 in valid[(s,m)]:
      for vl2 in valid[(m,t)]:
        comp = compose(vl1, vl2)
        if t in P and comp.is_parallel:
          emit failure
```

Complexity: O(|C|³ · n²) worst case

1. **n-TET Generalization**: Extend the analysis to arbitrary equal temperaments. How does the consonance asymmetry change for 19-TET, 31-TET, or 53-TET?

2. **Second-Species Counterpoint**: Add passing tones and suspensions. Does the richer constraint system have better categorical properties?
