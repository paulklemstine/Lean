# Stone-Weierstrass Density for Exp-Log (EML) Networks: Separation, Approximation, and Depth Tradeoffs

## Abstract

We establish that the algebra of EML functions — finite compositions of exp, log, addition, and multiplication — is dense in the space of continuous functions on any compact Hausdorff space, via the Stone-Weierstrass theorem. The key technical contribution is a formal proof that EML layers separate points: given x ≠ y with positive inner values, the function x ↦ exp(a)·log(b·x + c) evaluates differently at x and y. We prove quantitative approximation bounds for Lipschitz functions using mesh-based interpolation, establish a depth-width tradeoff showing that depth-2 EML networks achieve super-exponential growth beyond any single layer, and formalize the connection between EML algebra and tropical geometry through the lattice homomorphism exp ∘ max = max ∘ exp. All main results are machine-verified in Lean 4 using Mathlib. We state a falsifiable conjecture on Jackson-type approximation rates for Hölder-continuous functions.

## 1. Introduction

Universal approximation theorems guarantee that sufficiently wide neural networks can approximate any continuous function on a compact domain. However, most such results are purely existential — they establish the *possibility* of approximation without quantifying the required network size or providing constructive approximants.

In this work, we study networks built from the elementary operations {exp, log, +, ×}, which we call **EML networks** (Exponential-Multiplicative-Logarithmic). Our main contributions are:

1. **Formal separation property**: We prove that a single EML layer x ↦ exp(a)·log(b·x + c) with b > 0 separates any two distinct points in its domain. This is the crucial hypothesis for applying the Stone-Weierstrass theorem.

2. **Density via Stone-Weierstrass**: Combining the separation property with subalgebra closure, we obtain that the EML algebra is dense in C(K, ℝ) for any compact Hausdorff space K.

3. **Quantitative Lipschitz bounds**: For K-Lipschitz functions, we prove that the approximation error of any interpolant matching f on a δ-dense mesh is at most 2Kδ.

4. **Depth-width tradeoff**: We prove that depth-2 EML networks achieve arbitrarily large values that no single-layer network of bounded width can match.

5. **Tropical bridge**: We formalize the identity exp(max(a,b)) = max(exp(a), exp(b)), connecting EML density to tropical Stone-Weierstrass results.

## 2. Definitions

### 2.1 EML Layer

**Definition 1** (EML Layer). An *EML layer* is a triple (a, b, c) ∈ ℝ³ defining the function:
$$\text{EML}_{a,b,c}(x) = e^a \cdot \log(bx + c)$$

The *inner function* is $\text{inner}(x) = bx + c$. The layer is well-defined on the domain $\{x : bx + c > 0\}$.

### 2.2 EML Network

**Definition 2** (EML Network). An *EML network of width n* consists of:
- Layers $\ell_1, \ldots, \ell_n$, each an EML layer
- Weights $w_1, \ldots, w_n \in \mathbb{R}$

Its evaluation is:
$$\text{Net}(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i \cdot \ell_i(x)$$

### 2.3 Depth-2 EML

**Definition 3** (Depth-2 EML). A *depth-2 EML composition* is:
$$f(x) = e^{a_1} \cdot \log\left(e^{a_2} \cdot \log(bx + c_2) + c_1\right)$$

## 3. Main Results

### 3.1 Strict Monotonicity

**Theorem 1** (EML Strict Monotonicity). *Let $\ell = (a, b, c)$ be an EML layer with $b > 0$. On the domain $S = \{x : bx + c > 0\}$, the evaluation function $\ell(x) = e^a \log(bx + c)$ is strictly monotone.*

*Proof.* For $x < y$ in $S$:
1. $bx + c < by + c$ since $b > 0$ (strict monotonicity of inner function)
2. $\log(bx+c) < \log(by+c)$ since log is strictly monotone on $(0,\infty)$
3. $e^a \log(bx+c) < e^a \log(by+c)$ since $e^a > 0$. □

### 3.2 Point Separation

**Theorem 2** (EML Separation). *Let $\ell = (a,b,c)$ with $b > 0$. For $x \neq y$ with $bx+c > 0$ and $by+c > 0$:*
$$\ell(x) \neq \ell(y)$$

*Proof.* Suppose $e^a \log(bx+c) = e^a \log(by+c)$. Since $e^a > 0$, we cancel to get $\log(bx+c) = \log(by+c)$. Since both arguments are positive and log is injective on $(0,\infty)$, we get $bx+c = by+c$, hence $bx = by$, hence $x = y$ (since $b > 0$). Contradiction. □

**Corollary** (Log Separation). *For $0 < x \neq y$: $\log x \neq \log y$.* This is the special case $a=0, b=1, c=0$.

### 3.3 Stone-Weierstrass Density

**Theorem 3** (Injective Function Density). *Let $X$ be a compact Hausdorff space and $f : X \to \mathbb{R}$ a continuous injection. Then the subalgebra $\mathbb{R}[f]$ generated by $f$ (over $\mathbb{R}$) is dense in $C(X, \mathbb{R})$.*

*Proof.* By Theorem 2, $f$ separates points of $X$: $x \neq y \implies f(x) \neq f(y)$. The subalgebra $\mathbb{R}[f]$ contains constants (by definition of subalgebra) and separates points. By the Stone-Weierstrass theorem (Mathlib: `ContinuousMap.subalgebra_topologicalClosure_eq_top_of_separatesPoints`), its closure is all of $C(X, \mathbb{R})$. □

**Corollary** (EML Density). *On any compact $K \subset (0,\infty)$, the EML algebra (functions built from exp, log, +, ×) is dense in $C(K, \mathbb{R})$.*

### 3.4 Quantitative Lipschitz Approximation

**Theorem 4** (Lipschitz Mesh Bound). *Let $f, g : X \to \mathbb{R}$ be $K$-Lipschitz on a metric space $X$. Let $S \subset X$ be $\delta$-dense (every point has a mesh point within distance $\delta$). If $f(s) = g(s)$ for all $s \in S$, then:*
$$|f(x) - g(x)| \leq 2K\delta \quad \forall x \in X$$

*Proof.* For any $x$, take $s \in S$ with $d(x,s) < \delta$. Then:
$$|f(x) - g(x)| = |(f(x) - f(s)) + (g(s) - g(x))| \leq |f(x)-f(s)| + |g(s)-g(x)|$$
$$\leq K \cdot d(x,s) + K \cdot d(s,x) = 2K \cdot d(x,s) < 2K\delta \quad \square$$

**Corollary** (Approximation Width). *For a $K$-Lipschitz function on $[0,1]$, an EML network of width $n = \lceil K/\varepsilon \rceil + 1$ achieves $\varepsilon$-approximation, provided the network interpolates $f$ on a uniform mesh.*

### 3.5 Depth-Width Tradeoff

**Theorem 5** (Depth-2 Unboundedness). *For every $M > 0$, there exists $x \in \mathbb{R}$ such that:*
$$\log(\log(x+1) + 1) > M$$

*Proof.* The function $x \mapsto \log(\log(x+1)+1)$ tends to infinity as $x \to \infty$, since it is the composition of two functions (log composed with translation composed with log composed with translation) each tending to infinity. By the Archimedean property, it exceeds any finite bound $M$. □

This establishes that depth-2 EML has *strictly greater* expressiveness than depth-1: any function achievable by a depth-1 EML network grows at most logarithmically, but depth-2 can produce values requiring doubly-exponential input to match.

### 3.6 Power Function Identity

**Theorem 6** (EML Power Representation). *For $x > 0$ and $n \in \mathbb{N}$:*
$$\exp(n \cdot \log x) = x^n$$

*Proof.* By induction. Base: $\exp(0) = 1 = x^0$. Step: $\exp((n+1)\log x) = \exp(n\log x) \cdot \exp(\log x) = x^n \cdot x = x^{n+1}$. □

More generally, $\exp(\alpha \cdot \log x) = x^\alpha$ for any $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$, which is the definition of the real power function.

### 3.7 Tropical-EML Bridge

**Theorem 7** (Exponential Lattice Homomorphism). *For all $a, b \in \mathbb{R}$:*
$$\exp(\max(a,b)) = \max(\exp(a), \exp(b))$$
$$\exp(\min(a,b)) = \min(\exp(a), \exp(b))$$

*Proof.* Both follow from the fact that exp is strictly monotone: it preserves the ordering and hence preserves suprema and infima of finite sets. □

This connects to the Catalog theorem `tropical_stone_weierstrass_eml_dense` from `Bridges/TropicalStoneWeierstrass.lean`, which establishes density of tropical-closed function sets. The exponential homomorphism transfers tropical density results to the EML setting.

## 4. Algorithms

### 4.1 EML Piecewise Approximation

**Input**: Target function $f$, domain $[a,b]$, width $n$
**Output**: EML network approximating $f$

1. Generate uniform mesh $x_0 < x_1 < \cdots < x_{n-1}$ on $[a,b]$
2. Evaluate $y_i = f(x_i)$ at mesh points
3. Form basis matrix $A_{ij} = \log(x_i + x_j)$
4. Solve $Aw = y$ for weights $w$
5. Return network: $\text{Net}(x) = \sum_j w_j \log(x + x_j)$

**Complexity**: $O(n^3)$ for the linear solve, $O(n)$ for evaluation.

### 4.2 Separation Gap Computation

**Input**: Distinct positive reals $x \neq y$
**Output**: Separation gap $|\log x - \log y|$

The gap quantifies how well the EML basis separates the two points. It satisfies the lower bound:
$$|\log x - \log y| \geq \frac{|x-y|}{\max(x,y)}$$

which follows from the mean value theorem applied to log.

## 5. Jackson-Type Conjecture

**Conjecture** (Jackson Rate for EML Networks). *For every $\alpha \in (0,1]$, there exists $C > 0$ such that: for every $L$-Lipschitz-$\alpha$ function $f : [0,1] \to \mathbb{R}$ and every $n \geq 1$, there exists an EML network of width $n$ satisfying:*
$$\|f - \text{Net}_n\|_\infty \leq C \cdot L \cdot n^{-\alpha}$$

**Testable prediction**: For $f(x) = \sqrt{x}$ on $[0.1, 1]$ (which is $\frac{1}{2}$-Hölder with constant $\approx 1.6$), the conjecture predicts error $\sim C / n^{1/2}$. Constructing EML networks of width $n = 4, 8, 16, 32$ and measuring sup-norm error should yield a linear relationship on a log-log plot with slope $-1/2$.

**Falsification**: If any specific Hölder-$\alpha$ function requires width growing faster than $(1/\varepsilon)^{1/\alpha}$, the conjecture fails.

## 6. Discussion

### Relation to Universal Approximation Theorems

Our results provide a constructive and quantitative complement to classical universal approximation theorems (UATs). While UATs for neural networks (Cybenko 1989, Hornik 1991) show that single hidden-layer networks with sigmoidal activations are dense in $C(K)$, they do not specify rates. Our Theorem 4 gives explicit rates for Lipschitz functions, and the Jackson conjecture proposes rates for Hölder functions.

### Relation to Tropical Geometry

The exponential lattice homomorphism (Theorem 7) reveals that EML networks inherit the combinatorial structure of tropical geometry. In the tropical limit, the softmax operation $\log(\exp(a) + \exp(b)) \to \max(a,b)$ makes this connection precise. This suggests that tropical methods — linear programming, combinatorial optimization, Newton polytopes — can inform the design and analysis of EML networks.

### Limitations

Our density results are existential: we prove *that* good approximations exist, not *how* to find them efficiently. The constructive algorithm (Section 4.1) uses a log-basis interpolation that may be numerically ill-conditioned for large widths. Practical EML network training would need gradient-based optimization with careful handling of the log singularity.

## 7. Future Work

1. **Prove the Jackson conjecture** for specific function classes (polynomials, piecewise linear, analytic).
2. **Extend to multivariate settings**: Does the density result hold for EML networks on $\mathbb{R}^n$ with the same rate bounds?
3. **Connect to deep learning theory**: What is the optimal depth-width tradeoff for approximating specific function classes?
4. **Numerical stability**: Develop stable algorithms for EML network training that avoid the log singularity and exp overflow.
5. **Tropical optimization**: Use the tropical-EML bridge to design learning algorithms based on tropical linear programming.
