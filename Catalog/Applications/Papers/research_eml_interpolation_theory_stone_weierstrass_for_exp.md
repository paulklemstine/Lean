## Abstract

We develop a constructive approximation theory for **EML networks** — function classes generated from the transcendental primitives $\exp$ and $\log$ together with addition, multiplication, and scalar multiplication. The theory has two complementary pillars. First, a **qualitative** pillar: we identify a single explicit EML primitive, $g(t) = e^{a}\log(bt+c)$, prove it is strictly monotone (hence injective, hence point-separating) on the region where its argument is positive, and feed it into the Stone–Weierstrass theorem to conclude that the subalgebra it generates is uniformly dense in $C([lo,hi],\mathbb{R})$. The normalization $a=0$, $b=1$, $c=1-lo$ discharges the positivity side-condition uniformly on any interval, yielding a one-generator dense EML algebra. Second, a **quantitative** pillar: we exhibit a concrete single-exponential EML network $\text{emlQuadApprox}_h(x) = \tfrac{2}{h^2}(e^{hx}-1-hx)$ and prove the explicit, non-asymptotic uniform bound $\|\text{emlQuadApprox}_h - (\cdot)^2\|_{\infty,[0,1]} \le \tfrac{4}{9}h$ for all $0 < h \le 1$, giving an $O(1/n)$ Jackson-type rate (with $h=1/n$) for approximating $x^2$ on $[0,1]$. The two pillars convert the existential universal-approximation guarantee into a constructive one, with separation realized by an explicit primitive and rates carrying explicit constants. All results discussed here are fully formalized and machine-checked; this paper presents the mathematics, statements, and proof sketches.

**Keywords:** EML networks, exp-log functions, Stone–Weierstrass theorem, separation of points, strict monotonicity, universal approximation, Jackson-type rate, softplus, constructive approximation.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The existential gap

The universal approximation theorem (UAT) for neural networks asserts that a sufficiently wide single-hidden-layer network with a non-polynomial activation is dense in $C(K)$ for compact $K$. As classically stated it is an *existence* result: it guarantees an approximant without specifying width, weights, or an error constant. For applications — and for a foundational understanding of *why* a given activation class is expressive — one wants more: an explicit separating primitive (to certify density from first principles) and explicit, computable approximation rates (to size networks for a target tolerance).

### 1.2 EML networks

We study the function class generated by the two canonical transcendental functions and the field operations.

> **Definition 1 (EML function class, informal).** An *EML function* on a domain $D \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ is any function obtained by finitely many compositions of the primitives $\exp$, $\log$ (on positive arguments), addition $+$, multiplication $\times$, and multiplication by real scalars, starting from coordinate projections and constants.

This class is natural for several reasons. It is closed under the operations defining a subalgebra of $C(K,\mathbb{R})$, making Stone–Weierstrass directly applicable. It contains the **softplus** activation $\log(1+e^x)$ and its relatives, which appear throughout machine learning. And $\exp$ and $\log$ are mutually inverse strictly monotone bijections of $\mathbb{R}$ and $(0,\infty)$, a structural fact we exploit twice: $\log$ for separation, $\exp$ for rates.

### 1.3 Historical context

Three classical landmarks frame this work. **Weierstrass (1885)** proved that polynomials are uniformly dense in $C([a,b],\mathbb{R})$. **Stone (1937, 1948)** abstracted the mechanism: density follows from algebraic closure plus point separation, on any compact Hausdorff space. **Jackson (1911)** quantified the polynomial case, bounding the best uniform approximation error of a function by its smoothness (e.g. $O(1/n)$ for Lipschitz functions by degree-$n$ polynomials). The modern *universal approximation theorems* for neural networks (Cybenko 1989; Hornik 1991; Leshno et al. 1993; Pinkus 1999) are descendants of the Stone–Weierstrass circle of ideas, but most are stated existentially. Our aim is to give the EML class both a Stone-style qualitative theory (with an *explicit* separator) and a Jackson-style quantitative theory (with an *explicit* constant), for a transcendental primitive set rather than for polynomials.

### 1.4 Why $\exp$ and $\log$, specifically

The pair $(\exp,\log)$ is distinguished among transcendental functions. They are mutually inverse, each a strictly monotone bijection ($\exp:\mathbb{R}\to(0,\infty)$, $\log:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}$), and together they intertwine the additive and multiplicative structures of $\mathbb{R}$ via $\log(xy)=\log x+\log y$ and $e^{x+y}=e^xe^y$. The monotonicity supplies *separation* with no auxiliary construction, while the analyticity of $\exp$ — its everywhere-convergent Taylor series — supplies *rates* by truncation. Thus the same two primitives cover both halves of approximation theory. They are also of direct practical interest: the softplus activation $\operatorname{softplus}(x)=\log(1+e^x)$, a smooth surrogate for ReLU, is literally an EML function, as are log-sum-exp pooling and many normalizing-flow building blocks.

### 1.5 Contributions

1. **A concrete separating primitive (§3).** We prove $g(t)=e^a\log(bt+c)$ is strictly monotone on $\{t : bt+c>0\}$ for $b>0$ (`emlSep_strictMonoOn`), hence separates points (`emlSep_separates`), with an explicit interval normalization (`emlSep_separates_Icc`).
2. **One-generator EML density (§4).** The subalgebra generated by the single function $t\mapsto \log(t+1-lo)$ separates points of $[lo,hi]$ (`emlSepCM_separatesPoints`) and is therefore uniformly dense in $C([lo,hi],\mathbb{R})$ (`eml_adjoin_dense_on_Icc`).
3. **An abstract Stone–Weierstrass / pullback engine (§2, §5).** A reusable framework deriving density of point-separating EML subalgebras, with a density-transfer mechanism along continuous and injective maps (`eml_topologicalClosure_eq_top_of_separatesPoints`, `eml_universalApproximation`, `pullback_dense_of_injective`, `eml_pullback_universalApproximation`).
4. **An explicit Jackson-type rate (§6).** The single-exponential network $\text{emlQuadApprox}_h$ approximates $x^2$ on $[0,1]$ with the explicit bound $\tfrac{4}{9}h$ (`emlQuadApprox_rate`), giving an $O(1/n)$ rate.

## 2. Background: the Stone–Weierstrass engine

We work in $C(X,\mathbb{R})$, the continuous real functions on a compact Hausdorff space $X$, with the uniform (supremum) norm $\|f\|_\infty = \sup_{x\in X}|f(x)|$. By compactness this supremum is attained, and $(C(X,\mathbb{R}),\|\cdot\|_\infty)$ is a Banach space; uniform convergence is exactly convergence in this norm. A **subalgebra** $A \le C(X,\mathbb{R})$ is a linear subspace closed under pointwise multiplication and containing the constants. The *topological closure* $\overline A$ (also written $A.\text{topologicalClosure}$) is the smallest closed subalgebra containing $A$; saying $\overline A=\top$ is the same as saying $A$ is dense, i.e. every continuous function is a uniform limit of members of $A$.

The organizing principle of the entire development is a single reduction: *density questions for EML algebras reduce to a separation question for a single EML function.* Everything qualitative below is an instance of this reduction; the only creative content is exhibiting the separator (§3) and verifying its side-condition (positivity of the logarithm's argument).

> **Definition 2 (Separation of points).** A set $A \subseteq C(X,\mathbb{R})$ *separates points* if for all $x \neq y$ in $X$ there exists $h \in A$ with $h(x) \neq h(y)$.

> **Theorem 3 (Stone–Weierstrass; `eml_topologicalClosure_eq_top_of_separatesPoints`).** If $A \le C(X,\mathbb{R})$ is a subalgebra that separates points, then its topological closure is all of $C(X,\mathbb{R})$: $\overline{A} = \top$. Equivalently (`eml_dense_range_of_subalgebra_separatesPoints`), $A$ is dense; equivalently (`eml_exists_uniform_approx`), for every $f \in C(X,\mathbb{R})$ and $\varepsilon>0$ there is $g \in A$ with $\|g-f\|_\infty < \varepsilon$.

*Proof sketch.* This is the classical real Stone–Weierstrass theorem. The closure $\overline{A}$ is a closed subalgebra; one shows it is a sublattice (closed under $\max,\min$) by approximating $|{\cdot}|$ uniformly on a bounded interval by polynomials, hence $|f| = \sqrt{f^2}\in\overline A$, hence $\max(f,g)=\tfrac12(f+g+|f-g|)\in\overline A$. A closed point-separating sublattice containing constants is all of $C(X,\mathbb{R})$ by a two-point interpolation plus compactness argument. $\square$

This reduces *every* density question for EML algebras to the single combinatorial-geometric task of exhibiting separation.

### 2.1 Density transfer along maps (pullback engine)

For multivariate and structured domains it is useful to transport density along a continuous map. Given $\varphi \in C(X,Y)$, precomposition $g \mapsto g\circ\varphi$ is an algebra homomorphism $\text{precompAlgHom}(\varphi): C(Y,\mathbb{R}) \to C(X,\mathbb{R})$.

> **Definition 4 (Pullback subalgebra).** For $A \le C(Y,\mathbb{R})$, define $\varphi^*A := A.\!\operatorname{map}(\text{precompAlgHom}(\varphi)) = \{g\circ\varphi : g\in A\} \le C(X,\mathbb{R})$.

> **Theorem 5 (Injective pullback density; `pullback_dense_of_injective`).** If $X,Y$ are compact Hausdorff, $\varphi:X\to Y$ is continuous and injective, and $A\le C(Y,\mathbb{R})$ is dense ($\overline A=\top$), then $\varphi^*A$ is dense in $C(X,\mathbb{R})$.

*Proof sketch.* Precomposition is norm-contractive, $\|g\circ\varphi - h\circ\varphi\|_\infty \le \|g-h\|_\infty$ (`norm_comp_le`), hence continuous (`continuous_precomp`); so it carries closures into closures and $\overline{\varphi^*A} = \overline{\{g\circ\varphi: g\in\overline A\}}$. The closure of $\varphi^*A$ equals the subalgebra of functions *factoring through* $\varphi$ (`pullback_closure_eq_factorsThrough`), proved by exhibiting that subalgebra as closed: a uniform limit $f$ of functions constant on $\varphi$-fibers is itself constant on fibers, defines a function $\bar g$ on $\operatorname{range}\varphi$, and the Tietze extension theorem extends $\bar g$ to $Y$. When $\varphi$ is injective it is a closed embedding (compact-to-Hausdorff), so *every* $f\in C(X,\mathbb{R})$ factors through it (`factorsThrough_eq_top_of_injective`), again by Tietze. Hence the factors-through subalgebra is $\top$ and $\overline{\varphi^*A}=\top$. $\square$

> **Corollary 6 (`eml_pullback_universalApproximation`, `eml_pullback_exists_approx`).** If $A\le C(Y,\mathbb{R})$ separates points and $\varphi:X\to Y$ is injective continuous, then $\{g\circ\varphi : g\in A\}$ is dense in $C(X,\mathbb{R})$; every $f\in C(X,\mathbb{R})$ is within any $\varepsilon>0$ of some $g\circ\varphi$.

## 3. The monotone separation property

We now produce the separating primitive.

> **Definition 7 (EML separator; `emlSep`).** For real parameters $a,b,c$ define
> $$\text{emlSep}(a,b,c)(t) := e^{a}\cdot \log(b\,t + c).$$

> **Theorem 8 (Strict monotonicity; `emlSep_strictMonoOn`).** If $b>0$, then $t \mapsto \text{emlSep}(a,b,c)(t)$ is strictly increasing on the set $\{t \in \mathbb{R} : b\,t+c > 0\}$.

*Proof sketch.* Take $x<y$ with $bx+c>0$ and $by+c>0$. Since $b>0$, $0 < bx+c < by+c$. The logarithm is strictly increasing on $(0,\infty)$, so $\log(bx+c) < \log(by+c)$. Multiplying by $e^a>0$ preserves the strict inequality: $e^a\log(bx+c) < e^a\log(by+c)$. $\square$

> **Theorem 9 (Separation; `emlSep_separates`).** If $b>0$ and $x\ne y$ with $bx+c>0$, $by+c>0$, then $\text{emlSep}(a,b,c)(x) \ne \text{emlSep}(a,b,c)(y)$.

*Proof sketch.* Strict monotonicity on the positivity region implies injectivity there; distinct admissible inputs give distinct outputs. $\square$

**The positivity guard is essential.** The logarithm is monotone only on $(0,\infty)$; near a zero of $bt+c$ it diverges to $-\infty$ and monotonicity fails. Thus the domain restriction $\{bt+c>0\}$ is not cosmetic but load-bearing. The next result discharges it uniformly on a compact interval.

> **Theorem 10 (Interval separation; `emlSep_separates_Icc`).** For any $lo\le hi$ and distinct $x,y\in[lo,hi]$,
> $$\text{emlSep}(0,1,\,1-lo)(x) \ne \text{emlSep}(0,1,\,1-lo)(y).$$
> Explicitly, the separating function is $g(t)=\log(t+1-lo)$.

*Proof sketch.* With $a=0,b=1,c=1-lo$ the argument is $t+1-lo \ge lo + 1 - lo = 1 > 0$ for all $t\in[lo,hi]$, so positivity holds at both $x$ and $y$; apply Theorem 9 with $b=1>0$. $\square$

## 4. One-generator EML density on an interval

We package the separator as an element of $C([lo,hi],\mathbb{R})$ and generate an algebra from it.

> **Definition 11 (Continuous separator; `emlSepCM`).** Let $K=[lo,hi]$ (compact, Hausdorff). Define $\text{emlSepCM}(lo,hi)\in C(K,\mathbb{R})$ by
> $$t \mapsto \log(t + 1 - lo).$$
> Continuity holds because $t+1-lo \ge 1 > 0$ on $K$, so the composition $\log\circ(\text{affine})$ is continuous.

> **Theorem 12 (Generated algebra separates; `emlSepCM_separatesPoints`).** The subalgebra $\operatorname{adjoin}_\mathbb{R}\{\text{emlSepCM}(lo,hi)\}$ separates the points of $[lo,hi]$.

*Proof sketch.* The single generator already separates: for $x\ne y$ in $K$, the generator takes distinct values by Theorem 10. Since the generator lies in the adjoined subalgebra, the subalgebra inherits the separating witness. $\square$

> **Theorem 13 (One-generator density; `eml_adjoin_dense_on_Icc`).** The subalgebra generated by the single EML function $t\mapsto\log(t+1-lo)$ is uniformly dense in $C([lo,hi],\mathbb{R})$:
> $$\big(\operatorname{adjoin}_\mathbb{R}\{\text{emlSepCM}(lo,hi)\}\big).\text{topologicalClosure} = \top.$$

*Proof sketch.* Combine Theorem 12 (separation) with Theorem 3 (Stone–Weierstrass): a point-separating subalgebra of $C(K,\mathbb{R})$ over a compact Hausdorff $K$ has closure $\top$. $\square$

**Remark.** Theorem 13 is striking for its economy: a *single* logarithm, plus closure under $+,\times$ and scaling, reproduces all of $C([lo,hi],\mathbb{R})$. The richness of $\log$ as a transcendental seed is what makes one generator suffice.

## 5. The abstract EML universal approximation theorem

The interval result is an instance of a general principle, isolated for reuse.

> **Theorem 14 (EML universal approximation; `eml_universalApproximation`).** Let $X$ be compact Hausdorff and let $A \le C(X,\mathbb{R})$ be any subalgebra generated by EML primitives. If $A$ separates points, then $A$ is uniformly dense in $C(X,\mathbb{R})$.

*Proof sketch.* Immediate from Theorem 3; "EML-generated" only matters insofar as it provides a separating member, which is the sole hypothesis used. $\square$

Combined with the pullback engine (§2.1), Theorem 14 extends to structured domains: if a point-separating EML subalgebra lives on $Y$ and $\varphi:X\to Y$ is an injective continuous "feature map," then $\{g\circ\varphi\}$ is dense on $X$ (Corollary 6). This is the route to multivariate EML density: coordinatewise EML separators $g_i(x)=\log(x_i+1-lo_i)$ assembled into a feature map separate points of a box $\prod_i[lo_i,hi_i]$, yielding density on $C(K,\mathbb{R})$ for compact boxes $K\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ (see §8, Future Directions).

## 6. An explicit Jackson-type rate for $x^2$

Density is existential. We now give a *constructive* approximant for a concrete target with an explicit error constant, using only the exponential primitive.

> **Definition 15 (Quadratic EML network; `emlQuadApprox`).** For a step size $h>0$,
> $$\text{emlQuadApprox}_h(x) := \frac{2}{h^2}\big(e^{hx} - 1 - hx\big).$$
> This is an EML network: one exponential, two subtractions, two scalar multiplications.

> **Lemma 16 (Taylor identity).** For all $h>0$ and $x\in\mathbb{R}$,
> $$\text{emlQuadApprox}_h(x) = x^2 + \frac{2}{h^2}\sum_{k\ge 3}\frac{(hx)^k}{k!} = x^2 + \frac{h x^3}{3} + \frac{h^2 x^4}{12} + \cdots$$

*Proof sketch.* Expand $e^{hx}=\sum_{k\ge0}(hx)^k/k!$, subtract $1+hx$, leaving $\sum_{k\ge2}(hx)^k/k!$; multiply by $2/h^2$. The $k=2$ term is $\tfrac{2}{h^2}\cdot\tfrac{(hx)^2}{2}=x^2$; the $k\ge3$ terms each carry a positive power of $h$. $\square$

> **Theorem 17 (Explicit rate; `emlQuadApprox_rate`).** For every $h$ with $0<h\le 1$ and every $x\in[0,1]$,
> $$\big|\,\text{emlQuadApprox}_h(x) - x^2\,\big| \le \frac{4}{9}\,h.$$

*Proof sketch.* By Lemma 16 the error is $E_h(x)=\tfrac{2}{h^2}\sum_{k\ge3}(hx)^k/k! = 2\sum_{k\ge3} h^{k-2}x^k/k! \ge 0$. For $x\in[0,1]$ and $0<h\le1$ each term is increasing in both $x$ and $h$, so the supremum over the square is attained at $x=1,h=1$:
$$\sup E_h(x) \le E_1(1) = 2\sum_{k\ge3}\frac{1}{k!} = 2\Big(e - 1 - 1 - \tfrac12\Big) = 2\big(e-\tfrac52\big) \approx 0.4366.$$
Moreover $E_h(x)\le E_h(1) = 2\sum_{k\ge3}h^{k-2}/k! \le h\cdot 2\sum_{k\ge3}h^{k-3}/k!$, and bounding the remaining series for $0<h\le1$ gives the clean rational majorant $E_h(x)\le \tfrac{4}{9}h$, since $2(e-\tfrac52)\le \tfrac49$. The verification reduces to a finite exponential inequality discharged with standard `Real.exp` bounds. $\square$

> **Corollary 18 ($O(1/n)$ width rate).** Setting $h=1/n$ for a positive integer $n$ yields a single-exponential EML network with
> $$\big\|\,\text{emlQuadApprox}_{1/n} - (\cdot)^2\,\big\|_{\infty,[0,1]} \le \frac{4}{9n} = O\!\left(\frac1n\right).$$
> To achieve uniform error $\le\varepsilon$ it suffices to take $h = \tfrac{9}{4}\varepsilon$ (equivalently $n \ge \tfrac{4}{9\varepsilon}$).

**On tightness.** The empirical worst case $2(e-\tfrac52)\approx 0.4366$ shows the bound $4/9\approx0.4444$ is correct and nearly tight; the leading-order error slope is $1/3$ (the $\tfrac{hx^3}{3}$ term at $x=1$). The exact optimal constant lies in $[\tfrac13,\tfrac49]$; pinning it is a self-contained open problem (§8).

## 6b. A worked numerical example

To make the rate concrete, fix $h=\tfrac14$ (i.e. $n=4$). Then for $x\in[0,1]$,
$$\text{emlQuadApprox}_{1/4}(x) = 32\big(e^{x/4} - 1 - \tfrac{x}{4}\big).$$
Theorem 17 guarantees $\sup_{x\in[0,1]}|\text{emlQuadApprox}_{1/4}(x)-x^2|\le \tfrac49\cdot\tfrac14 = \tfrac{1}{9}\approx 0.1111$. The true worst-case error, attained near $x=1$, is $\approx 0.0888$, comfortably inside the certificate. Halving the step to $h=\tfrac18$ ($n=8$) provably halves the certificate to $\tfrac{1}{18}\approx0.0556$ and the empirical worst case drops to $\approx0.0430$ — the signature of a first-order $O(1/n)$ method. The following short table (computed by direct evaluation of the closed form) illustrates the geometric decay:

| $n=1/h$ | certificate $\tfrac49 h$ | empirical $\sup$ error | ratio error$/h$ |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0.4444 | 0.4366 | 0.4366 |
| 2 | 0.2222 | 0.1898 | 0.3795 |
| 4 | 0.1111 | 0.0888 | 0.3553 |
| 8 | 0.0556 | 0.0430 | 0.3440 |
| 16 | 0.0278 | 0.0212 | 0.3386 |
| 64 | 0.0069 | 0.0052 | 0.3346 |

The ratio error$/h$ decreases monotonically toward $1/3$, the leading Taylor slope, confirming both that the bound $4/9$ is a valid uniform majorant and that the asymptotically optimal constant is $1/3$ (see §8, Future Direction 2).

## 7. Algorithms

### 7.1 Interval separator / one-generator basis

Given $[lo,hi]$, the canonical separating primitive is $g(t)=\log(t+1-lo)$. Powers and linear combinations $\sum_k c_k\, g(t)^k$ form a dense basis; fitting a target $f$ reduces to least-squares on the design matrix $[\,g(t_i)^k\,]$. Complexity to assemble and solve for degree $d$ at $m$ sample points: $O(md)$ to build, $O(md^2 + d^3)$ to solve.

### 7.2 Quadratic EML network and tolerance sizing

To approximate $x^2$ on $[0,1]$ within $\varepsilon$: choose $h=\tfrac94\varepsilon$ (capped at $1$), return $x\mapsto \tfrac{2}{h^2}(e^{hx}-1-hx)$. Evaluation is $O(1)$ per point (one $\exp$). The guarantee $|{\cdot}|\le\tfrac49 h\le\varepsilon$ is certified, not heuristic.

## 8. Applications and discussion

**Constructive UAT.** Theorems 13–14 are density (existential) results, but §3 gives them a *named, explicit* separating witness, and §6 supplies a *fully constructive* approximant with a computable error constant for a concrete target. Together they exemplify the program of upgrading existential universal approximation to constructive approximation with rates.

**Softplus and the ML connection.** The softplus activation $\log(1+e^x)$ is an EML function; the strict monotonicity that yields separation (Theorem 8) is the same property that makes such activations well-conditioned for gradient-based training. EML theory thus offers a classical-analysis foundation for a class of activations used empirically.

**Reusable engine.** The pullback framework (§2.1, §5) is domain-agnostic: any injective continuous feature map transports EML density to a new domain, the mechanism behind multivariate extensions. Concretely, to obtain density on a box $K=\prod_i[lo_i,hi_i]\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ one builds the feature map $\varphi(x)=(g_1(x_1),\dots,g_n(x_n))$ with $g_i(t)=\log(t+1-lo_i)$; each coordinate $g_i$ is injective by Theorem 8, so $\varphi$ is injective, and Corollary 6 transports density of the polynomial algebra on $\varphi(K)$ back to $C(K,\mathbb{R})$.

**Comparison with polynomial approximation.** The classical Jackson theory gives degree-$n$ polynomials an $O(1/n)$ uniform rate for Lipschitz targets. Our $x^2$ result matches this order with a *single* exponential unit whose scale $h$ plays the role of $1/n$, and with a fully explicit constant. The conceptual difference is that polynomial bases are linear in fixed monomials, whereas the EML increment $\tfrac{2}{h^2}(e^{hx}-1-hx)$ is a *nonlinear* primitive whose Taylor structure makes the leading term exactly $x^2$ and pushes all error into higher, $h$-suppressed orders.

**Numerical conditioning.** A practical caveat: powers $g(t)^k$ of the logarithmic separator become increasingly collinear as $k$ grows (a Vandermonde-type ill-conditioning), so naive high-degree least-squares fitting requires regularization or an orthogonalized basis. This is an implementation matter, not a limitation of the density theorem, which is about existence of uniform approximants, not about the conditioning of any particular fitting scheme.

## 9. Future directions

**(1) Uniform Jackson rate for all Lipschitz functions on $[0,1]$.** *Conjecture:* every $L$-Lipschitz $f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}$ admits an EML network of width $O(n)$ — a sum of $n$ rescaled exponential increments — with $\|f-\text{net}_n\|_\infty \le C\,L/n$. *Insight:* the second-order increment $\tfrac{2}{h^2}(e^{hx}-1-hx)$ reproduces $x^2$ to $O(h)$; a telescoping/quadrature superposition of shifted increments should reproduce the integral form of any Lipschitz $f$ at the same first-order rate, turning the single-target $x^2$ bound into a class bound.

**(2) Sharp constant for the $x^2$ increment network.** *Conjecture:* the optimal uniform constant is $\|\text{emlQuadApprox}_h-(\cdot)^2\|_{\infty,[0,1]} = (\tfrac12+o(1))h$ as $h\to0$, attained at $x=1$; the proven $4/9$ is not tight. *Insight:* the leading Taylor term of the error is $\tfrac{h}{3}x^3 e^{\xi}$, whose supremum on $[0,1]$ is governed by $x=1$, giving asymptotic slope $\tfrac13$ before the $e^{hx}$ correction, so the true constant lies strictly below $4/9$. A matching lower bound via the value at $x=1$ would pin the constant.

**(3) Multivariate monotone separation on compact $K\subset\mathbb{R}^n$.** *Conjecture:* a finite family of coordinatewise EML monotone functions $g_i(x)=\log(x_i+1-lo_i)$ separates points of any box $\prod_i[lo_i,hi_i]$, hence the generated EML subalgebra is dense in $C(K,\mathbb{R})$. *Insight:* point separation in a product reduces to separation in *some* coordinate, each handled by the proven 1-D separator `emlSepCM`; only a "separate-in-some-coordinate" combinator is missing.

**(4) Depth-vs-width tradeoff for EML networks.** *Conjecture:* one extra $\log\circ\exp$ composition layer improves the $x^k$ rate from $O(1/n)$ (width-$n$, depth-1) to $O(1/n^2)$ (width-$n$, depth-2) on $[0,1]$. *Insight:* composing two exponential increments realizes a fourth-order finite difference, cancelling the leading $O(h)$ error of the single-layer construction.

---

## 10. Conclusion

EML networks — finite combinations of $\exp$, $\log$, $+$, $\times$ — admit a clean two-pillar approximation theory. The logarithm supplies a strictly monotone, hence point-separating, primitive that drives Stone–Weierstrass density, even from a single generator on an interval. The exponential supplies an explicit, Taylor-based increment that approximates $x^2$ with the certified constant $4/9$ and an $O(1/n)$ rate. The combination turns the existential universal approximation theorem into a constructive statement with an explicit separating function and explicit rates — a small but complete model of what constructive approximation theory for transcendental network classes can look like.
