# The EML Differential Operator Algebra: A Depth-Filtered Approach to Differential Galois Theory

## Abstract

We introduce the **EML Expression Algebra** — a formally verified algebraic framework for studying ordinary differential equations whose coefficients are built from exponentials, logarithms, and rational functions. Our central contribution is the **EML depth filtration**, a natural number-valued measure on expressions that counts the maximum nesting level of transcendental operations (exp, log). We prove the **Differential Closure Theorem**: symbolic differentiation does not increase EML depth. This yields a *differential filtered algebra* structure that provides a systematic obstruction theory for determining whether an ODE can have solutions within the EML function class. We formalize Abel's identity for Wronskians, proving that the Wronskian of any two solutions of a second-order linear ODE satisfies a first-order linear ODE determined by the coefficient p(x). We introduce the **EML Differential Operator Algebra** — a novel mathematical structure consisting of linear differential operators with EML coefficients, equipped with a bidimensional filtration by (differential order, EML depth). All results are machine-verified in Lean 4 with Mathlib.

**Keywords**: EML functions, differential equations, Galois theory, depth filtration, differential algebra, formal verification

## 1. Introduction

The exponential-logarithmic (EML) function class, defined by `eml(x,y) = exp(x) - log(y)`, has emerged as a fundamental object in computational mathematics. While individual properties of EML functions have been extensively studied — convexity, monotonicity, algebraic identities — the *differential equation theory* of functions built from exponentials and logarithms has received less systematic attention.

A natural question arises: given an ODE whose coefficients are built from exp, log, and rational operations, when can we expect its solutions to lie in the same function class? This is the EML analogue of the classical question in differential Galois theory: when does a linear ODE have Liouvillian solutions?

### 1.1 Main Contributions

1. **The EML Expression Algebra** (§2): An inductive type representing the algebra of functions generated by constants, the identity, field operations (+, ×, -, ⁻¹), and transcendental operations (exp, log). This is formalized as `EMLExpr` with evaluation, symbolic differentiation, and depth measurement.

2. **The Differential Closure Theorem** (§3): We prove that symbolic differentiation does not increase EML depth. This is the key structural property that makes the depth filtration compatible with the differential algebra structure.

3. **Abel's Identity for EML ODEs** (§4): We formalize and prove Abel's identity — the Wronskian of two solutions satisfies W' = -p·W — providing the foundation for differential Galois theory in the EML setting.

4. **The EML Differential Operator Algebra** (§5): A novel mathematical structure: the algebra of linear differential operators with EML coefficients, equipped with a bidimensional filtration by (order, depth). We prove this filtration is compatible with addition and the operator action.

5. **The Airy Obstruction** (§6): We formalize the Airy equation y'' = xy as having depth-0 (polynomial) coefficients and establish that its solutions, while governed by a depth-0 operator, exhibit growth incompatible with any finite EML depth.

### 1.2 Related Work

The Kovacic algorithm (1986) provides a decision procedure for determining whether a second-order linear ODE has Liouvillian solutions. Our depth filtration can be viewed as a refinement of the Liouvillian tower: whereas Liouvillian solutions are obtained by iterated adjoining of exponentials, logarithms, and algebraic functions, EML depth precisely tracks *how many layers* of transcendental operations are needed.

The classical differential Galois theory of Singer and van der Put establishes that the differential Galois group of a linear ODE is an algebraic group, and the solvability of the ODE by Liouvillian functions is equivalent to the solvability of this group. Our EML depth filtration provides a finer invariant: the *depth* of the solution, which measures the minimal nesting of transcendental operations.

## 2. The EML Expression Algebra

### 2.1 Definition

**Definition 2.1** (EML Expression). An *EML expression* is an element of the inductively defined type:

```
EMLExpr ::= const(c)  | var  | add(e₁, e₂)  | mul(e₁, e₂)
           | neg(e)   | inv(e) | exp(e) | log(e)
```

where c ∈ ℝ.

The crucial design decision is that `inv` (reciprocal) is a *primitive* operation, not derived from exp and log. If we had defined `inv(e) := exp(-log(e))`, the depth would increase by 2, destroying the closure property under differentiation.

### 2.2 Evaluation

**Definition 2.2** (Evaluation). The evaluation function `eval : EMLExpr → ℝ → ℝ` is defined recursively:

- `eval(const(c), x) = c`
- `eval(var, x) = x`
- `eval(add(e₁,e₂), x) = eval(e₁,x) + eval(e₂,x)`
- `eval(mul(e₁,e₂), x) = eval(e₁,x) · eval(e₂,x)`
- `eval(neg(e), x) = -eval(e,x)`
- `eval(inv(e), x) = (eval(e,x))⁻¹`
- `eval(exp(e), x) = exp(eval(e,x))`
- `eval(log(e), x) = log(eval(e,x))`

### 2.3 EML Depth

**Definition 2.3** (EML Depth). The *depth* of an EML expression measures the maximum nesting of transcendental operations:

- `depth(const(c)) = depth(var) = 0`
- `depth(add(e₁,e₂)) = depth(mul(e₁,e₂)) = max(depth(e₁), depth(e₂))`
- `depth(neg(e)) = depth(inv(e)) = depth(e)`
- `depth(exp(e)) = depth(log(e)) = depth(e) + 1`

The depth stratifies the EML algebra into layers:
- **Depth 0**: Rational functions (no exp or log)
- **Depth 1**: Functions like exp(x), log(x), exp(x)/x
- **Depth 2**: Functions like exp(exp(x)), log(log(x))
- **Depth k**: Functions with k nested layers of transcendental operations

## 3. The Differential Closure Theorem

### 3.1 Symbolic Differentiation

**Definition 3.1** (Symbolic Derivative). The symbolic derivative `symbDeriv : EMLExpr → EMLExpr` is defined by:

- `symbDeriv(const(c)) = const(0)`
- `symbDeriv(var) = const(1)`
- `symbDeriv(add(e₁,e₂)) = add(symbDeriv(e₁), symbDeriv(e₂))`
- `symbDeriv(mul(e₁,e₂)) = add(mul(symbDeriv(e₁), e₂), mul(e₁, symbDeriv(e₂)))`
- `symbDeriv(neg(e)) = neg(symbDeriv(e))`
- `symbDeriv(inv(e)) = neg(mul(symbDeriv(e), inv(mul(e,e))))`
- `symbDeriv(exp(e)) = mul(exp(e), symbDeriv(e))`
- `symbDeriv(log(e)) = mul(symbDeriv(e), inv(e))`

### 3.2 The Closure Theorem

**Theorem 3.2** (Differential Closure). *For any EML expression e, `depth(symbDeriv(e)) ≤ depth(e)`.*

*Proof sketch.* By structural induction on e. The key cases are:
- **exp(e)**: `symbDeriv = mul(exp(e), symbDeriv(e))`. The depth is `max(depth(e)+1, depth(symbDeriv(e)))`. By induction, `depth(symbDeriv(e)) ≤ depth(e) ≤ depth(e)+1`. So the max is `depth(e)+1 = depth(exp(e))`.
- **log(e)**: `symbDeriv = mul(symbDeriv(e), inv(e))`. The depth is `max(depth(symbDeriv(e)), depth(e))`. By induction, this is at most `depth(e) < depth(e)+1 = depth(log(e))`.
- **inv(e)**: `symbDeriv = neg(mul(symbDeriv(e), inv(mul(e,e))))`. All operations are algebraic (neg, mul, inv), so the depth equals `max(depth(symbDeriv(e)), depth(e)) ≤ depth(e) = depth(inv(e))` by induction.

This theorem extends immediately to iterated derivatives:

**Corollary 3.3** (Iterated Closure). *For any k ≥ 0, `depth(symbDerivIter(e, k)) ≤ depth(e)`.*

**Corollary 3.4** (Depth-0 Preservation). *If `depth(e) = 0`, then `depth(symbDeriv(e)) = 0`.*

This means that derivatives of rational functions remain rational — a well-known but now formally verified fact.

## 4. Abel's Identity and Wronskian Theory

### 4.1 Second-Order Linear ODEs

**Definition 4.1**. A *second-order linear ODE* is y'' + p(x)·y' + q(x)·y = 0. A solution is a function y satisfying this equation pointwise.

### 4.2 Abel's Identity

**Theorem 4.2** (Abel's Identity). *Let y₁, y₂ be solutions of y'' + p·y' + q·y = 0, both twice differentiable. Then the Wronskian W = y₁y₂' - y₂y₁' satisfies*

$$W'(x) = -p(x) \cdot W(x)$$

*Proof.* Computing directly:
$$W' = y₁'y₂' + y₁y₂'' - y₂'y₁' - y₂y₁'' = y₁y₂'' - y₂y₁''$$

From the ODE: $y_i'' = -py_i' - qy_i$. Substituting:
$$W' = y₁(-py₂' - qy₂) - y₂(-py₁' - qy₁) = -p(y₁y₂' - y₂y₁') = -pW$$

### 4.3 Consequences for EML Theory

When the coefficient p is zero (as in the Airy equation), Abel's identity gives W' = 0, so the Wronskian is constant. This provides a strong constraint: any two linearly independent solutions must have a non-vanishing Wronskian.

When p is an EML function of depth d, the solution W(x) = C·exp(-∫p(x)dx) involves an antiderivative of p. The antiderivative of an EML function of depth d may increase the depth (e.g., ∫exp(x)dx = exp(x) has depth 1, but ∫exp(exp(x))·exp(x)dx = exp(exp(x)) has depth 2). This observation motivates the study of antiderivative depth, which we leave for future work.

## 5. The EML Differential Operator Algebra

### 5.1 Definition

**Definition 5.1** (EML Differential Operator). A *second-order EML differential operator* is

$$L = a(x)D^2 + b(x)D + c(x)$$

where a, b, c are EML expressions and D = d/dx.

### 5.2 Bidimensional Filtration

**Definition 5.2**. The *operator depth* of L is `max(depth(a), depth(b), depth(c))`.

This defines a filtration:
$$\text{DiffOp}_{n,d} = \{L : \text{order}(L) \leq n, \text{depth}(L) \leq d\}$$

**Theorem 5.3** (Filtration Compatibility). *For operators L₁, L₂:*
$$\text{depth}(L₁ + L₂) \leq \max(\text{depth}(L₁), \text{depth}(L₂))$$

### 5.3 The Airy Operator

The Airy equation y'' = xy corresponds to the operator L = D² - x with depth 0. Despite having the simplest possible coefficients (polynomial), its solutions are transcendental functions that cannot be expressed in terms of elementary functions.

**Theorem 5.4**. *The Airy operator has depth 0. The operator equation L[f] = 0 is equivalent to the Airy ODE.*

## 6. The Airy Obstruction

### 6.1 Growth-Theoretic Analysis

The Airy equation y'' = xy has solutions Ai(x) and Bi(x) with asymptotic behavior:

$$\text{Ai}(x) \sim \frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi} x^{1/4}} \exp\left(-\frac{2}{3}x^{3/2}\right) \quad (x \to +\infty)$$

$$\text{Bi}(x) \sim \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi} x^{1/4}} \exp\left(\frac{2}{3}x^{3/2}\right) \quad (x \to +\infty)$$

The exponent $\frac{2}{3}x^{3/2}$ involves the fractional power $x^{3/2}$, which is NOT an EML function — it requires the operation $x \mapsto x^{3/2} = \exp(\frac{3}{2}\log x)$, which is a depth-1 EML expression but NOT a rational function.

### 6.2 The Obstruction Argument

The key insight is that depth-0 EML functions are rational functions, and their growth is polynomial. But Airy solutions grow super-polynomially (and indeed super-exponentially for large x due to the $x^{3/2}$ exponent).

More precisely:
1. Any depth-0 EML solution y would have rational derivatives y' and y'' (by the Depth-0 Preservation Theorem).
2. The ODE y'' = x·y then requires a rational function (y'') to equal x times a rational function (y).
3. This is consistent only if y is a rational function satisfying y'' = xy.
4. But no nonzero rational function satisfies y'' = xy (the degrees don't balance).

### 6.3 Formalization Status

We have formalized:
- The Airy equation as an EML ODE of depth 0
- The Depth-0 Preservation Theorem (derivatives of depth-0 expressions remain depth-0)
- Abel's identity for the Wronskian
- The operator equivalence between the Airy operator and the Airy ODE

The full transcendence proof (no EML function of *any* depth satisfies the Airy equation) requires deeper analysis of growth rates at infinity, which we identify as a key direction for future work.

## 7. Composition and Substitution Theory

### 7.1 Substitution Correctness

**Theorem 7.1**. *For EML expressions e and f: `eval(subst(e, f), x) = eval(e, eval(f, x))`.*

This says that syntactic substitution agrees with semantic composition — a fundamental correctness property.

### 7.2 Depth Subadditivity

**Theorem 7.2** (Depth Subadditivity). *`depth(subst(e, f)) ≤ depth(e) + depth(f)`.*

This means composing EML functions at most adds their depths. Combining with the Closure Theorem, we get:

**Corollary 7.3**. *If f is an EML function of depth d₁ and g is an EML function of depth d₂, then f ∘ g is EML of depth ≤ d₁ + d₂, and d/dx(f ∘ g) has depth ≤ d₁ + d₂.*

## 8. Discussion

### 8.1 The Significance of the Depth Filtration

The EML depth filtration provides a quantitative refinement of the classical division between algebraic and transcendental functions. Rather than a binary classification, it gives an infinite hierarchy:

$$\text{Rational} \subsetneq \text{Depth-1 EML} \subsetneq \text{Depth-2 EML} \subsetneq \cdots$$

Each level is closed under differentiation (Closure Theorem) and field operations (Algebra Closure). The depth serves as a complexity measure for transcendental functions that is:
- **Compositional**: depth(f ∘ g) ≤ depth(f) + depth(g)
- **Differential**: depth(f') ≤ depth(f)
- **Algebraic**: depth(f + g), depth(f · g) ≤ max(depth(f), depth(g))

### 8.2 Connection to Differential Galois Theory

In classical differential Galois theory, the Galois group of a linear ODE is an algebraic group, and solvability by Liouvillian functions corresponds to solvability of the Galois group. The EML depth filtration provides a finer invariant: the *minimum depth* needed to express a solution.

For the Airy equation, the differential Galois group is SL(2,ℂ) — a non-solvable group. This means the equation has no Liouvillian solutions, a fortiori no EML solutions. Our depth filtration gives a more elementary route to this conclusion through growth rate analysis.

### 8.3 Conjecture

**Conjecture 8.1** (EML Depth-Growth Correspondence). *An EML function of depth d has growth rate bounded by the (d+1)-fold iterated exponential tower: $|f(x)| \leq \exp^{(d+1)}(C|x|^N)$ for some constants C, N and sufficiently large |x|.*

This conjecture, if true, would provide a complete growth-theoretic obstruction theory for EML ODEs: if the solutions of an ODE grow faster than any iterated exponential tower, they cannot be EML.

**Testable prediction**: For the Airy equation, the growth exp(2x^{3/2}/3) is bounded by exp(exp(x)) for x ≥ 4.2 (depth-1 bound) but not by exp(Cx^N) for any C, N. This is consistent with Airy solutions not being depth-0 but potentially being depth-1 — which they are not, for the independent reason that x^{3/2} ≠ P(x)/Q(x).
