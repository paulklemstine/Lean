# Realization of All Even Incoherence Indices $\ge 4$ by Maximal Standard Frames

**Author:** Aristotle
**Date:** 2026-06-26
**Domain:** Applications (social choice / zero-sum combinatorics)

## Abstract

We study the *incoherence index* of *standard social decision frames*, modeled as
finite subsets of the cyclic group $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$. A frame's atoms are
admissible "majority-or-tie" outcomes; a *perfectly balanced sequence* is a
non-empty list of atoms summing to $0$; and the incoherence index is the length
of the shortest such sequence. We prove a *realization* theorem: for every even
integer $n \ge 4$ there is a **maximal** frame (one whose atoms generate
$\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$) with incoherence index exactly $n$. We complement this
with a *sharpness* result — $n$ is the greatest incoherence index attainable by
any non-empty frame on $n$ states, and it is attained — a *parity* theorem — any
frame all of whose atoms are odd under the parity character has even index — and
an *unboundedness* theorem — the spectrum of incoherence indices has no finite
ceiling. The realization witness is the sparsest possible maximal frame, the
single-generator frame $\{1\}$, whose index equals the additive order of $1$,
namely $n$. The development thereby reduces a question about deadlock in social
decision procedures to elementary additive order theory on cyclic groups.

## 1. Introduction

Social decision procedures aggregate individual preferences into collective
outcomes. A recurring structural concern is *incoherence*: the possibility that a
sequence of legitimate outcomes cancels perfectly, leaving the system in its
initial state and signalling a latent deadlock. The shortest length at which such
cancellation is forced is a quantitative robustness measure, the *incoherence
index*.

It is classical that incoherence indices are unbounded — no single integer caps
the index across all frames. This paper sharpens "unbounded" to "fully realized"
in the even regime. Our main theorem states that **every even $n \ge 4$ is the
incoherence index of a maximal frame**, and that $n$ is simultaneously the
largest index any frame on $n$ states can have. Thus the even spectrum is filled
without gaps, by structurally maximal witnesses.

The technical content is a translation: we model frames as finite subsets of
$\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ and balanced sequences as zero-sum sequences over that
group. Under this lens the incoherence index of a single-generator frame is the
additive order of the generator, which makes the extremal computation transparent
and the realization theorem a corollary of order theory.

### 1.1 Contributions

1. A precise cyclic-group model of standard social decision frames, balanced
   sequences, the incoherence index, and frame maximality (Definitions 1–5).
2. **Realization** (Theorem 9): every even $n \ge 4$ is the incoherence index of

3. **Sharpness** (Theorem 10): $n$ is the greatest index of any non-empty frame
   on $n$ states, and is attained.
4. **Parity** (Theorem 11): all-odd frames over even $n$ have even index.
5. **Unboundedness** (Theorem 12): the spectrum is unbounded.

All results are formally verified; this paper presents the mathematical content
with proof sketches.

## 2. Definitions

Throughout, $n$ is a positive integer and $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ denotes the
integers modulo $n$, written additively.

> **Definition 1 (Frame).** A *standard social decision frame* on $n$ social
> states is a finite set of atoms $F \subseteq \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$. Atoms
> represent admissible majority-or-tie residues.

> **Definition 2 (Balanced sequence).** A *perfectly balanced sequence* for a
> frame $F$ is a list $l = (a_1, \dots, a_k)$ of elements of
> $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ such that
> (i) $l$ is non-empty ($k \ge 1$);
> (ii) every entry lies in the frame, $a_i \in F$ for all $i$; and
> (iii) the entries sum to zero, $\sum_{i=1}^k a_i = 0$ in $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$.
> We write $\mathrm{IsBalanced}(F, l)$ for this predicate.

> **Definition 3 (Balanced lengths).** The set of *balanced lengths* of $F$ is
> $$\mathrm{BL}(F) = \{\, k \in \mathbb{N} : \exists\, l,\ \mathrm{IsBalanced}(F, l)\ \text{and}\ |l| = k \,\}.$$

> **Definition 4 (Incoherence index).** The *incoherence index* of $F$ is the
> infimum of its balanced lengths,
> $$\mathrm{index}(F) = \inf \mathrm{BL}(F),$$
> taken in $\mathbb{N}$ with the convention $\inf \emptyset = 0$. Equivalently, it
> is the length of a shortest perfectly balanced sequence when one exists, and $0$
> otherwise.

> **Definition 5 (Maximal frame).** A frame $F$ is *maximal* if its atoms generate
> the whole decision space:
> $$\langle F \rangle = \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z},$$
> where $\langle F \rangle$ is the additive subgroup generated by $F$. Maximal
> frames are exactly those with no unreachable social state.

The choice $\inf\emptyset = 0$ (matching `Nat.sInf` on the empty set) means that
index $0$ encodes "no balanced sequence exists." Every result below concerns
frames that do admit balanced sequences, for which the index is a genuine
positive length.

## 3. Foundational lemmas

> **Lemma 6 (Maximality of the unit frame; `isMaximal_singleton_one`).** For every
> $n \ge 1$, the single-generator frame $\{1\} \subseteq \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ is
> maximal.

*Proof sketch.* The subgroup generated by $\{1\}$ contains $1$, hence contains
$k \cdot 1$ for every $k$, i.e. every residue. Concretely, an arbitrary
$x \in \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ equals $x.\mathrm{val} \cdot 1$, exhibiting $x$ in
$\langle \{1\} \rangle$ via `AddSubgroup.mem_closure_singleton`. Thus
$\langle \{1\} \rangle = \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$. $\square$

> **Lemma 7 (Universal ceiling; `incoherenceIndex_le`).** For $n \ge 1$ and any
> non-empty frame $F$, $\mathrm{index}(F) \le n$.

*Proof sketch.* Pick any atom $a \in F$. The constant list $\mathrm{replicate}(n,a)$
of length $n$ has all entries in $F$, is non-empty, and sums to
$n \cdot a = 0$ in $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$. Hence $n \in \mathrm{BL}(F)$, and
since the index is the infimum of $\mathrm{BL}(F)$ we get
$\mathrm{index}(F) \le n$. $\square$

> **Lemma 8 (Index of the unit frame; `incoherenceIndex_singleton_one`).** For
> $n \ge 1$,
> $$\mathrm{index}(\{1\}) = n.$$

*Proof sketch.* We prove $\le$ and $\ge$.

*Upper bound.* By Lemma 7, $\mathrm{index}(\{1\}) \le n$.

*Lower bound.* Let $l$ be any balanced sequence for $\{1\}$ of length $k = |l|$.
Every entry of $l$ must equal $1$ (the only atom), so $l = \mathrm{replicate}(k,1)$
and $l.\mathrm{sum} = k \cdot 1$. Viewing this through
$\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$, the condition $l.\mathrm{sum} = 0$ reads
$n \mid k$ (via `ZMod.natCast_eq_zero_iff`). Since $l$ is non-empty, $k \ge 1$,
and $n \mid k$ forces $k \ge n$ (`Nat.le_of_dvd`). Hence every balanced length is
$\ge n$, so $\inf \mathrm{BL}(\{1\}) \ge n$.

Combining the two bounds gives equality. $\square$

Lemma 8 is the arithmetic heart of the paper: the index of $\{1\}$ is precisely
the additive order of $1$ in $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$, which is $n$.

## 4. Main results

> **Theorem 9 (Realization; `realization_even`).** For every even integer
> $n \ge 4$ there exists a maximal standard social decision frame
> $F \subseteq \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ with $\mathrm{index}(F) = n$.

*Proof sketch.* Take $F = \{1\}$. Since $n \ge 4 > 0$ we have $\mathrm{NeZero}\,n$.
By Lemma 6, $F$ is maximal; by Lemma 8, $\mathrm{index}(F) = n$. $\square$

*Remark.* The hypothesis $\mathrm{Even}(n)$ is the conjecture's explicit
constraint and is retained for faithfulness to the original statement, but the
construction works verbatim for every $n \ge 1$. The substantive content of the
theorem is therefore that the even values $4, 6, 8, \dots$ are *all* realized, and
realized by maximal frames.

> **Theorem 10 (Sharpness; `incoherenceIndex_isGreatest`).** For every even
> integer $n \ge 4$, the number $n$ is the greatest element of the set
> $$S_n = \{\, k : \exists\, F \subseteq \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}\ \text{non-empty with}\ \mathrm{index}(F) = k \,\},$$
> i.e. $n \in S_n$ and $k \le n$ for all $k \in S_n$.

*Proof sketch.* *Membership:* the frame $\{1\}$ is non-empty and has index $n$
(Lemma 8), so $n \in S_n$. *Upper bound:* if $k \in S_n$, witnessed by a
non-empty frame $F$ with $\mathrm{index}(F) = k$, then Lemma 7 gives $k \le n$.
Hence $n = \max S_n$. $\square$

Theorems 9 and 10 together establish that, on $n$ social states, the incoherence
index ranges over a set whose maximum is exactly $n$, and that this maximum is
realized by a maximal frame — there is no slack between the theoretical ceiling
and what is achievable.

> **Theorem 11 (Parity; `even_incoherenceIndex`).** Let $n$ be even, and let
> $\chi : \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ be the parity
> character (the canonical ring homomorphism induced by $2 \mid n$). If every atom
> of a frame $F$ is *odd*, meaning $\chi(a) = 1$ for all $a \in F$, then
> $\mathrm{index}(F)$ is even.

*Proof sketch.* Two cases.

*If $F$ admits no balanced sequence,* then $\mathrm{BL}(F) = \emptyset$ and
$\mathrm{index}(F) = \inf \emptyset = 0$, which is even.

*If $F$ admits a balanced sequence,* we show every balanced length is even. Let
$l = (a_1, \dots, a_k)$ be balanced. Applying the homomorphism $\chi$ and using
that it commutes with sums (`List.sum_hom`),
$$\chi\!\left(\sum_i a_i\right) = \sum_i \chi(a_i).$$
The left side is $\chi(0) = 0$. On the right, each $\chi(a_i) = 1$ since every
atom is odd, so the right side is $k \cdot 1 = k \pmod 2$. Therefore $k \equiv 0
\pmod 2$, i.e. $k$ is even. Every element of $\mathrm{BL}(F)$ is even; in
particular the infimum, which is attained (a least element of a non-empty set of
naturals, `Nat.sInf_mem`), is even. $\square$

Theorem 11 explains why the realization problem is naturally posed for *even*
indices: the all-odd frames over even $n$ form an entire family confined to the
even spectrum, and Theorem 9 shows their attainable indices reach every even
value $\ge 4$.

> **Theorem 12 (Unboundedness; `incoherence_unbounded`).** For every $N \in
> \mathbb{N}$ there exist $n$ and a frame $F \subseteq \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ with
> $\mathrm{index}(F)$ even and $\mathrm{index}(F) > N$.

*Proof sketch.* Choose an even $n > N$ with $n \ge 4$ (e.g. the least even number
exceeding $\max(N, 3)$). The unit frame $\{1\} \subseteq \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$
has index $n$ by Lemma 8, which is even and exceeds $N$. (More structurally, any
all-odd maximal frame works: Theorem 11 supplies evenness and Theorem 9 supplies
a value $\ge 4$ that can be taken above $N$.) Hence the spectrum of incoherence
indices has no finite upper bound. $\square$

## 5. The saturation contrast

The realization witness is the *sparsest* maximal frame. It is instructive that
*crowding* a maximal frame strictly lowers the index, so maximality alone does
not determine it.

**Worked example.** On $\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ consider $F = \{1, 3\}$. Both $1$
and $3$ generate $\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$, so $F$ is maximal. But $1 + 3 = 4
\equiv 0$, so $(1,3)$ is a balanced sequence of length $2$, and no length-$1$
balanced sequence exists ($1 \ne 0$, $3 \ne 0$). Hence $\mathrm{index}(\{1,3\}) =
2$, strictly below the index $\mathrm{index}(\{1\}) = 4$ of the sparse maximal
frame on the same clock.

This contrast isolates the structural mechanism: balanced sequences for $F$
remain balanced for any superset $G \supseteq F$, so enlarging the atom set can
only add balanced lengths and hence can only lower the infimum. The extremal
index $n$ is therefore achieved by *sparseness within maximality*, not by
maximality per se.

## 6. Algorithmic computation of the index

The model is fully effective. Given $n$ and a frame $F$, the incoherence index is
computable by a breadth-first search over partial sums in $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$.

**Idea.** A balanced sequence of length $k$ is a walk on the state graph whose
vertices are residues $0, 1, \dots, n-1$, with an edge $s \to s + a$ for each atom
$a \in F$. A balanced sequence is a non-empty walk from $0$ back to $0$. The
shortest one is the shortest non-trivial closed walk through $0$, found by BFS.

**Algorithm (Shortest Balanced Sequence via Residue BFS).**
```
Input:  n, frame F ⊆ {0,…,n-1}
Output: incoherence index of F (0 if none)

if F is empty: return 0
frontier ← { a mod n : a ∈ F }        # reachable partial sums after 1 step
if 0 ∈ frontier: return 1
visited ← frontier ∪ {0-as-start-marker handled separately}
length ← 1
while frontier nonempty and length ≤ n:
    next ← {}
    for s in frontier:
        for a in F:
            t ← (s + a) mod n
            if t == 0: return length + 1
            if t not in visited:
                add t to visited and to next
    frontier ← next
    length ← length + 1
return 0
```

The search visits each of the $n$ residues at most once, so it terminates in
$O(n \cdot |F|)$ time and $O(n)$ space. By Lemma 7 the answer never exceeds $n$,
so capping the loop at length $n$ loses nothing.

## 7. Applications and interpretation

- **Robustness budgeting.** $\mathrm{index}(F)$ is the minimum number of outcomes
  before a procedure can return to its initial state. Designers seeking maximal
  delay-before-deadlock should choose sparse maximal frames; Theorem 9 says any
  even target $\ge 4$ is achievable and Theorem 10 says $n$ is the best possible
  on $n$ states.
- **Parity diagnostics.** Theorem 11 gives a quick certificate: if every
  admissible outcome is odd under the parity character, the deadlock length is
  guaranteed even — useful for ruling out odd-length cancellations by inspection.
- **Zero-sum dictionary.** The incoherence index is exactly the minimal length of
  a zero-sum sequence over $F$ in $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$, linking the model to
  the Davenport constant and zero-sum Ramsey theory; the single-atom case is the
  additive order $n/\gcd(n,a)$.

## 8. Discussion

The arc of the development is from *qualitative* to *quantitative*. Unboundedness
(Theorem 12) says the indices have no ceiling; realization (Theorem 9) plus
sharpness (Theorem 10) describe the even spectrum exactly and identify the
extremal frames. The surprising structural fact is that maximal expressiveness
and maximal incoherence index coincide at the *sparsest* generator, while
crowding a maximal frame collapses the index (§5). The proofs reduce throughout
to additive order theory, making the social-choice phenomenon a faithful shadow
of elementary group arithmetic.

## 10. Conclusion

We have given a complete description of the even incoherence spectrum for standard
social decision frames: every even $n \ge 4$ is realized by a maximal frame
(Theorem 9), $n$ is the sharp maximum on $n$ states (Theorem 10), all-odd frames
are confined to even indices (Theorem 11), and the spectrum is unbounded
(Theorem 12). The single-generator frame $\{1\}$ — sparse yet maximal — is the
universal extremal witness, and its index is simply the additive order of $1$.
