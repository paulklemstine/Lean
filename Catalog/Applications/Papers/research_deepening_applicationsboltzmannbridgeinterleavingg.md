# The Path Space of Filtrations: Reparametrisation Closure, Geodesic Betweenness, and Busemann Convexity of the Interleaving Metric

## Abstract

The interleaving distance is the canonical metric of topological data analysis: it
measures how far apart two multiscale "shape biographies" (filtrations) are by the
smallest resolution shift that sandwiches each inside the other. Prior work in this
program established that the space of filtrations, equipped with the *extended*
interleaving distance, is (i) a genuine extended metric space with attained
infimum, (ii) isometric to the space of weight functions under the supremum
distance, and (iii) **geodesic**, via the explicit convex-interpolation path
`lerp(F, G, t)` whose interleaving distance grows exactly linearly in the parameter.

This paper studies the *path space* generated by these geodesics. We prove four
structural results that organize the geodesics into a coherent geometric object.
First, an **algebraic** law: the geodesic family is closed under reparametrisation
and composition — a linear interpolation of two points on the `F`–`G` geodesic is
again a point on that geodesic, at an affine parameter. Second, a **metric** law:
the full geodesic-segment additivity `d(s,u) + d(u,t) = d(s,t)` for any ordered
`s ≤ u ≤ t`, generalizing midpoint bisection. Third, an **analytic** law: Busemann
convexity of the distance function, `d(H, lerp(F,G,t)) ≤ (1−t)·d(H,F) + t·d(H,G)`,
the signature of non-positive curvature. Fourth, a bundled existence statement
certifying that the space is geodesic with a constant-speed clamped path. We isolate
the unifying principle — *geodesy is the sharp diagonal of convexity* — and discuss
consequences for shape averaging, topological barycenters, and optimization on shape
space.

All results are fully formalized and machine-checked, depending only on the standard
foundational axioms (propositional extensionality, the axiom of choice, and quotient
soundness); the sorry-count is zero.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and motivation

Topological data analysis (TDA) extracts qualitative shape from quantitative data by
tracking the evolution of a combinatorial complex across a range of scales. The
organizing object is a **filtration**: a nested family of simplicial complexes
indexed by a real scale parameter, recording when each simplex is "born." The
distance between two filtrations that the field has converged upon is the
**interleaving distance**, which underlies the celebrated stability theorems
guaranteeing that the topological summary of data is robust to perturbation.

This robustness is qualitative. The aim of the present line of work is to upgrade it
to a *quantitative, geometric* theory of the space of filtrations itself: not merely
"nearby data yields nearby summaries," but "the totality of filtrations forms a
well-behaved metric geometry in which one can interpolate, average, and optimize."

The immediate predecessor result established that the filtration space is
**geodesic**: between any two filtrations there is a shortest path realizing their
distance. The contribution of this paper is to study the *path space* — the
collection of all such geodesics — and to prove that it carries the three
structural layers (algebraic, metric, analytic) characteristic of a mature geodesic
geometry.

### 1.2 Contributions

We prove and formalize:

1. **`lerp_self`** — the degenerate geodesic: `lerp(F, F, t) = F`.
2. **`lerp_lerp`** — reparametrisation closure of the geodesic family.
3. **`eInterleavingDist_lerp_betweenness`** — the geodesic-segment additivity law.
4. **`eInterleavingDist_convex`** — Busemann convexity of the interleaving distance.
5. **`exists_constantSpeed_geodesic`** — a bundled certificate that the space is
   geodesic.

We further identify the conceptual through-line: the constant-speed *equality* of
the geodesic identity is exactly the convexity *inequality* restricted to the
endpoints' own geodesic, where the supremum's non-maximising slack vanishes.

## 2. Definitions

Throughout, `α` is an arbitrary vertex type and simplices are finite subsets
`σ ∈ Finset α`. Distances take values in the extended nonnegative reals
`ℝ≥0∞ = [0, ∞]`; `ofReal : ℝ → ℝ≥0∞` denotes the truncation-at-zero embedding
(`ofReal x = max(x, 0)` reinterpreted in `ℝ≥0∞`).

### Definition 2.1 (Filtration)

A **filtration** on `α` is a function `weight : Finset α → ℝ` satisfying

- **(grounding)** `weight(∅) ≤ 0`, and
- **(monotonicity)** `σ ⊆ τ ⟹ weight(σ) ≤ weight(τ)`.

Intuitively `weight(σ)` is the scale at which simplex `σ` is born; monotonicity
ensures that the sublevel set `{σ : weight(σ) ≤ t}` is a downward-closed abstract
simplicial complex for each `t`, so the family `t ↦ {weight ≤ t}` is a bona fide
filtration of complexes. Two filtrations are equal iff their weight functions agree
(extensionality, `ext_weight`).

### Definition 2.2 (Sublevel family)

For a filtration `F` and scale `t ∈ ℝ`, the **sublevel family** is
`sublevelFaces(F, t) = {σ : weight_F(σ) ≤ t}`. For `t ≥ 0` this set is an abstract
simplicial complex (it contains `∅` by grounding and is downward closed by
monotonicity).

### Definition 2.3 (δ-interleaving)

For `δ ≥ 0`, filtrations `F` and `G` are **δ-interleaved**, written
`Interleaved(F, G, δ)`, when

- `0 ≤ δ`,
- for all `t`, `sublevelFaces(F, t) ⊆ sublevelFaces(G, t + δ)`, and
- for all `t`, `sublevelFaces(G, t) ⊆ sublevelFaces(F, t + δ)`.

Each sublevel family of one filtration is contained in the other's after a uniform
`δ`-shift of scale. This is the combinatorial form of an interleaving of persistence
modules.

### Definition 2.4 (Extended interleaving distance)

The **extended interleaving distance** is

> `eInterleavingDist(F, G) = ⨅ { ofReal δ : Interleaved(F, G, δ) }`,

the infimum over all admissible shifts. When no interleaving exists the infimum is
over the empty family and equals `⊤`; this is the correct value and is precisely why
the *extended* (`ℝ≥0∞`-valued) formulation is used rather than a real-valued infimum,
which would corrupt the triangle inequality via the convention `inf ∅ = 0`.

### Definition 2.5 (Extended supremum distance)

The **extended supremum distance** between the weight functions is

> `weightSupEDist(F, G) = ⨆_{σ : Finset α} ofReal |weight_F(σ) − weight_G(σ)|`.

The index `Finset α` is always nonempty (it contains `∅`).

### Definition 2.6 (The interpolation path `lerp`)

For `0 ≤ t ≤ 1`, the filtration `lerp(F, G, t)` is defined by

> `weight_{lerp(F,G,t)}(σ) = (1 − t) · weight_F(σ) + t · weight_G(σ)`.

The convexity hypotheses `0 ≤ t` and `t ≤ 1` are exactly what make the combination a
valid filtration: grounding follows because `(1−t)·weight_F(∅) + t·weight_G(∅) ≤ 0`
when both endpoints are `≤ 0` and both coefficients are `≥ 0`; monotonicity follows
by taking the same nonnegative convex combination of the two endpoint monotonicity
inequalities.

## 3. Inherited results (the foundation)

The present paper builds on four established facts, which we state for self-
containment. Their proofs are not the subject of this paper, but each is essential to
what follows.

### Theorem 3.1 (Stability / isometry)

> `eInterleavingDist(F, G) = weightSupEDist(F, G)`.

That is, the interleaving distance equals the worst-case coordinatewise gap between
the weight functions. *Proof idea.* Two inclusions. (≥) Every interleaving witness
`δ` dominates every simplexwise gap, because membership of `σ` in a sublevel set is
governed by its weight; hence `ofReal|weight_F(σ) − weight_G(σ)| ≤ ofReal δ` for all
`σ`, and taking sup then inf gives `weightSupEDist ≤ eInterleavingDist`. (≤) When the
sup is finite, its real value `c` is itself an admissible shift: `|weight_F(σ) −
weight_G(σ)| ≤ c` for all `σ` is exactly the condition that `F` and `G` are
`c`-interleaved (the "weight-close-by" characterization of interleaving), so
`eInterleavingDist ≤ ofReal c = weightSupEDist`. The infimum is therefore attained. ∎

This isometry is the workhorse of the entire development: it converts every statement
about the abstract interleaving distance into a statement about a supremum of
absolute differences, where elementary real analysis applies.

### Theorem 3.2 (Endpoints of the path)

> `lerp(F, G, 0) = F` and `lerp(F, G, 1) = G`.

*Proof.* At `t = 0` the weight is `1·weight_F + 0·weight_G = weight_F`; at `t = 1` it
is `0·weight_F + 1·weight_G = weight_G`. Apply extensionality. ∎

### Theorem 3.3 (Linearity of weight gaps)

> For `s, t ∈ [0,1]` and every `σ`,
> `|weight_{lerp(s)}(σ) − weight_{lerp(t)}(σ)| = |s − t| · |weight_F(σ) − weight_G(σ)|`.

*Proof.* The difference of the two interpolated weights equals
`(t − s)·(weight_F(σ) − weight_G(σ))` by direct algebra; take absolute values and use
`|t − s| = |s − t|`. ∎

### Theorem 3.4 (Constant-speed geodesic identity)

> For `s, t ∈ [0,1]`,
> `eInterleavingDist(lerp(F,G,s), lerp(F,G,t)) = ofReal|s − t| · eInterleavingDist(F, G)`.

*Proof.* Rewrite both sides through the isometry (Theorem 3.1) into supremum
distances. By Theorem 3.3, every term of the supremum defining
`weightSupEDist(lerp(s), lerp(t))` is `ofReal(|s−t| · |weight_F(σ) − weight_G(σ)|)`.
The constant scalar `ofReal|s − t|` pulls out of the supremum (`ENNReal.mul_iSup`),
leaving exactly `ofReal|s−t| · weightSupEDist(F, G)`. ∎

Theorem 3.4 makes `lerp` a *constant-speed geodesic*: distance along the path is
linear in the parameter, so the path is the shortest one and is traversed at uniform
speed. It is the launch point of this paper.

## 4. Main results: the path space

We now state and sketch the four new structural results. They share a common
strategy: reduce to the supremum distance via Theorem 3.1, then perform elementary
manipulations on real absolute values, finally repackaging in `ℝ≥0∞`.

### 4.1 The degenerate geodesic

### Theorem 4.1 (`lerp_self`)

> For all `t ∈ [0,1]`, `lerp(F, F, t) = F`.

*Proof.* The interpolated weight is `(1 − t)·weight_F(σ) + t·weight_F(σ) =
weight_F(σ)` for every `σ`, by the ring identity `(1−t) + t = 1`. Apply
extensionality. ∎

This is the base case of the path algebra: the constant path at `F` is a geodesic,
the identity of the would-be groupoid.

### 4.2 Reparametrisation closure (the algebraic law)

### Theorem 4.2 (`lerp_lerp`)

> For all `a, b, t ∈ [0,1]`,
> `lerp(lerp(F,G,a), lerp(F,G,b), t) = lerp(F, G, (1 − t)·a + t·b)`,
> where the new parameter `(1 − t)·a + t·b` lies in `[0,1]` (a convex combination of
> two points of `[0,1]`).

*Proof.* Expand both interpolation layers on a fixed simplex `σ`. The left-hand side
has weight
```
(1 − t)·[(1 − a)·F + a·G] + t·[(1 − b)·F + b·G]
```
(suppressing the argument `σ` and writing `F = weight_F(σ)`). Collecting the
coefficient of `G` gives `c := (1 − t)·a + t·b`; the coefficient of `F` is then
`1 − c`. Hence the weight is `(1 − c)·F + c·G`, which is exactly the weight of
`lerp(F, G, c)(σ)`. The new parameter lies in `[0,1]` because it is a convex
combination of `a, b ∈ [0,1]` (verified by `nlinarith` from `0 ≤ a,b ≤ 1`,
`0 ≤ t ≤ 1`). Apply extensionality. ∎

**Interpretation.** Geodesics are closed under composition and re-timing: a geodesic
between two points of a geodesic is a sub-segment of the original. The affine
parameter `(1−t)·a + t·b` is precisely the formula for traversing the sub-interval
`[a, b]` of `[0,1]` at fraction `t`. This is the combinatorial skeleton of a
fundamental groupoid — paths compose to paths, and reparametrisations never leave the
family. There are no "new" geodesics generated by iterating the construction.

### 4.3 The geodesic-segment law (the metric law)

### Theorem 4.3 (`eInterleavingDist_lerp_betweenness`)

> For `s ≤ u ≤ t` in `[0,1]`,
> ```
> eInterleavingDist(lerp(s), lerp(u)) + eInterleavingDist(lerp(u), lerp(t))
>   = eInterleavingDist(lerp(s), lerp(t)).
> ```

*Proof.* Apply the constant-speed identity (Theorem 3.4) to each of the three
distances, abbreviating `d := eInterleavingDist(F, G)`:
```
ofReal|s − u|·d + ofReal|u − t|·d = ofReal|s − t|·d.
```
Because `s ≤ u ≤ t`, the absolute values resolve to `u − s`, `t − u`, and `t − s`
respectively. Factor `d` out of the left side (`add_mul` in reverse) and combine the
two `ofReal` terms (`ENNReal.ofReal_add`, valid since both arguments are
nonnegative): `ofReal((u − s) + (t − u)) = ofReal(t − s)`. The telescoping
`(u − s) + (t − u) = t − s` finishes the proof. ∎

**Interpretation.** The intermediate point `lerp(u)` lies *metrically between* the
endpoints: the path accrues no slack, no detour. This is the full additivity
characterization of a geodesic segment (Menger betweenness), strengthening the
special case of midpoint bisection (`u = 1/2`, `s = 0`, `t = 1`) to arbitrary
ordered triples.

### 4.4 Busemann convexity (the analytic law)

### Theorem 4.4 (`eInterleavingDist_convex`)

> For every filtration `H` and every `t ∈ [0,1]`,
> ```
> eInterleavingDist(H, lerp(F,G,t))
>   ≤ ofReal(1 − t)·eInterleavingDist(H, F) + ofReal t · eInterleavingDist(H, G).
> ```

*Proof.* Rewrite all three distances through the isometry (Theorem 3.1) to supremum
distances. It suffices to bound the supremand for a fixed simplex `σ`. The key
pointwise inequality is
```
|H(σ) − ((1 − t)·F(σ) + t·G(σ))| ≤ (1 − t)·|H(σ) − F(σ)| + t·|H(σ) − G(σ)|,
```
(again suppressing `σ` and writing `H = weight_H(σ)`, etc.). This is the convexity of
the absolute value: writing `H = (1−t)·H + t·H`, the inner expression is
`(1−t)·(H − F) + t·(H − G)`, and the triangle inequality with nonnegative weights
`1 − t, t` gives the bound. (Formally it is dispatched by case analysis on the three
signs via `abs_cases`/`nlinarith`.) Pushing through `ofReal` (additive and
multiplicative on nonnegative reals via `ENNReal.ofReal_add` and `ENNReal.ofReal_mul`)
and bounding each per-`σ` term by the corresponding supremum (`le_iSup`, monotone
under `gcongr`) yields the result after taking the supremum over `σ` on the left
(`iSup_le`). ∎

**Interpretation.** The distance to a fixed point is a convex function along the
geodesic. Convexity of the metric is the defining inequality of **Busemann
non-positive curvature** (a CAT(0)-flavoured condition): geodesics spread apart at
least linearly, distance functions have no local maxima along geodesics, and nearest-
point projections onto geodesics are well-behaved. The filtration space inherits this
benign curvature directly from the sup-metric through the isometry.

### 4.5 The space is geodesic

### Theorem 4.5 (`exists_constantSpeed_geodesic`)

> For all filtrations `F, G` there exists a path `γ : ℝ → Filtration α` with
> `γ(0) = F`, `γ(1) = G`, and for all `s, t ∈ [0,1]`,
> `eInterleavingDist(γ(s), γ(t)) = ofReal|s − t| · eInterleavingDist(F, G)`.

*Proof.* Take `γ(r) = lerp(F, G, clamp(r))` where `clamp(r) = min(1, max(0, r))`
projects `r` into `[0,1]` (so the path is total on `ℝ`). On `[0,1]` the clamp is the
identity, so `γ(0) = lerp(F,G,0) = F` and `γ(1) = lerp(F,G,1) = G` by Theorem 3.2,
and for `s, t ∈ [0,1]` the constant-speed identity (Theorem 3.4) applies verbatim. ∎

This is the textbook certificate that `(Filtration α, eInterleavingDist)` is a
geodesic space, packaging the explicit constant-speed geodesic into a single bundled
existence statement.

## 5. The unifying principle: geodesy is the sharp diagonal of convexity

The four results above are logically independent — algebraic, metric, analytic — yet
they spring from one structural fact about suprema. At the level of a *single*
simplex `σ`, the whole development reduces to the elementary convexity of the
absolute value:
```
|weight_H(σ) − ((1−t)·weight_F(σ) + t·weight_G(σ))|
      ≤ (1 − t)·|weight_H(σ) − weight_F(σ)| + t·|weight_H(σ) − weight_G(σ)|,   (★)
```
with equality precisely when `weight_H(σ) − weight_F(σ)` and
`weight_H(σ) − weight_G(σ)` have the *same sign*. The interleaving distance then takes
a **supremum** of such per-simplex quantities. Whether the global statement is an
equality or a strict inequality is governed entirely by one question: *is the
maximising simplex shared across the three suprema being compared?*

- **Collinear points (equality).** When all points compared lie on the *same*
  geodesic — as in the constant-speed identity (Theorem 3.4) and the betweenness law
  (Theorem 4.3) — every simplex moves along its own one-dimensional straight line at
  a rate proportional to its fixed endpoint gap `|weight_F(σ) − weight_G(σ)|`. The
  per-simplex relation `|weight_{lerp(s)}(σ) − weight_{lerp(t)}(σ)| =
  |s − t|·|weight_F(σ) − weight_G(σ)|` is an *exact* scaling, so the supremum's
  maximiser is the *same* fixed simplex (the `|F − G|`-maximiser) for every pair
  `s, t`. The scalar `ofReal|s − t|` factors straight out of the supremum and the
  inequality becomes an equality — no slack.

- **External observer (inequality).** For a generic third filtration `H`, the
  maximisers of `d(H, F)`, `d(H, G)`, and `d(H, lerp(F,G,t))` may be *different*
  simplices, and the sign condition in (★) may fail at the maximiser. The out-of-
  phase contributions then create a genuine **convexity defect**
  ```
  δ(H, F, G, t) := ofReal(1 − t)·d(H,F) + ofReal t · d(H,G) − d(H, lerp(F,G,t)) ≥ 0,
  ```
  which is strictly positive in general.

So *geodesy is the sharp (equality) face of convexity*: the constant-speed and
betweenness equalities are exactly the convexity relation (★) in the regime where the
supremum's maximising simplex is shared, while Busemann convexity is the same relation
in the regime where maximisers compete. This single asymmetry — *shared* worst-case
simplex versus *competing* worst-case simplices — also explains the failure of
geodesic uniqueness: the slack in non-maximising simplices is free to wander,
producing a convex family of distinct geodesics between a fixed pair.

## 6. Algorithms

Because the isometry (Theorem 3.1) reduces everything to finite suprema of absolute
differences, all quantities below are directly computable on finite simplex sets.

### Algorithm 6.1 (Interleaving distance via the isometry)

Given two filtrations specified by their weights on a finite simplex collection `S`,
compute `max_{σ ∈ S} |weight_F(σ) − weight_G(σ)|`. By Theorem 3.1 this equals the
interleaving distance. Complexity: `O(|S|)`.

### Algorithm 6.2 (Geodesic evaluation `lerp`)

Given `F, G`, a parameter `t ∈ [0,1]`, and a simplex `σ`, return
`(1 − t)·weight_F(σ) + t·weight_G(σ)`. Complexity: `O(1)` per simplex, `O(|S|)` to
materialize the whole interpolated filtration.

### Algorithm 6.3 (Betweenness / convexity-defect verification)

Given `F, G, H` and parameters, evaluate the left and right sides of Theorems 4.3 and
4.4 numerically using Algorithms 6.1–6.2; verify the additivity equality
(to floating tolerance) and the convexity inequality, and report the convexity defect
`δ`. Complexity: `O(|S|)`.

These algorithms turn the abstract geometry into a concrete numerical laboratory; the
accompanying demonstration code exercises each one.

## 7. Applications

- **Topological shape averaging.** The midpoint `lerp(F, G, 1/2)` is a canonical
  Fréchet-type average of two filtrations; the geodesic-segment law guarantees it
  bisects the distance, and convexity guarantees the averaging map contracts.
- **Barycenters and consensus.** For an ensemble of noisy filtrations, convexity of
  each distance function along geodesics (Theorem 4.4) makes the Fréchet functional
  `H ↦ Σ_i d(H, F_i)²` geodesically convex, the precise hypothesis under which
  barycenters exist, are essentially unique, and are computable by descent.
- **Interpolation and morphing.** Constant-speed paths provide controlled
  interpolation between snapshots of an evolving structure (e.g. successive frames of
  a deforming material), filling in missing time-steps without topological artefacts.
- **Optimization on shape space.** Non-positive-curvature-flavoured convexity is the
  ideal setting for gradient-style optimization: no spurious local optima along
  geodesics, well-defined projections, and provable convergence guarantees imported
  from CAT(0)/Busemann theory.

## 8. Discussion

The path space of filtrations is unusual in that its three structural layers are
*provably* aligned through a single elementary mechanism — the behaviour of suprema —
rather than coincidentally co-occurring. The reduction-to-supremum strategy (via the
isometry of Theorem 3.1) is both the proof engine and the conceptual key: it
explains why the geometry is so clean (it is the sup-metric in disguise) and where
its subtlety lives (the worst-case simplex can change with the observer).

A limitation worth stating plainly: geodesics are *not* unique. Because the metric is
a supremum, the non-maximising simplices carry slack that can be perturbed without
changing distances, so `lerp` is one geodesic among a convex family. Uniqueness fails
exactly when the worst-case simplex is not shared — the same asymmetry that separates
equality from inequality in Section 5. This is characteristic of `ℓ∞`-type geometries
and is the price of the worst-case (rather than aggregate) definition of distance.

## 9. Future work

A detailed program of future directions accompanies this work (see the
`future_directions` field of the package metadata). Highlights:

- **The convexity defect and non-unique geodesy.** Construct explicit triples
  `(F, G, H)` and parameters `t ∈ (0,1)` with strictly positive convexity defect
  `δ(H,F,G,t) > 0`, and exhibit genuinely distinct constant-speed geodesics between a
  fixed pair, quantifying the size of the geodesic family.
- **Curvature beyond convexity.** Determine whether the filtration space satisfies the
  stronger CAT(0) four-point or comparison-triangle conditions, or only the weaker
  Busemann convexity, and characterize the boundary precisely.
- **Geodesics inside the Vietoris–Rips locus.** Ask whether the interpolation of two
  diameter-type (geometric) filtrations remains geometric — the combinatorial-versus-
  geometric frontier of the theory.
- **A homotopical / fundamental-groupoid treatment.** Promote the reparametrisation
  algebra (`lerp_lerp`, `lerp_self`) to a full path-space and groupoid structure,
  including homotopies of paths and their compositions.

## 10. Conclusion

We have shown that the geodesics of the interleaving metric organize into a path space
with three mutually reinforcing structures: reparametrisation closure (algebra),
geodesic-segment additivity (metric), and Busemann convexity (analysis), together
with a bundled certificate that the space is geodesic. All four flow from one
principle — *geodesy is the sharp diagonal of convexity* — rooted in the elementary
behaviour of suprema under the isometry to the sup-metric. The space of multiscale
data shapes is thus not merely a metric space but a geodesic, non-positively-curved-
flavoured geometry, ready to host averaging, interpolation, and optimization.
