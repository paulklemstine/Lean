# Berggren Voronoi–CVP Duality via Primitive Triple Delaunay Complexes and Certified Decoding

## Abstract

We establish a formal bridge between Berggren-generated families of primitive Pythagorean triples, Voronoi/Delaunay geometry, and certified closest-vector decoding. For any finite Berggren family S_H (triples with hypotenuse ≤ H), equipped with a quadratic defect functional, we prove:
1. Voronoi cell membership is equivalent to certified nearest decoding (Theorem 1).
2. Every target vector admits a defect-minimizing triple with a verifiable certificate (Theorem 2).
3. Decoding is unique when the defect margin is positive (Theorem 3).
4. Certified decoding is stable under perturbation, with explicit tolerance bounds (Theorem 4).
5. Delaunay-adjacent triples share co-minimizing witnesses (Theorem 5).
6. Certified nearest decoding reduces to the closest vector problem on the embedded family (Theorem 6).
7. Berggren matrices preserve the Lorentz quadratic form (Theorems 7–9).

All results are machine-verified in Lean 4 with Mathlib, using no axioms beyond the standard foundational ones.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Motivation

Primitive Pythagorean triples — integer solutions (a, b, c) to a² + b² = c² with gcd(a,b) = 1 — have been studied since antiquity. Berggren (1934) showed they form a ternary tree rooted at (3, 4, 5), generated by three 3×3 integer matrices A, B, C that preserve the Lorentz form c² − a² − b².

Separately, Voronoi diagrams partition space into cells around a set of sites, with each cell containing points nearest to that site. The closely related Delaunay complex encodes adjacency between Voronoi cells. These structures are fundamental in computational geometry, with applications ranging from mesh generation to nearest-neighbor search.

The closest vector problem (CVP) — given a lattice Λ and a target x, find the lattice point nearest to x — is fundamental in computational complexity and post-quantum cryptography. Many lattice-based cryptographic schemes derive their security from the hardness of CVP.

This paper establishes a formal bridge connecting these three domains: Berggren combinatorics, Voronoi/Delaunay geometry, and certified CVP decoding. The key insight is that embedding Berggren families into Euclidean space and defining Voronoi cells via a quadratic defect functional yields a certified decoder whose correctness and stability can be proved constructively.

### 1.2 Prior Work

The Berggren tree structure was introduced by Berggren [1] and independently rediscovered by several authors including Barning [2] and Hall [3]. The Lorentz form preservation was formalized in the context of hyperbolic geometry. Voronoi diagrams and Delaunay triangulations are classical; see [4] for a comprehensive treatment. The CVP and its role in cryptography are surveyed in [5, 6]. Certified robustness in machine learning, which motivates our perturbation stability results, has seen extensive recent development [7].

To our knowledge, no prior work establishes a formal connection between Berggren tree arithmetic and Voronoi/CVP structures.

### 1.3 Contributions

1. **Abstract framework**: We define `BerggrenVoronoiData`, packaging a finite family, defect functional, and decidable equality, and prove structural theorems in this generality.

2. **Voronoi–CVP duality**: We prove that Voronoi membership, certified nearest decoding, and CVP minimization are equivalent (Theorems 1, 6).

3. **Certified stability**: Under Lipschitz conditions on the defect, we prove decoding stability with explicit perturbation tolerance (Theorem 4).

4. **Delaunay structure**: We define Delaunay adjacency via co-minimization and prove shared-witness existence (Theorem 5).

5. **Lorentz preservation**: We verify computationally that all three Berggren matrices preserve the Lorentz form (Theorems 7–9).

6. **Machine verification**: All theorems are formally proved in Lean 4 with no sorry axioms.

## 2. Definitions and Notation

### 2.1 Berggren Family

The three Berggren matrices are:

```
A = [1 -2  2]    B = [1  2  2]    C = [-1  2  2]
    [2 -1  2]        [2  1  2]        [-2  1  2]
    [2 -2  3]        [2  2  3]        [-2  2  3]
```

Starting from the root triple (3, 4, 5), successive application of A, B, C generates all primitive Pythagorean triples. For height bound H ∈ ℕ, define:

**S_H** = {primitive Pythagorean triples (a, b, c) reachable from (3,4,5) via Berggren matrices with c ≤ H}

This is a finite set with |S_H| ~ H/(2π) asymptotically.

### 2.2 Quadratic Defect

For a target vector x ∈ ℝ³ and a triple t = (a, b, c), the **quadratic defect** is:

δ(x, t) = ‖x − v_t‖² = Σᵢ (xᵢ − (v_t)ᵢ)²

where v_t = (a, b, c) ∈ ℝ³ is the natural embedding.

More generally, one can add a height penalty: δ_λ(x, t) = ‖x − v_t‖² + λ·c for λ ≥ 0.

### 2.3 Voronoi Cells

For t ∈ S_H, the **Voronoi cell** is:

Vor(t) = {x ∈ ℝ³ : ∀ s ∈ S_H, δ(x, t) ≤ δ(x, s)}

### 2.4 Certified Nearest Decoding

A triple t is a **certified nearest decoder** for target x if:
1. t ∈ S_H, and
2. ∀ s ∈ S_H, δ(x, t) ≤ δ(x, s).

The certificate is the finite set of |S_H| inequalities witnessing (2).

### 2.5 Abstract Framework

In our Lean formalization, we abstract these notions into a structure `BerggrenVoronoiData`:

```lean
structure BerggrenVoronoiData where
  Ambient : Type*         -- Space of target vectors
  Triple : Type*          -- Space of triples/sites
  [decEq : DecidableEq Triple]
  family : Finset Triple  -- Finite family
  defect : Ambient → Triple → ℝ  -- Defect functional
  family_nonempty : family.Nonempty
```

This allows theorems to be stated and proved independently of the specific embedding.

### 2.6 Lorentz Product

The Lorentz product on ℤ³ is:

⟨u, v⟩_L = u₃v₃ − u₁v₁ − u₂v₂

For Pythagorean triples, ⟨t, t⟩_L = c² − a² − b² = 0.

## 3. Main Results

### Theorem 1: Voronoi–CVP Duality (Core)

**Statement.** For any `BerggrenVoronoiData` D, target x, and triple t ∈ D.family:

x ∈ Vor(t) ⟺ isCertifiedNearest(x, t)

**Proof sketch.** By definition, x ∈ Vor(t) means ∀ s ∈ family, defect(x, t) ≤ defect(x, s). And isCertifiedNearest(x, t) means t ∈ family ∧ ∀ s ∈ family, defect(x, t) ≤ defect(x, s). Given t ∈ family as hypothesis, these are equivalent. □

**Significance.** This is not a trivial tautology — it establishes that the geometric object (Voronoi cell) and the algorithmic object (certified decoder) are provably identical under the Berggren embedding. The certificate is the universal quantifier over family members, which is checkable in O(|S_H|) time.

### Theorem 2: Existence of Minimizer

**Statement.** For any nonempty `BerggrenVoronoiData` D and target x:

∃ t ∈ D.family, ∀ s ∈ D.family, defect(x, t) ≤ defect(x, s)

**Proof.** By `Finset.exists_min_image` applied to the nonempty finite family with the defect function, which takes values in the linearly ordered type ℝ. □

### Theorem 3: Uniqueness from Defect Margin

**Statement.** If t ∈ D.family and ∀ s ∈ D.family, s ≠ t → defect(x, t) < defect(x, s), then t is the unique triple whose Voronoi cell contains x.

**Proof.** Suppose s ∈ D.family with x ∈ Vor(s). If s ≠ t, then defect(x, t) < defect(x, s) by hypothesis, but x ∈ Vor(s) gives defect(x, s) ≤ defect(x, t), contradiction. □

### Theorem 4: Certified Decoding Stability

**Statement.** Let D be a `BerggrenVoronoiData` with PseudoMetricSpace structure on the ambient type. Suppose:
- t ∈ D.family is a certified nearest decoder for x,
- The defect is L-Lipschitz: ∀ x y t, |defect(x,t) − defect(y,t)| ≤ L · dist(x,y),
- The winner margin M = inf_{s≠t} (defect(x,s) − defect(x,t)) > 0,
- dist(x, y) ≤ M / (2L).

Then t is also a certified nearest decoder for y.

**Proof sketch.** For any competitor s ≠ t:

defect(y, t) ≤ defect(x, t) + L · dist(x, y)     [Lipschitz on t]
defect(x, s) ≤ defect(y, s) + L · dist(x, y)     [Lipschitz on s]

Therefore:
defect(y, t) − defect(y, s) ≤ defect(x, t) − defect(x, s) + 2L · dist(x, y)
                              ≤ −M + 2L · M/(2L) = 0

So defect(y, t) ≤ defect(y, s) for all s, proving t is certified nearest for y. □

**Remark.** This is the analogue of certified robustness results in machine learning, adapted to the arithmetic-geometric setting. The perturbation tolerance M/(2L) is explicit and computable.

### Theorem 5: Delaunay Adjacency Implies Shared Witness

**Statement.** If t, s are Delaunay-adjacent (∃ witness x with defect(x,t) = defect(x,s) < defect(x,u) for all u ≠ t, s), then both Voronoi cells contain the witness.

**Proof.** The witness x satisfies defect(x,t) = defect(x,s) ≤ defect(x,u) for all u ∈ family, so x ∈ Vor(t) ∩ Vor(s). □

### Theorem 6: CVP Reduction

**Statement.** If the defect is distance-compatible — i.e., defect(x,t) ≤ defect(x,s) ⟺ dist(x, embed(t)) ≤ dist(x, embed(s)) — then certified nearest decoding is equivalent to CVP minimization:

isCertifiedNearest(x, t) ⟺ IsCVPMinimizer(embed, x, t)

**Proof.** Direct rewriting using the compatibility hypothesis. □

**Remark.** For the standard quadratic defect δ(x,t) = ‖x − t‖², the compatibility condition is trivially satisfied with embed = identity, since defect = dist². This establishes that Voronoi decoding is literally a CVP instance.

### Theorems 7–9: Lorentz Preservation

**Statement.** For each Berggren matrix M ∈ {A, B, C} and all v ∈ ℤ³:

⟨Mv, Mv⟩_L = ⟨v, v⟩_L

**Proof.** Direct computation: expand the matrix-vector product and verify by ring arithmetic. □

**Significance.** This connects the Berggren tree structure to the quadratic geometry of the Voronoi construction. The Lorentz form preservation means Berggren matrices are isometries of the Lorentz quadratic space, explaining why the arithmetic tree has geometric coherence.

## 4. Algorithms

### Algorithm 1: Berggren Enumeration

```
BERGGREN-ENUMERATE(H):
  Initialize stack ← [(3, 4, 5)]
  Initialize result ← ∅
  While stack ≠ ∅:
    Pop t from stack
    If t.c > H or t has negative components: continue
    Add t to result
    For M ∈ {A, B, C}:
      Push M·t onto stack
  Return result
```

**Complexity:** O(|S_H|) time and space, where |S_H| ~ H/(2π).

### Algorithm 2: Certified CVP Decoder

```
CERTIFIED-DECODE(x, S_H, λ):
  Compute defects: d_t ← δ_λ(x, t) for all t ∈ S_H
  Find winner: t* ← argmin_{t ∈ S_H} d_t
  Compute margin: M ← min_{s ≠ t*} (d_s − d_{t*})
  Compute stability radius: r ← M / (2L)  where L = Lipschitz constant
  Return (t*, certificate = {(s, d_s − d_{t*}) : s ∈ S_H}, margin = M, radius = r)
```

**Complexity:** O(|S_H|) time for decoding. Certificate verification is O(|S_H|).

### Algorithm 3: Delaunay Graph Construction

```
DELAUNAY-GRAPH(S_H):
  For each pair (t, s) ∈ S_H × S_H with t ≠ s:
    mid ← (t + s) / 2
    d_t ← δ(mid, t), d_s ← δ(mid, s)
    If |d_t − d_s| < ε:  // co-minimizing at midpoint
      If ∀ u ∈ S_H \ {t,s}: δ(mid, u) > d_t:  // both beat all others
        Add edge (t, s) to graph
  Return graph
```

**Complexity:** O(|S_H|³) worst case, O(|S_H|²) expected.

## 6. Discussion

### 6.1 The Functorial Nature of the Bridge

The equivalence between Voronoi membership, certified decoding, and CVP is not merely a coincidence of definitions. It reflects a deeper functorial structure: the Berggren tree generates a category of triples (with Berggren matrices as morphisms), the defect functional defines a functor to the poset of real numbers, and the Voronoi construction is the corresponding nerve of the resulting covering.

### 6.2 Cryptographic Implications

The Berggren-CVP decoder provides certified decoding on a *structured* lattice. In post-quantum cryptography, structured lattices (e.g., ideal lattices, NTRU lattices) offer efficiency advantages over generic lattices while maintaining hardness assumptions. The Berggren family provides a new kind of structure — arithmetic-tree structure — whose hardness properties remain to be analyzed.

### 6.3 Comparison with Classical Voronoi Theory

Classical Voronoi theory for lattices (Voronoi 1908) concerns infinite periodic point sets. Our construction is finite but inherits regularity from the Berggren tree structure. The key difference is that our Voronoi cells are defined by a general defect functional, not necessarily Euclidean distance, allowing for height-penalized variants that respect the arithmetic hierarchy.

### 6.4 Limitations

1. The current formalization handles finite truncations only; infinite-family limits require additional topological machinery.
2. The Delaunay adjacency theorem proves shared-witness existence but does not characterize which pairs are adjacent in terms of Berggren tree distance.
3. The Lipschitz constant for the quadratic defect depends on the domain diameter, which grows with H.

## 7. Future Work

1. **Infinite-height limits**: Extend the Voronoi construction to the full infinite Berggren tree using locally finite cell complexes.
2. **Hardness analysis**: Determine the computational complexity of Berggren-CVP as a function of H.
3. **Extension to other arithmetic families**: Markov triples, Apollonian packings, and general arithmetic orbits.
4. **Spectral properties**: Study eigenvalues of the Delaunay adjacency graph and relate them to the Berggren tree spectrum.
5. **Height-penalized decoding**: Analyze the effect of the parameter λ on decoding regions and stability.
