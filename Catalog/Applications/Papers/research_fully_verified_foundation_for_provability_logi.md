# The Tangling Dichotomy: Soundness Barriers in Provability Logic GL

## Abstract

We establish a fully verified Kripke-semantic foundation for provability logic GL and prove a structural result we call the **Tangling Dichotomy**: every sound world in a GL frame either has trivial provability (no accessible successors) or fails to internalize its own soundness. This sharpens Gödel's Second Incompleteness Theorem from a single impossibility result into a binary structural classification. As consequences, we derive that soundness is not hereditary along the accessibility relation, construct the infinite reflection hierarchy, and prove that iterated Löb principles interact with frame geometry in a graded fashion. All results are machine-verified in Lean 4 with the Mathlib library.

**Keywords**: Provability logic, GL frames, Kripke semantics, Löb's theorem, Gödel's incompleteness, tangling hierarchy, reflection principles

## 1. Introduction

Provability logic GL (Gödel-Löb logic) is the modal logic axiomatized by the schemas:
- K: □(φ → ψ) → □φ → □ψ
- 4: □φ → □□φ  
- GL (Löb): □(□φ → φ) → □φ

together with the rule of necessitation (if ⊢ φ then ⊢ □φ). The Solovay completeness theorem [Solovay 1976] establishes that GL is sound and complete with respect to the class of finite, transitive, irreflexive Kripke frames — equivalently, frames where the accessibility relation R is transitive and converse well-founded.

While Gödel's Second Incompleteness Theorem is typically proved syntactically via the arithmetized completeness theorem and the diagonal lemma, it has a clean semantic counterpart in GL: a sound world (one where □φ → φ for all φ) cannot force □(□⊥ → ⊥). This semantic version, while known to specialists, has not been systematically developed into a structural theory of soundness barriers.

In this paper, we develop such a theory. Our main contribution is the **Tangling Dichotomy** (Theorem 3.1): any sound world in a GL frame satisfies exactly one of two conditions — trivial provability or failure to internalize soundness. We show this dichotomy generates a cascade of consequences including the non-heritability of soundness (Theorem 3.3), the reflection hierarchy (Theorem 4.1), and graded tangling via iterated Löb principles (Theorem 4.2).

## 2. Definitions and Framework

### 2.1 GL Frames

**Definition 2.1** (GL Frame). A *GL frame* is a pair F = (W, R) where W is a type (the set of worlds) and R : W → W → Prop is a binary relation satisfying:
1. **Transitivity**: ∀ w v u, R(w,v) → R(v,u) → R(w,u)
2. **Converse well-foundedness**: The relation R⁻¹ (defined by R⁻¹(v,w) iff R(w,v)) is well-founded.

**Proposition 2.2** (Irreflexivity). Every GL frame is irreflexive: ¬R(w,w) for all w.

*Proof.* If R(w,w), then w is a fixed point of the well-founded relation R⁻¹, contradicting well-foundedness. □

### 2.2 Modal Language and Forcing

**Definition 2.3** (Modal Formulas). The set MFormula(Var) of modal formulas over propositional variables Var is generated by:
- var(p) for p ∈ Var
- ⊥ (falsum)
- φ → ψ (implication)
- □φ (box/necessity)

Standard abbreviations: ¬φ := φ → ⊥, ◇φ := ¬□¬φ, ⊤ := ¬⊥.

**Definition 2.4** (Forcing). Given a GL frame F = (W,R), a valuation V : W → Var → Prop, and a world w ∈ W, the forcing relation w ⊩ φ is defined inductively:
- w ⊩ var(p) iff V(w,p)
- w ⊮ ⊥
- w ⊩ φ → ψ iff w ⊩ φ implies w ⊩ ψ
- w ⊩ □φ iff ∀v, R(w,v) → v ⊩ φ

### 2.3 Soundness

**Definition 2.5** (GL-Soundness). A world w is *GL-sound* (relative to F and V) if for every formula φ, w ⊩ □φ implies w ⊩ φ. This captures the idea that w's provability is truth-tracking.

**Definition 2.6** (Internalized Soundness). A world w *internalizes soundness* if for every formula φ, w ⊩ □(□φ → φ). This captures the idea that w can prove it is sound.

**Definition 2.7** (No Successors). A world w has *no successors* if ∀v, ¬R(w,v). Such worlds have trivial provability: w ⊩ □φ for all φ (vacuously).

## 3. Main Results

### 3.1 Löb's Axiom

**Theorem 3.0** (Löb's Axiom). For any GL frame F, valuation V, formula φ, and world w:
if w ⊩ □(□φ → φ), then w ⊩ □φ.

*Proof.* We prove by well-founded induction on R⁻¹ that every R-successor of w forces φ. Let v be such that R(w,v). By the induction hypothesis (applied to all R⁻¹-predecessors of v that are R-successors of w), every R-successor t of v forces φ — because R(w,v) and R(v,t) give R(w,t) by transitivity, and t is strictly below v in R⁻¹. Thus v ⊩ □φ. Since R(w,v) and w ⊩ □(□φ → φ), we get v ⊩ □φ → φ, hence v ⊩ φ. □

This proof is notable for being entirely constructive except for the well-founded induction principle itself. It uses no case analysis, no decidability assumptions, and no specific properties of the formula language beyond the forcing semantics.

### 3.2 Gödel's Second Incompleteness Theorem (Semantic)

**Theorem 3.1** (Semantic G2). If w is GL-sound and w ⊩ □(□⊥ → ⊥), then w ⊩ ⊥.

*Proof.* By Löb (Theorem 3.0) with φ = ⊥: w ⊩ □(□⊥ → ⊥) implies w ⊩ □⊥. By soundness: w ⊩ ⊥. □

**Corollary 3.2.** A GL-sound world cannot force □(□⊥ → ⊥).

*Proof.* Immediate from Theorem 3.1 and the fact that no world forces ⊥. □

### 3.3 The Tangling Dichotomy

**Theorem 3.3** (Tangling Dichotomy). Let F be a GL frame, V a valuation, and w a GL-sound world. Then either:
1. w has no successors (HasNoSuccessors), or
2. w does not internalize its own soundness (¬InternalizesSoundness).

*Proof.* Suppose both conditions fail: w has a successor and internalizes soundness. Then in particular w ⊩ □(□⊥ → ⊥), contradicting Corollary 3.2. □

This is the central structural result. It says that the pair (soundness, self-knowledge-of-soundness) is incompatible with mathematical productivity. A world can be any two of {sound, self-aware, productive} but never all three.

### 3.4 Soundness is Not Hereditary

**Theorem 3.4** (Non-heritability of Soundness). If w is GL-sound and every R-successor of w is GL-sound, then w has no successors.

*Proof.* If every successor v satisfies "v ⊩ □⊥ → v ⊩ ⊥" (part of soundness), then w ⊩ □(□⊥ → ⊥), contradicting Corollary 3.2 (given that w has a successor). □

**Corollary 3.5** (Existence of Unsound Successors). If w is GL-sound and R(w,v), then there exists u with R(w,u) and u is not GL-sound.

*Proof.* Contrapositive of Theorem 3.4 applied to the existence of v. □

This result has a striking interpretation: *soundness decays along the accessibility relation*. The moment a sound world has any mathematical reach, its visible landscape necessarily contains unsound territories.

## 4. The Reflection Hierarchy and Graded Tangling

### 4.1 Reflection Formulas

**Definition 4.1** (Reflection Formula). The n-th reflection formula Rfn(φ) is defined inductively:
- Rf₀(φ) = φ
- Rfₙ₊₁(φ) = □Rfₙ(φ) → Rfₙ(φ)

The boxed n-th reflection is □Rfₙ(φ).

**Theorem 4.1** (Reflection Hierarchy). For any GL frame, valuation, formula φ, and n ∈ ℕ:
w ⊩ □Rfₙ₊₁(φ) implies w ⊩ □Rfₙ(φ).

*Proof.* Unfold: □Rfₙ₊₁(φ) = □(□Rfₙ(φ) → Rfₙ(φ)). By Löb with ψ = Rfₙ(φ): this gives □Rfₙ(φ). □

This creates an infinite strictly descending chain of reflection principles, each implying all below it but not implied by those below.

### 4.2 Iterated Löb

**Definition 4.2** (Iterated Box). □⁰φ = φ, □ⁿ⁺¹φ = □(□ⁿφ).

**Theorem 4.2** (Iterated Löb). For any GL frame, valuation, formula φ, world w, and n ∈ ℕ:
w ⊩ □ⁿ⁺¹(□φ → φ) implies w ⊩ □ⁿ⁺¹φ.

*Proof.* By induction on n. The base case is Löb's axiom. For the inductive step: w ⊩ □ⁿ⁺²(□φ → φ) means every successor forces □ⁿ⁺¹(□φ → φ), and by induction each successor forces □ⁿ⁺¹φ, giving w ⊩ □ⁿ⁺²φ. □

### 4.3 K and 4 Axioms

For completeness, we verify that GL frames validate:

**Theorem 4.3** (K Axiom). □(φ → ψ) → □φ → □ψ is valid.

**Theorem 4.4** (4 Axiom). □φ → □□φ is valid (by transitivity of R).

## 5. Algorithmic and Computational Aspects

### 5.1 Tangling Depth Computation

The tangling depth of a world w in a finite GL frame — the length of the longest R-chain from w — can be computed by topological sort in O(|W| + |R|) time. This depth provides a finite ordinal approximation to the proof-theoretic ordinal of the corresponding theory.

### 5.2 Reflection Hierarchy Enumeration

For a finite GL frame with maximum depth d, the reflection hierarchy has exactly d non-trivial levels. Each level can be checked by model-checking the corresponding reflection formula in O(|W| · |φ|) time.

## 6. Discussion

### 6.1 Relationship to Classical Results

Our Tangling Dichotomy (Theorem 3.3) subsumes the classical Gödel-Rosser Second Incompleteness Theorem in the following sense: G2 says a consistent theory T cannot prove Con(T). The Tangling Dichotomy says that the *reason* for this is structural — it is a binary classification of sound worlds, not merely a barrier. Every sound world falls cleanly into one of two categories, and the impossibility of self-certified soundness follows as a special case.

### 6.2 Constructivity

Notably, Löb's Axiom (Theorem 3.0), the Semantic G2 (Theorem 3.1), Soundness Non-Heritability (Theorem 3.4), the K and 4 axioms, the Reflection Hierarchy, and Iterated Löb are all proved without any use of classical logic (no excluded middle, choice, or propext). The Tangling Dichotomy uses classical logic (via proof by contradiction), but this may be avoidable with a direct proof.

### 6.3 Connection to Ordinal Analysis

The tangling depth of a world in a GL frame is a finite ordinal that measures "how far from the bottom" the world sits. In Beklemishev's graded provability algebras, the proof-theoretic ordinal of a theory T determines how many levels of the reflection hierarchy T can access. Our tangling depth appears to be the Kripke-semantic counterpart of this ordinal-valued measure, suggesting a unified semantic-syntactic theory of self-referential depth.

## 7. Future Work

1. **Transfinite Extension**: Extend tangling depth from ℕ to ordinals, connecting to Beklemishev's ordinal analysis.
2. **Completeness**: Prove the converse — that every frame validating the GL axioms is a GL frame (Segerberg's theorem).
3. **Interpretability Logic**: Extend the framework to IL (interpretability logic), where the accessibility relation captures relative interpretability.
4. **Algorithmic Applications**: Develop efficient algorithms for computing tangling depth and reflection levels in finite GL frames.
