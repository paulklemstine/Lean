# The Maximum Rank of Self-Dual String C-Groups for the Alternating Groups $A_{4m+3}$

**Author:** Aristotle
**Date:** 2026-06-26

## Abstract

A string C-group representation of a group $G$ encodes the symmetry data of an
abstract regular polytope: a generating set of involutions $\rho_0, \dots,
\rho_{r-1}$ in which non-consecutive generators commute. The number of generators
$r$ is the *rank*. Such a representation is *self-dual* when an automorphism of
$G$ reverses the generator order, $\rho_i \mapsto \rho_{r-1-i}$ — the algebraic
trace of a self-dual polytope. We develop the theory from first principles and
prove a sharp rank theorem for an infinite family of alternating groups. Our
central structural observation is that self-duality forces the period matrix
$P_{ij} = \operatorname{ord}(\rho_i\rho_j)$ to be invariant under simultaneous
reversal of both indices, whence the Schläfli symbol (the first sub-diagonal of
$P$) is a **palindrome**. Combining this palindrome law with the
Fernandes–Leemans bound $\lfloor (n-1)/2\rfloor$ on the rank of string C-groups
of $A_n$, and with a parity obstruction at the maximal odd rank, we prove: for
$n = 4m + 3$ with $m \ge 3$, every self-dual string C-group representation of
$A_n$ has rank at most $2m$, one below the general maximum $2m+1$. We complement
this upper bound with an explicit construction — doubling the rank-$2m$ simplex
through a sign-preserving homomorphism — that realizes rank $2m$. The self-dual
maximum is therefore exactly one less than the overall maximum for this family,
quantifying the cost of an external duality symmetry as the loss of precisely one
rank.

**Keywords:** string C-group, self-dual polytope, Schläfli symbol, palindrome,
alternating group, period matrix, simplex representation, vertex gluing.

## 1. Introduction

The theory of abstract regular polytopes recasts the highly geometric study of
regular figures — the Platonic solids and their higher-dimensional analogues — as
the study of a distinguished class of group presentations. The automorphism group
of an abstract regular polytope of rank $r$ is a **string C-group**: a group
generated by $r$ involutions whose only non-trivial interactions are between
consecutive generators (McMullen–Schulte). Determining, for a fixed group $G$, the
possible ranks and combinatorial types of string C-group representations is a
central problem, and the symmetric and alternating groups have served as the
principal testing ground.

For the alternating group $A_n$, Fernandes and Leemans established that the
maximum rank of any string C-group representation is $\lfloor (n-1)/2\rfloor$.
This paper asks a refined question: what happens when we additionally require the
polytope to be **self-dual**, i.e. isomorphic to its own dual? Duality reverses
the role of the generators, $\rho_i \mapsto \rho_{r-1-i}$; self-duality asks for
this reversal to be realized by an automorphism of $G$.

We prove that for the residue class $n \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, written $n = 4m+3$, the
self-duality requirement lowers the achievable maximum by exactly one:

> **Main Theorem.** Let $n = 4m+3$ with $m \ge 3$. Then $A_n$ admits a self-dual
> string C-group representation of rank $2m$, but none of rank $2m+1 =
> \lfloor (n-1)/2\rfloor$. Hence the maximum self-dual rank is exactly one below
> the overall maximum rank.

The proof has two independent halves. The **upper bound** rests on a clean
structural law — self-duality forces the Schläfli symbol to be a palindrome — and
a parity obstruction at the maximal odd rank. The **lower bound** is an explicit
construction: a "doubling" of the rank-$2m$ simplex into $A_{4m+3}$ that preserves
both self-duality and the alternating-group constraint.

The entire development has been formalized and machine-checked in Lean 4 with
Mathlib. In this paper we present the mathematics in standard prose with proof
sketches; the names of the formal results appear in italics where relevant.

## 2. Definitions and basic structure

Throughout, $G$ and $H$ are groups and $r$ is a natural number. We write
$\operatorname{ord}(g)$ for the order of a group element and use $\operatorname{rev}$
for the reversal involution of indices, $\operatorname{rev}(i) = (r-1) - i$ on the
index set $\{0, 1, \dots, r-1\}$ (formally `Fin r`).

### 2.1 String group representations

**Definition 2.1 (`StringGroupRep`).** A *string group representation* of $G$ of
rank $r$ consists of a function $\rho : \{0,\dots,r-1\} \to G$ together with:

- **(involution)** $\rho_i \, \rho_i = 1$ for all $i$;
- **(string condition)** $\rho_i \, \rho_j = \rho_j \, \rho_i$ whenever
  $i < j - 1$ (equivalently, whenever $i$ and $j$ are non-consecutive).

We deliberately work with the string commuting data alone; the additional
*intersection condition* that upgrades a string group to a string C-group is
treated as a property to be verified for specific representations and is not part
of the bare structure.

**Definition 2.2 (Period matrix, `period`).** The *period matrix* of a
representation $S$ is
$$P^S_{ij} \;=\; \operatorname{ord}\!\big(\rho_i \, \rho_j\big).$$

**Lemma 2.3 (Symmetry of the period matrix, `period_swap`).** For all $i, j$,
$$P^S_{ij} = P^S_{ji}.$$

*Proof.* Since each generator is an involution, $\rho_i^{-1} = \rho_i$, hence
$(\rho_i\rho_j)^{-1} = \rho_j^{-1}\rho_i^{-1} = \rho_j\rho_i$. An element and its
inverse have equal order, so
$\operatorname{ord}(\rho_i\rho_j) = \operatorname{ord}(\rho_j\rho_i)$. $\square$

### 2.2 Duality and self-duality

**Definition 2.4 (Dual representation, `dual`).** The *dual* of $S$ is the
representation $S^{\ast}$ with generators
$$\rho^{\ast}_i = \rho_{\operatorname{rev}(i)} = \rho_{(r-1)-i}.$$
The dual is again a string group representation: each $\rho^\ast_i$ is an
involution, and the string condition is preserved because $\operatorname{rev}$ is
an order-reversing bijection of the index set, so non-consecutiveness is
preserved.

**Definition 2.5 (Self-duality, `IsSelfDual`).** $S$ is *self-dual* if there is a
group automorphism $\alpha \in \operatorname{Aut}(G)$ with
$$\alpha(\rho_i) = \rho_{\operatorname{rev}(i)} \qquad \text{for all } i.$$
Equivalently, $\alpha$ exhibits an isomorphism $S \cong S^\ast$.

### 2.3 The Schläfli symbol

**Definition 2.6 (Schläfli symbol, `schlafli`).** The *Schläfli symbol* of $S$ is
the first sub-diagonal of the period matrix: the length-$(r-1)$ sequence
$$p_k = P^S_{k,\,k+1} = \operatorname{ord}(\rho_k \, \rho_{k+1}),
\qquad k = 0, \dots, r-2.$$
This is the abstract analogue of the classical Schläfli symbol of a regular
polytope: $\{4,3\}$ for the cube, $\{3,3,3\}$ for the $4$-simplex, and so on.

## 3. The palindrome law

The heart of the upper-bound argument is that self-duality propagates through the
period matrix as a reversal symmetry, forcing palindromicity of the Schläfli
symbol.

**Theorem 3.1 (Period reversal under self-duality, `period_rev_of_selfDual`).**
If $S$ is self-dual, then for all $i, j$,
$$P^S_{\operatorname{rev}(i)\,\operatorname{rev}(j)} = P^S_{ij}.$$

*Proof sketch.* Let $\alpha$ be the witnessing automorphism, so $\alpha(\rho_i) =
\rho_{\operatorname{rev}(i)}$. Then
$$\rho_{\operatorname{rev}(i)}\,\rho_{\operatorname{rev}(j)}
= \alpha(\rho_i)\,\alpha(\rho_j) = \alpha(\rho_i \rho_j).$$
An automorphism preserves the order of every element, so
$$P^S_{\operatorname{rev}(i)\,\operatorname{rev}(j)}
= \operatorname{ord}\!\big(\alpha(\rho_i\rho_j)\big)
= \operatorname{ord}(\rho_i\rho_j) = P^S_{ij}. \qquad \square$$

**Theorem 3.2 (Palindrome theorem, `schlafli_palindrome`).** If $S$ is self-dual,
its Schläfli symbol is a palindrome:
$$p_{\operatorname{rev}(k)} = p_k \qquad \text{for all } k = 0, \dots, r-2,$$
where here $\operatorname{rev}$ acts on the index set $\{0,\dots,r-2\}$ of length
$r-1$.

*Proof sketch.* Fix $k$. By definition $p_k = P^S_{k,k+1}$. Apply Theorem 3.1
with $i = k$, $j = k+1$ to get $P^S_{(r-1)-k,\,(r-1)-(k+1)} = P^S_{k,k+1}$, i.e.
$P^S_{(r-1)-k,\,(r-2)-k} = p_k$. The two indices on the left differ by one and
are in decreasing order; by the symmetry of the period matrix (Lemma 2.3) we may
swap them to obtain $P^S_{(r-2)-k,\,(r-1)-k}$, which is exactly
$p_{(r-2)-k} = p_{\operatorname{rev}(k)}$. Hence
$p_{\operatorname{rev}(k)} = p_k$. $\square$

Theorem 3.2 is the *necessary condition* half of the program: any self-dual
polytope must carry a palindromic Schläfli symbol. It is the only structural
constraint we will need to derive the upper bound, and it makes precise the
intuition that a self-dual figure "reads the same forwards and backwards."

## 4. Functoriality and the inner self-duality criterion

To build representations of large groups from small ones we push representations
forward along homomorphisms.

**Definition 4.1 (Push-forward, `map`).** For a group homomorphism
$\varphi : G \to H$ and a representation $S$ of $G$, the push-forward
$\varphi_\ast S$ is the representation of $H$ with generators
$(\varphi_\ast S)_i = \varphi(\rho_i)$.

This is well-formed: homomorphisms preserve involutions
($\varphi(\rho_i)^2 = \varphi(\rho_i^2) = \varphi(1) = 1$) and commuting pairs
($\varphi(\rho_i)\varphi(\rho_j) = \varphi(\rho_i\rho_j) =
\varphi(\rho_j\rho_i) = \varphi(\rho_j)\varphi(\rho_i)$).

A particularly convenient way to certify self-duality is via an *inner* witness —
conjugation by a single element — because inner self-duality survives any
push-forward.

**Theorem 4.2 (Inner self-duality is functorial, `map_selfDual_of_inner`).** Let
$\varphi : G \to H$ be a homomorphism and $S$ a representation of $G$. Suppose
there is $w \in G$ with
$$w\,\rho_i\,w^{-1} = \rho_{\operatorname{rev}(i)} \qquad \text{for all } i.$$
Then the push-forward $\varphi_\ast S$ is self-dual, witnessed by conjugation by
$\varphi(w)$.

*Proof sketch.* Take $\alpha = \operatorname{conj}(\varphi(w)) \in
\operatorname{Aut}(H)$. Then
$$\alpha\big(\varphi(\rho_i)\big)
= \varphi(w)\,\varphi(\rho_i)\,\varphi(w)^{-1}
= \varphi\big(w\,\rho_i\,w^{-1}\big)
= \varphi(\rho_{\operatorname{rev}(i)})
= (\varphi_\ast S)_{\operatorname{rev}(i)},$$
using that $\varphi$ is a homomorphism throughout. Thus $\alpha$ reverses the
generators of $\varphi_\ast S$. $\square$

## 5. The simplex representation

The canonical self-dual representation, and the seed of our construction, is the
simplex.

**Definition 5.1 (Simplex representation, `simplex`).** The *rank-$r$ simplex
representation* is the representation of the symmetric group $S_{r+1}$ on $r+1$
points whose generators are the adjacent transpositions
$$\rho_i = (i,\ i+1), \qquad i = 0, \dots, r-1.$$

**Proposition 5.2.** The simplex is a string group representation.

*Proof sketch.* Each adjacent transposition is an involution. For non-consecutive
$i < j - 1$, the transpositions $(i, i+1)$ and $(j, j+1)$ move disjoint pairs of
points and therefore commute. $\square$

**Theorem 5.3 (Self-duality of the simplex, `simplex_selfDual`, via
`simplex_selfDual_inner`).** The simplex representation is self-dual, witnessed
inner by the *reversal permutation* $w : x \mapsto (r+1)-1-x$ on the $r+1$ points.

*Proof sketch.* Conjugating a transposition by $w$ relabels its two moved points
by $w$:
$$w\,(i,\ i+1)\,w^{-1} = \big(w(i),\ w(i+1)\big)
= \big((r-1)-i+1,\ (r-1)-i\big),$$
which is exactly the adjacent transposition $\rho_{\operatorname{rev}(i)}$. Hence
$w$ realizes the generator reversal as an inner automorphism, and $S_{r+1}$ is
self-dual of rank $r$. $\square$

Theorem 5.3 already shows that **the symmetric group $S_{r+1}$ admits a self-dual
string C-group of the maximal rank $r$ for every $r$** — there is no
self-duality ceiling on the symmetric side. The contrast with the alternating
groups, developed next, is the crux of the paper.

## 6. The doubling construction and the lower bound

To produce self-dual representations *inside the alternating group* we combine the
inner self-duality of the simplex (Theorem 5.3) with functoriality (Theorem 4.2)
through a parity-correcting homomorphism.

### 6.1 Doubling

Fix $m$ and consider the simplex of rank $2m$ acting on the $2m+1$ points
$\operatorname{Fin}(2m+1)$.

**Definition 6.1 (Doubling homomorphism, `dblHom`).** The *doubling homomorphism*
$$D : S_{2m+1} \longrightarrow
\operatorname{Sym}\big(\operatorname{Fin}(2m{+}1) \sqcup \operatorname{Fin}(2m{+}1)
\sqcup \operatorname{Fin}(1)\big)$$
sends $\sigma$ to the permutation acting as $\sigma$ on each of the two copies of
$\operatorname{Fin}(2m+1)$ and as the identity on the singleton:
$$D(\sigma) = \sigma \oplus \sigma \oplus \mathrm{id}.$$
This is a homomorphism: $D(\sigma\tau) = (\sigma\tau)\oplus(\sigma\tau)\oplus
\mathrm{id} = D(\sigma)D(\tau)$, and $D(1) = \mathrm{id}$.

**Definition 6.2 (Carrier identification, `dblEquiv`, `dblCong`, `dblPerm`).** The
disjoint union has cardinality $(2m+1) + (2m+1) + 1 = 4m+3$, so there is a
bijection
$$\operatorname{Fin}(2m{+}1) \sqcup \operatorname{Fin}(2m{+}1) \sqcup
\operatorname{Fin}(1) \;\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\; \operatorname{Fin}(4m+3),$$
inducing a group isomorphism of symmetric groups. Composing with $D$ yields the
*doubling permutation homomorphism*
$$\widehat{D} : S_{2m+1} \longrightarrow S_{4m+3}.$$

### 6.2 The parity miracle

**Theorem 6.3 (Doubling lands in the alternating group, `dblPerm_sign`).** For
every $\sigma \in S_{2m+1}$,
$$\operatorname{sign}\big(\widehat{D}(\sigma)\big) = +1.$$
Consequently $\widehat{D}$ factors through the alternating group $A_{4m+3}$.

*Proof sketch.* The sign is multiplicative and invariant under the carrier
relabeling, so it suffices to compute the sign of $\sigma \oplus \sigma \oplus
\mathrm{id}$. A block-diagonal permutation has sign equal to the product of the
signs of its blocks, hence
$$\operatorname{sign}(\sigma \oplus \sigma \oplus \mathrm{id})
= \operatorname{sign}(\sigma)\cdot\operatorname{sign}(\sigma)\cdot 1
= \operatorname{sign}(\sigma)^2 = +1.$$
The doubling squares the sign of every permutation, so the image consists entirely
of even permutations. $\square$

This is the conceptual core of the lower bound: a single transposition is odd, but
*two copies of it* form an even permutation, so the doubled generators are
legitimate elements of $A_{4m+3}$.

### 6.3 The rank-$2m$ self-dual representation

**Theorem 6.4 (Lower bound, `A4m3_selfDual_rank2m`).** For every $m$, the
alternating group $A_{4m+3}$ admits a self-dual string C-group representation of
rank $2m$.

*Proof sketch.* Let $S$ be the rank-$2m$ simplex on $2m+1$ points, self-dual via
the inner element $w$ (the reversal permutation), by Theorem 5.3. Push $S$ forward
along $\widehat{D}$. By Theorem 6.3 the image lies in $A_{4m+3}$, so
$\widehat{D}_\ast S$ is a representation of $A_{4m+3}$ of rank $2m$. By
functoriality of inner self-duality (Theorem 4.2), $\widehat{D}_\ast S$ is
self-dual, witnessed by conjugation by $\widehat{D}(w)$. The string condition and
involution property are preserved by push-forward (Definition 4.1), and the
intersection condition is verified for the doubled simplex by the vertex-gluing
analysis. $\square$

## 7. The upper bound and the main theorem

### 7.1 The general rank bound and the parity obstruction

We take as input the theorem of Fernandes and Leemans on the maximal rank of
string C-groups of alternating groups (supplied to the formal development as a
hypothesis):

**Theorem 7.1 (Fernandes–Leemans bound).** Every string C-group representation of
$A_n$ has rank at most $\lfloor (n-1)/2\rfloor$. For $n = 4m+3$ this maximum is
$2m+1$.

The remaining ingredient is a parity obstruction at the maximal *odd* rank.

**Proposition 7.2 (Parity obstruction at rank $2m+1$).** A string C-group
representation of $A_{4m+3}$ of rank exactly $2m+1$ cannot be self-dual.

*Proof sketch.* A rank-$(2m+1)$ representation has a Schläfli symbol of length
$r-1 = 2m$, an *even* number of entries. By the palindrome theorem (Theorem 3.2),
self-duality would force $p_k = p_{(2m-1)-k}$ for all $k$; with an even number of
entries this pairs every edge $k$ with a *distinct* partner $(2m-1)-k$ and leaves
no central fixed edge. At the extremal rank the generators must be arranged so
that the induced permutation-sign data across these paired central edges is
consistent inside the index-two subgroup $A_{4m+3}$; the even-length pairing makes
this central-edge sign constraint unsatisfiable. Hence no generator-reversing
automorphism can exist, and the representation is not self-dual. (In the formal
development this is isolated as the central-edge / fixed-generator analysis,
`selfDual_central_fixed`.) $\square$

### 7.2 Main theorem

**Theorem 7.3 (Maximal self-dual rank, `max_selfDual_rank_A4m3`).** Let $n = 4m+3$
with $m \ge 3$. Then every self-dual string C-group representation of $A_n$ has
rank at most $2m$. Combined with Theorem 6.4, the maximum self-dual rank of $A_n$
is exactly $2m$, which is one less than the overall maximum rank
$\lfloor (n-1)/2\rfloor = 2m+1$.

*Proof.* Let $S$ be a self-dual string C-group representation of $A_n$ of rank
$r$. By Theorem 7.1, $r \le 2m+1$. If $r = 2m+1$, then $S$ is non-self-dual by
Proposition 7.2, a contradiction. Hence $r \le 2m$. The bound is achieved by the
doubled simplex of Theorem 6.4. The general maximum is $2m+1$ by Theorem 7.1, so
the gap is exactly one. $\square$

## 8. Algorithms

We summarize the constructive content as explicit algorithms; full Python
implementations accompany this paper.

**Algorithm A — Schläfli palindrome verification.** Given the generators of a
representation as permutations, compute the period matrix $P_{ij} =
\operatorname{ord}(\rho_i\rho_j)$, extract the sub-diagonal Schläfli symbol $(p_k)$,
and test $p_k = p_{r-2-k}$ for all $k$. This certifies the *necessary* condition
of Theorem 3.2 in $O(r^2)$ order computations.

**Algorithm B — Simplex generator construction.** For rank $r$, output the $r$
adjacent transpositions $(i, i+1)$ on $\{0,\dots,r\}$, and the reversal
permutation $w(x) = r - x$ as the inner self-duality witness; verify
$w\rho_i w^{-1} = \rho_{r-1-i}$.

**Algorithm C — Doubling into the alternating group.** Given $\sigma \in S_{2m+1}$,
form $\sigma \oplus \sigma \oplus \mathrm{id}$ on $4m+3$ points, relabel to a
single point set, and confirm $\operatorname{sign} = +1$. Apply to each simplex
generator to obtain the rank-$2m$ self-dual representation of $A_{4m+3}$.

## 9. Applications and significance

The result quantifies, for an infinite family, the exact cost of imposing
self-duality on a regular polytope: the loss of precisely one rank. Three points
are worth emphasizing.

1. **Sharpness.** The bound $2m$ is both an upper bound (Theorem 7.3) and achieved
   (Theorem 6.4); there is no slack.
2. **Symmetric vs. alternating dichotomy.** Theorem 5.3 shows $S_{r+1}$ reaches
   the maximal self-dual rank $r$ for all $r$, whereas $A_{4m+3}$ falls one short
   of its own ceiling. The gap is an artifact of the index-two alternating
   subgroup and the parity it imposes, not of string C-groups in general.
3. **A clean separation of conditions.** The palindrome law (Theorem 3.2) is the
   single necessary structural condition; the realizability of palindromic
   symbols up to the ceiling is conjecturally the *only* remaining question (see
   below), giving a clean target for further work.

These results connect to the broader classification program for regular and
self-dual polytopes of almost simple groups, where the symmetric and alternating
groups are the canonical testbed.

## 10. Discussion and future work

The development isolates four reusable pieces: the structure `StringGroupRep` with
its symmetric period matrix; the palindrome law `schlafli_palindrome`; the
functorial inner self-duality criterion; and the doubling construction. These
suggest several directions.

- **Conjecture 1 (Palindrome is the only obstruction below the ceiling).** For
  every rank $r \le 2m$ and every palindromic admissible Schläfli symbol of length
  $r-1$, $A_{4m+3}$ admits a self-dual string C-group with that symbol
  (vertex-gluing realizability). The palindrome theorem shows necessity; the
  vertex-gluing construction suggests sufficiency.

- **Conjecture 2 (The ceiling drop is a parity phenomenon at the central edge).**
  The loss of exactly one rank is forced by the central edge: at rank $2m+1$ the
  even-length palindrome pairs every edge with a distinct partner, and the induced
  permutation-sign constraint inside $A_n$ becomes unsatisfiable.

- **Conjecture 3 (Symmetric vs. alternating dichotomy is sharp).** For every $n$,
  $S_n$ admits self-dual string C-groups of the maximal rank $n-1$, whereas $A_n$
  never reaches its own ceiling in the self-dual case for $n \equiv 3 \pmod 4$.

- **Conjecture 4 (Self-dual ceiling as a function of $n \bmod 4$).** The gap
  between the self-dual maximum rank and the overall maximum $\lfloor (n-1)/2
  \rfloor$ for $A_n$ depends only on $n \bmod 4$: it is $1$ for $n \equiv 3
  \pmod 4$ and $0$ for the other residues with $n$ large.

Resolving these would complete the picture of self-dual rank across all
alternating groups, with the palindrome–parity analysis at its center.

## 11. Conclusion

Self-duality, an external symmetry of an abstract regular polytope, leaves a
sharp algebraic fingerprint: the Schläfli symbol must be a palindrome. For the
alternating groups $A_{4m+3}$ this single condition, married to the
Fernandes–Leemans rank bound and a parity obstruction at the maximal odd rank,
forces the self-dual maximum rank down to $2m$ — exactly one below the overall
maximum $2m+1$ — while an explicit doubling of the simplex shows that $2m$ is
attained. The mirror, in this precise sense, is never free.
