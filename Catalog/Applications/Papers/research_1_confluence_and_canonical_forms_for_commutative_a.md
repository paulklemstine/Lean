# Certified AC-Canonical Forms for Tropical Expressions: A Decision Procedure Kernel

## Abstract

We present a formally verified canonicalization procedure for the associative-commutative (AC) fragment of tropical (min-plus) expressions over ℝ. Given expressions built from constants, variables, binary min, and binary addition, our normalizer `normalize_ca` flattens same-headed operation trees, sorts children by a total order, and rebuilds right-associated canonical trees. We prove three theorems constituting a certified decision procedure kernel: (1) **soundness** — normalization preserves evaluation semantics; (2) **completeness** — AC-equivalent expressions normalize to the same canonical form; (3) **idempotence** — normalization is a closure operator. All proofs are machine-checked in Lean 4 with Mathlib, depending only on the standard axioms (propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound). The completeness theorem converts the decision problem "are these two tropical expressions AC-equivalent?" into a purely computational syntactic comparison.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Motivation

Tropical (min-plus) algebra replaces the conventional arithmetic operations with (min, +), yielding a semiring structure (ℝ ∪ {+∞}, min, +) that arises naturally in optimization, shortest-path algorithms, scheduling, and the theory of ReLU neural networks. Symbolic manipulation of tropical expressions—simplification, equivalence checking, and normal-form computation—is fundamental to these applications.

The equational theory of the tropical semiring includes:
- Associativity and commutativity of min
- Associativity and commutativity of +
- Distributivity: a + min(b, c) = min(a + b, a + c)
- Idempotence of min: min(a, a) = a
- Identity elements: min(a, +∞) = a, a + 0 = a

Deciding the full equational theory is nontrivial; indeed, the word problem for the free tropical semiring is related to deep questions in tropical geometry. However, the AC fragment—generated by associativity and commutativity alone, without distributivity or idempotence—admits a clean canonicalization that we formalize here.

### 1.2 Contributions

1. A formally verified comparison function `ble` on tropical expressions forming a total order, with machine-checked proofs of totality, antisymmetry, and transitivity.

2. A normalizer `normalize_ca` that flattens operation trees, sorts by `ble`, and rebuilds canonical forms.

3. Three certified theorems:
   - `eval_normalize_ca`: ∀ σ e, eval σ (normalize_ca e) = eval σ e
   - `normalize_ca_complete_ACEquiv`: ACEquiv e₁ e₂ → normalize_ca e₁ = normalize_ca e₂
   - `normalize_ca_idempotent`: ∀ e, normalize_ca (normalize_ca e) = normalize_ca e

4. Supporting infrastructure: permutation-invariance of fold-evaluation, sorted-permutation uniqueness, and a combined idempotence/fixpoint bundle theorem.

### 1.3 Related Work

AC-unification and AC-matching have been studied extensively in term rewriting (Stickel 1981, Fages 1984). Certified rewriting in proof assistants includes work by Contejean et al. on Coq-based completion and by Sozeau on rewriting in type theory. To our knowledge, this is the first fully verified AC-canonicalization for a tropical expression language.

## 2. Definitions and Notation

### 2.1 Tropical Expressions

```
TropExpr ::= const(r)           for r ∈ ℝ
           | var(n)             for n ∈ ℕ
           | tmin(e₁, e₂)      tropical minimum
           | add(e₁, e₂)       tropical addition
```

### 2.2 Evaluation

Given an environment σ : ℕ → ℝ:
```
eval(σ, const(r))      = r
eval(σ, var(n))        = σ(n)
eval(σ, tmin(e₁, e₂)) = min(eval(σ, e₁), eval(σ, e₂))
eval(σ, add(e₁, e₂))  = eval(σ, e₁) + eval(σ, e₂)
```

### 2.3 AC Congruence

The AC congruence `ACEquiv` is the smallest equivalence relation on `TropExpr` containing:
- `tmin_comm`: tmin(e₁, e₂) ≡ tmin(e₂, e₁)
- `tmin_assoc`: tmin(tmin(e₁, e₂), e₃) ≡ tmin(e₁, tmin(e₂, e₃))
- `add_comm`: add(e₁, e₂) ≡ add(e₂, e₁)
- `add_assoc`: add(add(e₁, e₂), e₃) ≡ add(e₁, add(e₂, e₃))
- Congruence: if e₁ ≡ e₁' and e₂ ≡ e₂', then tmin(e₁, e₂) ≡ tmin(e₁', e₂') and add(e₁, e₂) ≡ add(e₁', e₂')

### 2.4 Comparison Order

We define `ble : TropExpr → TropExpr → Bool` lexicographically:
- Constructor tags: const < var < tmin < add
- Within same constructor: lexicographic on components
  - const(r₁) ≤ const(r₂) iff r₁ ≤ r₂
  - var(n₁) ≤ var(n₂) iff n₁ ≤ n₂
  - tmin/add: lexicographic on (first child, second child)

**Theorem (ble properties).** `ble` is a total, antisymmetric, transitive comparison.

## 3. The Normalizer

### 3.1 Flattening

```
flattenMin(tmin(a, b)) = flattenMin(a) ++ flattenMin(b)
flattenMin(e)          = [e]        (for non-tmin e)

flattenAdd(add(a, b))  = flattenAdd(a) ++ flattenAdd(b)
flattenAdd(e)          = [e]        (for non-add e)
```

### 3.2 Sorting and Rebuilding

```
normalize_ca(const(r)) = const(r)
normalize_ca(var(n))   = var(n)
normalize_ca(tmin(a, b)) = rebuildMin(sort(flattenMin(normalize_ca(a)) ++ flattenMin(normalize_ca(b))))
normalize_ca(add(a, b))  = rebuildAdd(sort(flattenAdd(normalize_ca(a)) ++ flattenAdd(normalize_ca(b))))
```

where `sort = List.mergeSort ble` and `rebuildMin/rebuildAdd` construct right-associated trees.

### 3.3 Key Properties of Flattening

- `flattenMin(e)` is always nonempty
- Elements of `flattenMin(e)` are never tmin-headed
- `flattenMin(rebuildMin(l)) = l` when elements of l are non-tmin and l is nonempty
- Symmetric properties for flattenAdd/rebuildAdd

## 4. Main Results

### 4.1 Soundness

**Theorem (eval_normalize_ca).** For all σ and e: eval(σ, normalize_ca(e)) = eval(σ, e).

*Proof sketch.* By structural induction on e. For tmin(a, b):

```
eval(σ, normalize_ca(tmin(a, b)))
= eval(σ, rebuildMin(sort(L)))           where L = flattenMin(a') ++ flattenMin(b')
= evalMinList(σ, sort(L))               by eval_rebuildMin
= evalMinList(σ, L)                     by evalMinList_perm (sort is a permutation)
= min(evalMinList(σ, flattenMin(a')),
      evalMinList(σ, flattenMin(b')))    by evalMinList_append
= min(eval(σ, a'), eval(σ, b'))         by eval_flattenMin
= min(eval(σ, a), eval(σ, b))           by IH
= eval(σ, tmin(a, b))
```

The key supporting lemma is permutation-invariance of evalMinList, proved by induction on the permutation relation using commutativity and associativity of min on ℝ.

### 4.2 Completeness

**Theorem (normalize_ca_complete_ACEquiv).** If ACEquiv(e₁, e₂) then normalize_ca(e₁) = normalize_ca(e₂).

*Proof sketch.* By induction on the ACEquiv derivation.

- **refl, symm, trans**: Immediate from equality.
- **tmin_comm**: The flattened lists are `L₁ = flattenMin(a') ++ flattenMin(b')` and `L₂ = flattenMin(b') ++ flattenMin(a')`. These are permutations (by List.perm_append_comm). By the sorted-permutation-uniqueness theorem, `sort(L₁) = sort(L₂)`.
- **tmin_assoc**: After flattening, both sides produce lists that are permutations of `flattenMin(a') ++ flattenMin(b') ++ flattenMin(c')`. The key step uses `flattenMin(rebuildMin(sort(l))) = sort(l)` to unflatten the inner normalization.
- **cong_tmin/cong_add**: By IH, the normalized subexpressions are equal, so the flattened lists are identical.

The sorted-permutation-uniqueness theorem states: if l₁.Perm l₂ and both are sorted by a total antisymmetric order, then l₁ = l₂. This is `List.Perm.eq_of_pairwise` in Mathlib.

### 4.3 Idempotence

**Theorem (normalize_ca_idempotent).** For all e: normalize_ca(normalize_ca(e)) = normalize_ca(e).

*Proof sketch.* We prove a stronger bundle: for all e, simultaneously:
1. Elements of flattenMin(normalize_ca(e)) are fixed by normalize_ca
2. Elements of flattenAdd(normalize_ca(e)) are fixed by normalize_ca
3. normalize_ca(normalize_ca(e)) = normalize_ca(e)

By structural induction on e. For tmin(a, b):

- Let S = sort(flattenMin(a') ++ flattenMin(b')).
- |S| ≥ 2 (since each flattenMin contributes ≥ 1 element).
- Property (1): flattenMin(rebuildMin(S)) = S. Elements of S come from flattenMin(a') or flattenMin(b'), so are fixed by IH.
- Property (2): Since |S| ≥ 2, rebuildMin(S) is tmin-headed, so flattenAdd returns a singleton containing rebuildMin(S). Its fixedness follows from property (3).
- Property (3): S is pairwise-sorted, elements are non-tmin and fixed. By the auxiliary lemma `normalize_ca_rebuildMin_pairwise` (proved by list induction), normalize_ca(rebuildMin(S)) = rebuildMin(S).

The auxiliary lemma `normalize_ca_rebuildMin_pairwise` is the technical heart: by induction on the list, it shows that normalizing a right-associated tmin-tree of sorted, fixed, non-tmin elements produces the same tree.

## 5. Algorithms

### 5.1 Normalization Algorithm

```
ALGORITHM normalize_ca(e):
  CASE e OF
    const(r): RETURN const(r)
    var(n):   RETURN var(n)
    tmin(a, b):
      a' ← normalize_ca(a)
      b' ← normalize_ca(b)
      L ← flattenMin(a') ++ flattenMin(b')
      S ← mergeSort(L, ble)
      RETURN rebuildMin(S)
    add(a, b):
      a' ← normalize_ca(a)
      b' ← normalize_ca(b)
      L ← flattenAdd(a') ++ flattenAdd(b')
      S ← mergeSort(L, ble)
      RETURN rebuildAdd(S)
```

### 5.2 Complexity

Let n = |e| (number of nodes). The normalization runs in O(n log n) time:
- Recursive normalization: O(n) total work across all recursive calls.
- Flattening: O(n) per level.
- Sorting: O(k log k) where k is the number of children at each level.
- Overall: O(n log n) since the total number of sort operations is bounded by n.

Space complexity: O(n) for the output expression and intermediate lists.

### 5.3 AC-Equivalence Decision Procedure

```
ALGORITHM ac_equiv(e₁, e₂):
  RETURN normalize_ca(e₁) == normalize_ca(e₂)
```

By soundness, this implies semantic equality. By completeness, this detects all AC equivalences.

## 6. Why Full Semantic Completeness Fails

The unrestricted statement `∀ σ, eval(σ, e₁) = eval(σ, e₂) → normalize_ca(e₁) = normalize_ca(e₂)` is **false**. Counterexamples arise from:

1. **Distributivity**: `add(x, tmin(y, z))` and `tmin(add(x, y), add(x, z))` are semantically equal but not AC-equivalent.

2. **Min idempotence**: `tmin(x, x)` and `x` are semantically equal but not AC-equivalent (different numbers of nodes).

3. **Constant equalities**: `add(const(1), const(2))` and `const(3)` are semantically equal but syntactically distinct after AC normalization.

Our theorem precisely captures the AC fragment, providing a foundation on which these additional equivalences can be incrementally added.

## 7. Applications

### 7.1 Shortest-Path Simplification

Tropical matrix expressions encode shortest-path computations. AC normalization can detect syntactically redundant sub-computations and enable certified common-subexpression elimination.

### 7.2 ReLU Network Equivalence (AC Fragment)

ReLU networks compute tropical polynomials. Two network architectures that differ only in the AC arrangement of their min/addition operations can be detected as equivalent using our normalizer.

### 7.3 Proof Automation

The completeness theorem enables a reflection-based tactic: to prove `eval(σ, e₁) = eval(σ, e₂)` when the equality holds by AC reasoning, normalize both sides and check syntactic equality.

## 8. Discussion

### 8.1 Design Choices

- **ℝ coefficients**: We chose ℝ for generality, requiring noncomputable definitions for the comparison function. A restriction to ℚ or ℤ would yield computable normalization.

- **Right-associated rebuild**: The choice of right-association is arbitrary; left-association or balanced trees would work equally well. The key property is determinism given a fixed total order.

- **Lexicographic ordering**: Our comparison `ble` uses constructor tags then lexicographic recursion. Any total order would yield correct results; this choice gives intuitive canonical forms.

### 8.2 Limitations

- The normalizer does not handle distributivity, idempotence, or identity elements.
- The comparison function is noncomputable over ℝ (uses Classical.choice for decidability of ≤ on ℝ).
- The canonical form is not minimal in any complexity-theoretic sense.
