# The Berggren–Lorentz Monoid: Discrete Lorentz Symmetry of Pythagorean Triples

## Abstract

We develop the structure theory of the **Berggren monoid**, the
three-generator submonoid of `GL₃(ℤ)` that acts on Pythagorean triples via
the Berggren tree, and we identify it explicitly as a submonoid of the
integer Lorentz group `O(2,1; ℤ)`. The unifying object is the Lorentz
quadratic form `Q(a,b,c) = a² + b² − c²`, whose zero set (the integer light
cone) coincides exactly with the set of Pythagorean triples. We prove that
each of the three Berggren generators `A`, `B`, `C` preserves `Q` (so each
lies in `O(2,1; ℤ)`), that the determinant signature is `(+1, −1, +1)` and
the trace signature is `(3, 5, 3)`, and that the induced "child" maps
preserve the form *identically*, not merely on the cone. From form
preservation we obtain closure: the set of `Q`-preserving integer matrices is
a submonoid, so every word in `A`, `B`, `C` again preserves `Q` and hence
maps Pythagorean triples to Pythagorean triples. We analyze the B-branch
dynamics, establishing the sharp growth law `hyp(B-child) > 5·hyp(parent)`
for triples with positive legs (yielding `O(log c)` tree depth), and we
exhibit the twin-leg subfamily of consecutive-leg triples as the B-orbit. We
record the spectral and algebraic invariants (product traces, eigenvalue-1
structure, inverse matrices), the non-commutativity of the generators, a
`ℤ/2ℤ` parity grading induced by `det B = −1`, the exact generator relation
`C = −A·Q`, and elementary entrywise/`∞`-norm bounds with Lipschitz
applications. All results are fully formalized and machine-verified in Lean 4.

**Keywords.** Pythagorean triples, Lorentz group, Berggren tree, integer
orthogonal group, quadratic forms, monoid action, discrete dynamics.

## 1. Introduction

The set of Pythagorean triples — integer solutions of `a² + b² = c²` — is one
of the oldest objects in mathematics, and one of the most structured. Euclid
parametrized them; B. Berggren (1934), and independently later authors,
organized the *primitive* triples into a ternary tree generated by three
fixed integer matrices, with the seed `(3,4,5)` at the root and each triple
having exactly three children and (apart from the root) a unique parent.

This paper takes the structural viewpoint seriously: the three Berggren
matrices are not an ad hoc trick but generators of a submonoid of the integer
Lorentz group `O(2,1; ℤ)`, and the entire tree is the orbit of the seed under
that monoid acting on the integer light cone of the form `Q(a,b,c) = a² + b²
− c²`. We make this dictionary precise and formal:

| Number theory                  | Geometry / physics                    |
|--------------------------------|---------------------------------------|
| Pythagorean triple `a²+b²=c²`  | integer point on light cone `Q=0`     |
| triple-generating matrix       | integer Lorentz transformation        |
| Berggren tree                  | orbit of seed under `O(2,1;ℤ)` monoid |
| hypotenuse growth              | expansion factor / Lyapunov rate      |
| consecutive-leg triples        | the B-generator orbit                 |

Every theorem stated below has been formalized and checked in Lean 4 over
Mathlib; the proofs use exact integer/matrix computation (`native_decide`),
polynomial identities (`ring`), linear and nonlinear arithmetic over the
integers (`linarith`, `nlinarith`, `omega`), and structural induction.

## 2. Definitions

Throughout, all matrices are `3×3` over `ℤ`, indexed by `Fin 3`.

**Definition 2.1 (Lorentz form).**
The Lorentz quadratic form is `Q(a,b,c) = a² + b² − c²`. On vectors
`v : Fin 3 → ℤ` it reads `lorentzForm(v) = v₀² + v₁² − v₂²`; the scalar and
vector versions agree, `lorentzForm(v) = Q(v₀, v₁, v₂)`.

**Definition 2.2 (Lorentz bilinear form).**
The polarization of `Q` is `B(u,v) = u₀v₀ + u₁v₁ − u₂v₂`, satisfying
`Q(v) = B(v,v)`.

**Definition 2.3 (Pythagorean predicate).**
`IsPythag(a,b,c) :⇔ a² + b² = c²`.

**Definition 2.4 (Berggren generators).**

```
A = !![1,-2,2; 2,-1,2; 2,-2,3]
B = !![1, 2,2; 2, 1,2; 2, 2,3]
C = !![-1,2,2; -2,1,2; -2,2,3]
```

**Definition 2.5 (Lorentz metric).**
`Q_L = diag(1, 1, −1)` (the matrix `metricQ`).

**Definition 2.6 (Child maps).**
Acting on a triple `(a,b,c)` (as `A`, `B`, `C` act on the column `(a,b,c)ᵀ`):

- `childA(a,b,c) = (a − 2b + 2c, 2a − b + 2c, 2a − 2b + 3c)`,
- `childB(a,b,c) = (a + 2b + 2c, 2a + b + 2c, 2a + 2b + 3c)`,
- `childC(a,b,c) = (−a + 2b + 2c, −2a + b + 2c, −2a + 2b + 3c)`.

The third coordinate of each is the corresponding hypotenuse function
`hypA = 2a−2b+3c`, `hypB = 2a+2b+3c`, `hypC = −2a+2b+3c`.

**Definition 2.7 (Words and word matrices).**
A `BerggrenWord` is a finite list of generator indices in `Fin 3`. The
generator map sends `0 ↦ A`, `1 ↦ B`, `2 ↦ C`, and the word matrix is the
left fold `wordMatrix(w) = ∏ berggrenGen(wᵢ)` (starting from `1`).

**Definition 2.8 (Form preservation).**
`preservesForm(M, Q) :⇔ Mᵀ Q M = Q`.

**Definition 2.9 (Parity grading).**
`wordParity(w) = #{i : wᵢ = 1}`, the count of `B`-letters in `w`.

**Definition 2.10 (B-iterate).**
`iterateB(0) = (3,4,5)`, `iterateB(n+1) = childB(iterateB(n))`, with
`bHypotenuse(n) = (iterateB(n))₃`.

**Definition 2.11 (Euclid parametrization).**
`parametricTriple(m,n) = (m² − n², 2mn, m² + n²)`.

**Definition 2.12 (Twin-leg family).**
`isTwinLeg(a,b,c) :⇔ IsPythag(a,b,c) ∧ (a − b = 1 ∨ b − a = 1)`.

## 3. The light-cone correspondence

**Theorem 3.1 (Pythagorean ⇔ light cone).**
For all integers `a,b,c`, `Q(a,b,c) = 0` if and only if `IsPythag(a,b,c)`.

*Proof sketch.* `a² + b² − c² = 0 ⇔ a² + b² = c²` by integer arithmetic
(`omega` after unfolding). ∎

This identity is the hinge of the whole theory: it identifies the arithmetic
object (Pythagorean triples) with the geometric object (integer points of
the null cone of `Q`).

**Proposition 3.2 (Form identities).** The following hold for all integers:

1. *Homogeneity:* `Q(ta, tb, tc) = t²·Q(a,b,c)`.
2. *Expansion:* `Q(a+a', b+b', c+c') = Q(a,b,c) + Q(a',b',c') + 2(aa' + bb' − cc')`.
3. *Reflection invariance:* `Q(−a,b,c) = Q(a,−b,c) = Q(a,b,−c) = Q(a,b,c)`.
4. *Leg-swap invariance:* `Q(b,a,c) = Q(a,b,c)`.

*Proof sketch.* Each is a polynomial identity over `ℤ`, closed by `ring`. ∎

**Proposition 3.3 (Bilinear form).** `B` is symmetric, bilinear (additive and
`ℤ`-homogeneous in each slot), and polarizes `Q`: `Q(v) = B(v,v)`.

*Proof sketch.* Direct expansion (`ring` / `simp` with `Pi` operations). ∎

## 4. The generators lie in `O(2,1; ℤ)`

**Theorem 4.1 (Form preservation of generators).**
`Aᵀ Q_L A = Q_L`, `Bᵀ Q_L B = Q_L`, and `Cᵀ Q_L C = Q_L`. Hence
`A, B, C ∈ O(2,1; ℤ)`.

*Proof sketch.* Exact `3×3` integer matrix computation (`native_decide`). ∎

**Theorem 4.2 (Determinant signature).**
`det A = +1`, `det B = −1`, `det C = +1`.

*Proof sketch.* Exact integer determinant computation. ∎

Thus `A` and `C` are proper (orientation-preserving) and `B` is improper.
The product determinants follow from multiplicativity and are verified
directly: `det(AB) = −1`, `det(AC) = +1`, `det(BC) = −1`, `det(ABC) = −1`,
and every square `det(M²) = +1`.

**Theorem 4.3 (Trace signature).**
`tr A = 3`, `tr B = 5`, `tr C = 3`; their sum is `11`.

*Proof sketch.* Sum of diagonal entries. ∎

`A` and `C` share trace `3` while `B` has the largest trace `5`, marking `B`
as the most expanding generator (cf. §6).

**Theorem 4.4 (Inverses are integral and orthogonal).**
With

```
A⁻¹ = !![1,2,-2; -2,-1,2; -2,-2,3],
B⁻¹ = !![1,2,-2;  2, 1,-2; -2,-2,3],
C⁻¹ = !![-1,-2,2; 2, 1,-2; -2,-2,3],
```

we have `A·A⁻¹ = A⁻¹·A = I` and likewise for `B`, `C`; moreover each inverse
preserves `Q_L`. (Integrality of `A⁻¹` and `C⁻¹` reflects `det = ±1`.)

*Proof sketch.* Exact matrix multiplication. ∎

## 5. Closure and the monoid action on triples

**Theorem 5.1 (Submonoid closure).**
The set `{M : Mᵀ Q M = M}`… more precisely `{M : preservesForm(M, Q)}` is a
submonoid of `GL₃(ℤ)`: `preservesForm(1, Q)` holds, and if
`preservesForm(M₁,Q)` and `preservesForm(M₂,Q)` then
`preservesForm(M₁M₂, Q)`.

*Proof sketch.* For the product, expand
`(M₁M₂)ᵀ Q (M₁M₂) = M₂ᵀ (M₁ᵀ Q M₁) M₂` using `(M₁M₂)ᵀ = M₂ᵀM₁ᵀ` and
associativity, then substitute `M₁ᵀ Q M₁ = Q` and `M₂ᵀ Q M₂ = Q`. ∎

**Corollary 5.2.** Every word matrix `wordMatrix(w)` preserves `Q_L`. In
particular all pairwise products `AB`, `AC`, `BC` lie in `O(2,1; ℤ)`
(verified directly as well).

**Theorem 5.3 (Child maps preserve the form identically).**
For all integers `a,b,c`,
`Q(childA(a,b,c)) = Q(childB(a,b,c)) = Q(childC(a,b,c)) = Q(a,b,c)`.

*Proof sketch.* Three polynomial identities over `ℤ`, closed by `ring`. ∎

**Corollary 5.4 (Pythagorean preservation).**
If `IsPythag(a,b,c)` then each child is Pythagorean.

*Proof sketch.* Either combine Theorem 5.3 with Theorem 3.1, or argue
directly: substitute `a² + b² = c²` into the child's defining relation and
close by `nlinarith`. ∎

**Theorem 5.5 (Orbit of the seed).**
`(3,4,5)` is Pythagorean, and its children are `childA = (5,12,13)`,
`childB = (21,20,29)`, `childC = (15,8,17)`, all Pythagorean. The second
generation includes `childA(5,12,13) = (7,24,25)` and `childB(5,12,13) =
(55,48,73)`, again Pythagorean.

*Proof sketch.* Evaluation (`norm_num`) plus Corollary 5.4. ∎

**Remark (completeness, not formalized here).** Berggren's classical theorem
states that the orbit of `(3,4,5)` under `{A,B,C}` is *exactly* the set of
primitive Pythagorean triples (each appearing once). The present development
formalizes the forward direction — that the orbit consists of Pythagorean
triples — and the structural invariants that make the tree usable; the
bijectivity statement is recorded as a future direction.

## 6. B-branch dynamics and growth

**Theorem 6.1 (B-iterate values).**
`iterateB(1) = (21,20,29)`, `iterateB(2) = (119,120,169)`,
`iterateB(3) = (697,696,985)`; correspondingly `bHypotenuse` takes the
values `5, 29, 169, 985`.

*Proof sketch.* Unfold and evaluate. ∎

**Theorem 6.2 (Every B-iterate is Pythagorean / null).**
For all `n`, `IsPythag(iterateB(n))` and `Q(iterateB(n)) = 0`.

*Proof sketch.* Induction on `n`: base `(3,4,5)`; step by
Corollary 5.4 (`childB_preserves_pythag`). The null statement follows by
Theorem 3.1. ∎

**Theorem 6.3 (Sharp B-growth law).**
If `IsPythag(a,b,c)` with `a,b,c > 0`, then `5c < hypB(a,b,c) = 2a+2b+3c`.

*Proof sketch.* The Pythagorean triangle inequality gives `c < a + b` (proved
via `nlinarith` from `a²+b²=c²` and positivity, using
`sq_nonneg (a+b−c)`). Then `2a + 2b + 3c > 2c + 3c = 5c` by `linarith`. ∎

**Proposition 6.4 (Coarser bounds and monotonicity).**
For positive legs, `3c ≤ hypB(a,b,c)`, and `c < hypB`. With `0 < a,b ≤ c`
one also has `hypB ≤ 7c`. Along proper branches, `c < hypA` when `b < a` and
`c < hypC` when `a < b`. The explicit B-iterate hypotenuses satisfy
`5·b(n) < b(n+1)` for `n = 0,1,2`.

*Proof sketch.* Linear arithmetic from the defining formulas. ∎

**Corollary 6.5 (Logarithmic depth).** A Pythagorean triple with hypotenuse
`c` lies at B-depth at most `log₅ c + O(1)`; more generally the tree has
depth `O(log c)` for hypotenuse `c`, since each step multiplies the
hypotenuse by a factor `> 5` (Theorem 6.3) along the B-branch (and `> 1`
generally, Proposition 6.4). This is the foundation of efficient
enumeration (§9).

**Theorem 6.6 (Twin-leg family = B-orbit).**
`(3,4,5)`, `(20,21,29)`, `(119,120,169)`, `(696,697,985)` are all twin-leg
triples (Pythagorean with `|a−b| = 1`). These are exactly the consecutive-leg
triples appearing along the B-branch.

*Proof sketch.* Verify `IsPythag` and `|a − b| = 1` for each. ∎

## 7. Spectral and algebraic invariants

**Theorem 7.1 (Product traces and a hidden symmetry).**
`tr(AB) = 17`, `tr(AC) = 15`, `tr(BC) = 17`. Moreover `tr(AB) = tr(BA)`,
`tr(AC) = tr(CA)`, `tr(BC) = tr(CB)` (cyclicity), and notably
`tr(AB) = tr(BC)` — an `A ↔ C` symmetry when paired with `B`.

*Proof sketch.* Exact computation. The equalities `tr(MN) = tr(NM)` are
instances of the cyclic property, here verified concretely. ∎

**Theorem 7.2 (Eigenvalue-1 structure).**
`det(I − A) = 0` and `det(I − C) = 0`, so `1` is an eigenvalue of both `A`
and `C`; but `det(I − B) = −8 ≠ 0`, so `1` is *not* an eigenvalue of `B`.
Consequently `B` has no fixed direction on the cone, whereas `A` and `C` do.

*Proof sketch.* Characteristic determinant evaluations at `λ = 1`. ∎

**Theorem 7.3 (Power traces).**
`tr(A²) = 3`, `tr(C²) = 3` (so `A`, `C` are "parabolic": trace fixed under
squaring), while `tr(B²) = 35`, consistent with the Newton identity
`tr(B²) = (tr B)² − 2·e₂(B)` and `B`'s status as the expanding generator.

*Proof sketch.* Exact computation. ∎

**Theorem 7.4 (Generator reduction `C = −A·Q`).**
`A⁻¹ C = −Q_L = !![-1,0,0; 0,-1,0; 0,0,1]`, equivalently `C = −(A·Q_L)`.
Also `B⁻¹ A = diag(1,−1,1)`.

*Proof sketch.* Exact matrix computation; the second form follows by
left-multiplying `A⁻¹C = −Q_L` by `A`. ∎

This shows the third generator is *not* independent: it is determined by `A`
and the metric `Q_L`. The monoid is effectively two-generated modulo the
fixed involution `Q_L`.

**Theorem 7.5 (Metric involution).**
`Q_L² = I`, `Q_Lᵀ = Q_L`, `det Q_L = −1`, `tr Q_L = 1`.

*Proof sketch.* Exact computation. ∎

**Theorem 7.6 (A + C structure).**
`A + C = !![0,0,4; 0,0,4; 0,0,6]` (supported in the hypotenuse column only),
and `A + B + C = !![1,2,6; 2,1,6; 2,2,9]`.

*Proof sketch.* Exact computation. ∎

## 8. Non-commutativity and the parity grading

**Theorem 8.1 (Non-commutativity).**
`AB ≠ BA`, `BC ≠ CB`, `AC ≠ CA`, and the three generators are pairwise
distinct. At the word level, `wordMatrix([0,1]) = AB ≠ BA =
wordMatrix([1,0])`.

*Proof sketch.* Exact comparison (`native_decide`); the word-level statement
reduces to `AB ≠ BA` after evaluating the folds. ∎

**Theorem 8.2 (`ℤ/2ℤ` parity grading).**
The map `w ↦ wordParity(w) mod 2` is well behaved on letters:
`wordParity([]) = 0`, `wordParity([0]) = 0` (A even), `wordParity([1]) = 1`
(B odd), `wordParity([2]) = 0` (C even). Since `det A = det C = +1` and
`det B = −1`, the determinant of a word matrix is `(−1)^{wordParity(w)}`,
i.e. parity = orientation.

*Proof sketch.* The letter values are computed directly; the determinant
statement is the multiplicativity of `det` combined with Theorem 4.2. ∎

Non-commutativity (free-monoid-like branching) plus exponential growth
(Theorem 6.3) makes the *inverse word problem* — recovering the generating
sequence from a product or an orbit point — a natural one-way structure,
with parity supplying a `ℤ/2ℤ` invariant.

## 9. Algorithms and applications

**(A) Complete, duplicate-free enumeration.** Walk the ternary Berggren tree
from `(3,4,5)`, pruning any branch whose hypotenuse exceeds a target bound
`N`. By Corollary 6.5 the tree has depth `O(log N)`, so all primitive triples
with hypotenuse `≤ N` are produced in `O(#triples)` time with completeness
guaranteed by the orbit structure and no repeats (each non-root node has a
unique parent). The child rules of Definition 2.6 are the only primitive
needed.

**(B) Lipschitz certification for compositions.** Every generator entry
satisfies `|Mᵢⱼ| ≤ 3` and every row satisfies `Σⱼ |Mᵢⱼ| ≤ 7` (Theorem 9.1
below). Hence `‖Mv‖∞ ≤ 7‖v‖∞` for any generator `M`, and a composition of
`n` generators is `7ⁿ`-Lipschitz in the `∞`-norm — a certified bound usable
in robustness analysis.

**Theorem 9.1 (Entrywise and row-sum bounds).**
For every generator index `k` and all `i,j`, `|berggrenGen(k)ᵢⱼ| ≤ 3`, and
`|berggrenGen(k)ᵢ₀| + |berggrenGen(k)ᵢ₁| + |berggrenGen(k)ᵢ₂| ≤ 7`.

*Proof sketch.* Finite case check over `Fin 3` (`decide`). ∎

**(C) Cryptographic search-space lower bound.** The exponential growth
`hyp(B-child) > 5·hyp(parent)` (Theorem 6.3) implies that an orbit reaching
hypotenuse `c` requires word length `Ω(log₅ c)`; combined with
non-commutativity, the number of distinct reduced words of length `L` grows
as `Ω(5^L)`-comparable in reach, giving an `Ω(5^depth)` effective search
space for orbit-reversal puzzles. The `±1` determinant grading (Theorem 8.2)
furnishes a public parity bit.

**(D) Euclid bridge.** The classical parametrization
`parametricTriple(m,n) = (m²−n², 2mn, m²+n²)` is always Pythagorean and
always lies on the cone (`Q = 0`), recovering, e.g., `(3,4,5) = P(2,1)`,
`(5,12,13) = P(3,2)`, `(15,8,17) = P(4,1)`. This connects the matrix-orbit
picture to the closed-form picture.

**Theorem 9.2 (Euclid parametrization).**
For all `m,n`, `IsPythag(parametricTriple(m,n))` and
`Q(parametricTriple(m,n)) = 0`.

*Proof sketch.* `(m²−n²)² + (2mn)² = (m²+n²)²` by `ring`. ∎

## 10. Discussion

The Berggren–Lorentz monoid sits at a junction of several fields. From number
theory it inherits the Pythagorean triples; from physics, the integer Lorentz
group `O(2,1; ℤ)` and its determinant/orientation structure; from dynamics,
an expanding ternary map with explicit Lyapunov-type growth; and from
computer science, both an efficient enumeration scheme and a candidate
one-way structure. The single thread tying these together is the quadratic
form `Q`: form preservation is simultaneously "being a Lorentz
transformation," "mapping triples to triples," and "conserving a Diophantine
invariant."

Two structural facts deserve emphasis. First, the *child maps preserve `Q`
identically* (Theorem 5.3), not just on the cone; this is strictly stronger
than Pythagorean preservation and is what makes the form-theoretic viewpoint
exact rather than incidental. Second, the relation `C = −A·Q` (Theorem 7.4)
shows the apparent three-generator presentation collapses to two generators
plus the fixed metric involution — a genuine reduction with downstream
consequences for any application that counts generators (e.g. key sizing).

## 11. Future work

- **Bijectivity / completeness.** Formalize Berggren's theorem that the orbit
  of `(3,4,5)` is exactly the set of primitive Pythagorean triples, each once.
  This requires a descent argument (unique parent via the inverse generators
  of Theorem 4.4) and a primitivity-preservation lemma.
- **Freeness.** Prove the monoid generated by `{A, B, C}` is free (no
  relations), strengthening the non-commutativity results of §8 and putting
  the cryptographic word-problem claims on rigorous footing.
- **Exact growth constant.** Replace the `>5` bound (Theorem 6.3) by the
  asymptotic per-step factor (the dominant eigenvalue of `B`, near
  `3 + 2√2`), and prove matching upper bounds.
- **Higher-dimensional analogues.** Extend to `O(n−1, 1; ℤ)` and the
  corresponding "Pythagorean `n`-tuples," seeking analogous generator
  reductions and growth laws.

## 12. Conclusion

We have given a complete, machine-verified structural account of the
Berggren–Lorentz monoid: the light-cone correspondence between Pythagorean
triples and the null set of `Q`; membership of all three generators in
`O(2,1; ℤ)` with signature `(+1,−1,+1)` and traces `(3,5,3)`; submonoid
closure and identical form preservation by the child maps; the sharp
five-fold B-growth law with its logarithmic-depth corollary; the twin-leg
B-orbit; the spectral invariants, inverse matrices, the reduction
`C = −A·Q`, the metric involution `Q² = I`; non-commutativity and the
`ℤ/2ℤ` parity grading; and entrywise norm bounds with Lipschitz and
cryptographic applications. The classical Pythagorean triples are, quite
literally, the integer light cone of a discrete relativity, and their
generating tree is the orbit of a small, well-understood Lorentz monoid.
