# Thermodynamic Galois Duality via Closure Pressure Spectra and Equilibrium Correspondence

## Abstract

We establish a Galois-style duality between closure quotients and equilibrium faces for finite weighted closure dynamical systems. Given a finite state space $X$, generators $\text{Gen}$, step relations, and weight functions, we construct the weighted transfer matrix $A$ and prove three main results: (1) the partition function $Z_n = \sum_{x,y} (A^n)_{xy}$ is submultiplicative, ensuring well-definedness of the thermodynamic pressure; (2) equilibrium functionals (positive normalized eigenvectors of $A$) give rise to normalized additive functionals on the correspondence semiring, establishing one direction of an equilibrium–character correspondence; (3) closure quotients and equilibrium faces are connected by a Galois connection $Q \leq \Psi(F) \iff F \subseteq \Phi(Q)$, with formal verification in the Lean 4 proof assistant using the Mathlib library. We provide algorithms for computing these structures and demonstrate applications to state-space minimization, semantic compression, and phase detection.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Motivation

Classical thermodynamic formalism, developed by Ruelle, Sinai, and Bowen for symbolic dynamical systems, connects the asymptotic growth rate of weighted path counts (pressure) to the spectral radius of a transfer operator. This connection, a cornerstone of ergodic theory, has been applied to hyperbolic dynamics, multifractal analysis, and statistical mechanics.

We extend this framework to *closure dynamical systems*: finite-state systems where admissible transitions are generated by closure operators rather than simple graph adjacencies. This generalization is motivated by:

1. **Emergent meta-language (EML) theory**, where closure operators model semantic completion and grammatical closure in language dynamics.
2. **Algebraic approaches to dynamics**, where the semiring of weighted correspondences provides a natural algebraic dual to thermodynamic states.
3. **State-space reduction**, where closure-compatible quotients provide canonical simplifications of complex systems.

### 1.2 Main Contributions

Our main contributions are:

1. **Transfer matrix construction and properties** (Section 3): We construct the weighted transfer matrix from closure generators and prove nonnegativity, partition function submultiplicativity ($Z_{m+n} \leq Z_m \cdot Z_n$), and matrix-path identification.

2. **Equilibrium–character correspondence** (Section 4): We show that equilibrium functionals (positive normalized eigenvectors) define normalized additive functionals on the correspondence semiring, and prove elementary matrix decomposition and multiplication rules.

3. **Galois connection theorem** (Section 5): We prove that the maps $\Phi$ (quotient $\to$ face) and $\Psi$ (face $\to$ kernel) form a Galois connection, with formal antitone properties and closure/idempotence results.

4. **Formal verification**: All results are verified in Lean 4 with the Mathlib library, with no `sorry` axioms and only standard foundational axioms (`propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`).

### 1.3 Related Work

**Thermodynamic formalism.** The Ruelle–Perron–Frobenius theorem [Ruelle 1968, Bowen 1975] establishes pressure = log spectral radius for subshifts of finite type. Our work extends this to closure-generated dynamics.

**Galois connections in algebra.** The fundamental theorem of Galois theory establishes a correspondence between intermediate field extensions and subgroups of the Galois group. Our Galois connection is between quotient lattices and convex face lattices, analogous but in a thermodynamic rather than field-theoretic setting.

**Semiring theory.** The use of semiring characters as algebraic duals of dynamical states is inspired by Gelfand duality for commutative Banach algebras, adapted to the nonnegative (non-involutive) setting.

## 2. Definitions and Notation

### 2.1 Closure Dynamical Systems

**Definition 2.1** (Closure Dynamical System). A *finite weighted closure dynamical system* is a tuple $(X, \text{Gen}, \text{step}, w)$ where:
- $X$ is a finite set (state space)
- $\text{Gen}$ is a finite set (generators)
- $\text{step} : \text{Gen} \to X \to X \to \text{Prop}$ is a family of step relations
- $w : \text{Gen} \to \mathbb{R}$ is a weight function

### 2.2 Transfer Matrix

**Definition 2.2** (Transfer Matrix). The *weighted transfer matrix* $A \in M_{|X| \times |X|}(\mathbb{R})$ has entries:
$$A_{xy} = \sum_{g \in \text{Gen}} \exp(w(g)) \cdot \mathbf{1}[\text{step}(g, y, x)]$$

Note the convention: $A_{xy}$ counts weighted transitions *from* $y$ *to* $x$.

### 2.3 Partition Function and Pressure

**Definition 2.3** (Partition Function). The *partition function* of order $n$ is:
$$Z_n = \sum_{x \in X} \sum_{y \in X} (A^n)_{xy}$$

This equals the total weighted mass of all length-$n$ paths in the system.

**Definition 2.4** (Thermodynamic Pressure). The *pressure* is:
$$P = \limsup_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{n} \log Z_n$$

### 2.4 State Functionals

**Definition 2.5** (State Functional). A *state functional* on $X$ is a function $\mu : X \to \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$ with $\sum_{x \in X} \mu(x) = 1$.

**Definition 2.6** (Equilibrium Functional). An *equilibrium functional* for a matrix $A \in M_{|X| \times |X|}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})$ is a state functional $\mu$ together with an eigenvalue $r > 0$ satisfying:
$$\sum_{y \in X} A_{xy} \mu(y) = r \cdot \mu(x) \quad \forall x \in X$$

### 2.5 Galois Connection Maps

**Definition 2.7** (Face Kernel). For a set $F$ of state functionals, the *face kernel* is the equivalence relation:
$$\Psi(F) = \{(x,y) : \mu(x) = \mu(y) \text{ for all } \mu \in F\}$$

**Definition 2.8** (Quotient Face). For an equivalence relation $Q$ on $X$, the *quotient face* is:
$$\Phi(Q) = \{\mu : \mu(x) = \mu(y) \text{ whenever } Q(x,y)\}$$

### 2.6 Semiring Characters

**Definition 2.9** (Semiring Character). A *normalized semiring character* on a semiring $S$ is a function $\chi : S \to \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$ satisfying:
$$\chi(0) = 0, \quad \chi(1) = 1, \quad \chi(a+b) = \chi(a) + \chi(b), \quad \chi(ab) = \chi(a)\chi(b)$$

## 3. Transfer Matrix Properties

### 3.1 Nonnegativity

**Theorem 3.1** (Transfer Matrix Nonnegativity). For any closure dynamical system, $A_{xy} \geq 0$ for all $x, y \in X$.

*Proof sketch.* Each summand is either $\exp(w(g)) > 0$ or $0$. The sum of nonnegative terms is nonneg. □

### 3.2 Matrix Power Nonnegativity

**Theorem 3.2.** If $A_{xy} \geq 0$ for all $x, y$, then $(A^n)_{xy} \geq 0$ for all $x, y$ and all $n \geq 0$.

*Proof.* By induction on $n$. Base case: $(A^0)_{xy} = \delta_{xy} \geq 0$. Inductive step: $(A^{n+1})_{xy} = \sum_z A_{xz} (A^n)_{zy}$, a sum of products of nonneg terms. □

### 3.3 Partition Sum Properties

**Theorem 3.3** (Z₀ identity). $Z_0 = |X|$.

*Proof.* $Z_0 = \sum_{x,y} (A^0)_{xy} = \sum_{x,y} \delta_{xy} = |X|$. □

**Theorem 3.4** (Z₁ identity). $Z_1 = \sum_{x,y} A_{xy}$.

*Proof.* $A^1 = A$, so $Z_1 = \sum_{x,y} A_{xy}$. □

### 3.4 Submultiplicativity

**Theorem 3.5** (Partition Sum Submultiplicativity). If $A_{xy} \geq 0$ for all $x, y$, then:
$$Z_{m+n} \leq Z_m \cdot Z_n$$

*Proof.* Using $A^{m+n} = A^m \cdot A^n$:
$$Z_{m+n} = \sum_{x,y} (A^{m+n})_{xy} = \sum_{x,y} \sum_z (A^m)_{xz} (A^n)_{zy}$$

Rearranging:
$$= \sum_z \left(\sum_x (A^m)_{xz}\right) \left(\sum_y (A^n)_{zy}\right)$$

Let $a_z = \sum_x (A^m)_{xz}$ and $b_z = \sum_y (A^n)_{zy}$. These are nonneg. Then:
$$Z_{m+n} = \sum_z a_z b_z \leq \left(\sum_z a_z\right)\left(\sum_z b_z\right) = Z_m \cdot Z_n$$

The inequality follows from $b_z \leq \sum_{z'} b_{z'}$ (each nonneg term is at most the total), so:
$$\sum_z a_z b_z \leq \sum_z a_z \cdot \left(\sum_{z'} b_{z'}\right) = Z_m \cdot Z_n \qquad \square$$

### 3.5 Pressure Existence

**Corollary 3.6.** If $Z_1 > 0$, the sequence $\frac{1}{n} \log Z_n$ converges:
$$P = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{n} \log Z_n = \inf_{n \geq 1} \frac{1}{n} \log Z_n$$

*Proof.* Submultiplicativity implies $\log Z_{m+n} \leq \log Z_m + \log Z_n$ (subadditivity of log-partition function). By Fekete's lemma, $\frac{1}{n} \log Z_n$ converges to its infimum. □

### 3.6 Vector Formulation

**Theorem 3.7.** $Z_n = \mathbf{1}^T A^n \mathbf{1}$ where $\mathbf{1}$ is the all-ones vector.

*Proof.* $(A^n \mathbf{1})_x = \sum_y (A^n)_{xy}$, so $\mathbf{1}^T A^n \mathbf{1} = \sum_x \sum_y (A^n)_{xy} = Z_n$. □

## 4. Equilibrium–Character Correspondence

### 4.1 Equilibrium-Weighted Sum

**Definition 4.1.** For a state functional $\mu$ and a matrix $M$, the *equilibrium-weighted sum* is:
$$\chi_\mu(M) = \sum_{y \in X} \left(\sum_{x \in X} M_{xy}\right) \mu(y)$$

### 4.2 Additivity

**Theorem 4.1** (Additivity). $\chi_\mu(M + N) = \chi_\mu(M) + \chi_\mu(N)$.

*Proof.* Direct computation:
$$\chi_\mu(M+N) = \sum_y \left(\sum_x (M+N)_{xy}\right) \mu(y) = \sum_y \left(\sum_x M_{xy} + \sum_x N_{xy}\right) \mu(y)$$
$$= \sum_y \left(\sum_x M_{xy}\right) \mu(y) + \sum_y \left(\sum_x N_{xy}\right) \mu(y) = \chi_\mu(M) + \chi_\mu(N) \qquad \square$$

### 4.3 Zero Preservation

**Theorem 4.2.** $\chi_\mu(0) = 0$.

### 4.4 Eigenvector Evaluation

**Theorem 4.3** (Character Evaluates Transfer Matrix to Eigenvalue). If $\mu$ is an equilibrium functional for $A$ with eigenvalue $r$, then:
$$\chi_\mu(A) = r$$

*Proof.* By the eigenvector equation:
$$\chi_\mu(A) = \sum_y \left(\sum_x A_{xy}\right) \mu(y) = \sum_x \sum_y A_{xy} \mu(y) = \sum_x r \cdot \mu(x) = r \cdot \sum_x \mu(x) = r \qquad \square$$

### 4.5 Elementary Matrix Algebra

**Theorem 4.4** (Elementary Matrix Decomposition). Any matrix $M$ can be decomposed as:
$$M = \sum_{x,y} M_{xy} \cdot E_{xy}$$
where $E_{xy}$ is the elementary matrix with 1 at position $(x,y)$ and 0 elsewhere.

**Theorem 4.5** (Elementary Matrix Multiplication). $E_{ab} \cdot E_{cd} = \delta_{bc} \cdot E_{ad}$.

### 4.6 Main Correspondence

**Theorem 4.6** (Equilibrium–Character Correspondence). For any equilibrium functional $(\mu, r)$ of $A$, the map $\chi_\mu$ satisfies:
1. $\chi_\mu(0) = 0$ (zero preservation)
2. $\chi_\mu(M+N) = \chi_\mu(M) + \chi_\mu(N)$ (additivity)
3. $\chi_\mu(A) = r$ (eigenvalue recovery)

This establishes that every equilibrium functional gives rise to a normalized additive functional on the correspondence semiring that recovers the eigenvalue structure.

## 5. Galois Connection Theorem

### 5.1 Antitone Properties

**Theorem 5.1** (Quotient Face Antitonicity). $\Phi$ is antitone: if $Q_1 \leq Q_2$ (finer quotient), then $\Phi(Q_2) \subseteq \Phi(Q_1)$.

*Proof.* If $\mu \in \Phi(Q_2)$, then $Q_2(x,y) \implies \mu(x) = \mu(y)$. Since $Q_1 \leq Q_2$ means $Q_1(x,y) \implies Q_2(x,y)$, we get $Q_1(x,y) \implies \mu(x) = \mu(y)$, so $\mu \in \Phi(Q_1)$. □

**Theorem 5.2** (Face Kernel Antitonicity). $\Psi$ is antitone: if $F_1 \subseteq F_2$, then $\Psi(F_2) \leq \Psi(F_1)$.

### 5.2 The Fundamental Galois Connection

**Theorem 5.3** (Thermodynamic Galois Duality). For any equivalence relation $Q$ on $X$ and any set $F$ of state functionals:
$$Q \leq \Psi(F) \iff F \subseteq \Phi(Q)$$

*Proof.* Both sides unfold to the same statement with quantifiers reordered:

- $Q \leq \Psi(F)$ means: $\forall x, y, \; Q(x,y) \implies \forall \mu \in F, \; \mu(x) = \mu(y)$
- $F \subseteq \Phi(Q)$ means: $\forall \mu \in F, \; \forall x, y, \; Q(x,y) \implies \mu(x) = \mu(y)$

These are logically equivalent by rearranging the universal quantifiers. □

**Corollary 5.4** (Formal Galois Connection). The pair $(\Psi^{op}, \Phi)$ forms a `GaloisConnection` in the sense of Mathlib, between $(\text{Setoid } X)^{op}$ and $(\mathcal{P}(\text{StateFunctional } X), \subseteq)$.

### 5.3 Closure Properties

**Theorem 5.5.** $F \subseteq \Phi(\Psi(F))$ (closure enlargement for faces).

**Theorem 5.6.** $Q \leq \Psi(\Phi(Q))$ (closure coarsening for quotients).

*Proof.* Both follow from the Galois connection applied with the identity inequality/inclusion. □

### 5.4 Boundary Cases

**Theorem 5.7.** $\Phi(\bot) = \text{univ}$ (the discrete quotient admits all functionals).

**Theorem 5.8.** $\Psi(\emptyset) = \top$ (no functional constraints give the indiscrete quotient).

### 5.5 Character Kernel Connection

**Theorem 5.9.** A semiring character $\chi$ is closure-stable for a ring congruence $C$ if and only if $C \leq \ker(\chi)$.

*Proof.* Both sides are definitionally equal: $\forall a, b, \; C(a,b) \implies \chi(a) = \chi(b)$. □

## 6. Algorithms

### 6.1 Transfer Matrix Construction

**Input:** $(X, \text{Gen}, \text{step}, w)$

**Output:** Matrix $A \in M_{|X| \times |X|}(\mathbb{R})$

```
for x in X:
  for y in X:
    A[x,y] = 0
    for g in Gen:
      if step(g, y, x):
        A[x,y] += exp(w(g))
```

**Complexity:** $O(|X|^2 \cdot |\text{Gen}|)$ time, $O(|X|^2)$ space.

### 6.2 Pressure Computation

**Input:** Matrix $A$, tolerance $\epsilon$

**Output:** Pressure $P$ with error $< \epsilon$

```
eigenvalues = eigendecomposition(A)
rho = max(|lambda| for lambda in eigenvalues)
P = log(rho)
```

**Complexity:** $O(|X|^3)$ time (eigendecomposition), $O(|X|^2)$ space.

Alternative (iterative):
```
Z_prev = Z_1 = sum(A)
for n = 2, 3, ...:
  Z_n = sum(A^n)  # via repeated multiplication
  P_n = log(Z_n) / n
  if |P_n - P_{n-1}| < epsilon:
    return P_n
```

**Complexity:** $O(N \cdot |X|^3)$ time where $N$ is iterations to convergence.

### 6.3 Equilibrium Functional Computation

**Input:** Nonneg matrix $A$

**Output:** Normalized left Perron eigenvector $\mu$

```
eigenvalues, eigenvectors = eig(A^T)
idx = argmax(real(eigenvalues))
mu = |eigenvectors[:, idx]|
mu = mu / sum(mu)
```

**Complexity:** $O(|X|^3)$ time.

### 6.4 Canonical Quotient Computation

**Input:** Equilibrium functional $\mu$, tolerance $\epsilon$

**Output:** Partition of $X$ (canonical quotient classes)

```
classes = []
for x in X:
  placed = False
  for cls in classes:
    if |mu[x] - mu[cls[0]]| < epsilon:
      cls.append(x)
      placed = True
      break
  if not placed:
    classes.append([x])
return classes
```

**Complexity:** $O(|X|^2)$ time.

### 6.5 Phase Detection

**Input:** Matrix $A$, tolerance $\epsilon$

**Output:** Phase type, period, spectral circle eigenvalues

```
eigenvalues = eigendecomposition(A)
rho = max(|lambda| for lambda in eigenvalues)
spectral_circle = [lambda for lambda in eigenvalues
                   if ||lambda| - rho| < epsilon]
period = len(spectral_circle)
if period == 1:
  return "aperiodic (unique equilibrium)"
else:
  return f"periodic with period {period}"
```

**Complexity:** $O(|X|^3)$ time.

## 7. Applications and Examples

### 7.1 State-Space Minimization

Consider a 6-state system with hidden redundancy:

| | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | 0.0 | 0.0 | 1.5 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 0.5 |
| **1** | 0.0 | 0.0 | 1.5 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 0.5 |
| **2** | 0.8 | 0.8 | 0.0 | 0.7 | 0.7 | 0.0 |
| **3** | 0.5 | 0.5 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 1.2 |
| **4** | 0.5 | 0.5 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 1.2 |
| **5** | 0.0 | 0.0 | 1.0 | 0.6 | 0.6 | 0.0 |

The canonical quotient identifies states $\{0,1\}$ and $\{3,4\}$ as equivalent, reducing the system from 6 to 4 states while preserving the pressure exactly. The Galois connection guarantees this is the coarsest quotient with this property.

### 7.2 Phase Detection

For a parameterized family of 4-state systems with coupling parameter $\beta$:

| $\beta$ | $\rho(A)$ | Pressure | Period | Phase Type |
|---------|-----------|----------|--------|------------|
| 0.1 | 2.01 | 0.70 | 2 | Periodic |
| 1.0 | 3.09 | 1.13 | 2 | Periodic |
| 5.0 | 148.4 | 5.00 | 2 | Periodic |

The phase structure is detected via eigenvalues on the spectral circle.

### 7.3 Pressure Convergence

For primitive matrices, $P_n \to P$ at rate $O(\lambda_2/\lambda_1)^n$ where $\lambda_1, \lambda_2$ are the two largest eigenvalues. For nearly reducible matrices, convergence is slower. For periodic matrices, $P_n$ oscillates around $P$.

## 9. Discussion

### 9.1 Relationship to Classical Results

The pressure–spectral radius connection generalizes the well-known relationship for subshifts of finite type and finite Markov chains. Our framework extends this to closure-generated dynamics where admissibility is determined by semantic/algebraic closure rather than simple graph adjacency.

The Galois connection is a new result without direct classical precedent. While Galois connections between lattices of substructures and quotient structures are ubiquitous in universal algebra, the specific connection between *thermodynamic* (equilibrium faces) and *algebraic* (closure quotients) lattices appears to be novel.

### 9.2 Limitations

1. The current formalization treats finite systems only. Extension to countable systems requires additional measure-theoretic machinery.
2. The equilibrium-character correspondence is proved in the additive direction (characters are additive functionals). Full multiplicativity requires additional assumptions (irreducibility, extremality).
3. The Galois connection is between abstract quotients and functional faces; connecting to concrete physical phases requires additional interpretation.

### 9.3 Open Questions

1. Does the Galois connection upgrade to a categorical equivalence on suitable subcategories?
2. What is the tropical (zero-temperature) limit of the duality?
3. Can closure generators be reconstructed from equilibrium data?
4. What algebraic invariants of the semiring character space correspond to physically meaningful quantities?

## 10. Conclusion

We have established a rigorous Galois connection between closure quotients and equilibrium faces for finite weighted closure dynamical systems, verified in the Lean 4 proof assistant. This connection reveals that thermodynamic equilibrium data and algebraic quotient structure are dual perspectives on the same underlying mathematical reality. The framework provides computationally efficient algorithms for state-space minimization, phase detection, and semantic compression, opening a new research direction in thermodynamic algebraic semantics.
