# Berggren Orbits as Arithmetic Teleportation Skeletons: A Formally Verified Bridge from Pythagorean Dynamics to Quantum Circuit Compilation

## Abstract

We establish a formally verified bridge between the classical Berggren ternary tree of primitive Pythagorean triples and the structure of quantum stabilizer circuits. Our main contributions are:

1. **Word-level preservation**: Every word in the three Berggren generators, applied to the root triple (3,4,5), yields a primitive Pythagorean triple. (Theorem A)

2. **Euclidean shadow functoriality**: The Berggren generators act on Euclidean parameters (m,n) via 2×2 integer matrices, and this shadow map commutes with word evaluation through the parametrization. (Theorem C)

3. **SL(2,𝔽₃) generation**: The unit-determinant Euclidean shadow matrices, reduced modulo 3, generate all of SL(2,𝔽₃) ≅ Sp(2,𝔽₃), the symplectic group governing qutrit Clifford dynamics. (Theorem D)

4. **Circuit cost bound**: Berggren word length provides a certified lower bound on hypotenuse size: c ≥ 5 + |w| for any word w. (Theorem E)

5. **Lorentz invariance**: Word evaluation preserves the Lorentzian quadratic form Q(a,b,c) = a² + b² − c², confirming that the Berggren monoid is a subgroup of O(2,1;ℤ). (Theorem F)

All results are formally verified in Lean 4 with Mathlib, providing machine-checked certainty.

**Keywords:** arithmetic quantum compilation, Berggren tree, primitive Pythagorean triples, Clifford group, symplectic invariant, stabilizer circuits, quadratic form dynamics, certified circuit synthesis

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Motivation

The Berggren tree is a classical construction in number theory: three specific 3×3 integer matrices, applied iteratively to the root triple (3,4,5), generate every primitive Pythagorean triple exactly once [1,2]. This exhaustive generation theorem has been known since the 1930s and independently rediscovered multiple times.

Independently, quantum information theory has developed a rich structure theory for Clifford circuits — the subgroup of quantum gates generated by Hadamard, phase, and CNOT gates. For systems of dimension d (qudits), the Clifford group has a well-understood quotient to the finite symplectic group Sp(2n, 𝔽_d), where n is the number of qudits [3].

We observe that these two structures share a common algebraic substrate: both involve matrix groups preserving quadratic forms — the Berggren generators preserve the indefinite form Q(a,b,c) = a² + b² − c² over ℤ, while Clifford operations preserve a symplectic form over finite fields. Our contribution is to make this connection precise through a formally verified "shadow functor" from Berggren dynamics to symplectic dynamics over 𝔽₃.

### 1.2 Overview of Results

The key insight is that the Berggren action on triples lifts to a 2×2 action on Euclidean parameters, and this 2×2 action, reduced modulo 3, yields the full symplectic group Sp(2,𝔽₃) ≅ SL(2,𝔽₃). This provides:

- A canonical word encoding for primitive triples (addresses in the Berggren tree)
- A compositional shadow map to finite-state quantum dynamics
- A circuit cost model grounded in arithmetic growth

### 1.3 Related Work

The Berggren tree was introduced in [1] and its completeness proved in various ways [2,4]. The connection between integer quadratic forms and the Lorentz group is classical [5]. The structure of the Clifford group over finite fields is developed in [3,6]. To our knowledge, the explicit connection between Berggren dynamics and Clifford/symplectic structure is new.

## 2. Definitions and Notation

### 2.1 Berggren Generators

We define three 3×3 integer matrices:

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 & 2 \\ 2 & -1 & 2 \\ 2 & -2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}, \quad B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 2 \\ 2 & 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}, \quad C = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 2 & 2 \\ -2 & 1 & 2 \\ -2 & 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$$

A **Berggren word** is a finite sequence w = g₁g₂...gₖ where each gᵢ ∈ {A, B, C}.

The **word evaluation** on a triple t ∈ ℤ³ is defined by left-to-right application:
$$\text{evalWord}(g_1 g_2 \cdots g_k, t) = g_k(\cdots(g_2(g_1(t)))\cdots)$$

The **root triple** is r = (3, 4, 5).

### 2.2 Pythagorean and Primitiveness Predicates

A triple (a,b,c) ∈ ℤ³ is **Pythagorean** if a² + b² = c².

A Pythagorean triple is **primitive** if a > 0, b > 0, c > 0, and gcd(a,b) = 1.

(For Pythagorean triples with positive entries, gcd(a,b) = 1 implies gcd(a,b,c) = 1.)

### 2.3 Lorentzian Quadratic Form

$$Q(a,b,c) = a^2 + b^2 - c^2$$

The Pythagorean cone is {(a,b,c) : Q(a,b,c) = 0}, i.e., the light cone of (2+1)-dimensional Minkowski space over ℤ.

### 2.4 Euclidean Parametrization

Every primitive Pythagorean triple with a odd arises from coprime parameters m > n > 0 via:
$$a = m^2 - n^2, \quad b = 2mn, \quad c = m^2 + n^2$$

The root triple (3,4,5) corresponds to (m,n) = (2,1).

### 2.5 Euclidean Shadow Matrices

The Berggren action on triples induces a 2×2 action on Euclidean parameters:

$$E_A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad E_B = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad E_C = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$$

**Determinants:** det(E_A) = 1, det(E_B) = −1, det(E_C) = 1.

## 3. Main Results

### 3.1 Theorem A: Word-Level Preservation

**Theorem** (evalWord_preserves_primPyth). *For every Berggren word w, the triple evalWord(w, (3,4,5)) is a primitive Pythagorean triple.*

**Proof sketch.** By induction on |w|. The base case (3,4,5) is primitive Pythagorean by direct verification (3² + 4² = 5², gcd(3,4) = 1). The inductive step requires:

1. **Pythagorean preservation** (applyGen_pyth): Each generator preserves a² + b² = c². This follows by expanding the matrix product and applying nlinarith to the resulting polynomial identity.

2. **Coprimality preservation** (bergA_coprime, bergB_coprime, bergC_coprime): If gcd(a,b) = 1 and a² + b² = c², then gcd(a',b') = 1 for the child (a',b',c'). Proof: by contradiction, if prime p divides both a' and b', then p divides c' (since a'² + b'² = c'²). Using the explicit inverse formulas a = f(a',b',c'), b = g(a',b',c'), we show p | a and p | b, contradicting gcd(a,b) = 1.

3. **Positivity preservation** (applyGen_pos): Each generator maps positive triples with legs strictly less than hypotenuse to positive triples. This follows from the fact that for primitive Pythagorean triples, a < c and b < c.  □

### 3.2 Theorem B: Hypotenuse Growth

**Theorem** (applyGen_hyp_increase). *For any primitive Pythagorean triple (a,b,c) and any generator g, the child hypotenuse strictly exceeds the parent: c < (g · (a,b,c))₃.*

**Proof.** For generator A: the child hypotenuse is 2a − 2b + 3c. Since a < c and b < c (legs are strictly less than hypotenuse), we have 2a − 2b + 3c > 2a − 2c + 3c = 2a + c > c. The arguments for B and C are similar.  □

### 3.3 Theorem C: Euclidean Shadow Functoriality

**Theorem** (berggren_euclid_shadow_functorial). *For every Berggren word w and Euclidean parameters (m,n):*
$$\text{evalWord}(w, \text{euclid}(m,n)) = \text{euclid}(\text{shadowWord}(w, (m,n)))$$

*where euclid(m,n) = (m²−n², 2mn, m²+n²) and shadowWord applies the 2×2 shadow matrices.*

**Proof.** By induction on |w|. The base case is trivial. The inductive step uses the generator-level identity:

For generator A: bergA(m²−n², 2mn, m²+n²) = ((2m−n)²−m², 2(2m−n)m, (2m−n)²+m²), which equals euclid(2m−n, m) = euclid(E_A · (m,n)). Verification is by ring identity. Similarly for B and C.  □

### 3.4 Theorem D: SL(2,𝔽₃) Generation

**Theorem** (berggren_generates_SL2_F3). *The mod-3 reductions of E_A and E_C generate all of SL(2,𝔽₃).*

**Proof.** By exhaustive computation: the multiplicative closure of {E_A mod 3, E_C mod 3, I} under matrix multiplication modulo 3 has exactly 24 elements, all with determinant 1. Since |SL(2,𝔽₃)| = 3(3²−1) = 24, the closure equals SL(2,𝔽₃). Verified by native_decide in Lean 4.  □

**Significance.** SL(2,𝔽₃) ≅ Sp(2,𝔽₃) is the symplectic group governing qutrit Clifford dynamics. This establishes that the Berggren tree is an arithmetic lift of the qutrit stabilizer control layer.

### 3.5 Theorem E: Circuit Cost Bound

**Theorem** (berggren_depth_hyp_lower_bound). *For every Berggren word w:*
$$5 + |w| \leq c(\text{evalWord}(w, (3,4,5)))$$

*where c(·) denotes the hypotenuse.*

**Proof.** By induction on |w|. Base: c = 5 ≥ 5 + 0. Step: by Theorem B, the hypotenuse strictly increases, so c_{k+1} > c_k ≥ 5 + k, giving c_{k+1} ≥ 5 + k + 1.  □

**Interpretation.** The hypotenuse serves as a complexity certificate: a triple with hypotenuse c can only arise from words of length at most c − 5. Translated to the quantum shadow, this bounds the minimum number of Clifford gate operations needed to reach the corresponding stabilizer state.

### 3.6 Theorem F: Quadratic Form Invariance

**Theorem** (evalWord_preserves_Q). *For every Berggren word w and triple (a,b,c):*
$$Q(\text{evalWord}(w, (a,b,c))) = Q(a,b,c)$$

**Proof.** By induction on |w|. Each generator preserves Q by direct ring computation: Q(g·v) = Q(v) for g ∈ {A,B,C}.  □

## 4. The Arithmetic-to-Quantum Bridge

### 4.1 Architecture

The bridge consists of three layers:

```
Berggren Word Space                   ← syntax
       ↓ evalWord
Primitive Pythagorean Triples         ← integer semantics
       ↓ euclidParam⁻¹
Euclidean Parameters (m, n)           ← parameter space
       ↓ mod 3
SL(2, 𝔽₃) ≅ Sp(2, 𝔽₃) Elements     ← quantum semantics
```

The functoriality theorem (Theorem C) ensures that this diagram commutes: evaluating a word and then descending through the layers gives the same result as descending first and then evaluating the corresponding shadow operations.

### 4.2 The Shadow Functor

Formally, we have a monoid homomorphism:
$$\psi: \text{Free}(\{A,B,C\}) \to \text{Mat}(2 \times 2, \mathbb{Z}) \to \text{GL}(2, \mathbb{F}_3)$$

The image of ψ restricted to the det = 1 generators {A, C} is exactly SL(2, 𝔽₃). The generator B, with det(E_B) = −1, extends this to a subgroup of GL(2, 𝔽₃).

### 4.3 Why SL(2, 𝔽₃)?

The group SL(2, 𝔽₃) is the binary tetrahedral group, of order 24. It acts naturally on (𝔽₃)², and this action is the symplectic action on a single qutrit's Pauli algebra. The Berggren tree provides an *integer arithmetic presentation* of this finite group, where each generator corresponds to a specific qutrit Clifford operation.

## 6. Discussion

### 6.1 Implications for Quantum Compilation

Our results establish that the Berggren tree provides a *certified compilation index* for qutrit Clifford circuits. Given a Berggren word, one can:

1. Compute the corresponding primitive Pythagorean triple (guaranteed primitive by Theorem A)
2. Extract the mod-3 Euclidean shadow (compositional by Theorem C)
3. Obtain the corresponding SL(2,𝔽₃) element (covering all elements by Theorem D)
4. Read off the circuit cost from the word length (bounded by Theorem E)

### 6.2 The Lorentz Connection

Theorem F reveals the geometric backbone: Berggren generators preserve the Lorentzian form Q = a² + b² − c², placing them in O(2,1;ℤ). The determinant structure (det A = 1, det B = −1, det C = 1) provides a ℤ/2ℤ grading, distinguishing proper from improper Lorentz transformations. This grading persists in the quantum shadow as the determinant sign in GL(2, 𝔽₃).

### 6.3 Limitations

The current bridge operates at the level of qutrit (d = 3) Clifford dynamics. Extension to qubit (d = 2) dynamics requires a different reduction: the mod-2 reduction of the Euclidean matrices is trivial (all generators reduce to the identity). Extension to multi-qudit systems requires higher-dimensional analogues of the Berggren tree.

The cost bound c ≥ 5 + |w| is tight only in the growth rate: the actual hypotenuse grows exponentially, not linearly, with word length. Tighter bounds involving logarithmic relationships between |w| and log(c) would be more informative.

### 6.4 Non-Commutativity

The Berggren generators do not commute (Theorem: berggren_noncommutative). This non-commutativity is essential: it means that different words produce different triples, and the order of gate operations matters. This mirrors the non-commutativity of quantum gates, and is part of what makes the correspondence meaningful rather than degenerate.

## 7. Future Work

1. **Full Berggren completeness in Lean 4**: Formalize the theorem that every primitive Pythagorean triple appears in the Berggren tree (the descent/uniqueness theorem).

2. **Qubit bridge via alternative reductions**: The mod-2 reduction is trivial, but mod-5 or other prime reductions may yield qubit-relevant groups. Investigate SL(2, 𝔽₅) and its relation to icosahedral symmetry.

3. **Multi-qudit extension**: Generalize from 3×3 Berggren matrices to higher-dimensional quadratic-form-preserving groups and their finite-field shadows.

4. **Optimal circuit synthesis**: Use the Berggren descent algorithm as a circuit optimization strategy — the unique descent path provides a canonical normal form for circuits.

5. **Tropical resource theory**: Formalize the min-plus composition law on circuit costs and prove that Berggren word composition respects it.
