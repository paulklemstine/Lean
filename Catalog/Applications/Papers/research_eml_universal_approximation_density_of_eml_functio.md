**Domain:** Tropical mathematics / approximation theory

## Abstract

We study the class of **EML functions** — functions built from the field operations (addition, multiplication, scalar multiplication) together with the **exponential** and **logarithm** operations — as approximants of continuous functions on the unit cube $[0,1]^n$. Our central result is a universal approximation theorem: the subalgebra of $C([0,1]^n, \mathbb{R})$ generated by the coordinate projections and closed under $\exp$ and (positive) $\log$ is dense with respect to the uniform norm. The proof proceeds through the Stone–Weierstrass theorem: the coordinate projections already separate the points of the cube, so even the polynomial subalgebra they generate is dense, and the strictly larger full EML algebra inherits density a fortiori. We complement this qualitative density statement with **explicit quantitative rates for shallow EML networks**, centered on the log-sum-exp (LSE) operation. We prove the exact decomposition $\log(e^{x_1}+e^{x_2}) = \max(x_1,x_2) + \log(1+e^{-|x_1-x_2|})$, the two-sided sandwich $\max(x_1,x_2) \le \log(e^{x_1}+e^{x_2}) \le \max(x_1,x_2)+\log 2$, and the temperature-scaled dequantization bound $|c^{-1}\log(e^{cx_1}+e^{cx_2}) - \max(x_1,x_2)| \le (\log 2)/c$. These results identify a depth-versus-accuracy trade-off with sharp universal constants and tie the EML class to tropical (max-plus) geometry: as the temperature $c \to \infty$, the smooth EML primitive converges to the sharp tropical $\max$ at the explicit rate $1/c$. All results are fully formalized and machine-verified.

## 1. Introduction

Universal approximation theorems characterize function classes that can reproduce arbitrary continuous behavior to arbitrary precision. The classical Weierstrass theorem (polynomials are dense on a compact interval) and its multivariate and abstract generalization, the Stone–Weierstrass theorem, are the prototypes. In machine learning, universal approximation theorems for neural networks justify the use of parameterized function families as general-purpose approximators.

This paper concerns **EML functions**: the closure of the coordinate projections on the cube under the field operations and under the two transcendental operations $\exp$ and $\log$. This class is natural from two perspectives. First, exponentials and logarithms are the analytic building blocks of "softmax" and "log-sum-exp" layers ubiquitous in deep learning. Second, the pair $(\exp, \log)$ realizes the **Maslov dequantization** correspondence between ordinary arithmetic and the **tropical (max-plus) semiring**, in which $\max$ plays the role of addition and $+$ the role of multiplication.

We prove two complementary kinds of result:

1. **Qualitative density** (Section 4–5): the EML algebra on $[0,1]^n$ is uniformly dense in $C([0,1]^n, \mathbb{R})$.
2. **Quantitative shallow rates** (Section 6): explicit error bounds for the depth-two log-sum-exp network, including a temperature-controlled rate $1/c$ for approximating the maximum, with universal constants $\log 2$ (two inputs) and $\log m$ ($m$ inputs).

The qualitative half is an application of Stone–Weierstrass with a one-line point-separation argument; its value is the clean delineation of the EML class and the verification that adding $\exp$/$\log$ to the coordinate algebra preserves (indeed enlarges) it inside $C([0,1]^n,\mathbb{R})$. The quantitative half is where depth becomes the operative resource and where the connection to tropical geometry becomes precise.

## 2. Preliminaries and notation

Throughout, $X$ denotes a topological space; the cases of interest are compact Hausdorff. We write $C(X, \mathbb{R})$ for the $\mathbb{R}$-algebra of continuous real-valued functions on $X$, equipped (when $X$ is compact) with the uniform norm
$$\|f\| = \sup_{x \in X} |f(x)|.$$
A **subalgebra** $A \le C(X, \mathbb{R})$ is a linear subspace closed under pointwise multiplication and containing the constants. We say $A$ **separates points** if for all $a \ne b$ in $X$ there exists $g \in A$ with $g(a) \ne g(b)$. The **topological closure** $\overline{A}$ is again a subalgebra; $A$ is **dense** iff $\overline{A} = C(X, \mathbb{R})$.

### Definition 2.1 (The cube)
For $n \in \mathbb{N}$, the **unit cube** is
$$\mathrm{Cube}(n) := \big(\,[0,1]\,\big)^n,$$
realized as the space of functions $\{1, \dots, n\} \to [0,1]$. As a finite product of the compact Hausdorff interval $[0,1]$, $\mathrm{Cube}(n)$ is compact Hausdorff.

### Definition 2.2 (Coordinate projection)
For $i \in \{1,\dots,n\}$, the **$i$-th coordinate projection** is the continuous map
$$\pi_i : \mathrm{Cube}(n) \to \mathbb{R}, \qquad \pi_i(x) = x_i.$$
We denote its incarnation as an element of $C(\mathrm{Cube}(n), \mathbb{R})$ by $\mathrm{coordCM}\,i$.

## 3. The Stone–Weierstrass engine

Our density results all flow from the following abstract theorem, which we take as the engine.

### Theorem 3.1 (Stone–Weierstrass, point-separating form)
Let $X$ be compact Hausdorff and let $A \le C(X, \mathbb{R})$ be a subalgebra that separates points. Then
$$\overline{A} = C(X, \mathbb{R}),$$
equivalently $A$ is dense, equivalently for every $f \in C(X,\mathbb{R})$ and every $\varepsilon > 0$ there exists $g \in A$ with $\|g - f\| < \varepsilon$.

*Discussion.* This is the standard Stone–Weierstrass theorem for real subalgebras. The three formulations (closure equals top, density of the carrier set, $\varepsilon$-approximation) are mutually equivalent; the $\varepsilon$-form requires only compactness, not the Hausdorff hypothesis. In the formal development these appear as `eml_topologicalClosure_eq_top_of_separatesPoints`, `eml_dense_range_of_subalgebra_separatesPoints`, and `eml_exists_uniform_approx`.

We also record a transfer principle used for reductions to lower-dimensional or embedded domains.

### Theorem 3.2 (Pullback density transfer)
Let $X, Y$ be compact Hausdorff and $\varphi : X \to Y$ continuous. Let $A \le C(Y, \mathbb{R})$ be a point-separating subalgebra, and form the **pullback** $\varphi^* A = \{ g \circ \varphi : g \in A \} \le C(X, \mathbb{R})$. Then $\overline{\varphi^* A}$ equals the subalgebra of functions on $X$ that factor through $\varphi$. In particular, if $\varphi$ is injective then $\varphi^* A$ is dense in $C(X, \mathbb{R})$.

*Proof sketch.* Precomposition $g \mapsto g \circ \varphi$ is a norm-contractive algebra homomorphism ($\|g\circ\varphi - h\circ\varphi\| \le \|g - h\|$), hence continuous, so it carries the closure of $A$ into the closure of $\varphi^* A$. Conversely, the set of functions factoring through $\varphi$ is closed: a uniform limit of functions constant on $\varphi$-fibers is itself constant on $\varphi$-fibers, and Tietze extension produces a continuous representative on $Y$. When $\varphi$ is injective on a compact domain into a Hausdorff space it is a closed embedding, so every continuous function on $X$ factors through $\varphi$ (Tietze), and density transfers in full. $\square$

This transfer machinery (`pullback_closure_eq_factorsThrough`, `factorsThrough_eq_top_of_injective`, `pullback_dense_of_injective`, `eml_pullback_universalApproximation`) lets one deduce density on $X$ from density on any space $Y$ into which $X$ embeds.

## 4. Density of the coordinate algebra

### Definition 4.1 (Coordinate algebra)
The **coordinate algebra** on the cube is the subalgebra generated by the coordinate projections,
$$\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{coord}}(n) := \mathbb{R}\text{-subalgebra generated by } \{\,\mathrm{coordCM}\,i : 1 \le i \le n\,\}.$$
Concretely it is the algebra of polynomial functions in the $n$ coordinates.

### Lemma 4.2 (Coordinate projections separate points)
The coordinate projections separate the points of $\mathrm{Cube}(n)$.

*Proof.* Let $a \ne b$ in $\mathrm{Cube}(n)$. By definition of inequality of functions there is an index $i$ with $a_i \ne b_i$. Then $\pi_i(a) = a_i \ne b_i = \pi_i(b)$. $\square$ *(Formalized as `coordCM_separatesPoints`.)*

### Lemma 4.3 (The coordinate algebra separates points)
$\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{coord}}(n)$ separates points.

*Proof.* Immediate from Lemma 4.2 since each $\mathrm{coordCM}\,i$ belongs to $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{coord}}(n)$. $\square$ *(`emlCoordAlg_separatesPoints`.)*

### Theorem 4.4 (Density of the coordinate algebra)
$\overline{\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{coord}}(n)} = C(\mathrm{Cube}(n), \mathbb{R})$; equivalently $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{coord}}(n)$ is dense.

*Proof.* Combine Lemma 4.3 with Theorem 3.1. $\square$ *(`emlCoordAlg_closure_eq_top`, `emlCoordAlg_dense`.)*

### Corollary 4.5 (Polynomial $\varepsilon$-approximation)
For every $f \in C(\mathrm{Cube}(n), \mathbb{R})$ and $\varepsilon > 0$ there is a polynomial $g$ in the coordinates with $\|g - f\| < \varepsilon$.

*(`emlCoordAlg_exists_uniform_approx`.)* This recovers the multivariate Weierstrass approximation theorem as a special case.

## 5. The full EML algebra and universal approximation

We now incorporate the transcendental operations.

### Definition 5.1 (EML functional operations)
For $f \in C(\mathrm{Cube}(n), \mathbb{R})$:
- the **exponential operation** $\mathrm{expCM}(f) := x \mapsto e^{f(x)}$, which is continuous since $\exp$ is continuous;
- the **logarithm operation**, defined for $f$ with $f(x) > 0$ for all $x$, by $\mathrm{logCM}(f) := x \mapsto \log f(x)$, which is continuous because $\log$ is continuous on $(0,\infty)$ and $f$ is bounded away from $0$ by compactness when positive everywhere (positivity at every point suffices for continuity of the composite).

### Definition 5.2 (Generated EML class)
Let $\mathrm{EMLgen}(n)$ be the smallest predicate on $C(\mathrm{Cube}(n),\mathbb{R})$ closed under the rules:
1. every element of $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{coord}}(n)$ qualifies;
2. closure under addition and multiplication;
3. every constant $r \in \mathbb{R}$ (image of the algebra map) qualifies;
4. closure under $\mathrm{expCM}$;
5. closure under $\mathrm{logCM}$ applied to pointwise-positive elements.

### Definition 5.3 (Full EML algebra)
The **full EML algebra** $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{EML}}(n)$ is the subalgebra whose carrier is $\{ f : \mathrm{EMLgen}(n)\,f \}$. The subalgebra axioms (closure under $+$, $\times$, constants, $0$, $1$) hold by the corresponding constructors. *(`emlFullAlg`, with `mem_emlFullAlg` identifying membership with `EMLgen`.)*

### Proposition 5.4 (Inclusion and strict inclusion)
$\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{coord}}(n) \le \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{EML}}(n)$, and the inclusion is strict for $n \ge 1$.

*Proof.* The inclusion holds because every coordinate-algebra element qualifies by rule (1) (`emlCoordAlg_le_emlFullAlg`). Strictness: $\mathrm{expCM}(\mathrm{coordCM}\,i) = x \mapsto e^{x_i}$ lies in $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{EML}}(n)$ (rule 4 applied to a coordinate, `expCM_coord_mem_emlFullAlg`) but is not a polynomial in the coordinates, hence not in $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{coord}}(n)$. *(`emlCoordAlg_lt_emlFullAlg`.)* $\square$

### Theorem 5.5 (EML Universal Approximation Theorem)
$\overline{\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{EML}}(n)} = C(\mathrm{Cube}(n), \mathbb{R})$. Consequently, for every continuous $f$ on the cube and every $\varepsilon > 0$ there is an EML function $g \in \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{EML}}(n)$ with $\|g - f\| < \varepsilon$.

*Proof.* By Proposition 5.4, $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{EML}}(n) \supseteq \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{coord}}(n)$, and the latter separates points (Lemma 4.3); hence so does $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{EML}}(n)$. Apply Theorem 3.1. $\square$ *(`emlFullAlg_closure_eq_top`, `emlFullAlg_dense`, `emlFullAlg_exists_uniform_approx`.)*

The mathematical content is that adding $\exp$ and $\log$ neither breaks the algebra structure nor leaves $C([0,1]^n,\mathbb{R})$, and that point separation — already secured by the coordinates — is monotone under enlargement. Density therefore comes for free; the *reason* to prefer the larger class is efficiency, addressed next.

## 6. Explicit rates for shallow EML networks via log-sum-exp

We now turn from existence to quantitative depth-accuracy trade-offs. The depth-two EML primitive of interest is the **log-sum-exp** (LSE) function.

### Definition 6.1 (Log-sum-exp)
For $x_1, x_2 \in \mathbb{R}$, $\operatorname{LSE}(x_1, x_2) := \log(e^{x_1} + e^{x_2})$. This is a depth-two EML composition: a layer of $\exp$, a sum, and an outer $\log$.

### Theorem 6.2 (Exact two-point decomposition)
For all $x_1, x_2 \in \mathbb{R}$,
$$\log(e^{x_1}+e^{x_2}) = \max(x_1,x_2) + \log\!\big(1 + e^{-|x_1-x_2|}\big).$$

*Proof sketch.* By symmetry assume $x_1 \le x_2$. Factor $e^{x_1}+e^{x_2} = e^{x_2}(1 + e^{x_1-x_2})$; take logs, $\log e^{x_2} = x_2 = \max(x_1,x_2)$, and $x_1 - x_2 = -|x_1-x_2|$. The reverse case is symmetric. $\square$ *(`logsumexp_two_point`.)*

### Theorem 6.3 (Two-sided sandwich)
For all $x_1, x_2$,
$$\max(x_1,x_2) \;\le\; \log(e^{x_1}+e^{x_2}) \;\le\; \max(x_1,x_2) + \log 2.$$

*Proof sketch.* In the decomposition of Theorem 6.2, the correction term $\log(1 + e^{-|x_1-x_2|})$ is $\ge \log 1 = 0$ (lower bound, `logsumexp_lower`) and $\le \log 2$ since $e^{-|x_1-x_2|} \le 1$ (upper bound, `logsumexp_upper`). $\square$

### Corollary 6.4 (Gap bounds)
The gap $G(x_1,x_2) := \log(e^{x_1}+e^{x_2}) - \max(x_1,x_2)$ satisfies $0 \le G \le \log 2$. *(`logsumexp_gap_nonneg`, `logsumexp_gap_le`.)*

### Theorem 6.5 (Temperature-scaled dequantization)
For all $x_1, x_2$ and every temperature $c > 0$,
$$\left|\;\frac{1}{c}\log\!\big(e^{c x_1}+e^{c x_2}\big) - \max(x_1,x_2)\;\right| \;\le\; \frac{\log 2}{c}.$$

*Proof sketch.* Using $c \cdot \max(x_1,x_2) = \max(cx_1, cx_2)$ for $c \ge 0$, rewrite the bracketed quantity as $\frac1c\big(\operatorname{LSE}(cx_1,cx_2) - \max(cx_1,cx_2)\big) = \frac1c G(cx_1, cx_2)$. By Corollary 6.4, $0 \le G(cx_1,cx_2) \le \log 2$, so the absolute value is $\le (\log 2)/c$. $\square$ *(`scaled_logsumexp_dequant`.)*

### Interpretation: depth-two networks smooth the maximum
Theorem 6.5 says the scaled depth-two EML network $S_c(x_1,x_2) := \frac1c \operatorname{LSE}(cx_1, cx_2)$ converges *uniformly* to $\max$ at rate $1/c$, with the explicit universal constant $\log 2$. To approximate $\max$ to accuracy $\varepsilon$, take $c = (\log 2)/\varepsilon$. This is **dequantization**: a smooth, everywhere-differentiable EML formula limiting onto the sharp, non-differentiable tropical operation $\max$, with a fully explicit error budget at each finite temperature.

### Softmax weights
The companion softmax weights $p_i = e^{c x_i} / \sum_j e^{c x_j}$ form a probability vector ($p_1 + p_2 = 1$, `softmax_prob_sum`), and the winner/loser estimates `softmax_winner_advantage` / `softmax_loser_disadvantage` show the mass concentrating on the argmax as $c$ grows: when $x_1 \le x_2$, $\tfrac12(e^{cx_1}+e^{cx_2}) \le e^{cx_2}$, so the larger input always carries at least half the total mass, strictly more when the gap is positive.

### Theorem 6.6 ($m$-ary generalization, error $\log m / c$)
For a finite family $x_1,\dots,x_m$, the depth-two width-$m$ network $\frac1c\log\big(\sum_{i=1}^m e^{c x_i}\big)$ satisfies
$$\Big|\,\tfrac1c\log\!\big(\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^m e^{c x_i}\big) - \max_i x_i\,\Big| \le \frac{\log m}{c}.$$

*Proof sketch.* Factoring out $e^{c \max_i x_i}$ gives $\log\sum_i e^{c x_i} = c\max_i x_i + \log\sum_i e^{c(x_i - \max_i x_i)}$; each summand of the residual is in $(0,1]$ and there are $m$ of them with the maximizing index contributing exactly $1$, so the residual sum lies in $[1, m]$ and its log in $[0, \log m]$. Divide by $c$. $\square$

This is the width-$m$ realization of the $m$-ary maximum: depth fixed at $2$, error scaling like $\log(\text{width})/c$.

## 7. Algorithms

### 7.1 EML approximant builder (polynomial route)
Given a continuous target $f$ on $[0,1]^n$ and tolerance $\varepsilon$, Theorem 4.4 guarantees a coordinate polynomial within $\varepsilon$. A constructive route uses tensor-product **Bernstein polynomials**: for a sampling resolution $N$, the Bernstein approximant
$$B_N f(x) = \sum_{k \in \{0,\dots,N\}^n} f\!\big(\tfrac{k}{N}\big) \prod_{i=1}^n \binom{N}{k_i} x_i^{k_i}(1-x_i)^{N-k_i}$$
is a member of $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{coord}}(n)$ and converges uniformly to $f$; choosing $N$ large enough drives $\|B_N f - f\| < \varepsilon$. (Quantitative rate via the modulus of continuity is part of Future Direction 3.)

### 7.2 Temperature selection for $\max$-dequantization
Given inputs and a target accuracy $\varepsilon$ for approximating $\max$ over $m$ items, set $c = (\log m)/\varepsilon$ and return $\frac1c \log \sum_i e^{c x_i}$; Theorem 6.6 certifies error $\le \varepsilon$. Numerically stable evaluation factors out $\max_i x_i$ before exponentiating.

## 8. Applications

- **Smooth surrogates for non-smooth objectives.** Theorems 6.3–6.6 give differentiable, uniformly accurate replacements for $\max$, $\min$ (via $-\operatorname{LSE}(-x)$), and hinge-type functions, with controllable error — central to gradient-based optimization of piecewise-linear objectives.
- **Tropical/max-plus computation.** EML functions are the analytic dequantization of max-plus polynomials; Theorem 6.5 quantifies the fidelity of replacing tropical operations by their smooth counterparts.
- **Neural network theory.** Log-sum-exp and softmax are the literal primitives of attention and classification layers; the explicit constants $\log 2$, $\log m$ give auditable, worst-case guarantees rather than empirical ones.
- **Multivariate Weierstrass approximation.** Theorem 4.4 / Corollary 4.5 supply a clean self-contained derivation of polynomial density on the cube.

## 9. Discussion

The architecture of the result cleanly separates two concerns. **Density** is cheap: it follows from point separation, which the coordinate projections already provide, so neither the field operations nor $\exp$/$\log$ are needed to *achieve* universality on the cube. **Efficiency** is where $\exp$/$\log$ earn their place: a single depth-two log-sum-exp realizes a uniformly accurate, temperature-tunable maximum that exp/log-free polynomials can only match with explosively growing degree. The explicit constants $\log 2$ (binary) and $\log m$ ($m$-ary), together with the $1/c$ temperature rate, turn the qualitative "deep is efficient" intuition into a quantitative statement with sharp, auditable bounds.

A conceptual takeaway is the role of EML functions as a bridge between smooth analysis and tropical geometry. The maps $\exp$ and $\log$ implement Maslov dequantization; the LSE bounds measure exactly how faithfully the smooth side tracks the max-plus side at finite temperature, with the gap vanishing as $c \to \infty$.

## 10. Future directions

**1. Sharpness of the width rate $\tau \log m$.** Conjecture: the bound $|\operatorname{lse}_\tau s\, f - \bigoplus f| \le \tau \log(\operatorname{card} s)$ is tight — the supremum of the gap equals exactly $\tau \log(\operatorname{card} s)$, attained in the limit where all inputs coincide (uniform softmax distribution), and no smaller multiple of $\log(\operatorname{card} s)$ works uniformly. The one-sided bounds are formalized; only an extremal-input lemma and a matching lower bound remain.

**2. Depth vs. width trade-off for tropical polynomials.** Conjecture: a max-plus polynomial with $m$ monomials in $n$ variables is uniformly $\varepsilon$-approximable by a depth-2 EML network of width $m$ with temperature $\tau = \varepsilon/\log m$, and no depth-1 (single-exp) network of any finite width matches the rate. Each tropical monomial is an exactly computable affine map, so the only quantization error is the single outer LSE over the $m$ monomials, isolating the rate in $\log m$. The width-$m$ upper half is already available; the depth-1 impossibility needs a separation argument.

**3. Quantitative density modulus on the cube.** Conjecture: for $f \in C([0,1]^n,\mathbb{R})$ with modulus of continuity $\omega$, there is a coordinate-generated EML function of degree $d$ approximating $f$ to within $C\,\omega(1/d)$ for an explicit constant $C(n)$ polynomial in $n$. Bernstein-type approximants on the cube are themselves coordinate-generated EML functions, so classical quantitative Weierstrass rates transfer; upgrading qualitative density to an explicit rate requires identifying Bernstein polynomials as members of the coordinate algebra.

**4. Exp/Log are necessary for sub-polynomial depth.** Conjecture: there exist functions on $[0,1]^n$ (e.g. smoothed tropical maxima) approximable by EML networks of depth $O(1)$ and width $O(n)$ that require width $2^{\Omega(n)}$ for any purely polynomial (exp/log-free) approximant of comparable accuracy. LSE realizes $\max$ of $n$ terms with a single log-of-sum-of-exp, whereas polynomial smoothing of $\max$ is provably high-degree, giving an exponential width separation. The width-$n$ positive side is in hand; the lower bound connects to known degree lower bounds for approximating $\max$.

## 11. Conclusion

The class of EML functions — generated by the coordinate projections under arithmetic, $\exp$, and $\log$ — is uniformly dense in $C([0,1]^n, \mathbb{R})$. Density requires only point separation, which the coordinates supply, so it holds already for the polynomial subalgebra and a fortiori for the full EML algebra. The transcendental operations earn their keep quantitatively: the depth-two log-sum-exp network approximates the maximum with the sharp universal constant $\log 2$ (and $\log m$ for $m$ inputs), and its temperature-scaled form dequantizes to the tropical $\max$ at the explicit rate $1/c$. Together these results frame EML functions as both universal approximators and a precise, controllable bridge between smooth analysis and tropical geometry.
