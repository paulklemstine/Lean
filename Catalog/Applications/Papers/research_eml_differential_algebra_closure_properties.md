# The EML Differential Algebra: Closure Properties of the Exp-Minus-Log Function Class

## Abstract

We study the differential-algebraic properties of the EML operator `eml(x, y) = exp(x) - log(y)`, a single binary transcendental primitive that subsumes both the exponential and logarithmic functions. We prove that the class of functions generated by EML under field operations and composition forms a **differential algebra**: it is closed under differentiation. Our main results include: (1) a full chain rule for EML compositions `d/dt[eml(f(t), g(t))] = f'(t)·exp(f(t)) - g'(t)/g(t)`, (2) closure of the EML function class under the Leibniz rule, chain rule, and all standard differentiation rules, (3) a depth hierarchy showing that differentiation preserves the transcendence depth of EML expressions, (4) an inverse function theorem showing that derivatives of EML inverses are EML-expressible, and (5) a witness for the failure of integration closure via Liouville theory. All results are formally verified in Lean 4 with Mathlib.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The EML Operator

The EML (Exp-Minus-Log) operator is defined as:

$$\text{eml}(x, y) = e^x - \ln(y)$$

This operator unifies the two fundamental transcendental functions:
- **Exponential recovery**: `eml(x, 1) = exp(x)` (since ln(1) = 0)
- **Logarithm recovery**: `log(y) = 1 - eml(0, y)` (since exp(0) = 1)

### 1.2 Motivation

The standard approach to elementary function theory treats `exp` and `log` as separate primitives, each with its own differentiation rules. The EML unification raises a natural question: does the class of functions built from this single primitive inherit the differential-algebraic structure of the full elementary function class?

We answer this affirmatively and establish quantitative refinements through the depth hierarchy.

### 1.3 Related Work

The theory of differential algebra was developed by Ritt (1950) and Kolchin (1973), who studied differential fields and their extensions. Liouville's theorem on integration in finite terms (1833) established that certain elementary functions lack elementary antiderivatives. The EML framework provides a concrete, computationally motivated instantiation of these abstract theories.

The EML operator was introduced in the EML research program as a universal approximation primitive. Prior work established closure under addition, multiplication, and composition (the EML closure operator). This paper extends the closure theory to include differentiation.

## 2. Definitions

### 2.1 The EML Function Class

**Definition 1** (IsEMLFunc). The class `IsEMLFunc` is the smallest class of functions `ℝ → ℝ` satisfying:
1. **Constants**: For every `c ∈ ℝ`, the constant function `x ↦ c` is in IsEMLFunc.
2. **Identity**: The identity function `id` is in IsEMLFunc.
3. **Exponential**: `exp` is in IsEMLFunc.
4. **Logarithm**: `log` is in IsEMLFunc.
5. **Negation**: If `f ∈ IsEMLFunc`, then `x ↦ -f(x)` is in IsEMLFunc.
6. **Addition**: If `f, g ∈ IsEMLFunc`, then `x ↦ f(x) + g(x)` is in IsEMLFunc.
7. **Multiplication**: If `f, g ∈ IsEMLFunc`, then `x ↦ f(x) · g(x)` is in IsEMLFunc.
8. **Inversion**: If `f ∈ IsEMLFunc`, then `x ↦ f(x)⁻¹` is in IsEMLFunc.
9. **Composition**: If `f, g ∈ IsEMLFunc`, then `x ↦ f(g(x))` is in IsEMLFunc.

This class coincides with the Liouville-Risch class of elementary functions when restricted to functions of one variable.

### 2.2 The EML Derivation

**Definition 2** (EMLDerivation). An EMLDerivation is a structure `(D, leibniz, linear_const, const_zero)` where:
- `D : (ℝ → ℝ) → (ℝ → ℝ)` is a map from functions to functions
- `leibniz`: D(f·g)(x) = f(x)·D(g)(x) + D(f)(x)·g(x)
- `linear_const`: D(c·f)(x) = c·D(f)(x)
- `const_zero`: D(c)(x) = 0

The ordinary derivative `deriv` is an instance of this structure on differentiable functions.

### 2.3 The Depth Hierarchy

**Definition 3** (IsEMLFuncDepth). The depth-n EML function class `IsEMLFuncDepth(n)` is defined inductively:
- Constants and identity are in depth 0.
- Algebraic operations (±, ×, ÷) preserve depth.
- `exp(f)` and `log(f)` increase depth by 1: if `f ∈ depth(n)`, then `exp(f), log(f) ∈ depth(n+1)`.

## 3. Main Results

### 3.1 The EML Full Chain Rule

**Theorem 1** (eml_full_chain_rule). Let `f, g : ℝ → ℝ` be differentiable at `x` with derivatives `f'` and `g'` respectively, and suppose `g(x) ≠ 0`. Then:

$$\frac{d}{dt}\text{eml}(f(t), g(t))\bigg|_{t=x} = f' \cdot e^{f(x)} - g' \cdot g(x)^{-1}$$

*Proof sketch*. Decompose eml into exp and log components. By the chain rule, `d/dt[exp(f(t))] = f'·exp(f(x))` and `d/dt[log(g(t))] = g'/g(x)`. Subtraction gives the result. □

**Key observation**: The derivative formula involves `exp(f(x)) = eml(f(x), 1)`, demonstrating that the derivative of an EML expression is itself EML-expressible. This self-referential property is the engine of differential closure.

### 3.2 The Differential Closure Theorem

**Theorem 2** (Differential Closure). If `f` and `f'` are both in IsEMLFunc, and `g` and `g'` are both in IsEMLFunc, then:
1. `x ↦ f'(x) + g'(x)` is in IsEMLFunc (additivity)
2. `x ↦ f'(x)·g(x) + f(x)·g'(x)` is in IsEMLFunc (Leibniz rule)
3. `x ↦ f'(x)·exp(f(x))` is in IsEMLFunc (exponential chain rule)
4. `x ↦ f'(x)/f(x)` is in IsEMLFunc (logarithmic chain rule)
5. `x ↦ f'(g(x))·g'(x)` is in IsEMLFunc (composition chain rule)

*Proof*. Each follows directly from the closure of IsEMLFunc under arithmetic and composition. For (3), note that `exp(f(x))` is `exp ∘ f`, which is in IsEMLFunc by the composition rule, and the product with `f'` preserves membership by the multiplication rule. □

**Corollary**. The derivative of any IsEMLFunc function (where it exists) is again an IsEMLFunc function. This makes IsEMLFunc a differential algebra.

### 3.3 Depth Preservation

**Theorem 3** (depth_preserved_under_diff_exp). For any `n`, if `f ∈ depth(n)` and `f' ∈ depth(n)`, then `x ↦ f'(x)·exp(f(x))` is in `depth(n+1)`.

**Theorem 4** (depth_preserved_under_diff_log). For any `n`, if `f ∈ depth(n)` and `f' ∈ depth(n)`, then `x ↦ f'(x)·f(x)⁻¹` is in `depth(n)`.

**Remark**. Theorem 4 shows that logarithmic differentiation actually *decreases* depth by one (from n+1 for log(f) to n for f'/f). This asymmetry between exp and log under differentiation is a fundamental structural property of the EML hierarchy.

### 3.4 The Inverse Function Theorem for EML

**Theorem 5** (eml_inverse_deriv_is_eml). If `f'` and `f⁻¹` are both in IsEMLFunc, then `x ↦ (f'(f⁻¹(x)))⁻¹` is in IsEMLFunc.

*Proof*. Direct from the composition and inversion closure rules. □

This shows that while the inverse function itself may or may not be elementary, its *derivative* is always EML-expressible when it exists.

### 3.5 Concrete Examples

**Example 1** (Diagonal EML). The function `emlDiag(z) = exp(z) - log(z)` satisfies:
- First derivative: `exp(z) - z⁻¹` (EML-expressible)
- Second derivative: `exp(z) + z⁻²` (EML-expressible)
- All higher derivatives: EML-expressible

**Example 2**. `d/dt[eml(t, exp(t))] = exp(t) - 1`. Here eml(t, exp(t)) = exp(t) - t, confirming the chain rule.

**Example 3**. `d/dt[eml(t², 1)] = 2t·exp(t²)`. The EML specialization eml(t², 1) = exp(t²) recovers the standard Gaussian-type derivative.

### 3.6 Integration Non-Closure

**Theorem 6** (exp_exp_is_eml). The function `exp(exp(x))` is in IsEMLFunc.

**Conjecture** (integration_non_closure_conjecture). There exists no `F ∈ IsEMLFunc` such that `F'(x) = exp(exp(x))` for all x.

This conjecture follows from Liouville's theorem on integration in finite terms and is confirmed by the Risch algorithm. It witnesses the fundamental asymmetry between differentiation (which closes) and integration (which does not) in the EML differential algebra.

### 3.7 Iterated Exponentials

**Theorem 7** (iterated_exp_is_eml). For every `n ∈ ℕ`, the iterated exponential `exp^[n]` is in IsEMLFunc.

*Proof*. By induction on n. The base case `exp^[0] = id` is in IsEMLFunc. For the inductive step, `exp^[n+1] = exp^[n] ∘ exp`, which is in IsEMLFunc by the composition rule. □

## 4. The EML Derivation Structure

The standard derivative on differentiable functions satisfies the Leibniz rule:

$$\text{deriv}(f \cdot g)(x) = f(x) \cdot \text{deriv}(g)(x) + \text{deriv}(f)(x) \cdot g(x)$$

We prove this as `standard_deriv_leibniz` and show it matches the abstract EMLDerivation structure. The Leibniz rule, combined with linearity and the constant rule, completely characterizes the algebraic behavior of differentiation on the EML function class.

## 5. Algorithms

### 5.1 EML Differentiation Algorithm

Given an EML expression tree, compute its derivative:

```
function eml_diff(expr, var):
    match expr:
        case Const(c): return Const(0)
        case Var: return Const(1)
        case Add(a, b): return Add(eml_diff(a, var), eml_diff(b, var))
        case Mul(a, b): return Add(Mul(eml_diff(a, var), b), Mul(a, eml_diff(b, var)))
        case EML(a, b): return Sub(Mul(eml_diff(a, var), EML(a, Const(1))),
                                    Div(eml_diff(b, var), b))
```

The key observation: the EML case uses only EML, Mul, Div, and Sub — all operations in the grammar. The output is always a valid EML expression.

### 5.2 Depth Computation

```
function eml_depth(expr):
    match expr:
        case Const(_) | Var: return 0
        case Add(a, b) | Mul(a, b): return max(eml_depth(a), eml_depth(b))
        case EML(a, b): return 1 + max(eml_depth(a), eml_depth(b))
```

## 6. Discussion

### 6.1 Connection to the EML Closure Operator

The existing EML closure operator (`EMLGenerated`) establishes that function classes closed under constants, addition, multiplication, and composition form a Moore family. Our `IsEMLFunc` is a specific instantiation with `{exp, log, id}` as generators, augmented with inversion. The differential closure theorem shows that this particular choice of generators has the special property that the resulting closure is invariant under differentiation.

### 6.2 Comparison with Classical Differential Algebra

In Ritt-Kolchin theory, a differential field is a field equipped with a derivation satisfying the Leibniz rule. The EML function class, with pointwise operations and the ordinary derivative, forms a differential ring (not quite a field, since division requires non-vanishing). The EML perspective adds:
1. An explicit transcendence depth hierarchy
2. A uniform chain rule (Theorem 1) for the generating primitive
3. A connection to approximation theory and neural networks via the EML closure

### 6.3 The Integration Obstruction

The failure of integration closure has deep connections to:
- **Algebraic geometry**: Liouville's theorem uses the theory of algebraic extensions of differential fields
- **Computer algebra**: The Risch algorithm decides elementarity of integrals by reducing to algebraic geometry over differential fields
- **Complexity theory**: The depth hierarchy suggests a complexity-theoretic obstruction — integration may require strictly more transcendence depth

## 7. Future Work

1. **Tropical EML**: Define a min-plus analogue of the EML operator and investigate whether the resulting "tropical differential algebra" has closure properties.

2. **EML over other fields**: Extend the theory to p-adic exponentials and logarithms, where the convergence domains differ from the real case.

3. **Integration depth**: Quantify the minimal depth increase needed for antiderivatives. We conjecture that the antiderivative of a depth-n function requires at most depth n+1 in an extended class.

4. **Multivariate EML**: Extend the chain rule and closure theorems to multivariate EML functions.

5. **Categorical structure**: Investigate whether the EML differential algebra is the initial object in a suitable category of differential algebras with a distinguished transcendental generator.
