# The Secret Bandwidth of a Prime

*How much can the way a polynomial falls apart tell you about a number — and why the answer is decided by a single, austere group-theoretic quantity.*

## A code hidden in plain sight

Take the polynomial $x^3 + x + 1$. It has no rational roots and no rational factorization; over the ordinary integers it is as indivisible as a prime number. But reduce it modulo a prime $p$ and it suddenly becomes brittle. Modulo $5$ it stays whole — an irreducible cubic. Modulo $11$ it cracks into a line and a quadratic, $(x - a)(x^2 + bx + c)$. Modulo $47$ it shatters completely into three separate linear factors.

Which of these three fates befalls a given prime is not random, although it looks random. It is a number-theoretic fingerprint, and it is the oldest kind of secret in mathematics. For $x^2 + 1$ the fingerprint is the law of quadratic reciprocity: the polynomial splits exactly when $p \equiv 1 \pmod 4$. Knowing $p$ modulo $4$ tells you the shape of the factorization, and knowing the shape tells you $p$ modulo $4$. The information flows in both directions, perfectly, without loss.

For $x^3 + x + 1$ that clean two-way street does not exist. There is no modulus $m$ whatsoever such that $p \bmod m$ determines how the cubic factors. This is the well-known frontier where abelian class field theory stops and the non-abelian world begins. And yet *some* information clearly leaks: the cubic factors into three linear pieces or stays irreducible only when $-31$ is a square modulo $p$; it breaks as a line-times-quadratic only when $-31$ is a non-square. So the residue class of $p$ does not determine the shape, but it constrains it.

The obvious question — how much? — turns out to have an exact and startlingly simple answer, and this article is about that answer.

## Measuring a leak in bits

Information theory gives us the right instrument. If $X$ and $Y$ are two random readings of the same underlying object, their **mutual information**
$$I(X ; Y) = H(X) + H(Y) - H(X, Y)$$
measures, in bits, how much learning one of them tells you about the other. Here $H$ is Shannon entropy, $H(X) = -\sum_x \Pr[X = x]\log_2 \Pr[X = x]$; the quantity $I$ is zero exactly when the two readings are statistically independent, and it equals $H(X)$ exactly when $Y$ determines $X$ outright.

So set $X$ = the residue class of a random prime $p$ modulo a suitable modulus, and set $T$ = the *splitting type* of $p$: the multiset of degrees of the irreducible factors of our polynomial modulo $p$. For a cubic the possible types are $[1,1,1]$, $[2,1]$ and $[3]$; for a quartic they are $[1,1,1,1]$, $[2,1,1]$, $[2,2]$, $[3,1]$ and $[4]$. The number $I(X ; T)$, in bits per prime, is the exact capacity of the channel connecting "where $p$ lies in the arithmetic progressions" to "how the polynomial falls apart at $p$".

The classical picture tells us the two endpoints. If the polynomial's splitting field has abelian Galois group, reciprocity applies and $I = H(T)$: the channel is perfect, the residue class knows everything. If the splitting field is very non-abelian, reciprocity fails and the channel is worse — but by how much, and following what rule?

## The answer: a group's abelianization, and nothing else

Here is the law. Let $G$ be the Galois group of the splitting field — the group of symmetries permuting the roots of the polynomial. Let $G'$ be its **commutator subgroup**, generated by all elements $aba^{-1}b^{-1}$; the quotient $G^{\mathrm{ab}} = G/G'$ is the **abelianization**, the largest abelian group onto which $G$ can be projected. Then:

> **The Type-Channel Law.** For an appropriate modulus $m^*$ (the conductor of the abelian characters of $G$),
> $$I(p \bmod m^* \; ; \; T) \;=\; I(\text{coset of Frobenius in } G^{\mathrm{ab}} \; ; \; T) \;=\; H(T) - H(T \mid \text{coset}),$$
> and this quantity never exceeds $\log_2 [G : G']$.

The engine behind it is Chebotarev's density theorem of 1922, which says that as $p$ ranges over the primes, the Frobenius symbol — the canonical group element attached to $p$ — is equidistributed over $G$. The splitting type is precisely the cycle type of that group element acting on the roots. And class field theory says that the residue class of $p$ sees exactly one thing about Frobenius: which coset of $G'$ it lies in. Everything else about Frobenius is invisible to arithmetic progressions.

So the entire channel collapses into a purely finite, purely group-theoretic computation: **put the uniform distribution on $G$, read off each element's cycle type and its coset in the abelianization, and compute the mutual information of those two readouts.** No primes required. The number of bits a polynomial leaks per prime is a property of a finite group of size at most a few dozen.

The cap deserves emphasis because it is counterintuitive. The quantity $\log_2 [G:G']$ knows nothing about how many splitting types there are. Take $x^4 - x - 1$, whose Galois group is the full symmetric group $S_4$ on four roots. It has five distinct splitting types, distributed over $S_4$ with entropy
$$H(T) = \tfrac{3}{2} + \tfrac{3}{8}\log_2 3 \approx 2.09436 \text{ bits}.$$
More than two bits of structure in the factorization shapes. But the abelianization of $S_4$ is just $C_2$ — the sign of a permutation — so $[S_4 : A_4] = 2$ and the channel is capped at exactly **one bit**. And exactly one bit is what comes out: since the sign of a permutation is a function of its cycle type, the type determines the coset, and the channel runs at full capacity. A reader with unlimited primes and unlimited patience, watching the factorization shapes of $x^4 - x - 1$, extracts exactly one bit per prime about the residue class of $p$ — never the two-plus bits that the shapes themselves carry. Over half the structure is invisible to every arithmetic progression, forever.

## The six fields, in closed form

The law is not an asymptotic statement or an inequality; each channel is an exact real number. Writing $L_3 = \log_2 3 = 1.58496\ldots$, here are seven fields chosen to exercise every phenomenon (the two cubics share the group $S_3$), with their Galois groups, their type entropies, their channel capacities, their caps, and the "loss" (cap minus capacity):

| Defining polynomial | $G$ | $G^{\mathrm{ab}}$ | $H(T)$ | Channel $I$ | Cap $\log_2[G{:}G']$ | Loss |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $x^3+x+1$ | $S_3$ | $C_2$ | $\tfrac23 + \tfrac{L_3}{2} \approx 1.45915$ | $\mathbf{1}$ | $1$ | $0$ |
| $x^3-x+1$ | $S_3$ | $C_2$ | $\tfrac23 + \tfrac{L_3}{2} \approx 1.45915$ | $\mathbf{1}$ | $1$ | $0$ |
| $x^4-x-1$ | $S_4$ | $C_2$ | $\tfrac32 + \tfrac{3L_3}{8} \approx 2.09436$ | $\mathbf{1}$ | $1$ | $0$ |
| $x^4+8x+12$ | $A_4$ | $C_3$ | $\tfrac{3L_3}{4} \approx 1.18872$ | $L_3 - \tfrac23 \approx \mathbf{0.91830}$ | $L_3 \approx 1.58496$ | $\tfrac23$ |
| $x^4-2$ | $D_4$ | $C_2 \times C_2$ | $\tfrac52 - \tfrac{3L_3}{8} \approx 1.90564$ | $\tfrac94 - \tfrac{3L_3}{8} \approx \mathbf{1.65564}$ | $2$ | $\tfrac{3L_3}{8}-\tfrac14 \approx 0.34436$ |
| $x^4-2x^2+9$ | $V_4$ | $V_4$ | $2 - \tfrac{3L_3}{4} \approx 0.81128$ | $2 - \tfrac{3L_3}{4} \approx \mathbf{0.81128}$ | $2$ | $\tfrac{3L_3}{4} \approx 1.18872$ |
| $x^4+x^3+x^2+x+1$ | $C_4$ | $C_4$ | $\tfrac32$ | $\mathbf{1.5}$ | $2$ | $\tfrac12$ |

Each row is a theorem, not a measurement. And each row was checked against honest primes: counting splitting shapes for every prime up to tens of thousands and reading the residue dial for each field reproduces these numbers to within a few thousandths of a bit, which is exactly the finite-sample noise one expects.

Look at the third row again — the cap in action. Look at the fourth: the group $A_4$ has abelianization $C_3$, allowing $\log_2 3 \approx 1.585$ bits, but the channel delivers only $0.91830$. The reason is visible in the group: the splitting type $[3,1]$ — a cubic factor and a linear one — occurs in *both* non-trivial cosets of the abelianization. Seeing $[3,1]$ tells you that Frobenius is a 3-cycle but not which of the two "directions" it runs in, and the residual ambiguity is exactly $\tfrac23$ of a bit. The loss is not slack in an estimate; it is a conditional entropy, computed on the nose.

## Completeness: when is a channel perfect?

Which fields run their channel at full capacity? The answer is almost tautologically clean once stated correctly, and it converts an analytic question into a combinatorial one.

> **Completeness Criterion.** A field's splitting-type channel attains the cap $\log_2[G : G']$ **if and only if** the splitting type determines the abelianization coset: that is, if and only if any two group elements with the same cycle type lie in the same coset of $G'$.

The proof rests on a small but sharp fact about entropy: the conditional entropy $H(Y \mid X)$ vanishes exactly when $X$ determines $Y$ on the sample space. One direction is easy; the other requires noting that each cell of the joint table contributes a nonnegative amount to $H(Y\mid X)$, and that a cell contributes zero only when it is empty or when it fills its entire row — so a vanishing total forces every row of the joint table to be concentrated in a single column, which is exactly determinism. Combining this with the identity $\log_2[G:G'] - I = H(\text{coset} \mid T)$ gives the criterion.

This explains the whole table at a glance. In $S_3$ and $S_4$, the sign of a permutation is a function of its cycle type — a transposition is odd, a 3-cycle is even, and so on — so the type determines the coset and the channel is complete. In $A_4$, $D_4$, $V_4$ and $C_4$ some single type is spread over two or more cosets, and the shortfall is precisely the resulting conditional entropy. In $S_4$ the reverse failure also occurs: the even coset $A_4$ carries three different types, so while the channel is complete *relative to its cap*, it sits strictly below the full type entropy $H(T)$.

The criterion also settles the extreme cases at once. If $G$ is **abelian**, the commutator subgroup is trivial, the coset readout separates every single group element, and the channel delivers the whole of $H(T)$ for *every* conceivable type readout — this is reciprocity, recovered as a one-line corollary. If $G$ is **perfect**, meaning $G' = G$, then there is only one coset, the readout is constant, and the channel is exactly $0$. The smallest interesting perfect group is $A_5$, the symmetry group of the icosahedron and the obstruction to solving the quintic by radicals: a quintic field with Galois group $A_5$ leaks nothing at all. Its factorization shapes, however intricate, are statistically independent of every arithmetic progression. Insolvability by radicals and total informational silence turn out to be the same phenomenon wearing different clothes.

## The reversal: abelian is not the same as rich

Here is where intuition breaks, pleasantly.

One expects abelian fields to be the "transparent" ones and non-abelian fields to be the "opaque" ones. Compare the two four-element pictures in the table: $V_4$, the Klein four-group, which is abelian and equal to its own abelianization; and $D_4$, the dihedral group of order eight, the symmetry group of a square, which is genuinely non-abelian.

By a natural structural measure — the branching of the factorization behaviour — the abelian $V_4$ field looks the richer of the two. But the channels order them the other way:
$$I(V_4) = 2 - \tfrac{3}{4}\log_2 3 \approx 0.81128 \quad < \quad I(D_4) = \tfrac{9}{4} - \tfrac{3}{8}\log_2 3 \approx 1.65564 .$$
The non-abelian field leaks more than twice as much per prime as the abelian one. Both sit under the same two-bit ceiling, and both obey the same law — the reversal is not a violation of anything, it is two instances of a single identity.

The reason is worth internalising. $V_4$ is its own abelianization, so its cap is the full $2$ bits; but its three non-identity elements are all double transpositions, giving the same splitting type $[2,2]$, so three of its four cosets are indistinguishable to the type reader and most of the capacity is wasted. $D_4$ has a smaller relative abelianization but a readout that separates its cosets far better. **The richness of a channel is decided by how well the readout separates the cosets, not by whether the group is abelian.** Abelianness sets the ceiling; separation determines how much of the ceiling is used.

## Semiprimes: multiplying the secret

Cryptography rarely hands you a prime. It hands you $N = pq$, a product of two primes, and asks what you can learn. So ask the same question one level up.

A reader of such an $N$ — one who can see the factorization shapes of both prime factors but cannot tell which shape belongs to which factor — holds an *unordered pair* of splitting types. The residue class of $N$, by multiplicativity of the abelian characters, reads the *product* of the two Frobenius cosets. Modelling the two Frobenius elements as independent and uniform, the semiprime channel
$$I_2 = I\big(\text{coset}(xy)\;;\;\{T(x), T(y)\}\big)$$
also has exact closed forms. With $L_3 = \log_2 3$ and $L_5 = \log_2 5$:

| Field | $G$ | $I_2$ | numerically |
|---|---|---|---|
| $x^3+x+1$, $x^3-x+1$ | $S_3$ | $1$ | $1.00000$ |
| $x^4-x-1$ | $S_4$ | $1$ | $1.00000$ |
| $x^4+8x+12$ | $A_4$ | $L_3 - \tfrac{10}{9}$ | $0.47385$ |
| $x^4-2$ | $D_4$ | $\tfrac{39}{16} - \tfrac34 L_3 + \tfrac{5}{64} L_5$ | $1.43018$ |
| $x^4-2x^2+9$ | $V_4$ | $\tfrac{19}{8} - \tfrac{21}{16} L_3$ | $0.29474$ |
| $x^4+x^3+x^2+x+1$ | $C_4$ | $\tfrac54$ | $1.25000$ |

Three things stand out. First, the $C_2$ cap survives multiplication: the five-type $S_4$ field still cannot push more than one bit through a semiprime, because the cap is a theorem about the abelianization and not about the number of types. Second, the reversal survives too, and sharpens — $D_4$'s $1.43018$ bits sit *above* the one-bit quadratic ceiling, since its abelianization is two-dimensional. A non-abelian field can outperform every abelian quadratic one. Third, there is a wall:

> **The Which-Factor Wall.** Telling the reader which of the two factors carried which splitting shape changes the channel by exactly zero bits: replacing the unordered pair $\{T(x), T(y)\}$ by the ordered pair $(T(x), T(y))$ leaves $I_2$ unchanged, for every field in the table.

The ordering of the factors is pure decoration. All the arithmetic information lives in the multiset.

## Why any of this matters

Three reasons, in ascending order of ambition.

**A budget for side channels.** Many number-theoretic constructions expose factorization behaviour in some form — through timing, through failure modes, through auxiliary outputs. The law gives a hard, computable budget for how much an adversary can extract per observation: not an estimate, not an inequality with an unknown constant, but the exact real number $I = H(T) - H(T\mid\text{coset})$, computable from a finite group table of size $|G|$. And because the cap is $\log_2[G:G']$, choosing a field with small abelianization is a design decision with a quantified consequence. Choosing a perfect Galois group makes the leak exactly zero.

**A quantitative frontier for reciprocity.** The abelian/non-abelian divide has always been described qualitatively: abelian extensions are governed by congruence conditions, non-abelian ones are not. The law replaces that dichotomy with a number. Every field is *partly* abelian — its abelianization content — and the type channel measures that part exactly, in bits, for the whole spectrum from perfect groups (zero) to abelian groups (everything). Reciprocity is not on or off; it has a capacity.

**A completed program.** The identity unifies a family of earlier statements — the flatness of certain channels, criteria for when a channel is non-trivial, the binary laws for quadratic fields, tables of small groups, the abelian pair laws at the semiprime level — as projections of one sentence: *the complete symmetric residue channel of splitting statistics equals the mutual information between the splitting type and the abelianization coset.* Everything else is arithmetic on finite group tables.

## The last word: an ancient fact, newly weighed

There is something quietly satisfying about the endpoint. The impossibility of solving the general quintic by radicals is the statement that $A_5$ is perfect — that it cannot be broken down into abelian layers. Two centuries later, the same fact says something unexpected about information: a quintic field with that symmetry group is not merely hard to describe by congruences, it is *informationally silent*. No arithmetic progression, no modulus, no amount of data, will ever tell you anything about how its polynomial falls apart.

Between that silence and the perfect transparency of quadratic reciprocity lies a continuum, and the continuum has a coordinate. It is $\log_2$ of the index of the commutator subgroup, discounted by exactly how much the factorization shapes fail to see the cosets. Every field sits at a definite point on it. Now we can say where.
