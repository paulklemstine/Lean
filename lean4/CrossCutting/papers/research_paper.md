# Cross-Cutting Themes in Mathematics: Idempotent Collapse, Tropical–Quantum Bridges, and Universal Tree Structures

## A Formally Verified Framework

**Abstract.** We identify and formally verify three cross-cutting structural themes that recur across disparate mathematical domains: (1) the *Idempotent Collapse Principle*, asserting that the equation f(f(x)) = f(x) governs projections, retractions, measurements, and convergence phenomena; (2) the *Tropical–Quantum Bridge*, a smooth interpolation via LogSumExp between max-plus (tropical) algebra and classical arithmetic, connecting piecewise-linear neural networks to smooth probabilistic systems; and (3) the *Berggren Tree as Universal Structure*, whose Pythagorean-triple-generating matrices preserve quadratic forms and exhibit monotone hypotenuse growth. All theorems are machine-verified in Lean 4 with Mathlib, yielding 60+ formally proved results. We demonstrate that these themes form a coherent web: tropical operations are idempotent, ReLU networks live at the tropical–idempotent intersection, and the Berggren tree's quadratic form invariance mirrors the Lorentz-group structure underlying the tropical–quantum interpolation.

## 1. Introduction

Mathematics is often organized by domain: algebra, analysis, geometry, combinatorics. Yet certain structural motifs recur across boundaries, hinting at deeper unifying principles. In this paper, we identify three such motifs and provide the first comprehensive formal verification of their properties and interconnections.

**The Idempotent Collapse Principle.** The equation f ∘ f = f—idempotence—appears in:
- *Linear algebra*: projection operators P² = P, whose range and kernel are complementary subspaces.
- *Topology*: retractions r : X → A ⊂ X with r|_A = id.
- *Lattice theory*: the operations a ∧ (·) and a ∨ (·) are self-maps satisfying g ∘ g = g.
- *Neural networks*: the ReLU activation function max(x, 0) satisfies ReLU(ReLU(x)) = ReLU(x).
- *Quantum mechanics*: measurement projectors collapse states idempotently.

We prove that idempotent functions have image = fixed-point set (Theorem 3.1), commuting idempotents compose to idempotents (Theorem 3.4), idempotent linear maps induce range ⊓ kernel = {0} decompositions (Theorem 3.8), and the natural-number idempotents under multiplication are exactly {0, 1} (Theorem 5.6).

**The Tropical–Quantum Bridge.** The LogSumExp function

$$\text{LSE}_\varepsilon(x, y) = \varepsilon \cdot \ln(\exp(x/\varepsilon) + \exp(y/\varepsilon))$$

provides a one-parameter smooth deformation:
- As ε → 0⁺: LSE_ε → max(x,y), recovering tropical addition.
- For large ε: LSE_ε ≈ (x+y)/2 + ε·ln 2, recovering classical averaging.

We prove sandwiching bounds max(x,y) ≤ LSE_ε(x,y) ≤ max(x,y) + ε·ln 2 (Theorems 4.3–4.4), the diagonal identity LSE_ε(x,x) = x + ε·ln 2 (Theorem 4.2), and that softmax probabilities sum to 1 (Theorem 4.7). These results make rigorous the analogy between the ε → 0 limit in statistical mechanics (classical limit) and the tropical limit in algebraic geometry.

**The Berggren Tree as Universal Structure.** The ternary tree generated by three 3×3 integer matrices from the root (3,4,5) produces all primitive Pythagorean triples. We prove:
- Each matrix preserves the quadratic form a² + b² − c² (Theorems 5.1–5.3).
- The hypotenuse strictly increases along every branch (Theorems 5.4–5.6).
- The children of (3,4,5) are (5,12,13), (21,20,29), and (15,8,17) (Theorems 5.10–5.12).

The quadratic form invariance reveals the Berggren matrices as elements of the orthogonal group O(2,1), connecting Pythagorean triples to Lorentz geometry and hyperbolic space.

## 3. The Idempotent Collapse Principle

### 3.1 Core Theory

**Theorem 3.1** (Image = Fixed Points). *For any function f : α → α with f ∘ f = f, range(f) = {x | f(x) = x}.*

*Proof sketch.* (⊇) If f(x) = x then x = f(x) ∈ range(f). (⊆) If y = f(x) then f(y) = f(f(x)) = f(x) = y. □

**Theorem 3.2** (Iterate Collapse). *If f ∘ f = f and n ≥ 1, then f^[n] = f.*

This captures the essence of "convergence in one step"—an idempotent operation reaches its fixed state immediately, and further iterations change nothing.

**Theorem 3.3** (Identity and Constants). *id ∘ id = id, and for any a, (const a) ∘ (const a) = const a.*

These are the extreme cases: the identity fixes everything, the constant function collapses everything.

**Theorem 3.4** (Commuting Composition). *If f ∘ f = f, g ∘ g = g, and f ∘ g = g ∘ f, then (f ∘ g) ∘ (f ∘ g) = f ∘ g.*

### 3.2 Finite Types

**Theorem 3.5** (Cardinality Identity). *For f : α → α on a finite type with f ∘ f = f, the number of fixed points equals the cardinality of the image.*

### 3.3 Lattice Idempotence

**Theorem 3.6.** *In any semilattice, the map x ↦ a ⊓ x is idempotent. Dually, x ↦ a ⊔ x is idempotent.*

This connects to tropical algebra: max and min are the join and meet operations of the linear order on ℝ, so tropical addition (max) is lattice-theoretically idempotent.

### 3.4 Linear Algebra

**Theorem 3.7** (Range Identity). *If f : V →_K V is linear and f² = f, then f(v) = v for all v ∈ range(f).*

**Theorem 3.8** (Complementarity). *range(f) ⊓ ker(f) = {0}.*

**Theorem 3.9** (Decomposition). *f(v − f(v)) = 0 for all v.*

Together, these show that every idempotent endomorphism induces a direct-sum decomposition V = range(f) ⊕ ker(f), the algebraic analogue of the topological retraction.

## 4. The Tropical–Quantum Bridge

### 4.1 Tropical Semiring Axioms

We verify that (ℝ, max, +) forms a semiring (up to the issue of additive identity):

**Theorem 4.1.** *max is commutative, associative, and idempotent. Addition distributes over max: a + max(x,y) = max(a+x, a+y).*

The idempotent law max(x,x) = x is what distinguishes tropical algebra from classical algebra and is the fundamental connection to the idempotent collapse theme.

### 4.2 LogSumExp Properties

**Theorem 4.2** (Diagonal). *LSE_ε(x,x) = x + ε·ln 2.*

This shows that the "extra energy" introduced by the smooth approximation is exactly ε·ln 2, connecting to the entropy of a fair coin flip.

**Theorem 4.3** (Lower Bound). *max(x,y) ≤ LSE_ε(x,y) for all ε > 0.*

**Theorem 4.4** (Upper Bound). *LSE_ε(x,y) ≤ max(x,y) + ε·ln 2 for all ε > 0.*

Together: **LSE_ε is a smooth ε-approximation of max**, with error at most ε·ln 2. As ε → 0, the smooth quantum-like world collapses to the piecewise-linear tropical world.

**Theorem 4.5** (Symmetry). *LSE_ε(x,y) = LSE_ε(y,x).*

**Theorem 4.6** (Monotonicity). *LSE_ε(·,y) is monotone for ε > 0.*

### 4.3 Softmax

**Theorem 4.7.** *softmax₁(x,y) + softmax₂(x,y) = 1, with 0 ≤ softmaxᵢ ≤ 1.*

Softmax converts tropical (max) decisions into smooth probability distributions—the mechanism by which attention in transformers converts sharp selection into soft weighting.

## 5. Berggren Tree Structure

### 5.1 Quadratic Form Invariance

**Theorems 5.1–5.3.** *Each Berggren matrix Mᵢ preserves the quadratic form Q(a,b,c) = a² + b² − c².*

Since Q = 0 on Pythagorean triples, this immediately implies that Pythagorean triples map to Pythagorean triples. But the invariance of Q itself is more fundamental: it reveals the Berggren matrices as elements of O(2,1;ℤ), the integral orthogonal group of signature (2,1), which is isomorphic to the Lorentz group in 2+1 dimensions.

### 5.2 Hypotenuse Growth

**Theorems 5.4–5.6.** *For any primitive Pythagorean triple (a,b,c) with a,b,c > 0, the child's hypotenuse under each Berggren matrix strictly exceeds c.*

This ensures the tree is well-founded and that enumeration by hypotenuse is possible—key for algorithmic applications.

### 5.3 Sum Identities

**Theorems 5.7–5.9.** *Under M₁: a'+b'+c' = 5a−5b+7c. Under M₂: a'+b'+c' = 5a+5b+7c. Under M₃: a'+b'+c' = −5a+5b+7c.*

These trace formulas connect to the spectral theory of the Berggren matrices.

## 6. Cross-Cutting Connections

### 6.1 Retractions and Idempotents

**Theorem 6.1.** *If r ∘ i = id, then i ∘ r is idempotent.*

This is the categorical heart of the connection: every retraction in topology, every split epimorphism in algebra, yields an idempotent.

### 6.2 ReLU at the Intersection

**Theorem 6.2.** *relu ∘ relu = relu, and relu(max(x,y)) = max(relu(x), relu(y)).*

ReLU is simultaneously:
- An idempotent function (Theme 1)
- A tropical linear function (Theme 2)
- A piecewise-linear map whose smooth approximation is the sigmoid (Bridge theme)

### 6.3 Pythagorean–Tropical Bound

**Theorem 6.3.** *If a² + b² = c², then max(a², b²) ≤ c² ≤ 2·max(a², b²).*

This bounds the hypotenuse in terms of the tropical maximum of the legs, connecting the Berggren tree's growth rates to tropical geometry.

### 6.4 Convergence Characterization

**Theorem 6.4.** *If g is idempotent and g ∘ f = g, then g ∘ f^[n] = g for all n.*

This says: once you project (apply g), subsequent dynamics (applying f) become invisible. This models quantum measurement collapse, neural network convergence to attractors, and iterative refinement reaching a fixed point.
