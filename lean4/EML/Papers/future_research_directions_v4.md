## I. Pure Mathematics

### 1.1 RESOLVED: Depth Hierarchy (Levels 1-2)

**Result:** We have proved that EML-DEPTH(2) ⊋ EML-DEPTH(1): the function exp(exp(x)) cannot be represented as exp(ax + b) for any constants a, b. This is the first step in establishing a strict depth hierarchy.

**Next steps:**
- **P-M1:** Prove EML-DEPTH(d) ⊊ EML-DEPTH(d+1) for all d ≥ 1. *Difficulty: Hard. Impact: Very High.*
  - **Approach:** Show that the (d+1)-fold iterated exponential exp^{d+1}(x) is not in EML-DEPTH(d). This requires showing that no composition of d EML operations can produce exp^{d+1}(x), even with arbitrary constants.
  - **Possible technique:** Counting growth rates. The function exp^{d+1}(x) has order of growth strictly between hyper(d) and hyper(d+1) in the Knuth up-arrow notation, while EML-DEPTH(d) functions are bounded by hyper(d+1) growth.

### 1.2 RESOLVED: No Identity Element

**Result:** EML(x, e) ≠ x and EML(e, x) ≠ x for any fixed e ∈ ℝ and all x ∈ ℝ.

**Next steps:**
- **P-M2:** Classify all EML idempotents: for which x does EML(x, x) = x? *Difficulty: Medium.*
  - We know there are no positive fixed points. Are there negative fixed points? The equation exp(x) − ln(x) = x has no real solutions for x > 0, but the domain constraint ln(x) requires x > 0. So there are **no** EML diagonal fixed points on ℝ.

- **P-M3:** Study the EML semigroup action. The map T_c(x) = EML(x, c) for fixed c > 0 defines a family of transformations. What is the structure of the semigroup generated by {T_c : c > 0}? *Difficulty: Hard.*

### 1.3 Complex EML Theory

- **P-M4:** Classify all fixed points of the complex EML map z ↦ EML(z, z). *Difficulty: Medium.*
  - In the complex plane, exp(z) − ln(z) = z may have solutions (the branch cut of log introduces new possibilities).

- **P-M5:** Determine the Julia set of the complex EML map z ↦ EML(z, c) for various c ∈ ℂ. *Difficulty: Medium. Impact: High.*
  - The exponential component suggests Cantor bouquet-type Julia sets, while the logarithmic component introduces branch cut discontinuities.

- **P-M6:** Prove or disprove: every Liouvillian function (solution of an ODE solvable by quadratures) is expressible as a finite complex EML tree. *Difficulty: Very Hard. Impact: Very High.*
  - This would establish EML completeness for a well-defined class of functions.

### 1.4 EML Number Theory

- **P-M7:** Determine K_EML(π) — the minimum EML tree size that evaluates to π from constant 1. *Difficulty: Very Hard. Impact: High.*
  - Known bounds: K_EML(π) ≥ 5 (from transcendence), K_EML(π) ≤ 40 (conjectured, via arctan series).
  - **Related:** Is π in the EML closure of {1}? This asks whether some finite tree of EML operations, with only 1 at every leaf, evaluates exactly to π.

- **P-M8:** Compute K_EML(γ) for Euler's constant γ ≈ 0.5772. Since it's unknown whether γ is rational, this connects EML complexity to a famous open problem. *Difficulty: Open Problem Level.*

- **P-M9:** Study the density of the EML number tower {EML compositions from 1} in ℝ. Is it dense? Does it have Hausdorff dimension 1? *Difficulty: Hard.*

### 1.5 Dynamical Systems

- **P-M10:** Prove the one-minus-log map g(x) = 1 − ln(x) has a neutral fixed point at x = 1 with specific orbit behavior. We know g'(1) = −1 (neutral), but the orbit structure near 1 is not classified. *Difficulty: Medium.*
  - Is the fixed point a center (all nearby orbits periodic) or a focus (spiraling)?
  - For the second iterate g²(x) = 1 − ln(1 − ln(x)), determine the stability.

- **P-M11:** Characterize the 2D EML map Φ(x,y) = (EML(x,y), EML(y,x)). Does it have periodic orbits? Invariant curves? Strange attractors? *Difficulty: Hard. Impact: Medium.*

- **P-M12:** Study the random EML iteration: x_{n+1} = EML(x_n, c_n) where c_n are i.i.d. random variables. What is the stationary distribution (if it exists)? *Difficulty: Hard.*

## II. Complexity Theory

### 2.1 Circuit Complexity

- **P-C1:** Prove that computing x × y requires at least 9 EML nodes. *Difficulty: Very Hard. Impact: Very High.*
  - **Approach:** Show that any tree with ≤ 8 EML nodes and variables x, y at leaves cannot represent the bilinear function x·y over all positive reals.
  - **Possible technique:** Degree analysis. Each EML node applies exp (infinite degree) and subtracts log (fractional degree). The "effective algebraic degree" must reach exactly 2 for multiplication.

- **P-C2:** Determine the EML circuit complexity of the n-variable sum x₁ + x₂ + ... + xₙ. *Difficulty: Hard.*
  - Current: O(n) EML nodes via sequential addition.
  - Can we do O(n / log n) with recomputation? Lower bound?

- **P-C3:** Is there an n-variable function computable by EML circuits of size O(n) but not by EML circuits of depth O(log n)? This is the EML analogue of NC ≠ P. *Difficulty: Very Hard.*

### 2.2 Kolmogorov Complexity

- **P-C4:** Define and study EML Kolmogorov complexity: K_EML(x) = minimum tree size to compute x from constant 1. *Difficulty: Medium.*
  - Relate to classical Kolmogorov complexity.
  - Show K_EML is uncomputable (by standard diagonal argument).

- **P-C5:** For which algebraic numbers α is K_EML(α) known exactly? *Difficulty: Hard.*
  - K_EML(1) = 0 (trivially).
  - K_EML(e) = 1 (one EML node).
  - K_EML(e^e) = 2 (two EML nodes).
  - K_EML(0) = 3 (three EML nodes — verified).
  - K_EML(e − 1) = 2 (two nodes — verified).
  - What is K_EML(2)? K_EML(√2)? K_EML(φ) where φ = (1+√5)/2?

### 2.3 Communication Complexity

- **P-C6:** In a two-party EML computation where Alice holds x and Bob holds y, how many bits must they exchange to compute EML(x, y) to n bits of precision? *Difficulty: Medium.*

## III. Hardware

### 3.1 FPGA Prototype (Near-term, 6-12 months)

- **P-H1:** Implement a complete OISCC on an FPGA with CORDIC-based exp and ln. Target: Xilinx Artix-7. *Difficulty: Medium. Impact: Very High.*
  - **Milestone 1:** CORDIC exp unit, verified against IEEE 754.
  - **Milestone 2:** CORDIC ln unit with domain checking.
  - **Milestone 3:** Full EML pipeline (exp − ln in one clock cycle).
  - **Milestone 4:** Stack memory controller (BRAM).
  - **Milestone 5:** Instruction fetch and decode (1-bit opcode + immediate).
  - **Milestone 6:** UART I/O for debugging.
  - **Milestone 7:** Demo programs: arithmetic, Kalman filter, sigmoid.

- **P-H2:** Measure actual power consumption and compare with ARM Cortex-M0 running equivalent algorithms. *Difficulty: Medium. Impact: High.*

### 3.2 Analog Circuit (Medium-term, 1-2 years)

- **P-H3:** Design and fabricate an analog EML circuit using BJTs. *Difficulty: Hard. Impact: Very High.*
  - Target: < 100 transistors for the EML unit.
  - Challenge: achieving > 8 bits accuracy with process variation.

- **P-H4:** Analog stack: use sample-and-hold circuits as stack elements. How deep a stack is practically achievable before signal degradation becomes unacceptable? *Difficulty: Hard.*

### 3.3 ASIC (Long-term, 3-5 years)

- **P-H5:** Full ASIC design combining digital CORDIC EML with on-chip SRAM. *Difficulty: Very Hard. Impact: Very High.*
  - Target process: 65nm or 28nm CMOS.
  - Target power: < 100 µW.
  - Target speed: 1-10 MHz.

## IV. Software

### 4.1 Compiler

- **P-S1:** Build a compiler from arithmetic expressions to optimal PUSH/EML sequences. *Difficulty: Medium. Impact: High.*
  - Phase 1: Direct translation (correct but not optimal).
  - Phase 2: Common subexpression elimination.
  - Phase 3: Stack depth minimization.
  - Phase 4: Register allocation (if registers are added).

- **P-S2:** Prove compiler correctness in Lean 4. *Difficulty: Hard. Impact: Very High.*
  - Formalize the compilation rules and prove that the generated OISCC program computes the same function as the source expression.

### 4.2 Macro Library

- **P-S3:** Build a verified standard library of EML macros. *Difficulty: Medium.*
  - Each macro: named instruction sequence with formal correctness proof.
  - Include: EXP, LN, ADD, SUB, MUL, DIV, POW, SQRT, SIN, COS, SIGMOID, SOFTMAX.

### 4.3 Simulator

- **P-S4:** Build a cycle-accurate OISCC simulator with performance counters. *Difficulty: Low-Medium.*
  - Track: instruction count, stack depth, energy estimate.
  - Integrate with compiler for end-to-end benchmarking.

## V. Applications

### 5.1 Machine Learning (TinyML)

- **P-A1:** Implement MNIST inference on OISCC and measure accuracy vs. instruction count. *Difficulty: Medium. Impact: High.*
  - Target: > 95% accuracy with < 500K EML instructions per image.

- **P-A2:** Implement a simple RNN (recurrent neural network) for time-series prediction. *Difficulty: Medium.*
  - Natural fit: RNNs use tanh and sigmoid, both EML-computable.

- **P-A3:** Train a neural network *on* the OISCC (backpropagation via EML). *Difficulty: Hard.*
  - The sigmoid derivative σ'(x) = σ(x)(1−σ(x)) requires only one additional multiplication.

### 5.2 Medical Devices

- **P-A4:** Design an OISCC-based glucose monitor. *Difficulty: Hard. Impact: Very High.*
  - Sensor → ADC → Kalman filter → prediction → alert
  - Total: ~200 EML instructions per measurement cycle.
  - Power budget: < 50 µW (battery life > 1 year on coin cell).

- **P-A5:** Design an OISCC-based cochlear implant signal processor. *Difficulty: Very Hard.*
  - Real-time FFT via Goertzel algorithm: ~76 EML per frequency bin per sample.
  - 20 frequency bins × 16 kHz = ~24M EML/sec → 25 MHz clock.

### 5.3 Space and Defense

- **P-A6:** Design a radiation-hardened OISCC for space applications. *Difficulty: Hard.*
  - The simple circuit (300 transistors analog, ~7500 gates digital) is inherently radiation-tolerant.
  - Triple modular redundancy: 3 × 300 = 900 transistors — still simpler than a single Cortex-M0.

### 5.4 Environmental Sensing

- **P-A7:** Design an OISCC-based smart dust mote. *Difficulty: Hard. Impact: High.*
  - Solar-powered, wireless sensor node.
  - Compute: temperature compensation, Kalman filtering, threshold detection.
  - Target power: < 10 µW active, < 1 µW sleep.

### 5.5 Cryptography

- **P-A8:** Formal cryptanalysis of the EML hash function. *Difficulty: Very Hard.*
  - Is it pre-image resistant? Second-pre-image resistant? Collision-resistant?
  - Compare with SHA-3 in terms of security per gate.

- **P-A9:** Design an EML-based pseudorandom number generator. *Difficulty: Medium.*
  - The chaotic dynamics of iterated EML maps may provide statistical randomness.

### 5.6 Financial Computing

- **P-A10:** Implement Black-Scholes option pricing on OISCC. *Difficulty: Medium.*
  - Black-Scholes requires: exp, ln, sqrt, erf — all EML-computable.
  - Potential for ultra-low-latency pricing in hardware.

## VI. Theoretical Computer Science Connections

### 6.1 Analog Computation Theory

- **P-T1:** Place the OISCC precisely in the analog computation hierarchy (BSS model). *Difficulty: Medium.*
  - Without branching: the OISCC computes algebraic-exponential functions.
  - With branching (if-then-else on sign of top of stack): full BSS machine.
  - How does the EML instruction set compare to the BSS model's {+, −, ×, ÷, <} instruction set?

### 6.2 Algebraic Complexity Theory

- **P-T2:** Relate EML circuit complexity to algebraic circuit complexity. *Difficulty: Hard.*
  - Standard algebraic circuits use {+, ×}. EML circuits use {exp, ln, −}.
  - Is there a polynomial simulation between them? Or a super-polynomial gap?

### 6.3 Information Theory

- **P-T3:** What is the channel capacity of the EML channel (input → EML → output, with additive Gaussian noise)? *Difficulty: Hard.*
  - The exponential amplification in the first argument and logarithmic compression in the second create an unusual noise characteristic.

### 6.4 Category Theory

- **P-T4:** Describe the OISCC in categorical terms. *Difficulty: Medium.*
  - Programs are morphisms in a suitable category.
  - EML is a natural transformation between the exp and log functors?
  - The stack machine has a monoidal structure.

## Conclusion

The OISCC research program sits at a rare intersection: deep mathematical foundations (formally verified), practical engineering potential (sub-milliwatt computing), and broad application reach (AI, medicine, space, finance). The most impactful near-term activities are (1) the FPGA prototype, (2) proving the multiplication lower bound, and (3) MNIST inference benchmarking. These three results would constitute a compelling paper for a top venue and attract significant further interest.

The long-term prize — an ASIC that computes transcendental functions natively at microwatt power — would be a genuine breakthrough in computing architecture. The formal verification ensures the mathematical foundation is unshakeable. What remains is engineering, creativity, and persistence.
