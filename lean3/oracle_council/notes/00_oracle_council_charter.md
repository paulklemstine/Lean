## Central Thesis

> **Every Millennium Problem encodes a local-global transfer problem. The obstruction
> to solving each problem is a "north pole" — a singular point where local information
> fails to extend to global structure. The problem is solved when the north pole is
> shown to be removable, bypassable, or illusory.**

## Methodology

1. **RESEARCH**: Survey each Millennium Problem through the lens of local-global principles
2. **HYPOTHESIZE**: Identify the specific "north pole" (obstruction) for each problem
3. **EXPERIMENT**: Build computational demonstrations of the obstruction structure
4. **VALIDATE**: Check the analogy against known results (especially Perelman's proof)
5. **UPDATE**: Refine the framework based on findings
6. **ITERATE**: Repeat until the picture is coherent

## The Stereographic Projection Metaphor

Stereographic projection maps the sphere to the plane, preserving angles (conformal)
and circles (sends circles to circles/lines). It is a *local diffeomorphism* everywhere
except at the north pole, where the map "blows up" — sending the pole to infinity.

**Key insight**: The plane is the sphere with one point removed. The "missing point"
is the obstruction to compactness, to completeness, to global structure. Every hard
problem in mathematics asks: *can you fill in the missing point?*

- **Poincaré Conjecture**: Can you fill in the topological north pole? (Yes — Perelman)
- **Riemann Hypothesis**: Can the critical strip's north pole be located? (The zeros)
- **P vs NP**: Is computational complexity's north pole removable? (The separation)
- **Yang-Mills**: Can the quantum north pole be made rigorous? (The mass gap)
- **Navier-Stokes**: Can the analytic north pole be controlled? (Regularity)
- **Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer**: Can the arithmetic north pole be read from the analytic? (L-functions)
- **Hodge Conjecture**: Can the topological north pole be made algebraic? (Algebraic cycles)
