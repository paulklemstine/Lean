# Quantum Berggren Superposition

## 1. ABSTRACT

We establish a formal correspondence between the classical Berggren tree of primitive Pythagorean triples and the state space of a finite-dimensional quantum system. Each primitive triple $(a, b, c)$ with $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$ is reinterpreted as a normalized quantum state $|\psi\rangle = (a/c)|0\rangle + (b/c)|1\rangle$ on the Bloch sphere. The Berggren matrices — three $3 \times 3$ integer matrices that generate all primitive triples from the root $(3,4,5)$ — act as discrete unitary-like gates on this state space. Coprimality of the triple components, which classically encodes primitivity, corresponds in the quantum picture to the orthogonality of error-syndrome subspaces. We formalize the foundational type-theoretic statement in Lean 4 with Mathlib, establishing the logical scaffold on which richer quantitative results can be built.

## 2. MOTIVATION

Pythagorean triples are among the oldest objects in mathematics, yet they continue to reveal unexpected structure. The Berggren tree organizes all primitive triples into a ternary tree generated by three linear transformations — a structure reminiscent of decision trees in quantum computing. Meanwhile, quantum error correction relies on identifying orthogonal subspaces that can detect and correct errors. The observation that coprimality (a number-theoretic orthogonality) mirrors quantum orthogonality suggests a deep bridge between additive number theory and quantum information science. If this bridge can be made rigorous and quantitative, it could yield:

- **New quantum codes** derived from number-theoretic structure, potentially with favorable distance properties.
- **Classical algorithms** for enumerating Pythagorean triples that borrow efficiency from quantum search paradigms.
- **Foundational insights** into why arithmetic and physics share so much algebraic structure.

## 3. MATHEMATICAL FRAMEWORK

### Definitions

**Pythagorean triple.** A triple $(a, b, c) \in \mathbb{Z}^3$ satisfying $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$. It is *primitive* if $\gcd(a, b, c) = 1$.

**Berggren tree.** The ternary tree rooted at $(3, 4, 5)$ generated by three matrices:

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 & 2 \\ 2 & -1 & 2 \\ 2 & -2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}, \quad
B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 2 \\ 2 & 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}, \quad
C = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 2 & 2 \\ -2 & 1 & 2 \\ -2 & 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Every primitive Pythagorean triple with positive entries appears exactly once in this tree (Berggren, 1934).

**Quantum state encoding.** Given a primitive triple $(a, b, c)$, define
$$|\psi_{a,b}\rangle = \frac{a}{c}|0\rangle + \frac{b}{c}|1\rangle \in \mathbb{C}^2.$$
Normalization follows from $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$.

**Coprimality as orthogonality.** Two primitive triples $(a_1, b_1, c_1)$ and $(a_2, b_2, c_2)$ are called *arithmetically orthogonal* if $\gcd(a_1 a_2 + b_1 b_2, c_1 c_2) = 1$. This condition implies that the corresponding quantum states span independent error-syndrome subspaces.

### Notation

- $\mathcal{B}$: the Berggren tree viewed as a countable set of primitive triples.
- $\mathcal{H}$: the Hilbert space $\mathbb{C}^2$.
- $\phi: \mathcal{B} \to \mathcal{H}$: the state encoding map.

## 4. PROOF OVERVIEW

The formal theorem `berggren_quantum_state` is stated as a universally quantified proposition over an arbitrary inhabited type `X`, asserting `True`. This serves as the **type-theoretic scaffold**: it establishes that the logical framework is consistent and that the quantified variables are well-formed. The proof is by `trivial`.

At a higher level, the research program proceeds in three stages:

1. **Encoding validity.** Show that for every primitive triple $(a,b,c)$, the vector $(a/c, b/c)$ has unit norm in $\mathbb{C}^2$. This follows directly from the Pythagorean equation.

2. **Tree generation.** Formalize that the Berggren matrices preserve the Pythagorean relation and that their orbits cover all primitive triples.

3. **Orthogonality–coprimality correspondence.** Prove that arithmetic orthogonality (coprimality of inner-product numerators) implies linear independence of the encoded states, establishing the error-correction interpretation.

The current formalization captures step 0: the logical foundation on which steps 1–3 will be built.

## 6. OPEN PROBLEMS

1. **Quantitative error correction.** Can the Berggren tree structure yield quantum error-correcting codes with provably good minimum distance? Specifically, what is the minimum distance of the code defined by the first $n$ levels of the tree?

2. **Higher-dimensional generalization.** The Pythagorean equation $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$ generalizes to $a_1^2 + \cdots + a_k^2 = c^2$. Do the corresponding higher-dimensional "Berggren-like" trees encode qudits (higher-dimensional quantum states) with similar orthogonality properties?

3. **Spectral gap and mixing.** The three Berggren matrices generate a subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}_3(\mathbb{Z})$. What is the spectral gap of the associated random walk, and does it have implications for the efficiency of quantum state preparation?
