# Future Directions — Moment Fingerprint Classification

Derived from the five research cycles formalized in
`Catalog/Algebra/MomentFingerprintClassification.lean`,
`…Duality.lean`, `…Positivity.lean` and `…BetaLadder.lean`
(all proofs complete, 0 sorries, standard axioms only).

## What the cycles established

1. **Closed forms and the recursion.**  The antiderivative (integration-by-parts)
   recursion `M_{k+2} = ((k+3)π/8)·M_k` holds for *both* parities at once and,
   with `M₀ = M₁ = 1`, characterizes the whole Wigner-surmise fingerprint
   (`gueMoment_unique_of_recursion`).  Iterating it gives
   `M_{2m} = (2m+1)‼(π/8)^m` and `M_{2m+1} = (m+1)!(π/4)^m`.
2. **Full moment ordering.**  For every `k ≥ 2` the regimes are strictly ordered,
   and in the β-family this becomes the five-level ladder
   `1 < M_k(GSE) < M_k(GUE) < M_k(GOE) < k!` (`full_moment_ladder`).
3. **The speculated raw-moment coincidence is false**, but an *index-halving
   duality* is true: `M_{2m+1}/P_{m+1} = (π/4)^m`, `M_{2m}·m! = P_{2m+1}(π/16)^m`.
4. **One statistic classifies.**  The second moment separates the three coarse
   regimes with constant `sepConst = 3π/8 − 1` (sharp), and the five β-regimes
   with `ladderGap = 3π/128`; `n ≥ 127` resp. `n ≥ 738` spacings suffice under a
   `1/√n` fluctuation bound.
5. **Rigidity of the rigid bucket.**  For a mean-one finite spectrum the
   empirical second moment equals the empirical variance, so `M₂ = 1` forces
   every spacing to be exactly `1`, and `M₂ = 1 + ε` bounds the mean absolute
   deviation from the picket fence by `√ε`.
6. **Structural positivity.**  Every Hankel form of the surmise fingerprint is
   positive semidefinite; the order-3 Hankel determinant is `π²(9π−28)/256`, and
   its positivity is *equivalent* to `π > 28/9`.

## Conjecture 1 — β-Monotonicity of the Entire Moment Fingerprint

For the mean-one surmise family with density `∝ s^β exp(-b_β s²)`, the moment
`M_k(β) = Γ((k+β+1)/2)·Γ((β+1)/2)^{k-1} / Γ((β+2)/2)^k` is *strictly decreasing in
the real parameter `β > 0`* for every fixed `k ≥ 2`, so the spectral ladder is a
continuum, not just the three points `β = 1, 2, 4`.

The key insight is that the whole ladder is a statement about strict convexity of
`log Γ`: the second moment is `exp(Δ²log Γ)` evaluated on a half-integer lattice,
so β-monotonicity is exactly the monotone decrease of the trigamma function along
half-steps.  Why now?  The discrete instances (`full_moment_ladder`) are already
proved here for `β ∈ {1,2,4}` by an elementary coefficient-comparison principle
(`lt_of_coeff_lt`); Mathlib already contains `Real.convexOn_log_Gamma` and the
Bohr–Mollerup machinery, so the continuum statement is now within reach.

## Conjecture 2 — Carleman Determinacy of the Wigner Fingerprint

The Wigner surmise is *moment-determinate*: any probability measure on `[0,∞)`
whose moments are `(2m+1)‼(π/8)^m` and `(m+1)!(π/4)^m` equals the surmise.  More
strongly, `∑_k M_k^{-1/2k} = ∞` (Carleman's condition) holds, with the explicit
rate `M_k^{1/2k} ≍ √(πk/16)`.

The key insight is that the decay `M_k/k! ≤ 2·2^{-⌊k/2⌋}` proved here
(`gueMoment_div_poissonMoment_le`) already places the fingerprint inside the
sub-factorial class, where Carleman's series diverges; determinacy then upgrades
the "moment fingerprint" from an invariant to a complete classifier of the law
itself.  Why now?  The generating-series statement
(`gue_egf_summable`, radius `≥ √2`) is proved, and analytic continuation of the
moment-generating function is the standard bridge from summability to
determinacy.

## Conjecture 3 — Optimal Sample Complexity of the Second-Moment Classifier

For an `n`-level spectrum whose spacings are i.i.d. from one of the five regimes,
the second-moment classifier errs with probability at most
`exp(-c n (ladderGap)²)` for an absolute constant `c > 0`, and no classifier based
on any *single* moment `k ≥ 3` achieves a larger exponent: the second moment is
the optimal single-moment statistic.

The key insight is that the ladder gaps shrink like `π/2^k` for higher moments
while the fluctuation scale grows like `√(M_{2k} − M_k²)`, so the
signal-to-noise ratio is maximized at `k = 2` — the gap-to-variance ratio is a
computable function of the closed forms already proved.  Why now?  The exact
moments of all orders and all three β are in hand, so the signal-to-noise ratio is
an explicit elementary expression in `π`, and Mathlib's Chernoff/Hoeffding
inequalities can supply the tail bound.

## Conjecture 4 — Hankel Determinant Ladder

Let `D_r(β)` be the `r`-th Hankel determinant of the mean-one surmise fingerprint.
Then for every `r ≥ 2` the ladder persists:
`0 = D_r(rigid) < D_r(GSE) < D_r(GUE) < D_r(GOE) < D_r(Poisson) = ∏_{j<r} (j!)²`,
and `D_r(β)` is a polynomial in `π` with integer coefficients whose leading term
is `c_r π^{r(r-1)/2}`.

The key insight is that `D_3(GUE) = π²(9π − 28)/256` is *not* an accident: the
recursion `M_{k+2} = ((k+3)π/8)M_k` makes the Hankel matrix a `π`-deformation of
the Hilbert-type matrix of the exponential law, so its determinant is a
`π`-polynomial with a factorized leading coefficient.  Why now?  The general PSD
statement `gueMoment_hankel_psd` is proved, which gives `D_r ≥ 0` for free; the
remaining content is strict positivity plus the explicit polynomial, both finite
algebraic computations for each `r`.

## Conjecture 5 — Rigidity from the Second Moment Alone

*Status: the finite-spectrum (empirical) case is now **proved** in
`MomentFingerprintPositivity.lean`.*  For a mean-one configuration of `n`
spacings, `sum_sq_dev_eq` identifies the empirical second moment with the
empirical variance, `rigid_of_empSecondMoment_eq_one` shows that `M₂ = 1` forces
*every* spacing to equal `1` (exact picket fence), and
`meanAbsDev_le_sqrt_variance` gives the quantitative form: `M₂ = 1 + ε` forces
mean absolute deviation at most `√ε`.

What remains open is the continuum statement: if a mean-one probability law on
`[0,∞)` has second moment `1 + ε`, then its Wasserstein-1 distance to `δ₁` is at
most `√ε`, with equality only in the two-point family; and consequently a
sequence of spectra with empirical second moment `1 + o(1)` converges weakly to
the picket fence.

The key insight is that the classifier's rigid bucket is not merely a labelling
convention: `M₂ − 1` *is* the variance, so the rigid regime is characterized by a
vanishing statistic rather than by proximity to a model value.  Why now?  The
empirical case is proved here by Cauchy–Schwarz on a `Finset`, and the passage to
general laws only needs the `L¹ ≤ L²` bound together with Mathlib's Wasserstein
(`ProbabilityTheory`) API, so the discrete proof transfers essentially verbatim.
