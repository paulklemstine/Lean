### Question 1: Can we model the whole graph of emitters and absorption events in time?

**YES — formally proved.** File: `PhotonEventGraph.lean`

The complete graph of photon emission/absorption events forms a **directed acyclic graph (DAG)** in Minkowski spacetime. Key proved theorems:
- **`time_monotone`**: Causal paths are strictly time-increasing (no backwards-in-time signaling)
- **`no_causal_loop`**: The photon event graph has no cycles — it is provably a DAG
- **`on_shell`**: Every photon satisfies px² + py² = E² (the massless dispersion relation = Pythagorean condition)
- **`equal_energy`**: Entangled photon pairs have equal energy (from momentum conservation)

### Question 2: Can we read entangled photon networks from the number line?

**YES, with important caveats discovered through formal disproof.** Files: `NumberLineEncoding.lean`, `EntanglementNetwork.lean`

**Positive results (proved ✓):**
- Every photon state bijects with a Gaussian integer, which bijects with ℕ (`photon_encoding_bijective`)
- Any finite photon network encodes injectively as a single natural number (`encodeGraph_injective`)
- Infinite histories encode as reals in [0,1], injectively for non-degenerate histories (`encodeHistory_injective_nonDegenerate`)
- Entanglement = Gaussian conjugation: given a photon's address n ∈ ℕ, its partner's address is computable (`entangledPartnerCode`)
- Entanglement matchings require even photon count (`entanglement_requires_even`)
- Bell/CHSH inequality formalized for local hidden variable models (`bell_chsh_bound`)

**Negative results (disproved ✗ — the most interesting findings!):**
- Binary encoding is **NOT** injective on all infinite histories (0.111...₂ = 1.000...₂ ambiguity)
- ℕ-encoded photon states are **NOT** dense in ℝ (ℕ is discrete — photon codes live at integer addresses with gaps between them)
- Bit decoding fails at boundary cases (all-true history maps to 1.0, breaking floor-based extraction)

### The Deepest Insight

**Entanglement is conjugation on the number line.** Photon states are Gaussian integers z = px + py·i. Entangled partners are conjugates z̄ = px − py·i. Under the encoding ℤ[i] ↔ ℕ, entanglement becomes a computable involution on the natural numbers — the entire entanglement network is readable as arithmetic on ℕ.
