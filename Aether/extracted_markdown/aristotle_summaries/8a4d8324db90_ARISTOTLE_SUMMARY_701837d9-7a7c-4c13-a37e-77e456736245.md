# Summary of changes
I've written **"Foundations of Verified Mathematics: A First Course for Undergraduates, Seventh Edition, 2074"** — a complete textbook from the future, saved as `TextbookFromTheFuture.md` in your project root.

The textbook imagines mathematics education fifty years from now, after proof assistants have become universal in mathematical practice. It includes:

- **A Preface** describing the unification of proof assistants into a Universal Proof Framework and the rise of AI proof agents.
- **Chapter 1** on Propositions, Types, and Universes — grounding students in dependent type theory with historical context about the "Dependency Crisis" of the 2020s.
- **Chapter 5** on writing verified theorems, including a worked example (irrationality of √2) and guidance on when AI gets stuck.
- **Chapter 7** on the five generations of proof automation, from simple rewriting to autonomous neural proof agents.
- **Chapter 14** on AI-Human collaborative proving, featuring a fictional case study of the resolution of the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer Conjecture in 2051.
- **Chapter 16** on the (fictional) verified proof of P ≠ NP in 2067.
- **Chapter 17** on open problems that remain unsolved even in 2074, including the Riemann Hypothesis and Collatz Conjecture.
- **Appendices** covering legacy proof assistants (Lean 4, Coq), the history of their unification, and ethical considerations in automated mathematical discovery.

The textbook is written in a warm, rigorous, pedagogical style — complete with exercises, historical notes, fictional quotes, and a dedication to Kevin Buzzard. It explores themes of human-AI collaboration, the nature of mathematical understanding, equity of access, and what it means to "know" a theorem that only a machine can prove.
