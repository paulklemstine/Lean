# Summary of changes
I've written a comprehensive book on quantum theory, saved in the `QuantumTheoryBook/` directory. The book is titled **"Quantum Theory: From Foundations to Frontiers"** and is contained in `QuantumTheory.md` (~2,500 lines, ~45,000 words).

**Structure — 7 Parts, 27 Chapters:**

- **Part I: The Classical Crisis** (Ch. 1–2) — The triumphs of classical physics and the anomalies (black-body radiation, photoelectric effect, atomic spectra) that demanded a revolution.

- **Part II: The Birth of Quantum Mechanics** (Ch. 3–5) — Planck's quantization, Einstein's photon, Bohr's atom, de Broglie's matter waves, and wave–particle duality.

- **Part III: The Mathematical Framework** (Ch. 6–10) — The Schrödinger equation, Born interpretation, Hilbert spaces, Dirac notation, operators and observables, the Heisenberg uncertainty principle, and symmetry/conservation laws.

- **Part IV: Quantum Mechanics in Action** (Ch. 11–15) — The hydrogen atom, spin and angular momentum, identical particles and the Pauli exclusion principle, quantum tunneling, and perturbation/variational methods.

- **Part V: Entanglement and Information** (Ch. 16–19) — The EPR paradox, Bell's theorem and experimental tests of nonlocality, quantum computing (Shor's and Grover's algorithms, error correction), and quantum cryptography (BB84, no-cloning theorem).

- **Part VI: Quantum Field Theory** (Ch. 20–23) — Second quantization and Fock space, QED and Feynman diagrams, the Standard Model (particles, gauge symmetry, Higgs mechanism), and renormalization/the renormalization group.

- **Part VII: Interpretations and Foundations** (Ch. 24–27) — The measurement problem, Schrödinger's cat, decoherence, Copenhagen interpretation, hidden variables (Bohmian mechanics), many-worlds, consistent histories, QBism, quantum gravity (string theory, loop quantum gravity), and open questions.

**Appendices:** Mathematical prerequisites, key experiments table, historical timeline, comprehensive glossary, and further reading recommendations.
