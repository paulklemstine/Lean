### 1. Berggren Semigroup Freeness (Full Proof)

- **Theorem Statement**: For any two distinct Berggren words w₁ ≠ w₂ ∈ {M₁, M₂, M₃}*, we have evalBerggrenWord(w₁) ≠ evalBerggrenWord(w₂). Equivalently, the only word evaluating to the identity matrix is the empty word.
- **Proof Strategy**:
  - (A) **Action faithfulness**: Show each primitive Pythagorean triple (a,b,c) with c > 5 has a unique Berggren parent (exactly one Mᵢ⁻¹·(a,b,c) is a positive triple with smaller hypotenuse). This reduces freeness to the tree structure theorem.
  - (B) **Trace rigidity**: Different words produce matrices with different traces. Use the trace recurrence from the Cayley-Hamilton relation.
  - (C) **Ping-pong lemma**: Define appropriate half-spaces for each generator and show the generators satisfy the ping-pong criterion for free semigroup actions.
- **Why This Is Revolutionary**: Establishes the Berggren semigroup as a certified free semigroup, immediately yielding collision-resistant hash functions.
- **Catalog Leverage**: Build on `depth2_action_faithful`, `berggren_singleton_injective`, `evalBerggrenWord_det`.
- **Research Mode**: prove
- **Estimated Depth**: 4

### 2. Displacement–Hypotenuse Duality (Quantitative Bound)

- **Theorem Statement**: For any Berggren word w of depth d ≥ 1, |log(c/5) - Δ(w)| ≤ C for a universal constant C (we conjecture C = log(5) works).
- **Proof Strategy**:
  - (A) **Induction on word length**: Show |log(c'/c) - δ(g)| ≤ K for each generator step, where δ(g) is the displacement increment. Sum over the word.
  - (B) **Spectral decomposition**: Write A = evalBerggrenWord(w) and decompose the root triple in the eigenbasis. The dominant eigenvalue term gives log(c) ≈ Δ(A) and the subdominant terms give the error.
  - (C) **Busemann function**: Use the projective action of SO(2,1) on the boundary of H² to relate log(c) to the Busemann function, which differs from displacement by a bounded amount.
- **Why This Is Revolutionary**: Makes precise the "gravitational redshift" interpretation — the hypotenuse is literally the exponential of a Lorentzian distance.
- **Catalog Leverage**: Build on `M₂_hypotenuse_formula`, `M₂_eigenvalue_product`, `evalBerggrenWord_preserves_lightcone`.
- **Research Mode**: prove
- **Estimated Depth**: 5

## Under-explored Territory

### Berggren Tree as a Cayley Graph
The Berggren tree can be viewed as a (directed) Cayley graph of the free semigroup on 3 generators. The connection to geometric group theory (word metrics, growth rates, boundary at infinity) is largely unexplored in the formalized setting.

### Higher-Dimensional Analogues
The Minkowski form Q(a,b,c) = a²+b²-c² generalizes to Q(a₁,...,aₙ,c) = Σaᵢ²-c² for Pythagorean (n+1)-tuples. The corresponding Berggren-like generators would lie in O(n,1;ℤ). This is completely uncharted territory.

### Inverse Problem Complexity
The computational complexity of the Berggren descent (finding the unique word w such that evalBerggrenWord(w)·(3,4,5) = (a,b,c)) is not well understood. It appears to be O(log c) but a tight formal bound is lacking.

### Tropical Berggren Tree
Replacing multiplication by addition and addition by min gives a "tropical" Berggren tree. The tropical displacement would be a piecewise-linear function, potentially connecting to tropical geometry.

## Cross-Domain Bridges

### Lorentzian Geometry ↔ Modular Forms
The group O(2,1;ℤ) is intimately related to GL(2,ℤ) via the exceptional isomorphism PSL(2,ℤ) ≅ SO⁺(2,1;ℤ). This connects the Berggren tree to modular forms, Hecke operators, and the theory of automorphic forms. The Berggren generators should correspond to specific elements of PSL(2,ℤ) acting on the upper half-plane.

### Pythagorean Triples ↔ Gaussian Integers
A Pythagorean triple a²+b²=c² corresponds to the norm equation |a+bi|² = c² in ℤ[i]. The Berggren generators should have natural interpretations as transformations of Gaussian integers, connecting to algebraic number theory.

### Spectral Radius ↔ Lyapunov Exponents
The spectral radius 3+2√2 of M₂ is the Lyapunov exponent of the M₂ branch. For random walks on the Berggren tree, the average Lyapunov exponent (1/3)·log(3+2√2) controls the typical hypotenuse growth. This connects to ergodic theory and random matrix products.

## Open Problems Encountered

1. **Exact constant in displacement–hypotenuse duality**: What is the optimal constant C such that |log(c/5) - Δ(w)| ≤ C for all Berggren words w? Numerical evidence suggests C = log(5) ≈ 1.61.

2. **Growth rate of mixed words**: For a random Berggren word of depth d with k copies of M₂, what is the expected hypotenuse? The answer should be approximately 5 · (3+2√2)^k · poly(d-k), but making this precise requires understanding the interaction between parabolic and hyperbolic steps.

3. **Primitive root characterization**: Is (3,4,5) the unique triple that could serve as the root of a Berggren-type tree, or could other triples (e.g., (5,12,13)) also be roots of trees generating all primitive triples?

4. **Connection to Markov triples**: The Markov equation x²+y²+z² = 3xyz also defines a tree of integer solutions (the Markov tree). Is there a structural connection between the Berggren and Markov trees, perhaps through their respective quadratic forms?
