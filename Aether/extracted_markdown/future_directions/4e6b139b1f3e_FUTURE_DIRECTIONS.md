# Future Directions: GL(1) Langlands Correspondence Formalization

## Synthesis

The formal verification of the algebraic skeleton of the GL(1) Langlands correspondence opens five interconnected research directions. The **topological enrichment** (Direction 1) and **p-adic completion** (Direction 2) provide the infrastructure needed for the **full Artin reciprocity isomorphism** (Direction 3), which is the grand challenge. In parallel, the **GL(2) extension** (Direction 4) tests whether our algebraic framework genuinely scales to higher rank, and the **conductor formalization** (Direction 5) bridges the algebraic theory to concrete arithmetic computations. Together, these directions form a path from the verified algebraic skeleton to a complete formal proof of class field theory and beyond.

All five directions build on the core theorems established here: `principal_family_is_restricted`, `character_descends_to_idele_class_group`, `principal_trivial_character_equiv_quotient_character`, `character_ext_of_generators`, and `quotient_map_of_subgroup_map` in `LanglandsGL1/RestrictedProduct.lean` and `LanglandsGL1/CharacterDescent.lean`.

## Direction 1: Topological Restricted Products and Continuous Characters

**Conjecture:** The restricted product of locally compact abelian groups with compact open subgroups, endowed with the restricted product topology, is itself locally compact abelian, and every continuous homomorphism to ℂˣ trivial on the principal subgroup descends to a continuous homomorphism on the quotient (equipped with the quotient topology).

**Test:** Formalize the restricted product topology in Lean 4 using Mathlib's `TopologicalSpace` and `TopologicalGroup` infrastructure. Verify that the topology on the restricted product (generated by basic opens of the form "finitely many coordinates in open sets, remaining coordinates in the integral subgroup") satisfies the `TopologicalGroup` axioms. Then prove that `QuotientGroup.lift` preserves continuity when the kernel is closed.

A disproof would consist of an explicit counterexample: a restricted product where the lifted map fails to be continuous in the quotient topology.

**Impact:** This would bridge the gap between our algebraic formalization and the analytic theory. Hecke characters are by definition *continuous* characters of the idèle class group; without topology, this constraint is invisible.

**Catalog References:** `LanglandsGL1/RestrictedProduct.lean` (restrictedSubgroup), `LanglandsGL1/CharacterDescent.lean` (character_descends_to_idele_class_group)

**Proof Strategy:** Use Mathlib's `TopologicalSpace.IsTopologicalBasis` to define the restricted product topology via basic opens. Prove local compactness via Tychonoff's theorem restricted to finitely many non-compact factors. For continuity of descent, use `QuotientMap.continuous_iff`.

**Domain Bridges:** Topology, functional analysis, harmonic analysis (Pontryagin duality)

**Lineage:** Extends `RestrictedProductData` with topological data; extends `character_descends_to_idele_class_group` with continuity.

**Ambition:** ★★★☆☆ — Infrastructure-level; requires significant Mathlib topology work but is well-understood mathematically.

## Direction 2: Formal p-adic Completions and Valuation Theory

**Conjecture:** The p-adic numbers ℚ_p, formalized as the completion of ℚ with respect to the p-adic absolute value, satisfy all axioms of `ValuationIdeleData` when instantiated with the set of rational primes as places, and the diagonal embedding ℚˣ → ∏_p ℚ_pˣ lands in the restricted product with ℤ_pˣ as integral subgroups — recovering the classical idèle group of ℚ.

**Test:** Construct `ValuationIdeleData ℚ` using Mathlib's `Padic` and `PadicInt` types. Verify that `Padic.valuation` satisfies the multiplicativity and finite support axioms. Instantiate `principal_family_is_restricted` to obtain the classical theorem that every nonzero rational has non-trivial p-adic valuation at only finitely many primes.

A disproof scenario: if Mathlib's `Padic.valuation` has incompatible normalization or type (e.g., valued in ℤ vs. ℕ vs. WithTop ℤ), the instantiation would require non-trivial adapter work.

**Impact:** This connects our abstract algebraic framework to concrete number-theoretic objects, enabling formal proofs about actual number fields rather than abstract axioms.

**Catalog References:** `LanglandsGL1/RestrictedProduct.lean` (ValuationIdeleData, principal_family_is_restricted)

**Proof Strategy:** Use `multiplicity.Finsupp.finite` for the finite support condition. Adapt `Padic.valuation` to our ℤ-valued convention. Build a `ValuationIdeleData ℚ` instance.

**Domain Bridges:** Algebraic number theory, p-adic analysis, valuation theory

**Lineage:** Direct instantiation of `ValuationIdeleData` with concrete arithmetic.

**Ambition:** ★★★☆☆ — Requires navigating Mathlib's p-adic infrastructure but mathematically straightforward.

## Direction 3 (Grand Challenge): Full Artin Reciprocity Isomorphism for ℚ

**Conjecture:** There exists a formally verified isomorphism of topological groups between the idèle class group ℚˣ\𝔸_ℚˣ/ℝ₊ˣ and the profinite completion ℤ̂ˣ ≅ ∏_p ℤ_pˣ, recovering the Artin reciprocity isomorphism for the rationals.

**Test:** Formalize the profinite group ℤ̂ˣ as a projective limit of (ℤ/nℤ)ˣ. Construct an explicit map from the idèle class group (formalized using Direction 2's infrastructure) to ℤ̂ˣ. Prove it is a bijective continuous homomorphism with continuous inverse.

A disproof: if the map fails to be injective (i.e., there exist non-principal idèles in its kernel), the formalization would expose an error in the construction.

**Impact:** This would be the first machine-verified proof of a non-trivial class field theory theorem. It would validate the algebraic skeleton built here and demonstrate that the framework scales to genuine arithmetic.

**Catalog References:** `LanglandsGL1/CharacterDescent.lean` (proto_artin_reciprocity_descends, principal_trivial_character_equiv_quotient_character)

**Proof Strategy:** 
1. Formalize the connected component of the identity in 𝔸_ℚˣ as ℝ₊ˣ.
2. Show the quotient by ℚˣ · ℝ₊ˣ is compact (requires topology).
3. Use the Chinese Remainder Theorem to identify the quotient with ℤ̂ˣ.
4. Verify the Artin map sends the local uniformizer π_p to the Frobenius at p.

**Domain Bridges:** Algebraic number theory, profinite group theory, global class field theory

**Lineage:** Builds on Directions 1 and 2; uses `proto_artin_reciprocity_descends` as the algebraic core.

**Ambition:** ★★★★★ — Paradigm-shifting. Would be the first formally verified class field theory theorem.

## Direction 4 (Grand Challenge): GL(2) Langlands via Automorphic Forms on Adèles

**Conjecture:** The restricted product framework formalized here extends to GL(2): the group GL_2(𝔸_ℚ) can be formalized as a restricted product of GL_2(ℚ_p) with GL_2(ℤ_p) as integral subgroups, and automorphic forms on GL_2(𝔸_ℚ) (functions satisfying appropriate invariance, growth, and eigenvalue conditions) can be formalized using the `RestrictedProductData` structure with non-abelian local groups.

**Test:** Generalize `RestrictedProductData` to allow non-commutative groups (replacing `CommGroup` with `Group`). Define a type `AutomorphicForm` on the restricted product as a function satisfying left-invariance by GL_2(ℚ) and right-invariance by a compact open subgroup. Verify that the space of automorphic forms is a module over the Hecke algebra.

A disproof: if the non-commutativity of GL_2 causes the restricted product subgroup construction to fail (e.g., if the proof of `isRestrictedFamily_mul` requires commutativity in an essential way), we would need to restructure the framework.

**Impact:** This would demonstrate that our GL(1) formalization is not a dead end but genuinely serves as a template for the full Langlands program.

**Catalog References:** `LanglandsGL1/RestrictedProduct.lean` (RestrictedProductData, restrictedSubgroup, isRestrictedFamily_mul)

**Proof Strategy:** The proof of `isRestrictedFamily_mul` uses `Subgroup.mul_mem_cancel_left/right`, which works for non-commutative groups. So the restricted product construction should generalize directly. The new challenge is defining automorphic forms as sections satisfying representation-theoretic conditions.

**Domain Bridges:** Representation theory, harmonic analysis, modular forms

**Lineage:** Generalizes `RestrictedProductData`; motivates by the connection to elliptic curves via modularity.

**Ambition:** ★★★★★ — Would open GL(2) formalization in Lean 4.

## Direction 5: Conductor Theory and Computational Class Field Theory

**Conjecture:** For a Hecke character χ of the idèle class group of ℚ with values in ℂˣ, the *conductor* (the smallest modulus m such that χ factors through (ℤ/mℤ)ˣ) can be characterized formally as the product of local conductors, and this factorization can be computed algorithmically from the local character data at each place.

**Test:** Formalize the conductor of a character in the finite-place model. Define the local conductor at place p as the smallest k such that χ is trivial on 1 + p^k ℤ_p (in the p-adic model) or equivalently as the denominator order of the exponent a_p in our rational model. Prove the conductor equals ∏_p p^{f_p} where f_p is the local conductor.

Computational test: For S = {2, 3, 5, 7}, enumerate all characters of order dividing 12 that are principal-trivial, compute their conductors, and verify the factorization matches the direct calculation.

**Impact:** Connects the algebraic framework to concrete computations in number theory. Conductors determine the level of associated modular forms, so this bridges GL(1) to GL(2).

**Catalog References:** `LanglandsGL1/CharacterDescent.lean` (character_ext_of_generators, principal_trivial_character_equiv_quotient_character)

**Proof Strategy:** Use the finite-place model where conductors are products of prime powers. The local conductor at p is determined by the order of the exponent a_p in ℚ/ℤ. Prove ∏ p^{ord(a_p)} gives the global conductor.

**Domain Bridges:** Computational number theory, modular forms, algebraic number theory

**Lineage:** Builds on the character framework; connects to Dirichlet characters and L-functions.

**Ambition:** ★★★☆☆ — Computationally testable, mathematically concrete, directly useful.
