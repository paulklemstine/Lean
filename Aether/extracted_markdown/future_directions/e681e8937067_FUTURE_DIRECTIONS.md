# Future Directions: Tropical Satake Theory

## Hypothesis 1: Tropical Satake Extends to a Full Semiring Isomorphism

**Conjecture.** The tropical Satake map extends to a semiring isomorphism
$$\mathcal{S} : (\Lambda^+_n \to_0 \mathbb{Z}, \oplus, \odot) \xrightarrow{\sim} (\text{OrbitMin}_n, \oplus, \odot)$$
where the left side is the free min-plus algebra on dominant weights with tropical convolution, and the right side is the subsemiring of W-invariant tropical polynomials generated by orbit-min basis elements.

Specifically:
- $\mathcal{S}(f \oplus g) = \mathcal{S}(f) \oplus \mathcal{S}(g)$ (compatibility with min),
- $\mathcal{S}(f \odot g) = \mathcal{S}(f) \odot \mathcal{S}(g)$ (compatibility with tropical convolution/addition).

**Test.** Formalize the tropical Hecke convolution product and verify the semiring homomorphism properties computationally for GL_3, GL_4, GL_5. Then prove the identities abstractly using the distributivity lemma `tropical_plus_distributes_over_min`.

**Impact.** Would complete the tropical Satake isomorphism as a full algebraic equivalence, not just a bijection on generators. This is the strongest possible form of the theorem and would be directly analogous to the classical Satake isomorphism.

## Hypothesis 2: Tropical Satake Extends to Other Root Systems

**Conjecture.** For Weyl groups of types B_n, C_n, and D_n, the orbit-min construction yields an injective tropical Satake map from dominant weights to W-invariant tropical polynomials. Specifically:

- For type B_n / C_n: the Weyl group is the hyperoctahedral group (signed permutations), acting on ℤ^n by permutations and sign changes. The tropical Schur polynomial is
$$s_\mu^{\text{trop}}(z) = \min_{w \in W(B_n)} \sum_i \mu(w(i)) \cdot z(i)$$
and this map is injective on dominant weights for B_n (μ_1 ≥ ... ≥ μ_n ≥ 0).

- For type D_n: the Weyl group is the subgroup of signed permutations with an even number of sign changes.

**Test.** Implement the signed permutation action computationally. Verify injectivity for B_2, B_3, B_4 and D_3, D_4 by brute-force fingerprinting of dominant weights. If successful, formalize the abstract proof, which should follow the same set-pairing argument used for GL_n.

**Impact.** Would extend the tropical Satake isomorphism to all classical groups, covering the majority of reductive groups of interest in the Langlands program. The proof architecture (permutation reindexing + set-pairing injectivity) should transfer with only the group-theoretic inputs changing.

## Hypothesis 3: Tropical Hecke Convolution = Minkowski Addition of Permutahedra

**Conjecture.** Under the tropical Satake isomorphism, min-plus convolution of Hecke basis elements corresponds to Minkowski addition of the associated permutahedra:
$$\Pi(\mu) + \Pi(\nu) = \Pi(\mu + \nu) \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad s_\mu^{\text{trop}} \odot s_\nu^{\text{trop}} = s_{\mu+\nu}^{\text{trop}}$$
where + on the left is Minkowski sum and ⊙ on the right is pointwise tropical multiplication (= ordinary addition of values).

More precisely: if the tropical product of Hecke basis elements 1_{KμK} ⊙ 1_{KνK} decomposes as ⊕_λ c_λ 1_{KλK}, then the tropical Littlewood-Richardson coefficients c_λ satisfy a Minkowski decomposition rule for permutahedra.

**Test.**
1. Compute Minkowski sums of permutahedra for μ, ν ∈ Λ^+_3 with small entries.
2. Compute the tropical convolution product of the corresponding Hecke basis elements.
3. Verify that the vertices of the Minkowski sum correspond to the support of the tropical product.
4. Check whether the simplest case (s_μ ⊙ s_ν = s_{μ+ν}) holds, i.e., whether the tropical Schur map is multiplicative for the pointwise sum of dominant weights.

**Impact.** Would establish a concrete polyhedral interpretation of the tropical Hecke algebra, linking representation-theoretic convolution to convex geometry. This could lead to new algorithms for computing tropical Littlewood-Richardson coefficients.

## Hypothesis 4: Tropical Schur Polynomials Detect the Majorization Order

**Conjecture.** For dominant weights μ, ν with the same coordinate sum (Σ μ_i = Σ ν_i), the following are equivalent:
1. μ majorizes ν (μ ≻ ν in the dominance order),
2. s_μ^{trop}(z) ≤ s_ν^{trop}(z) for all z with z_0 ≥ z_1 ≥ ... ≥ z_{n-1} ≥ 0.

If true, this means the tropical Schur polynomial provides a functional encoding of the majorization lattice.

**Test.**
1. For n = 3, 4, 5, enumerate all pairs of dominant weights with the same sum.
2. Check whether majorization ⟹ pointwise ≤ on dominant test vectors.
3. Check the converse: search for counterexamples where pointwise ≤ holds but majorization fails.
4. If the conjecture holds computationally, seek a proof using the rearrangement inequality and the relationship between permutahedra and majorization polytopes.

**Impact.** Would provide a new characterization of the majorization order via tropical polynomials. Since majorization is fundamental in matrix theory, statistical mechanics, and quantum information, this would connect tropical Satake theory to a wide range of applications.

## Hypothesis 5: Orbit-Min Basis Elements Have Polynomial-Size Tropical Circuits

**Conjecture.** For each dominant weight μ ∈ Λ^+_n, the tropical Schur polynomial s_μ^{trop} admits a min-plus circuit (using min and + gates) of size O(n^c) for some constant c, rather than the naive O(n! · n) from enumerating all permutations.

**Test.**
1. For n = 3, 4, 5, 6, synthesize optimal or near-optimal min-plus circuits for specific tropical Schur polynomials using brute-force search over circuit topologies.
2. Measure the circuit size as a function of n for the "staircase" weight μ = (n, n-1, ..., 1).
3. Investigate whether the Hungarian algorithm's O(n³) procedure for evaluating min-assignment can be "compiled" into a fixed min-plus circuit.
4. Study the relationship to the circuit complexity of the permanent (which is the classical analogue of the min-assignment).

**Impact.** Polynomial-size circuits would make tropical Schur polynomials practical for real-time optimization in large-scale symmetric problems. It would also connect tropical Satake theory to algebraic complexity theory, specifically the VP vs. VNP question in its tropical formulation.
