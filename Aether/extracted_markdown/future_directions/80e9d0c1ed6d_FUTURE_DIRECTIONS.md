# Future Directions: Iterated Set-Local Distortion of Hausdorff Dimension

This cycle extended the *single-composition* distortion calculus of
`Geometry.QuasiSymmetricComposition` to its **iterated** form in
`Geometry.IteratedDistortion`. The earlier file established that the set-local
antilipschitz class is closed under one composition and that bi-Lipschitz /
bi-Hölder distortion exponents multiply. The natural dynamical question it left
open was what happens under *repeated* application of a single good map on a
forward-invariant piece — the setting of iterated function systems and discrete
dynamics. We answered the finite-orbit version: for a forward-invariant set `s`
(`f '' s ⊆ s`),

- the set-local Lipschitz / antilipschitz classes are closed under iteration with
  constant `K ^ n` (`lipschitzOnWith_iterate`, `antilipschitzOnWith_iterate`);
- a forward-invariant bi-Lipschitz map preserves Hausdorff dimension along its
  whole forward orbit, `dimH (f^[n] '' s) = dimH s` (`dimH_iterate_image_eq`);
- a forward-invariant Hölder map collapses dimension at a *geometric* rate,
  `dimH (f^[n] '' s) ≤ dimH s / r ^ n` (`dimH_iterate_image_le_of_holderOn`);
- the attractor obeys the trivial-but-foundational upper bound
  `dimH (⋂ₙ f^[n] '' s) ≤ dimH s` (`dimH_attractor_le`).

The structural lesson is that the entire theory is *functorial in the iterate*:
once a class is closed under composition and restriction, the `n`-th iterate is
governed by the `n`-th power of the one-step data, and the dimension estimates
follow by a uniform induction whose base is single-map invariance and whose step
is the composition theorem. The directions below probe the one genuinely missing
piece — passage from the finite orbit to the infinite attractor — and the
multi-map IFS generalization.

---

### Direction 1: Lower bound for the attractor under uniform completeness

**Hypothesis.** If `f` is bi-Lipschitz on a *complete*, forward-invariant `s` with
`f '' s` *closed* in `s`, then the attractor satisfies the matching lower bound
`dimH s ≤ dimH (⋂ₙ f^[n] '' s)`, hence equality with the orbit dimension proved
this cycle. More generally, when `f` is a contraction the nested images shrink to
the genuine IFS attractor and dimension is *exactly* preserved in the limit.

**Test.** Strengthen `dimH_attractor_le` to a two-sided statement: prove the
descending sequence `f^[n] '' s` has closed terms, use completeness to show the
intersection is nonempty and inherits a left inverse in the limit, then push
`AntilipschitzOnWith.le_dimH_image` through the intersection via a
monotone-limit / `iSup`–`iInf` exchange for `dimH`.

**The key insight is** that the finite orbit invariance already proved is exactly
the per-level estimate of a monotone-limit argument; the only new analytic input
is a completeness/closedness condition guaranteeing the intersection does not lose
mass, so the dimension is continuous along the nested sequence.

**Why now.** `dimH_iterate_image_eq` supplies the uniform per-`n` equality and
`dimH_attractor_le` supplies the easy half; the remaining work is a single
limiting lemma, not new distortion theory.

**If true.** Yields a clean dimension-invariance theorem for IFS-type attractors
generated by one contraction-like map. **If false.** The break must occur at the
limit, isolating precisely where finite invariance fails to survive intersection —
itself a sharp structural fact about `dimH` and nested compacta.

### Direction 2: Multi-map iterated function systems and the dimension of self-similar sets

**Hypothesis.** For a finite family `f₁, …, f_m` each set-local bi-Lipschitz on a
common forward-invariant `s` with `fᵢ '' s ⊆ s`, every finite word
`f_{i₁} ∘ ⋯ ∘ f_{i_k}` preserves dimension on `s`, so the whole *word orbit*
`{ f_w '' s : w ∈ words }` is dimension-homogeneous, and the union over a fixed
length `⋃_{|w|=k} f_w '' s` has dimension `dimH s`.

**Test.** Replace the single-map induction by induction over words: closure under
composition (`AntilipschitzOnWith.comp`, `LipschitzOnWith.comp`) already handles
each letter; then use `dimH` of a finite union `= ⨆` (max) to lift to the union
over words of fixed length.

**The key insight is** that the iterate `f^[n]` is just the special case of the
length-`n` word in a one-letter alphabet, so the proofs of this cycle are the
restriction of the IFS statement to a single generator — the composition closure
lemmas are alphabet-agnostic.

**Why now.** The composition and iteration machinery is in place; only the
finite-union (`Finset`-indexed `iSup`) bookkeeping for `dimH` must be added.

**If true.** Connects the calculus to the classical similarity-dimension theory of
self-similar sets. **If false.** A failure would expose an interaction between
distinct generators (e.g. overlap) that single-map iteration cannot see.

### Direction 3: The geometric Hölder bound is sharp on snowflakes

**Hypothesis.** The geometric bound `dimH (f^[n] '' s) ≤ dimH s / r ^ n` of
`dimH_iterate_image_le_of_holderOn` is *attained*: on a self-similar Cantor set
with a snowflake metric there is a Hölder map of exponent `r` realizing equality
for every `n`, so the constant `r ^ n` cannot be improved.

**Test.** Construct on a standard Cantor set an explicit `r`-Hölder forward map
(snowflaking the metric `d ↦ d^r`) and compute both sides; alternatively, attempt
to *disprove* sharpness by deriving a uniformly smaller universal exponent.

**The key insight is** that snowflaking the metric by `d ↦ d^r` turns the identity
into an exactly-`r`-Hölder map whose iterate is exactly `r^n`-Hölder, so the
geometric decay rate is forced rather than merely sufficient.

**Why now.** We have the upper bound in hand and an exact-invariance corollary at
`r = 1`; the natural next probe is equality at `r ≠ 1`.

**If true.** Certifies the iterated Hölder theorem as optimal. **If false.** A
universal improvement would signal that the single exponent `r` is the wrong
invariant and that a finer gauge-function rate governs iterated snowflaking.

### Direction 4: From orbit dimension to orbit *measure* under iteration

**Hypothesis.** The iterated maps not only fix dimension but give two-sided
control of the `d`-dimensional Hausdorff *measure* along the orbit: for a
bi-Lipschitz forward-invariant `f`, `μH^d (f^[n] '' s)` stays comparable to
`μH^d s` with comparison constants `K ^ (n d)`, and for a Hölder map the measure
distorts as `μH^{d} (f^[n] '' s) ≲ μH^{d · r^n}(s)`.

**Test.** Replace `HolderOnWith.dimH_image_le` and the bi-Lipschitz invariance by
the underlying Hausdorff-measure inequalities (`HolderOnWith.hausdorffMeasure_image_le`
and the Lipschitz measure bound) and re-run the same iteration induction, tracking
constants instead of discarding them.

**The key insight is** that the dimension proofs of this cycle already factor
through Hausdorff-measure inequalities at each level, so the measure-level
iteration statement sits exactly one abstraction layer below what we proved and
inherits the same `K^n` / `r^n` bookkeeping.

**Why now.** The inductive scaffold (forward invariance + per-step image rewrite)
is reusable verbatim; only the per-step inequality is swapped from dimension to
measure.

**If true.** Upgrades the iterated theory from a dimension calculus to a measure
calculus, the natural home for rectifiability and energy along orbits. **If
false.** The obstruction reveals that iterated dimension invariance is strictly
coarser than iterated measure comparability — a structural discovery in itself.

### Direction 5: A topological-entropy / dimension dichotomy for the forward orbit

**Hypothesis.** For a forward-invariant set-local *Lipschitz* map (one-sided, not
necessarily antilipschitz), the orbit dimension sequence `n ↦ dimH (f^[n] '' s)`
is monotone non-increasing and its limit equals the dimension of the maximal
invariant subset `⋂ₙ f^[n] '' s`; the *rate* of decrease is controlled by the
Lipschitz constant `K`, giving a quantitative link between contraction strength
and dimensional collapse.

**Test.** Prove monotonicity directly from `LipschitzOnWith.dimH_image_le` applied
on the nested invariant pieces (no antilipschitz hypothesis needed), then identify
the limit with the attractor dimension using Direction 1's limiting lemma; finally
bound the decrement using the Hölder/Lipschitz quantitative estimate.

**The key insight is** that dropping the antilipschitz half turns the *equality*
`dimH_iterate_image_eq` into a *monotone inequality*, and monotone bounded
sequences in `ℝ≥0∞` converge — so the orbit dimension always has a well-defined
limit even when invariance fails, converting a rigidity theorem into a dynamical
convergence theorem.

**Why now.** The one-sided Lipschitz half of every theorem this cycle is already
isolated (the antilipschitz hypothesis is only used for the lower bound), so the
monotone version is a direct weakening of proven results.

**If true.** Provides a dimension-theoretic Lyapunov functional for set-local
contractions. **If false.** A non-monotone orbit dimension would reveal that
set-local Lipschitz control alone cannot prevent dimension from oscillating,
sharpening the necessity of the invariance hypothesis.
