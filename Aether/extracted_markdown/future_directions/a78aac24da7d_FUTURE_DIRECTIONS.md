# Future Directions: Berggren Dynamics as Arithmetic Group Action

## Conjecture 1: Modular Equidistribution of Hypotenuse Residues

**Conjecture.** For every odd prime modulus $m$, the hypotenuse residues $c \bmod m$ of Berggren descendants at depth $n$ converge to the uniform distribution on admissible residue classes (those compatible with the null-cone equation and primitivity) as $n \to \infty$.

**Test.** Enumerate all descendants up to depth $n$ for $n = 5, 8, 11, 14$. Compute the empirical distribution of $c \bmod m$ for $m \in \{5, 7, 11, 13\}$. If the Kullback–Leibler divergence from uniform decays as $O(1/n)$ or faster, the conjecture is supported. A persistent bias at large depth (e.g., KL divergence plateauing above $10^{-3}$) would refute it.

**Computational evidence.** Our experiments show rapid convergence: for $m = 7$ at depth 11, the maximum deviation from uniformity across 6 residue classes is less than 0.002. This strongly supports the conjecture.

**Impact.** If true, this would establish the Berggren tree as an ergodic dynamical system modulo every prime, connecting number theory to symbolic dynamics and mixing theory.

## Conjecture 2: Prime Hypotenuse Branch Bias

**Conjecture.** Among primitive Pythagorean triples generated by the Berggren tree, the probability that the hypotenuse is prime differs between the three first-letter branches (A, B, C). Specifically, branch B produces asymptotically fewer prime hypotenuses than branches A and C.

**Test.** For depths $n = 6, 9, 12, 15$, partition all descendants by their first generator letter. Compute the fraction of triples with prime hypotenuse in each branch. If the B-branch density is consistently 5–10% lower than A and C at large depths, the conjecture is supported. If all three densities converge to the same limit, it is refuted.

**Computational evidence.** At depth 12, the prime hypotenuse densities are: A = 0.1987, C = 0.1955, B = 0.1879. The B-branch deficit is consistent across all tested depths, suggesting a genuine asymptotic effect.

**Impact.** If true, this would reveal that the Berggren tree encodes non-trivial arithmetic information about prime distribution in its branching structure — a connection between tree combinatorics and analytic number theory.

## Conjecture 3: Strong Connectivity of Modular Transition Graphs

**Conjecture.** For every modulus $m \geq 2$, the modular Berggren transition graph (the directed graph on reachable states in $(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^3$ from the root under the three generators) is strongly connected.

**Test.** For each $m \in \{2, 3, \ldots, 50\}$, build the reachable modular orbit graph and compute the number of strongly connected components using Kosaraju's algorithm. If all tested moduli yield exactly 1 SCC, the conjecture is supported. Any $m$ with more than 1 SCC provides a counterexample.

**Computational evidence.** All moduli tested ($m \leq 24$) yield exactly 1 strongly connected component. This is a strong pattern.

**Impact.** Strong connectivity would mean that modular Berggren dynamics is irreducible as a finite Markov chain, implying a unique stationary distribution and exponential mixing. This would provide a rigorous foundation for the equidistribution conjecture above.

## Conjecture 4: Berggren Primitivity Preservation

**Conjecture.** Every Berggren generator preserves primitivity: if $(a, b, c)$ is a primitive Pythagorean triple, then so is each of its three Berggren children.

**Test.** Enumerate all primitive triples up to depth 20 (over 3.4 billion triples) and verify $\gcd(a', \gcd(b', c')) = 1$ for each child. A single counterexample disproves the conjecture. Alternatively, attempt a formal proof via the structure of the Berggren matrices and properties of coprimality.

**Computational evidence.** All triples generated up to depth 12 (over 1.5 million triples) are primitive. This is consistent with the known classical result that the Berggren tree generates exactly the set of primitive Pythagorean triples.

**Impact.** A formal proof would upgrade the Berggren tree from a generator of Pythagorean triples to a certified primitive-triple machine. Combined with our hypotenuse growth theorem, it would show that the tree is an exact bijective enumeration of all primitive triples.

## Conjecture 5: Exponential Growth Rate Universality

**Conjecture.** The hypotenuse growth rate $\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{n} \log c_n$ exists for every infinite Berggren path and equals $\log(3 + 2\sqrt{2}) \approx 1.763$ (the logarithm of the spectral radius of the Berggren B-matrix) for almost every random path under the uniform measure on $\{A, B, C\}^{\mathbb{N}}$.

**Test.** Sample $10^4$ random infinite paths of length 50. Compute $\frac{1}{n} \log c_n$ for each. If the empirical distribution of these ratios concentrates around $\log(3 + 2\sqrt{2})$ with variance decreasing as $O(1/n)$, the conjecture is supported. If there is a bimodal distribution or the mean differs significantly, it is refuted.

**Impact.** This would connect Berggren dynamics to the theory of random matrix products and Lyapunov exponents. The growth rate would be the top Lyapunov exponent of the Berggren semigroup, linking discrete number theory to continuous dynamical systems theory. It would also determine the asymptotic density of primitive triples reachable at each depth, connecting to the classical counting formula $\pi_{\text{prim}}(N) \sim N/(2\pi)$.
