# Future Directions: Higher-Dimensional Tropical Morse Theory

## Synthesis

The results established in this work — the single-simplex Euler step, the f-vector decomposition, and the surface edge-face relation — form the combinatorial foundation of a higher-dimensional tropical Morse calculus. They demonstrate that the graph-level tropical Morse framework extends cleanly to simplicial complexes of arbitrary dimension, preserving the fundamental conservation law (signed event sum = χ) while gaining access to richer topological structure. The cross-domain bridge to simplicial isomorphism opens connections to complexity theory, while the double-counting arguments for surfaces connect to discrete differential geometry. The five directions below push this foundation toward a complete higher-dimensional theory with applications spanning pure mathematics, materials science, and machine learning.

## Direction 1: Betti-Number-Level Tropical Morse Theory via Chain Complexes

**Conjecture:** For a finite simplicial complex K with generic monotone weights, each simplex insertion in the filtration changes exactly one Betti number by ±1 (in dimension d or d−1 for a d-simplex), and the parity of the change is determined by the rank of the boundary map restricted to the current filtration level.

**Test:** Implement the boundary matrix reduction algorithm for small triangulated surfaces (f-vector bounded by 50). For each simplex insertion, compute the rank change of the boundary operators. Verify that exactly one Betti number changes per step, and that the change is always ±1. Generate 100 random weight assignments on each of the three standard surfaces. The conjecture is falsified if any insertion changes two or more Betti numbers simultaneously, or changes a Betti number by more than 1.

**Impact:** This would complete the transition from Euler-level tropical Morse theory to persistent-homology-level tropical Morse theory, enabling full barcode computation within the tropical framework. It would make the tropical Morse spectrum a complete topological invariant (up to barcode equivalence) rather than merely an Euler characteristic detector.

**Catalog References:**
- `Pythagorean/TropicalMorse/Theorems.lean : euler_char_from_filtration`
- `Pythagorean/TropicalMorse/HigherSimplicial.lean : add_simplex_euler_step`
- `Pythagorean/TropicalMorse/HigherSimplicial.lean : euler_char_fvector_sum`

**Proof Strategy:** Define chain groups C_d(K) as free abelian groups generated by d-simplices. Define boundary operators ∂_d : C_d → C_{d-1}. For each simplex insertion, show the rank of ∂_d changes by at most 1 (by the interlacing of matrix singular values under rank-1 updates). Use the rank-nullity theorem to connect rank changes to Betti number changes.

**Domain Bridges:** Persistent homology (TDA), algebraic topology, linear algebra (matrix perturbation theory).

**Lineage:** Direct extension of `add_simplex_euler_step`, replacing the signed contribution (−1)^d with the precise (d, Δβ_k) pair.

**Ambition:** Grand challenge. This would unify tropical Morse theory with persistent homology.

## Direction 2: Tropical Morse Signatures as Topological Materials Fingerprints

**Conjecture:** For crystallographic simplicial complexes derived from atomic coordination shells, the tropical Morse spectrum (with weights given by interatomic distances) determines the topological type of the coordination network and distinguishes distinct crystal phases that X-ray diffraction cannot separate.

**Test:** Construct Delaunay triangulations from the atomic coordinates of three well-studied polymorphic materials (e.g., SiO₂ quartz vs. cristobalite vs. tridymite). Assign edge weights as interatomic distances, extended to higher-dimensional faces by max-weight convention. Compute tropical Morse spectra and event profiles. Compare with X-ray diffraction fingerprints. The conjecture is falsified if two phases with distinct X-ray signatures have identical tropical Morse spectra, or if two phases with identical X-ray signatures also have identical spectra.

**Impact:** Would establish tropical Morse theory as a tool for materials discovery, complementing existing diffraction and density-functional methods with topological sensitivity.

**Catalog References:**
- `Pythagorean/TropicalMorse/HigherSimplicial.lean : euler_char_iso_invariant`
- `Pythagorean/TropicalMorse/HigherSimplicial.lean : surface_edge_face_relation`

**Proof Strategy:** Formalize the Delaunay complex construction for point clouds in ℝ³. Define the coordination simplicial complex as the restriction to atoms within a cutoff radius. Prove that the resulting complex satisfies monotone weight conditions, enabling filtration analysis.

**Domain Bridges:** Topological materials science, crystallography, condensed matter physics.

**Lineage:** Extends `euler_char_iso_invariant` to a practical non-isomorphism test for atomic networks.

**Ambition:** Solid extension with potential for paradigm shift in materials characterization.

**The key insight is** that atomic coordination networks naturally carry distance-based weights that make them weighted simplicial complexes, and the tropical Morse spectrum captures topological features (voids, channels, ring structures) that are invisible to pairwise correlation functions.

**Why now?** High-throughput computational materials databases (Materials Project, AFLOW) provide millions of crystal structures ready for topological analysis, and the formal verification infrastructure ensures the mathematical framework is trustworthy for materials-critical applications.

## Direction 3: Tropical Morse Theory vs. the Weisfeiler-Leman Hierarchy

**Conjecture:** For k ≥ 2, there exist pairs of non-isomorphic k-dimensional simplicial complexes that are indistinguishable by (k+1)-WL on their face-incidence graphs but are distinguished by their dimension-(k+1) tropical Morse event profiles under generic weights.

**Test:** Construct Cai-Fürer-Immerman-type gadget complexes in dimensions 2 and 3. Run (k+1)-WL on the face-incidence graph (represented as a colored hypergraph). Compute tropical Morse spectra with random weights. The conjecture is falsified if (k+1)-WL always separates every pair that TMS separates in the tested dimension range (k = 2, 3).

**Impact:** Would establish a formal separation between the tropical Morse hierarchy and the Weisfeiler-Leman hierarchy, placing tropical Morse invariants in the landscape of combinatorial distinguishing power and connecting to the graph isomorphism problem.

**Catalog References:**
- `Pythagorean/TropicalMorse/Theorems.lean : tms_strictly_expressive_over_WL1`
- `Pythagorean/TropicalMorse/HigherSimplicial.lean : different_euler_char_not_iso`

**Proof Strategy:** Generalize the CFI construction to higher-dimensional complexes. Show that the face-incidence graph of the CFI complex inherits the WL-indistinguishability. Then prove that the tropical Morse spectrum detects the topological difference (an extra torsion element in homology) that WL cannot see.

**Domain Bridges:** Finite model theory, descriptive complexity, graph isomorphism.

**Lineage:** Direct generalization of `tms_strictly_expressive_over_WL1` from 1-WL to higher WL levels.

**Ambition:** Grand challenge. A clean separation result would be a major contribution to both combinatorics and complexity theory.

**The key insight is** that WL operates on local neighborhood structure of the incidence graph, while tropical Morse spectra detect global topological features (homology classes) that cannot be captured by local refinement of bounded depth.

**Why now?** Recent breakthroughs in understanding the WL hierarchy for hypergraphs and simplicial complexes (Morris et al. 2022, Bodnar et al. 2021) provide the technical framework for rigorous comparison, and our formal verification infrastructure enables machine-checked separation proofs.

## Direction 4: Geometric Deep Learning on Cell Complexes via Tropical Features

**Conjecture:** Message-passing neural networks on cell complexes augmented with tropical Morse spectral features achieve strictly higher expressive power (in the sense of distinguishing non-isomorphic complexes) than standard message-passing networks, with the gap provably captured by the difference between TMS and WL.

**Test:** Implement a cell complex neural network (CW-Net) with and without tropical Morse features on the ZINC molecular dataset and a synthetic simplicial complex classification benchmark. Measure classification accuracy and expressiveness (fraction of non-isomorphic pairs correctly distinguished). The conjecture is falsified if the TMS-augmented model achieves no statistically significant improvement over the baseline on either benchmark.

**Impact:** Would provide theoretically grounded feature engineering for geometric deep learning, replacing ad hoc topological descriptors with principled invariants whose mathematical properties are formally verified.

**Catalog References:**
- `Pythagorean/TropicalMorse/HigherSimplicial.lean : euler_char_fvector_sum`
- `Pythagorean/TropicalMorse/Theorems.lean : tms_strictly_expressive_over_WL1`

**Proof Strategy:** Define the tropical Morse feature map as a function from weighted complexes to feature vectors (signed event sums by dimension, f-vector entries, event profiles). Prove that this map is more expressive than WL color histograms (using `different_euler_char_not_iso`). Show that adding these features to a message-passing framework provably increases the class of distinguishable complexes.

**Domain Bridges:** Geometric deep learning, graph neural networks, topological data analysis.

**Lineage:** Extends the WL expressiveness results from the graph-level theory to a practical ML application.

**Ambition:** Solid extension with immediate practical applications.

**The key insight is** that tropical Morse features are *provably* informative topological descriptors that can be computed efficiently and integrated into differentiable learning pipelines, unlike persistent homology features which require specialized vectorization schemes.

**Why now?** The explosion of interest in higher-order message passing networks (CWN, SAN, CIN) creates an immediate demand for theoretically grounded features beyond what WL-type aggregation provides, and the formal verification of our framework ensures these features have guaranteed mathematical properties.

## Direction 5: Tropical Sheaf Theory on Simplicial Complexes

**Conjecture:** The tropical Morse spectrum of a weighted simplicial complex K can be recovered as the Euler characteristic of a sheaf of tropical semirings on K, where sections over a simplex σ are tropical polynomials constrained by the weight of σ, and the sheaf cohomology captures the obstruction to extending local tropical sections.

**Test:** For small complexes (f-vector bounded by 20), explicitly construct the tropical sheaf and compute its cohomology using the Čech complex. Compare the resulting Euler characteristic with the signed event sum. The conjecture is falsified if the sheaf Euler characteristic differs from the combinatorial signed sum for any tested complex.

**Impact:** Would place tropical Morse theory within the framework of sheaf theory, connecting it to the powerful machinery of homological algebra and potentially leading to tropical analogues of the Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch theorem for simplicial complexes.

**Catalog References:**
- `Pythagorean/TropicalMorse/HigherSimplicial.lean : eulerChar_filtration_eq`
- `Pythagorean/TropicalMorse/HigherSimplicial.lean : euler_char_fvector_sum`

**Proof Strategy:** Define a presheaf on the face poset of K assigning tropical semiring modules to each simplex. Prove the gluing axiom under a monotonicity condition on weights. Use spectral sequences to relate sheaf cohomology to the filtration-based Euler computation. The key technical challenge is defining appropriate restriction maps between tropical modules on faces of different dimensions.

**Domain Bridges:** Tropical geometry, algebraic geometry, sheaf theory, homological algebra.

**Lineage:** Extends the tropical geometry perspective from the spectral/filtration viewpoint to a full sheaf-theoretic framework.

**Ambition:** Grand challenge. Would open an entirely new field: tropical sheaf topology.

**The key insight is** that the filtration subcomplex construction already has the structure of a presheaf (restriction = inclusion), and the Euler characteristic computation is a shadow of the deeper sheaf-cohomological story.

**Why now?** Recent advances in computational sheaf theory (Curry 2014, Ghrist 2014) and tropical homological algebra (Jell-Shaw-Smacka 2019) provide the mathematical infrastructure needed to make this program rigorous, and our formal verification tools can ensure correctness of the new sheaf constructions.
