# Future Directions: Tropical Scheme Theory and Tropical Gröbner Bases

The file `Tropical/Scheme/Groebner.lean` establishes the scaffolding of a tropical
scheme theory over the min-plus semiring `TropR = Tropical (WithTop ℝ)`. Tropical
ideals are realized as `Ideal (MvPolynomial σ TropR)` — subsemimodules of the
tropical polynomial semiring closed under tropical linear combinations — and we prove:

- `tropPoly_add_idem`: the tropical polynomial semiring is additively idempotent
  (`p + p = p`), the defining structural break from classical scheme theory;
- `leadExp_add_mem`: the leading-exponent set of a tropical ideal is an upward-closed
  monomial set (the tropical "initial ideal" lives in exponent space);
- `exists_finite_leadGenerators`: Dickson finiteness of that monomial set;
- `exists_tropicalGroebnerBasis`: every tropical ideal admits a finite Gröbner basis
  with respect to any monomial order.

These are the existence/termination half of a tropical Buchberger theory. The following
conjectures push toward the *algorithmic* and *geometric* half. Each is stated to be
testable: it can be falsified by a single explicit counterexample in
`MvPolynomial (Fin n) TropR`.

---

## Direction 1: Tropical S-pair criterion (Buchberger's theorem, tropical form)

**Conjecture.** Let `G ⊆ I` be a finite generating set of a tropical ideal `I` with
`m.degree`-leading exponents `{deg g}`. Then `G` is a tropical Gröbner basis
(`IsTropicalGroebnerBasis m I G`) **iff** for every pair `g, h ∈ G` the tropical
S-polynomial `m.sPolynomial g h` "reduces to a polynomial whose leading exponent lies
in the monomial set generated by `{deg g}`" under tropical multivariate division.

**The key insight is** that even though the tropical semiring has no subtraction, the
*leading-exponent bookkeeping* of Buchberger's algorithm is purely order-theoretic:
the cancellation that classically defines an S-polynomial is replaced by the condition
that the two competing minima at the lcm exponent are achieved simultaneously, which is
exactly the balancing condition of tropical geometry. Mathlib already provides
`MonomialOrder.sPolynomial` and the S-pair lemmas `sPolynomial_monomial_mul`, so the
statement is expressible verbatim over `MvPolynomial σ TropR`.

**Why now?** We have just proved `leadExp_add_mem` and `exists_finite_leadGenerators`,
which give the up-set structure and finiteness the S-pair criterion quantifies over;
the remaining work is to define a tropical division/reduction relation and connect it to
`sPolynomial`, all on top of API that exists in this Lean/Mathlib version.

## Direction 2: Tropical Hilbert Basis Theorem (Noetherianity of tropical ideals)

**Conjecture.** Over finitely many variables, the lattice of tropical ideals satisfies
the ascending chain condition: every chain `I₀ ≤ I₁ ≤ ⋯` of `Ideal (MvPolynomial (Fin n) TropR)`
stabilizes. Equivalently, `IsNoetherian` holds for the tropical polynomial semiring as a
module over itself, despite the coefficient semiring being non-Noetherian as a ring
(it has no subtraction).

**The key insight is** that Noetherianity is forced not by the coefficients but by the
*exponent monoid* `Fin n →₀ ℕ` being well-quasi-ordered (Dickson). The map
`I ↦ leadExp m I` is monotone into the lattice of monomial up-sets, and a stabilizing
chain of monomial up-sets pulls back to a stabilizing chain of ideals once each
inclusion is detected on leading terms.

**Why now?** `exists_finite_leadGenerators` already packages Dickson's lemma for
`leadExp`; lifting it to an ACC statement is the natural next theorem and would give the
tropical analogue of the Hilbert Basis Theorem — a headline result with a short path
from the lemmas now proven.

## Direction 3: Tropical Nullstellensatz via the corner locus

**Conjecture.** For a tropical ideal `I` define its tropical variety `V(I) ⊆ ℝⁿ` as the
set of points where, for every `f ∈ I`, the minimum defining `f` is attained at least
twice (the corner / non-differentiability locus). Then a point `x` belongs to `V(I)` iff
it belongs to `V(g)` for every `g` in any tropical Gröbner basis `G` of `I`; i.e. a
Gröbner basis is a *certificate* for the tropical variety.

**The key insight is** that the tropical Gröbner basis controls leading terms, and the
corner locus of a tropical polynomial is determined by ties among its lowest-degree
contributions along each direction — precisely the data a Gröbner basis normalizes.
This converts a quantifier over the whole (infinite) ideal into a check over a finite
basis.

**Why now?** `exists_tropicalGroebnerBasis` supplies the finite certifying set, and the
catalog already contains tropical-hypersurface and convexity infrastructure
(`Tropical.AmoebaRonkin`, `Tropical.Convexity`, `Tropical.LSEConvexity`) to define the
corner locus rigorously; the bridge between them is now buildable.

## Direction 4: Stable rank / Matroid structure of tropical ideals (Maclagan–Rincón)

**Conjecture.** For a tropical ideal `I` and each total degree `d`, the degree-`d` slice
`I_d ⊆ (degree-≤ d monomials)` of the tropical subsemimodule is the set of "vectors" of a
valuated matroid; consequently the function `d ↦ rank(I_d)` is well-defined and the
Gröbner basis of Direction 1 computes it.

**The key insight is** that tropical linear dependence (closure under min and tropical
scaling) is governed by a circuit/exchange axiom identical to the valuated-matroid
exchange axiom, so each graded piece of a tropical ideal carries matroid data even when
the global object is not free. The finite Gröbner basis gives an explicit cocircuit
generating set per degree.

**Why now?** The genuine tropical-ideal definition in this file (a `Submodule` over the
idempotent semiring, *not* merely a tropicalized classical ideal) is exactly the object
of Maclagan–Rincón's theory of tropical ideals; with finiteness (`exists_finite_leadGenerators`)
and idempotency (`tropPoly_add_idem`) in hand, the matroid axioms become finite,
checkable statements ready for formalization.
