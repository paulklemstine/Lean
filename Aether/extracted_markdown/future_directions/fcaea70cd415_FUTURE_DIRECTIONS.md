# Future Directions: Quantum Enumerative Combinatorics

## Synthesis

The formalization of Krawtchouk polynomials and the quantum MacWilliams framework opens a systematic path from discrete orthogonal polynomials through quantum code classification to topological quantum order. The five directions below form a coherent program: Direction 1 (Pauli group formalization) removes the structural axiom at the foundation; Direction 2 (modular forms) reveals deep number-theoretic structure in weight enumerators; Direction 3 (higher-dimensional BT bounds) extends the isoperimetric theory; Direction 4 (tropical LP bounds) provides new computational tools; Direction 5 (fracton codes) tests the limits of topological order. Together, these directions would establish quantum enumerative combinatorics as a rigorous mathematical discipline with machine-verified foundations.

## Direction 1: Full Pauli Group Formalization and MacWilliams as a Theorem

**Conjecture:** The quantum MacWilliams identity B_j = (1/2^(n-k)) Σ_i A_i K_j(i; n) can be proved as a formal theorem (not an axiom) from the character orthogonality of the n-qubit Pauli group and the Poisson summation formula over Z₂^{2n}.

**Test:** Formalize the Pauli group P_n as the group (Z₂)^{2n} × Z₄ with its natural symplectic structure. Define the trace inner product Tr(E†F) and prove character orthogonality. Then derive the MacWilliams identity for CSS codes (the simplest case) as a first milestone.

**The key insight is:** The Pauli group has a natural symplectic structure over Z₂ that makes it amenable to Fourier analysis. The MacWilliams identity is the quantum Fourier transform of the weight distribution, and the Krawtchouk polynomials are the Fourier kernel.

**Why now?** Mathlib now has substantial group theory (subgroups, quotients, characters) and the symplectic group infrastructure is growing. The CSS reduction makes the first case tractable without full symplectic machinery.

**Impact:** Removes the last structural axiom from the MacWilliams framework, making all bounds certified from first principles. Would be the first complete formalization of a quantum information identity.

**Catalog References:** `Physics/QuantumMacWilliams/WeightEnumerator.lean` (MacWilliamsCode structure), `Catalog/Physics/PauliClosureFoundations.lean` (Pauli matrix definitions)

**Proof Strategy:** CSS decomposition → classical MacWilliams for each component → combination lemma → general stabilizer case via Clifford group conjugation.

**Domain Bridges:** Group theory ↔ quantum information ↔ harmonic analysis

**Lineage:** Builds directly on `MacWilliamsCode` structure, would eliminate the axiom.

**Ambition:** Grand challenge — requires ~2000 lines of new formalization.

## Direction 2: Weight Enumerators as Modular Forms (Grand Challenge)

**Conjecture:** The weight enumerator generating function W(q) = Σ_j A_j q^j for self-dual CSS codes is a modular form (or quasi-modular form) for the Clifford group action, analogous to how the theta function of an even unimodular lattice is a modular form for SL₂(Z).

**Test:** Compute W(q) for the [[8,0,4]] self-dual code (the octacode) and verify that it transforms as a modular form of weight 4 under the generators of the Clifford group. If the transformation law fails, the conjecture is falsified.

**The key insight is:** The Clifford group acts on weight enumerators by MacWilliams-type transforms. For self-dual codes, the weight enumerator is invariant under this action — exactly the defining property of a modular form. The ring of Clifford invariants (by Rains-Sloane) is generated by specific polynomials, analogous to Eisenstein series.

**Why now?** Recent advances in modular forms in Mathlib (Eisenstein series, modular discriminant) provide the algebraic framework. The connection between codes and lattices (Construction A) is well-understood classically.

**Impact:** Would connect quantum coding theory to the Langlands program and open quantum codes to techniques from analytic number theory. Potential applications to the quantum unique games conjecture.

**Catalog References:** `Physics/QuantumMacWilliams/Krawtchouk.lean`, `Catalog/Physics/QuantumE8ModularForms.lean`

**Proof Strategy:** Define Clifford group action on weight enumerators → prove invariance for self-dual codes → identify the ring of invariants with a space of modular forms → use Molien's theorem for dimension counting.

**Domain Bridges:** Number theory ↔ quantum information ↔ representation theory

**Lineage:** Extends the weight enumerator framework to deep algebraic structure.

**Ambition:** Grand challenge — requires connecting three deep mathematical domains.

## Direction 3: Higher-Dimensional Bravyi-Terhal Bounds via Cellular Homology

**Conjecture:** For a D-dimensional local stabilizer code, the Bravyi-Terhal bound k · d^{D/(D-1)} ≤ C_D · n holds with an explicit constant C_D computable from the isoperimetric profile of Z^D.

**Test:** Formalize the D=3 case and verify that the Haah cubic code [[n, k, d]] with n = L³, k = 2, d ~ L satisfies the bound with k · d^{3/2} ≤ C₃ · n.

**The key insight is:** The Bravyi-Terhal bound is fundamentally an isoperimetric inequality: logical operators must span a topologically non-trivial cycle, and the minimum such cycle has size determined by the isoperimetric profile. In D=2, this gives the cycle-area relation d² ≤ n/k.

**Why now?** The D=2 case is verified in our formalization (toric code saturation). Mathlib's growing simplicial/cellular homology infrastructure makes the higher-dimensional generalization tractable. Recent physics results on fracton codes provide new test cases.

**Impact:** Would resolve open questions about the scaling limits of 3D quantum codes and clarify when topological order can persist in higher dimensions.

**Catalog References:** `Physics/QuantumMacWilliams/WeightEnumerator.lean` (toric_saturates_bt), `Catalog/Physics/ToricCode.lean`

**Proof Strategy:** Define stabilizer complex as a CW-complex → prove the cycle/boundary exact sequence → apply isoperimetric inequality in Z^D → derive the parameter bound.

**Domain Bridges:** Algebraic topology ↔ quantum information ↔ geometric analysis

**Lineage:** Direct extension of the D=2 BT results.

**Ambition:** Solid extension — requires ~1000 lines building on existing toric code work.

## Direction 4: Tropical Linear Programming Bounds

**Conjecture:** The tropicalization of the Delsarte linear programming bound for quantum codes yields a piecewise-linear optimization problem whose solution provides bounds at least as strong as the classical LP bound, and strictly stronger for degenerate codes.

**Test:** Implement the tropical LP bound computationally for n ≤ 20 and compare with the standard LP bound. If the tropical bound is strictly tighter for any degenerate code, the conjecture is confirmed.

**The key insight is:** The tropical concavity theorem (proved in our formalization) implies that the weight enumerator constraints become linear in the tropical limit. The Newton polytope of the weight enumerator contains more information than the LP relaxation, because it encodes the multiplicative structure of the Krawtchouk transform.

**Why now?** Our tropical concavity proof provides the mathematical foundation. Tropical optimization (min-plus linear programming) has efficient algorithms that scale to n ~ 100.

**Impact:** Would provide a new family of bounds on quantum codes that are computationally tractable and potentially tighter than existing methods. Applications to quantum LDPC code design.

**Catalog References:** `Physics/QuantumMacWilliams/WeightEnumerator.lean` (tropical_concavity), `Catalog/Physics/TropicalQuantum/Advanced.lean`

**Proof Strategy:** Tropicalize the MacWilliams identity → formulate the dual LP in the tropical semiring → prove weak/strong duality for tropical LP → compare with classical LP value.

**Domain Bridges:** Tropical geometry ↔ optimization ↔ quantum information

**Lineage:** Builds on tropical_concavity and the MacWilliams framework.

**Ambition:** Solid extension — computationally testable, builds on verified foundations.

## Direction 5: Fracton Codes Beyond Bravyi-Terhal

**Conjecture:** Haah's cubic code (a 3D fracton code) with parameters [[L³, 2, d(L)]] satisfies d(L) = Ω(L^α) for some α > 0 but α < 1, meaning it violates the "obvious" BT generalization k · d^{3/2} ≤ C · n while still satisfying a weaker bound.

**Test:** Compute the minimum distance of Haah's cubic code for L = 2, 3, ..., 12 and fit the scaling d(L) ~ L^α. If α ≥ 1, the standard BT bound holds; if α < 1, fracton order provides a genuine violation.

**The key insight is:** Fracton codes have an intermediate form of topological order — their logical operators are not membrane-like (as in toric codes) but have fractal structure. This means the isoperimetric argument of Bravyi-Terhal, which assumes membrane-like logical operators, may not apply.

**Why now?** Recent numerical work suggests d(L) grows sub-linearly in L for Haah's code. Formal verification of the BT bound (our D=2 result) provides the baseline against which fracton violations can be measured.

**Impact:** Would clarify the boundary between topological order and fracton order, with implications for self-correcting quantum memory (a major open problem in quantum information).

**Catalog References:** `Physics/QuantumMacWilliams/WeightEnumerator.lean` (BT bound), `Catalog/Physics/CechStabilizerCode.lean`

**Proof Strategy:** Formalize Haah's cubic code → compute weight enumerators → check BT bound → characterize the scaling of d(L) via tropical analysis.

**Domain Bridges:** Condensed matter physics ↔ algebraic topology ↔ quantum information

**Lineage:** Tests the limits of the BT framework established in this work.

**Ambition:** Grand challenge — touches on open problems in quantum many-body physics.
