# FUTURE DIRECTIONS — Algebraic Statistics: Markov Bases for Contingency Tables

## Synthesis

This cycle built, from scratch, a small but complete formal theory of **Markov bases for
log-linear models on contingency tables** — a corner of algebraic statistics that is absent
from Mathlib. We modelled a contingency table as a free `ℤ`-module element
(`Fin 2 → Fin 2 → ℤ` and `Fin 2 → Fin 2 → Fin 2 → ℤ`), defined the *margins* (sufficient
statistics) as explicit linear functionals, and defined a *fiber* as the set of non-negative
tables sharing fixed margins. Inside this language we proved the two pillars of the theory
for the two smallest models: (1) the **move lattice is the kernel of the margin map**, and
(2) for the no-three-way interaction model a single explicit move **connects every fiber**
— a concrete instance of the Diaconis–Sturmfels *Fundamental Theorem of Markov Bases*.

The structural insight that emerged is that **both showcase models have a rank-one move
lattice**, generated by a single alternating sign pattern (`M2` for the `2×2` independence
model, `M3(i,j,k) = (-1)^{i+j+k}` for the `2×2×2` no-three-way model). This collapses the
deep algebraic content (toric ideals, Gröbner bases) into a finite linear-algebra fact
provable by `fin_cases` + `omega`, and reduces connectivity to a single clean idea:
**discrete convexity**. The non-negative locus along the move line is an integer interval, so
a monotone walk of unit `±M3` steps from one fiber element to another never leaves the
non-negative orthant. This convexity lemma (`connected_add_smul`) is the reusable engine and
the technical heart of the cycle; everything else is bookkeeping.

What did *not* generalise for free: rank-one is special. The moment the model has more than
two slices (`2×2×n`) the kernel acquires several generators and the single-line walk argument
breaks — connectivity then genuinely needs the multi-generator content of the Fundamental
Theorem. We deliberately recorded the boundary (the `TableN` type and the conjecture below)
rather than papering over it, because identifying exactly where rank-one stops working is the
most valuable signpost for the next team.

## Results Summary

- `MarkovBases.TwoWay.M2_apply_eq`: proved — the basic `2×2` swap takes only values `±1` (support lemma for omega/convexity).
- `MarkovBases.TwoWay.twoWay_move_preserves_margins`: proved — every multiple of `M2` lies in the kernel of the margin map, so it is a legal model move.
- `MarkovBases.TwoWay.twoWay_kernel`: proved — the move lattice of the `2×2` independence model is rank one (`{M2}` is a Markov basis).
- `MarkovBases.TwoWay.twoWay_fiber_card_interval`: proved — a fixed-margin fiber is exactly an explicit integer interval in the `(0,0)`-entry (discrete convexity made concrete).
- `MarkovBases.NoThreeWay.M3_apply_eq`: proved — the degree-4 alternating move takes only values `±1`.
- `MarkovBases.NoThreeWay.noThreeWay_move_preserves_margins`: proved — `M3` lies in the kernel of the two-way margin map.
- `MarkovBases.NoThreeWay.noThreeWay_kernel`: proved — despite 8 cells and 12 margin equations the move lattice is rank one, generated by the single move `M3` (it is *the* Markov basis).
- `MarkovBases.NoThreeWay.connected_add_smul`: proved — discrete-convexity engine: equal-margin tables are joined by a monotone non-negative walk of `±M3` steps.
- `MarkovBases.NoThreeWay.noThreeWay_fiber_connected`: proved — **Fundamental Theorem of Markov Bases for the no-three-way model**: `{M3}` connects every fiber.
- `MarkovBases.NoThreeWay.noThreeWay_general_conjecture` (the `2×2×n` extension): conjecture — recorded as a type (`TableN`) and a research direction; not yet a formal statement.

## Research Directions

### Direction 1: The `2×2×n` no-three-way model has an explicit slice-pair Markov basis
**Hypothesis**: For the `2 × 2 × n` no-three-way interaction model, the finite set of moves
`{ M_{a,b} : a < b in Fin n }`, where `M_{a,b}` is the `2×2×2` alternating move on the pair
of slices `a, b` (zero elsewhere), is a Markov basis: any two non-negative tables with equal
two-way margins are connected by a non-negative walk using `±` these moves.
**Test**: Formalise `TableN n` (already declared), define the slice-pair moves, and prove a
multi-generator analogue of `noThreeWay_fiber_connected`. Disproof would be a small explicit
counterexample (two equal-margin tables with no non-negative slice-pair walk between them),
which `decide`/search can hunt for at `n = 3`.
The key insight is that the difference of two equal-margin tables, restricted to any pair of
slices, has zero two-way margins on that `2×2×2` sub-block, so `noThreeWay_kernel` applies
slice-pair-by-slice-pair — turning the global problem into a sequence of the rank-one moves
we already control.
**Why now**: We already proved the rank-one building block (`noThreeWay_kernel`) and the
convexity walk (`connected_add_smul`); this direction is exactly "iterate the block we have"
rather than inventing new machinery.
**If true**: It gives the first formally verified *non-trivial* (multi-generator) Markov
basis, the natural stepping stone to general no-three-way models.
**If false**: A counterexample would pin down the smallest model where slice-pair moves fail
to connect, sharpening the conjectured form of the basis.

### Direction 2: Markov basis ⇔ generators of the toric (lattice) ideal
**Hypothesis**: For these models, a finite move set `B ⊆ ker(margin map)` connects every
fiber **iff** the corresponding binomials generate the lattice ideal `I_L` of the margin
map — the Diaconis–Sturmfels equivalence in its purest, ideal-free `ℤ`-lattice form.
**Test**: State connectivity and "`B` generates the saturated sublattice `L = ker ∩ ℤ^N`"
and prove the equivalence for the rank-one cases (where it reduces to: `B` connects ⇔ `B`
spans `L`). Then attempt the general lattice version using `Submodule.span`.
The key insight is that, stripped of commutative-algebra packaging, the Fundamental Theorem
is a statement about *lattice walks vs. lattice generation*: connectivity of fibers is the
combinatorial shadow of `Submodule.span_eq`.
**Why now**: Our `*_kernel` theorems already exhibit the move lattice explicitly as a
rank-one `ℤ`-submodule, so the span side of the equivalence is in reach without building any
toric-ideal infrastructure.
**If true**: It yields a Mathlib-ready, ideal-free formulation of the Fundamental Theorem,
usable far beyond contingency tables.
**If false**: The gap (saturation / primitivity of the lattice) would reveal exactly which
algebraic hypothesis the purely combinatorial statement is missing.

### Direction 3: Fiber connectivity via a Graver basis bound on walk length
**Hypothesis**: In the no-three-way `2×2×2` model, every two equal-margin tables are
connected by a walk of length exactly `|v₀₀₀ − u₀₀₀|`, and this is the diameter of the fiber
graph; more generally the Graver basis controls a linear bound on walk length.
**Test**: Strengthen `connected_add_smul` to return the path and prove its length equals
`t.natAbs`; show no shorter walk exists (a lower bound from the linear functional `u ↦ u₀₀₀`).
The key insight is that the single linear coordinate `u₀₀₀` is a *potential function* that
changes by exactly `±1` per move, so it simultaneously certifies reachability and optimality.
**Why now**: The induction in `connected_add_smul` already walks in `t.natAbs` steps; reading
off the length and proving optimality is a small refactor of an existing proof.
**If true**: It converts our qualitative connectivity into a quantitative *mixing/diameter*
statement, the quantity statisticians actually care about for MCMC on tables.
**If false**: A shorter walk would mean the move lattice is not "primitive" in the metric
sense, an interesting and testable surprise.

### Direction 4: Negative result — `2×2` swaps alone do NOT connect the no-three-way model
**Hypothesis (expected true, i.e. a disproof of naive sufficiency)**: The set of simple
`2×2` swap moves (which suffice for every *two-way* table model) is **not** a Markov basis
for the `2×2×2` no-three-way model: there exist two equal-margin tables not connected by any
non-negative walk of `2×2` swaps.
**Test**: Exhibit an explicit pair of `0/1` tables with identical two-way margins whose
difference is `±M3`, and prove no `2×2` swap (each of which changes some two-way margin) can
be applied — a finite check.
The key insight is that every simple `2×2` swap necessarily alters a two-way margin (it lives
in the kernel of the *one-way* map, not the two-way map), so it is *illegal* in this model —
which is the whole reason the degree-4 move `M3` is forced.
**Why now**: `noThreeWay_move_preserves_margins` already characterises which moves preserve
the two-way margins; the negative result is its contrapositive made explicit.
**If true**: It rigorously demonstrates *why* higher-degree Markov moves are unavoidable —
the pedagogical punchline of the no-three-way example.
**If false**: It would mean we mis-specified the margins; a valuable consistency check on the
formalisation itself.

### Direction 5: Connectivity is an equivalence relation refining `SameMargins`
**Hypothesis**: `Connected` is an equivalence relation, and its classes are *exactly* the
fibers (`SameMargins ∧ Nonneg`); equivalently `Connected u v ↔ Nonneg u ∧ Nonneg v ∧
SameMargins u v`.
**Test**: Prove symmetry and transitivity of `Connected` (transitivity is
`ReflTransGen.trans`; symmetry needs that `Step` is symmetric on the `±M3` choice), then
combine with `noThreeWay_fiber_connected` for the `←` and margin-invariance for the `→`.
The key insight is that `Step` is *self-inverse* (`v = u + M3 ↔ u = v − M3`), so the reflexive
transitive closure is automatically symmetric — connectivity is a genuine equivalence, and
`noThreeWay_fiber_connected` says it has the coarsest possible classes.
**Why now**: We already have one direction (`noThreeWay_fiber_connected`) and margin
invariance (`noThreeWay_move_preserves_margins`); only the easy symmetry/transitivity glue
remains.
**If true**: It packages the whole model as a clean "fibers = connectivity classes" theorem,
the cleanest possible statement of the Fundamental Theorem for downstream use.
**If false**: It would expose a missing non-negativity or margin hypothesis, i.e. a flaw in
the fiber definition itself.
