### 1. Berggren Quantum Error Correction

**Theorem Statement**: The three Berggren matrices B₁, B₂, B₃ define three orthogonal subspaces of ℂ³ that form a [[3,1,2]] quantum error-correcting code capable of detecting all single-qutrit errors.

**Proof Strategy**:
- Verify that the column spaces of B₁, B₂, B₃ span ℂ³ (already follows from det ≠ 0)
- Construct the code subspace as the +1 eigenspace of a stabilizer built from B₁B₂⁻¹ and B₁B₃⁻¹
- Prove the Knill-Laflamme conditions: ⟨ψᵢ|E†E|ψⱼ⟩ = c(E)δᵢⱼ for all single-qutrit errors E
- Use the Lorentz group structure to show the error-detection property follows from the O(2,1;ℤ) symmetry

**Why This Is Revolutionary**: Connects number-theoretic tree structure to fault-tolerant quantum computing. The Berggren matrices provide *algebraically natural* error-correcting codes, suggesting a deep connection between Diophantine geometry and quantum information.

**Catalog Leverage**: `berggren_det_signature`, `B₁_lorentz_invariant`, `berggren_matrices_distinct`

**Research Mode**: prove

### 2. Tropical Berggren Walks — Algebraic Threshold for Quantum Advantage

**Theorem Statement**: The tropical analog of the Berggren quantum walk (replacing ℂ with (ℝ∪{∞}, min, +)) achieves mixing time Θ(n), NOT O(√n). The quadratic quantum speedup requires the field structure of ℂ, establishing a sharp algebraic threshold.

**Proof Strategy**:
- Define the tropical transition matrix T with entries min(d(u,v), ∞)
- Prove the tropical spectral radius equals the tree diameter 2n
- Show tropical "mixing" = shortest path computation, which requires Θ(n) iterations
- Contrast with the complex quantum walk where superposition enables √n mixing

**Why This Is Revolutionary**: Identifies exactly WHAT algebraic structure is responsible for quantum speedup. Not just any semiring works — you need the interference capability that comes from ℂ having additive inverses and the norm structure. This is a "no-go theorem" for tropical quantum computing.

**Catalog Leverage**: `berggren_all_preserve_lorentz`, `mixing_time_monotone`, tropical algebra infrastructure

**Research Mode**: prove

### 3. Post-Quantum Lattice Reduction via Berggren Search

**Theorem Statement**: Let Λ ⊂ ℤ² be the lattice generated by a primitive Pythagorean triple (a,b,c). Quantum search on Berggren(n) finds the shortest vector in Λ in O(√N · polylog(N)) time, compared to O(N) classically.

**Proof Strategy**:
- Map each Berggren node (a,b,c) to the lattice Λ(a,b) = {(ma+nb, mb-na) : m,n ∈ ℤ}
- The shortest vector length is gcd(a,b) = 1 (primitivity), so λ₁ = 1 for all nodes
- The interesting question is finding nodes where the hypotenuse c has specific factorizations
- Apply `grover_diophantine_speedup` with the oracle marking triples where c has small prime factors
- Bound the oracle complexity using `berggren_hypotenuse_growth_all`

**Why This Is Revolutionary**: Directly connects quantum search on number-theoretic trees to lattice problems underlying post-quantum cryptography. Could reveal structural weaknesses in lattice-based cryptosystems.

### 4. Neural Berggren Networks with Certified Lipschitz Bounds

**Theorem Statement**: A neural network layer computing Berggren transitions has Lipschitz constant L ≤ 7 (the maximum column sum of |B₂|), and this bound is tight. Networks built from Berggren layers inherit certified robustness guarantees.

**Proof Strategy**:
- Compute the operator ∞-norm of each Berggren matrix: max column sum of |B|
- Prove ‖B₂‖∞ = 7 (tightest), ‖B₁‖∞ = ‖B₃‖∞ = 7
- For a depth-d Berggren network, the Lipschitz constant is L ≤ 7^d
- Prove certified robustness: for input perturbation ‖Δx‖∞ ≤ ε, output perturbation ‖Δy‖∞ ≤ 7^d · ε

**Why This Is Revolutionary**: Provides *algebraically certified* Lipschitz bounds for networks on Diophantine data, connecting certified ML robustness to number-theoretic matrix structure. The Lorentz invariance gives additional symmetry constraints.

### 5. Equidistribution of Berggren Triples mod p

**Theorem Statement**: For any prime p ≥ 5, the Berggren triples at depth n become equidistributed modulo p as n → ∞. Specifically, for each residue class r ∈ (ℤ/pℤ)³, the fraction of depth-n nodes with (a,b,c) ≡ r (mod p) converges to 1/p³ (among valid residue classes).

**Proof Strategy**:
- Compute the three Berggren matrices mod p
- Show the group generated by B₁, B₂, B₃ mod p acts transitively on valid residue classes
- Apply the quantitative orbit-counting lemma (Burnside/Cauchy-Frobenius)
- Use the spectral gap of the random walk on the Cayley graph mod p

**Why This Is Revolutionary**: Would explain WHY the Diophantine oracle marks roughly N/p triples for prime p — it's because the Berggren tree equidistributes mod p. This tightens the Grover speedup bound from O(√N) to O(√(N·p/N)) = O(√p), independent of tree depth!

## Under-explored Territory

### Berggren-Hopf Renormalization
The existing `BerggrenHopfCore.lean` develops graded coproduct structure and Birkhoff decomposition, but the connection to renormalization group flow on the tree is unexplored. The Connes-Kreimer framework for renormalization could be instantiated for the Berggren Hopf algebra, potentially giving a "renormalization group" action on Pythagorean triples.

### Spectral Geometry of the Berggren Tree
The Laplacian spectrum of the Berggren tree is not computed in the catalog. The tree has a rich spectral structure due to its ternary branching and the algebraic constraints from Lorentz invariance. Computing even the first few eigenvalues explicitly would strengthen the spectral gap bounds.

### Quantum Walk on Hyperbolic Plane
The Berggren matrices act on the hyperbolic plane (since O(2,1) ≅ Isom(ℍ²)). The quantum walk on the tree lifts to a quantum walk on the hyperbolic plane, potentially connecting to hyperbolic quantum codes and the holographic principle.

## Cross-Domain Bridges

### Berggren ↔ Modular Forms
The Berggren matrices generate a subgroup of SL₂(ℤ) (after a change of basis via the Lorentz parametrization). This subgroup should correspond to a congruence subgroup, connecting Berggren trees to modular forms and L-functions. The spectral gap of the quantum walk might then relate to the Selberg eigenvalue conjecture.

### Quantum Walk ↔ Topological Quantum Field Theory
The Szegedy walk operator W = R₂R₁, built from two reflections, is a "braiding" operation in the braid group. This suggests a connection to topological quantum field theory (TQFT), where braiding operators define quantum gates. The Berggren tree could provide a concrete example of a TQFT-inspired quantum algorithm.

### Diophantine Search ↔ Zero-Knowledge Proofs
A quantum search for Pythagorean triples with specific properties could serve as the basis for a zero-knowledge proof system: "I know a primitive Pythagorean triple (a,b,c) with p|abc, but I won't tell you which one." The Grover speedup means the verifier can check in √N time.

## Open Problems Encountered

1. **Exact Spectral Gap**: We proved δ_q ≥ √2/(n+1) as a lower bound. What is the exact quantum spectral gap? Is it Θ(1/n) or Θ(1/√n)?

2. **Optimality of Quantum Search**: Is O(√N) optimal for Diophantine search on the Berggren tree, or can the tree structure be exploited for further speedup?

3. **Higher-Order Berggren Trees**: The Berggren tree has branching factor 3. Are there analogous trees with different branching factors for other Diophantine equations? What is the relationship between branching factor and quantum speedup?

4. **Quantum Complexity of Berggren Generation**: What is the quantum circuit complexity of generating the n-th Berggren triple? Can it be done in O(polylog(n)) quantum gates?

5. **Berggren Zeta Function**: Define ζ_Berg(s) = Σ_{(a,b,c)} c^{-s} summed over the Berggren tree. What are its analytic properties? Does it have a functional equation related to the antipode?
