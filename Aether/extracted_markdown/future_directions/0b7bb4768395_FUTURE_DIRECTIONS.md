# Future Directions: Resolvent Geometry and the Curvature of Negative Dependence

## Synthesis

The resolvent geometry framework established in this work reveals that conditional negative semidefiniteness (CondNSD) of logarithmic Hessians is the hidden geometric invariant underlying negative dependence across multiple mathematical domains. The formal verification of the negative outer product sum theorem, the Laplacian energy identity, and the product-of-linear-forms result creates a stable foundation for five research directions that together would constitute a new field at the intersection of probabilistic combinatorics, matrix analysis, spectral graph theory, and polynomial geometry.

The unifying thread is the vision that **curvature of generating polynomials is the right language for negative dependence**. The directions below systematically extend this vision: Direction 1 attacks the central conjecture (Lorentzian → CondNSD), Direction 2 extracts algorithmic consequences, Direction 3 bridges to information geometry and statistical physics, Direction 4 pursues tropical and valuated extensions, and Direction 5 aims at the grand challenge of discrete Ricci curvature for matroids.

## Direction 1: The Lorentzian CondNSD Conjecture

**Conjecture:** For every homogeneous multilinear polynomial p with nonneg coefficients that is Lorentzian (Brändén–Huh), and with p(1) > 0, the logarithmic Hessian ∂²(log p)|_{x=1} is conditionally negative semidefinite.

**Test:** Systematically verify on:
- Basis generating polynomials of all matroids on ≤ 8 elements (using matroid enumeration databases).
- Random Lorentzian polynomials generated by the Brändén–Huh closure operations (products, convolutions, projections).
- Volume polynomials of polytopes (which are Lorentzian by the Hodge–Riemann relations).
A single positive eigenvalue on the zero-sum subspace disproves the conjecture.

**Impact:** If true, this would establish CondNSD as the geometric characterization of the Lorentzian class at the generating function level, providing a new spectral certificate for negative dependence that is checkable in O(n³) time. If false, the counterexample would reveal the precise boundary between geometric and non-geometric negative dependence.

**Catalog References:** `Catalog/Pythagorean/ResolventGeometry.lean` (CondNegSemidef definition, negSemidefForm_neg_outer_sum, productLogHessian_condNegSemidef), `Catalog/Speculative/AutoResearch/DPPLorentzian.lean` (IsDPPLorentzian definition, dpp_partition_function_lorentzian conjecture).

**Proof Strategy:** For degree-2 Lorentzian polynomials, the Hessian IS the coefficient matrix (up to normalization), and Lorentzianity directly implies at most one positive eigenvalue. The log-Hessian at 1 subtracts a rank-1 positive term (the outer product of marginals), which should push the spectrum negative on zero-sum vectors. For higher degrees, use induction on degree via the derivative operator: if ∂_e p is Lorentzian for all e, the Hessian of log p decomposes into a sum of lower-degree Hessians plus correction terms. The key lemma would be: "CondNSD is preserved under the Brändén–Huh closure operations."

**Domain Bridges:** Algebraic geometry (Hodge–Riemann relations for polytope volumes), combinatorial optimization (matroid intersection), probability theory (strong Rayleigh measures).

**Lineage:** Extends `productLogHessian_condNegSemidef` from the product-of-linear-forms case to the full Lorentzian closure.

**Ambition:** Grand challenge — would unify the algebraic (Lorentzian) and geometric (curvature) theories of negative dependence.

The key insight is that the CondNSD property should be preserved under the three Lorentzian closure operations (multiplication by nonneg scalars, partial differentiation, and certain linear changes of variables), making it an inductive property of the Lorentzian class.

Why now? The formal infrastructure for CondNSD verification (definitions, closure properties, certificate framework) is now in place, and the computational pipeline can test thousands of instances systematically. The Brändén–Huh theory provides the inductive structure needed for the proof.

## Direction 2: Spectral Certificates for Algorithmic Negative Dependence

**Conjecture:** For every matroid M on ground set [n] with rank r, the CondNSD eigenvalues of the log-Hessian of the basis generating polynomial B_M are bounded below by a function of the matroid's connectivity and girth, yielding explicit concentration inequalities for matroid-based sampling algorithms.

**Test:** Compute CondNSD eigenvalues for all connected matroids on ≤ 9 elements. Correlate the spectral gap (smallest nonzero eigenvalue magnitude on zero-sum subspace) with known matroid parameters: connectivity, girth, circumference, number of bases. Fit regression models to identify the controlling parameters.

**Impact:** Would provide the first spectral approach to negative dependence concentration inequalities, potentially improving sampling bounds for matroid-constrained optimization by a factor of O(n) over existing methods based on bounded differences.

**Catalog References:** `Catalog/Pythagorean/ResolventGeometry.lean` (CondNegSemidef, negLaplacian_negSemidefForm, condNegSemidef_of_certificate), `Catalog/Pythagorean/SparseLorentzianCertificates.lean` (BasisFamily, indepCount bounds).

**Proof Strategy:** For graphic matroids, the log-Hessian relates to the Kirchhoff Laplacian via matrix-tree theorem, and spectral gap bounds follow from Cheeger-type inequalities. For general matroids, use the exchange graph (whose edges connect bases differing by a single exchange) and relate its Laplacian spectrum to the polynomial Hessian. The Laplacian certificate framework from `condNegSemidef_of_certificate` provides the formal verification tool.

**Domain Bridges:** Spectral graph theory (Cheeger inequality, effective resistance), combinatorial optimization (matroid intersection, sampling), machine learning (DPP-based diverse subset selection).

**Lineage:** Builds on `negLaplacian_negSemidefForm` (Laplacian → NSD) and the certificate framework.

**Ambition:** Solid extension — connects existing Catalog theorems to algorithmic applications with concrete performance bounds.

The key insight is that the CondNSD eigenvalues of the log-Hessian provide a spectral gap for the "repulsion energy" of the measure, which should translate directly into concentration bounds via a Poincaré-type inequality on the zero-sum subspace.

Why now? The Laplacian energy identity is now formally verified, providing the bridge from polynomial Hessians to graph spectra. Matroid enumeration databases make systematic computation feasible. The connection between spectral gaps and concentration inequalities is well-established in probability theory, so the missing piece was the spectral interpretation of polynomial Hessians — which this work provides.

## Direction 3: Information Geometry of Repulsive Measures

**Conjecture:** The log-Hessian ∂²(log p)|_{x=1} of a generating polynomial, restricted to the zero-sum subspace, defines a Riemannian metric (the "repulsion metric") on the space of perturbation directions. For DPPs, this metric is isometric to the effective resistance metric of the resolvent graph with edge weights L_{ij}².

**Test:** For DPPs on ≤ 10 elements, compute the effective resistance matrix from the resolvent L and compare with the pseudoinverse of the log-Hessian restricted to zero-sum vectors. Verify the isometry numerically. For products of linear forms, compute the repulsion metric and compare with the Fisher information metric of the associated exponential family.

**Impact:** Would establish a precise dictionary between negative dependence and information geometry, enabling the transfer of powerful tools (Cramér-Rao bounds, natural gradient methods, geodesic convexity) to combinatorial probability. Could lead to new entropy inequalities for repulsive measures.

**Catalog References:** `Catalog/Pythagorean/ResolventGeometry.lean` (dppResolventHessian, condNegSemidef_of_hadamard_sq), `Catalog/Speculative/AutoResearch/DPPLorentzian.lean` (dppPartitionFunction, dpp_partitionFunction_eval_ones).

**Proof Strategy:** For DPPs, use the resolvent formula H_{ij} = -L_{ij}² to compute the pseudoinverse of H on the zero-sum subspace explicitly in terms of L. The effective resistance R_{ij} = (e_i - e_j)^T L^+ (e_i - e_j) where L^+ is the pseudoinverse. Show that the two metrics agree up to a constant factor by expressing both in terms of the spectral decomposition of L.

**Domain Bridges:** Information geometry (Fisher metric, natural gradient), statistical physics (susceptibility matrices, fluctuation-dissipation relations), network science (effective resistance, commute times).

**Lineage:** Extends `dppResolventHessian_symm` and the resolvent formula to a full geometric structure.

**Ambition:** Grand challenge — would create a new interface between information geometry and combinatorial probability.

The key insight is that the log-Hessian is the **susceptibility matrix** of the generating measure (in statistical physics language), and CondNSD is the statement that the system has **repulsive response**: perturbing one variable's weight decreases others' inclusion probabilities. The effective resistance metric quantifies this repulsion geometrically.

Why now? The resolvent formula H = -L² is now formally verified, providing the explicit connection between Hessian geometry and resolvent structure. Information geometry has matured as a field with powerful tools (Amari, Ay et al.), but has not been systematically applied to discrete repulsive measures. The formal infrastructure makes it possible to verify the geometric identities rigorously.

## Direction 4: Tropical Resolvent Geometry

**Conjecture:** The tropical analogue of the log-Hessian — defined via the second-order terms in the tropical expansion of the generating polynomial at the all-ones point — satisfies a tropical analogue of CondNSD for Lorentzian polynomials, expressible as a condition on the regular subdivision of the Newton polytope.

**Test:** For basis generating polynomials of uniform matroids U(r,n) and graphic matroids of small graphs, compute the tropical Hessian and check the tropical CondNSD condition. Compare with the regular matroid polytope subdivisions from the TOPCOM software.

**Impact:** Would extend resolvent geometry to the tropical world, connecting the curvature theory to the rapidly developing field of tropical geometry and valuated matroids. Could provide new combinatorial certificates for negative dependence that are purely polyhedral (no eigenvalue computation needed).

**Catalog References:** `Catalog/Pythagorean/ResolventGeometry.lean` (CondNegSemidef framework), `Catalog/Pythagorean/SparseLorentzianCertificates.lean` (matroid basis families).

**Proof Strategy:** Define tropical CondNSD as: for all v with ∑ vᵢ = 0, the tropical quadratic form max_{ij}(vᵢ + H_{ij} + vⱼ) is achieved at a pair (i,j) with H_{ij} ≤ 0. For Lorentzian polynomials, the tropical Hessian should be related to the Bergman fan of the associated matroid. Use the matroid polytope machinery to verify the tropical condition.

**Domain Bridges:** Tropical geometry (Newton polytopes, regular subdivisions), valuated matroid theory (Dress–Wenzel), optimization (tropical linear programming).

**Lineage:** Extends the curvature framework from ℝ to the tropical semiring.

**Ambition:** Solid extension with grand challenge elements — tropical geometry has its own technical challenges but the conceptual transfer is natural.

The key insight is that the Hessian geometry should "tropicalize" cleanly because the CondNSD condition is fundamentally about sign patterns (nonpositivity), which have natural tropical analogues. The tropical Hessian replaces sums with maxima and products with sums, but the geometric content — downward curvature on mass-preserving directions — should persist.

Why now? Tropical geometry has undergone rapid development (Maclagan–Sturmfels, Joswig et al.), and the connection to Lorentzian polynomials via the Brändén–Huh theory provides the bridge. The formal CondNSD framework is now available for comparison.

## Direction 5: Discrete Ricci Curvature for Matroids

**Conjecture:** For every matroid M, the basis exchange graph G_M (whose vertices are bases and edges connect bases differing by a single exchange) admits a notion of discrete Ricci curvature — defined via the CondNSD Hessian of the basis generating polynomial — that is nonneg and bounded below by a function of the matroid's connectivity.

**Test:** Compute the proposed Ricci curvature for all matroids on ≤ 8 elements. Verify nonnegativity. Correlate with the Ollivier-Ricci curvature of the exchange graph. Check whether the Ricci bound implies diameter bounds consistent with the matroid polytope structure.

**Impact:** Would create a new bridge between discrete differential geometry and matroid theory, potentially yielding Bonnet-Myers-type diameter bounds for matroid polytopes and new proofs of matroid concentration inequalities via curvature-dimension conditions.

**Catalog References:** `Catalog/Pythagorean/ResolventGeometry.lean` (full theorem package), `Catalog/Pythagorean/SparseLorentzianCertificates.lean` (BasisFamily, matroid basis structure).

**Proof Strategy:** Define the Ricci curvature of edge (B₁, B₂) in the exchange graph as the infimum of the CondNSD quadratic form H(v,v) over unit zero-sum vectors v supported on the symmetric difference B₁ △ B₂. This localizes the global CondNSD condition to individual edges. For uniform matroids, compute explicitly using the known Hessian structure. For general matroids, use the exchange property to bound the Ricci curvature in terms of the matroid's girth.

**Domain Bridges:** Discrete differential geometry (Ollivier-Ricci curvature, Bakry-Émery theory), geometric group theory (CAT(0) cube complexes), matroid theory (basis exchange), Riemannian geometry (Bonnet-Myers, Bishop-Gromov).

**Lineage:** Synthesizes the entire resolvent geometry framework with discrete curvature theory.

**Ambition:** Grand challenge — would establish an entirely new field of "matroid Riemannian geometry."

The key insight is that CondNSD of the global Hessian should localize to nonneg Ricci curvature on the exchange graph, because the zero-sum condition corresponds to the "mass transport" interpretation of Ollivier-Ricci curvature. The formal framework provides the tools to make this localization precise.

Why now? Discrete Ricci curvature (Ollivier, Lin-Lu-Yau, Bakry-Émery) has emerged as a powerful tool in graph theory and network science. The missing ingredient was a curvature notion intrinsic to the polynomial/matroid structure rather than imported from the graph. The CondNSD Hessian provides exactly this intrinsic curvature, and the formal verification ensures the foundational results are solid.
