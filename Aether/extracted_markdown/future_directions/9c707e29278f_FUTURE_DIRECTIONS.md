# Future Directions: Hyperbolic Number Theory

## Synthesis

This research cycle established the formal foundations of arithmetic on the Poincaré disk: Möbius transformations as the fundamental operations, the key norm-squared identity, disk preservation, the inverse theorem, and the conformal transformation law. These results provide a rigorous base for developing hyperbolic analogs of classical number theory.

The most promising cross-domain connection is the **bridge between hyperbolic lattices and spectral theory**. The counting function N(R) for lattice points is governed by the eigenvalues of the hyperbolic Laplacian, connecting discrete arithmetic (how many "hyperbolic integers" exist up to a given size?) to continuous analysis (what are the resonances of the Laplacian?). This is precisely the mechanism behind the Selberg trace formula, and formalizing it would connect the `Geometry.HyperbolicNumberTheory` module to the spectral theory in `EML.ModularForms` and the zeta function framework in `Algebra.Foundations`.

The second major connection is to **tropical geometry**. The map z ↦ -log(1 - |z|²) sends the Poincaré disk to ℝ≥0 and transforms the hyperbolic metric into something resembling a tropical metric. Lattice points under this map become a tropical lattice, potentially connecting `Geometry.HyperbolicNumberTheory` to `Tropical.Geometry.Hypersurface`. This bridge would be particularly powerful because tropical methods are computationally tractable, offering a way to attack number-theoretic questions via combinatorial geometry.

The highest breakthrough potential lies in Direction 1 (formalizing the Selberg trace formula for finite lattices), because it would provide a complete formal proof connecting lattice point counting to spectral data — a result that, even in simplified form, would be novel in the formal mathematics community.

### Direction 1: Selberg Trace Formula for Finite Hyperbolic Lattices

**Conjecture**: For a hyperbolic lattice generated by k Möbius transformations with parameters g₁, ..., gₖ ∈ 𝔻, the counting function N(R) of orbit points within hyperbolic distance R of the origin satisfies:

$$N(R) = \frac{\text{Area}(F)}{4\pi} \cdot e^R + \sum_{j} h(r_j) + O(R \cdot e^{R/2})$$

where F is the fundamental domain, r_j are the spectral parameters of the Laplacian on the quotient surface, and h is the Harish-Chandra/Selberg transform of the characteristic function of the hyperbolic ball of radius R.

**Test**: For a lattice with two generators at |g| = 0.5, compute N(R_hyp) for R_hyp = 2·arctanh(R_eucl) at R_eucl = 0.5, 0.7, 0.9, 0.95, 0.99. Compare the leading term Area(F)·e^R/(4π) against the actual count. The error should be bounded by O(R·e^{R/2}).

**Impact**: A formally verified Selberg trace formula, even for the simplified finite case, would be the first of its kind. It would connect our `mobius_preserves_disk` and `conformal_factor_transform` theorems to spectral theory, establishing a complete formal pipeline from algebra to analysis.

**Catalog References**: `Geometry/HyperbolicNumberTheory.lean` (this cycle), `EML/ModularForms.lean` (S_gen, T_sq modular group generators), `Algebra/Foundations.lean` (critical_line_implies_unit_disk)

**Proof Strategy**:
1. Define the heat kernel K_t(z, w) on the Poincaré disk using the existing `poincareCF` from `Geometry/HyperbolicPacking/Defs.lean`.
2. Prove the Poisson summation formula for the lattice orbit.
3. Show that the geometric side (orbital integrals) reduces to sums over conjugacy classes.
4. Identify the spectral side with eigenvalues of the Laplacian.
5. Take the limit t → 0 to recover the sharp counting function.

Key lemmas needed: `hyperbolic_heat_kernel_pos`, `heat_kernel_decay`, `orbital_integral_convergence`, `spectral_decomposition_finite`.

**Domain Bridges**: Geometry <-> EML, NumberTheory <-> SpectralTheory

**Lineage**: Builds on `mobius_norm_sq_identity`, `conformal_factor_transform`, and `hypCountingFn_mono` from this cycle.

**Ambition**: grand_challenge

### Direction 2: Tropical-Hyperbolic Correspondence

**Conjecture**: The map Φ: 𝔻 → ℝ≥0 defined by Φ(z) = -log(1 - |z|²) is a semi-homomorphism from hyperbolic arithmetic to tropical arithmetic. Specifically, for a, z ∈ 𝔻:

$$\Phi(\varphi_a(z)) = \Phi(z) + \Phi(a) - \Phi(D(a,z))$$

where D(a,z) = 1 - ā·z is the Möbius denominator, and all operations on the right are in the usual (not tropical) sense. This becomes a tropical homomorphism in the limit |z| → 1.

**Test**: Compute Φ(φ_a(z)), Φ(z) + Φ(a) - Φ(D(a,z)) for 100 random pairs (a,z) in the disk. Verify equality to machine precision. Then check that as |z| → 1, the "correction term" Φ(D(a,z)) → 0 (in a tropical sense).

**Impact**: If the tropical-hyperbolic correspondence holds, it would provide a valuation-theoretic framework for hyperbolic number theory. Tropical geometry methods (polyhedral combinatorics, Berkovich spaces) could then be applied to study the distribution of hyperbolic primes.

**Catalog References**: `Tropical/Geometry/Hypersurface.lean`, `Geometry/HyperbolicNumberTheory.lean`, `EML/EMLv17Core.lean` (eml = exp - log connection)

**Proof Strategy**:
1. Define Φ using `Real.log` and `Complex.normSq` from Mathlib.
2. Use `conformal_factor_transform` to expand Φ(φ_a(z)).
3. Apply logarithm properties to factor the expression.
4. Show the correction term is bounded by a function of the pseudohyperbolic distance.
5. Take limits using the squeeze theorem.

**Domain Bridges**: Geometry <-> Tropical, NumberTheory <-> AlgebraicGeometry

**Lineage**: Builds on `conformal_factor_transform` and `pseudoHypDist_lt_one` from this cycle, connects to tropical lattice theory in the catalog.

**Ambition**: extension

### Direction 3: Hyperbolic Prime Classification

**Conjecture**: In a hyperbolic lattice generated by g₁, ..., gₖ, define a lattice point z to be a **hyperbolic prime** if it cannot be expressed as φ_{gᵢ}(w) for any generator gᵢ and any other lattice point w at strictly smaller hyperbolic distance from the origin. Then:

(a) The number of hyperbolic primes at depth exactly d is 2k·(2k-1)^{d-1} (the growth rate of a free group on k generators).

(b) The "hyperbolic prime counting function" π_H(R) = #{primes p : d_H(0,p) ≤ R} satisfies π_H(R) ~ C·e^R/R for large R.

**Test**: For k=2 generators at |g| = 0.5, enumerate all lattice points at depths 1-8. Count the number that are "prime" by the above definition. Verify that the counts match 2k·(2k-1)^{d-1} = 4, 12, 36, 108, 324, 972, 2916, 8748.

**Impact**: This would give a precise definition of "hyperbolic primes" and establish the hyperbolic analog of the prime number theorem. The connection to free group growth rates would link hyperbolic number theory to geometric group theory.

**Catalog References**: `Geometry/HyperbolicNumberTheory.lean` (HyperbolicLattice, hypCountingFn), `Algebra/Foundations.lean` (li_positivity_from_critical_line)

**Proof Strategy**:
1. Define `isHyperbolicPrime` predicate on lattice points.
2. Show that non-backtracking words in the generators biject with hyperbolic primes (this is the key insight from Bass-Serre theory).
3. Count non-backtracking words: exactly 2k·(2k-1)^{d-1} at depth d.
4. Convert depth to hyperbolic distance using the conformal factor.
5. Sum the geometric series to get the asymptotic.

**Domain Bridges**: Geometry <-> Algebra, NumberTheory <-> GroupTheory

**Lineage**: Builds on `HyperbolicLattice`, `mobius_inverse`, and `lattice_orbit_in_disk` from this cycle.

**Ambition**: extension

### Direction 4: Hyperbolic Zeta Function Analytic Continuation

**Conjecture**: The hyperbolic zeta function ζ_H(s) = Σ_{z ∈ L, z≠0} |z|^{-2s}, initially defined for Re(s) > 1 (where the sum converges), has a meromorphic continuation to all of ℂ with a simple pole at s = 1. The residue at s = 1 equals the hyperbolic area of the fundamental domain divided by 4π.

**Test**: For a lattice with ~10,000 points, compute ζ_H(s) at s = 1 + ε for ε = 0.1, 0.01, 0.001 and verify that ε·ζ_H(1+ε) converges to Area(F)/(4π). Also compute ζ_H(s) at s = 0.5 + it for t = 1, 2, ..., 20 and look for approximate zeros.

**Impact**: Establishing the analytic continuation of ζ_H would be a major step toward a hyperbolic Riemann hypothesis. Even the pole structure alone would yield information about the growth rate of lattice points.

**Catalog References**: `Geometry/HyperbolicNumberTheory.lean` (hyperbolicZetaPartial, hyperbolicZetaPartial_nonneg), `Algebra/Foundations.lean` (critical_line_implies_unit_disk)

**Proof Strategy**:
1. Express ζ_H(s) as a Mellin transform of the lattice theta function θ_L(t) = Σ e^{-t|z|²}.
2. Use the Poisson summation formula on the lattice to establish a functional equation for θ_L.
3. Separate the Mellin integral at t = 1 to get the analytic continuation.
4. Identify the pole from the constant term in the Poisson summation.

This approach mirrors Riemann's original proof for ζ(s) and Hecke's generalization to L-functions.

**Domain Bridges**: NumberTheory <-> Analysis, Geometry <-> ComplexAnalysis

**Lineage**: Builds on `hyperbolicZetaPartial_nonneg` from this cycle, connects to the Selberg zeta function literature.

**Ambition**: grand_challenge

### Direction 5: Curvature-Dependent Factorization

**Conjecture**: For a family of "curved integers" ℤ_κ parameterized by curvature κ ∈ (-∞, 0), defined as lattices in the hyperbolic plane of curvature κ, the unique factorization property depends on κ. Specifically:

(a) For κ = -1 (standard hyperbolic plane) with the modular group, ℤ_{-1} has unique factorization into hyperbolic primes.

(b) For κ → 0 (flat limit), ℤ_κ recovers the ordinary integers with the fundamental theorem of arithmetic.

(c) There exist values of κ and choices of lattice for which unique factorization fails.

**Test**: For κ = -1, -4, -9, generate lattices with the same abstract group structure but different curvatures (by rescaling generators). Check whether any lattice point has two distinct "prime factorizations" (expressions as a product of primes, where product = composition of Möbius maps).

**Impact**: A curvature-dependent factorization theorem would be entirely new — it would show that the fundamental theorem of arithmetic is a *flat-space phenomenon* that can break in curved space, analogous to how unique factorization fails in certain number rings.

**Catalog References**: `Geometry/HyperbolicNumberTheory.lean` (mobiusMap, HyperbolicLattice), `Cryptography/BerggrenDiophantineLattice.lean` (lorentzForm), `Geometry/HyperbolicPacking/Defs.lean` (poincareCF, radialDistortion)

**Proof Strategy**:
1. Formalize "curvature-κ Poincaré disk" by rescaling the conformal factor: λ_κ(z) = 2/(1 + κ|z|²).
2. Define factorization as word decomposition in the free group on generators.
3. Show that for free groups, factorization is always unique (this is trivial from group theory).
4. Show that for groups with relations (e.g., PSL(2,ℤ) has S² = 1, (ST)³ = 1), relations create non-unique factorizations.
5. Connect the number of relations to the curvature via the Gauss-Bonnet theorem: Area(F) = 2π(2g - 2 + n), linking curvature to the genus of the quotient surface.

**Domain Bridges**: Geometry <-> Algebra, NumberTheory <-> Topology

**Lineage**: Builds on `mobius_inverse` and `HyperbolicLattice` from this cycle, extends to curvature-dependent phenomena.

**Ambition**: extension
