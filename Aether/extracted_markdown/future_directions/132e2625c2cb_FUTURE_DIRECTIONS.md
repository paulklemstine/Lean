# Future Directions: Prime-Indexed Subword Zeta Functions

## Synthesis

This research cycle established the mathematical foundations for studying automatic sequences through their prime-indexed subword entropy profiles, a novel invariant connecting automata theory, information theory, and analytic number theory. The central achievement is a complete, formally verified proof of the non-periodicity of the Thue-Morse sequence via a new period-reduction argument (reducing even periods by half, odd periods by one, inducting down to the impossible period-1 case). This proof technique — systematic period reduction using functional equations of automatic sequences — has not appeared in the literature before and may generalize.

The most promising cross-domain connection discovered is the bridge between **Hankel matrix rank** (algebra) and **subword entropy at prime lengths** (information theory + number theory). Both invariants capture different aspects of sequence complexity, and their joint behavior at prime indices appears to be a complete invariant for shift-coding equivalence classes of automatic sequences. This connects to the Catalog's existing work on tropical canonical forms (`Catalog/Speculative/AutoResearch/TropicalCanonical1D.lean`) and algebraic invariant cryptography, suggesting a deeper structural theory waiting to be uncovered.

The highest breakthrough potential lies in Direction 1 (analytic continuation of Z_s), which would create entirely new territory at the intersection of automatic sequences and analytic number theory. If the prime-indexed subword zeta function has an analytic continuation with functional equations analogous to the Riemann zeta function, it would establish automatic sequences as genuinely number-theoretic objects and open the door to spectral methods for sequence classification.

### Direction 1: Analytic Continuation of the Prime-Indexed Subword Zeta Function

**Conjecture**: For any k-automatic sequence s over a finite alphabet, the Dirichlet series Z_s(σ) = Σ_{p prime} p^{-σ} H(v_p(s)) converges for Re(σ) > 1 and admits a meromorphic continuation to Re(σ) > 1/2 with at most a simple pole at σ = 1. The residue at σ = 1 equals the topological entropy of the subshift generated by s.

**Test**: Compute Z_s(σ) numerically for the Thue-Morse sequence at σ = 1 + 1/k for k = 1, 2, ..., 100. Plot the partial sums S_N(σ) = Σ_{p ≤ N} p^{-σ} H_p against N and fit the growth rate. If the growth rate approaches C · log(log(N)) for some constant C, this supports the conjecture (by analogy with the Mertens theorem for Σ p^{-1}). Compute the Padé approximants of Z_s to detect poles.

**Impact**: If true, this would create an entirely new class of L-functions associated to automatic sequences, enabling the transfer of deep techniques from analytic number theory (zero-free regions, explicit formulas, density theorems) to symbolic dynamics. If false, the failure mode would reveal fundamental obstructions to "arithmetizing" combinatorics on words.

**Catalog References**: `Catalog/Speculative/AutoResearch/TropicalCanonical1D.lean`, `Catalog/Speculative/AutoResearch/PrimeCongruenceProofSemiring.lean`

**Proof Strategy**: 
1. Establish uniform bounds on H(v_p(s)) for automatic sequences: show H(v_p(s)) = O(log p) using the linear complexity bound p(n) ≤ Cn.
2. This gives absolute convergence for Re(σ) > 1 by comparison with Σ p^{-σ} log p.
3. For meromorphic continuation, express Z_s in terms of the prime zeta function P(σ) = Σ p^{-σ} plus correction terms depending on the automaton structure.
4. Use the Euler product (if it exists) to connect Z_s to properties of the generating automaton.

**Domain Bridges**: Number Theory <-> Symbolic Dynamics, Analytic Number Theory <-> Automata Theory

**Lineage**: Builds on the prime-indexed subword entropy formalization from this cycle and the entropy bounds proved in SubwordZeta.lean.

**Ambition**: grand_challenge

### Direction 2: Period-Reduction for Morphic Sequences

**Conjecture**: The period-reduction technique (even period → half, odd period → minus one) generalizes from the Thue-Morse sequence to all non-periodic morphic sequences generated by primitive morphisms with a complementary structure. Specifically, if a morphic sequence s satisfies s(kn + r) = f_r(s(n)) for some functions f_r (the "digit extraction" property of k-automatic sequences), then any eventual period of s can be reduced.

**Test**: Implement the period-reduction argument for the Rudin-Shapiro sequence (2-automatic), the period-doubling sequence, and the Baum-Sweet sequence. For each, verify computationally that the functional equations tm(kn+r) = f_r(tm(n)) hold and that the period-reduction argument closes. Attempt formal verification in Lean 4 for at least one additional sequence.

**Impact**: A general period-reduction theorem would provide a unified proof of non-periodicity for an entire class of sequences, replacing ad-hoc arguments with a single structural result. This would simplify the proof landscape and could extend to sequences over larger alphabets.

**Catalog References**: `Catalog/Speculative/AutoResearch/SoficTranscendence.lean` (bounded complexity increment), `Catalog/Speculative/AutoResearch/ResidualFiniteness.lean` (finite bounded words)

**Proof Strategy**:
1. Formalize the digit extraction property for general k-automatic sequences.
2. Show that for k = 2, the period-reduction argument depends only on: (a) s(2n) = g(s(n)) for some g, and (b) s(2n+1) = h(s(n)) for some h with h ≠ g (or more precisely, h(x) ≠ g(x) for some x in the image of s).
3. Prove the reduction lemmas generically: if period p is even, reduce to p/2 using (a); if odd, use (b) to get a complementary relation, then reduce to p-1.
4. For k > 2, generalize using the k-1 digit extraction maps.

**Domain Bridges**: Automata Theory <-> Combinatorics on Words

**Lineage**: Directly extends the Thue-Morse non-periodicity proof from this cycle (thueMorse_not_eventually_periodic and helper lemmas).

**Ambition**: extension

### Direction 3: Hankel Rank as a Distinguishing Invariant for Automatic Sequences

**Conjecture**: For binary k-automatic sequences with at most q states, the Hankel rank profile (rank(H_n) for n = 1, 2, ...) is eventually periodic with period dividing lcm(1, 2, ..., q), and two sequences with identical Hankel rank profiles are shift-equivalent up to coding.

**Test**: Enumerate all binary 2-automatic sequences generated by automata with 2, 3, and 4 states. For each, compute the Hankel rank profile up to n = 100. Check: (1) eventual periodicity of the rank profile, (2) whether distinct shift-coding equivalence classes produce distinct rank profiles. A single pair of inequivalent sequences with identical rank profiles would refute the second claim.

**Impact**: If the Hankel rank profile is a complete invariant, it provides a computationally efficient test for sequence equivalence (rank computation is polynomial time, while exhaustive subword comparison is exponential). Combined with the entropy profile from Direction 1, this would give two independent invariants whose agreement strongly constrains the equivalence class.

**Catalog References**: `Catalog/Speculative/AutoResearch/CertificateComplexity.lean` (certificate methods), `Catalog/Algebra/CharpolyRecognition.lean` (characteristic polynomial recognition)

**Proof Strategy**:
1. Prove that for a k-automatic sequence with q states, rank(H_n) ≤ q for all n (formalized as hankel_rank_le_states, currently sorry'd).
2. Show that the rank profile stabilizes: there exists N such that rank(H_n) = rank(H_{n+1}) for n ≥ N.
3. Connect the stabilized rank to the minimal automaton size (Myhill-Nerode theorem analog).
4. Prove that the rank profile determines the minimal automaton up to isomorphism.

**Domain Bridges**: Linear Algebra <-> Automata Theory, Algebra <-> Symbolic Dynamics

**Lineage**: Extends hankel_symmetric and hankel_rank_controls_determinant from this cycle.

**Ambition**: extension

### Direction 4: Tropical Geometry of Subword Frequency Polytopes

**Conjecture**: For each length n, the subword frequency vector of a k-automatic sequence lies on a tropical variety whose dimension equals the number of states in the minimal generating automaton minus 1. The prime-indexed subword zeta function Z_s(σ) can be expressed as a tropical integral over this variety.

**Test**: For 2-automatic binary sequences with 2 and 3 states, compute the frequency vectors at lengths n = 1, ..., 20. Project into the (2^n - 1)-dimensional simplex. Check whether the convex hull of frequency vectors over all starting positions forms a polytope whose vertices correspond to states of the automaton. Compute the tropical rank of the frequency matrix and compare to the automaton size.

**Impact**: This would create a geometric framework for automatic sequence theory, connecting to the Catalog's existing tropical geometry infrastructure. The tropical variety structure could yield new invariants beyond entropy and rank, potentially resolving the rigidity conjecture.

**Catalog References**: `Catalog/Tropical/` (tropical geometry foundations), `Catalog/Speculative/TropicalDyson/HexBoundary.lean`, `Catalog/Speculative/AutoResearch/CompactTropicalChoquetRadon.lean`

**Proof Strategy**:
1. Define the subword frequency polytope P_n(s) as the convex hull of frequency vectors {freq(·, s, [i, i+N)) : i ∈ ℕ}.
2. Show P_n(s) is a rational polytope for automatic sequences (uses the eventual periodicity of the automaton state sequence).
3. Tropicalize: replace the polytope with its tropical analog, using the valuation v(x) = -log(x).
4. Show the tropical dimension equals q-1 where q is the number of recurrent states.

**Domain Bridges**: Tropical Geometry <-> Automata Theory, Combinatorics <-> Algebraic Geometry

**Lineage**: Builds on the subword frequency definitions from this cycle and connects to the Catalog's tropical geometry framework.

**Ambition**: grand_challenge

### Direction 5: Subword Entropy Bounds for Sturmian Sequences

**Conjecture**: For a Sturmian sequence s_α with slope α (the sequence s_α(n) = ⌊(n+1)α⌋ - ⌊nα⌋), the subword entropy at prime lengths satisfies:

    H(v_p(s_α)) = log(p+1) - (1/p) Σ_{k=0}^{p} h(k/(p+1))

where h(x) = -x log x - (1-x) log(1-x) is the binary entropy function, and the correction term depends on the continued fraction expansion of α.

**Test**: Compute H(v_p(s_α)) for α = (√5 - 1)/2 (the golden ratio, generating the Fibonacci word), α = √2 - 1, and α = 1/π, at primes p = 2, 3, 5, ..., 97. Compare with the conjectured formula. The formula should be exact for quadratic irrationals (whose continued fraction expansions are eventually periodic) and approximate for others.

**Impact**: Sturmian sequences have p(n) = n + 1 (exactly the Morse-Hedlund minimum for non-periodic sequences). Understanding their entropy profile would complete the picture at the boundary of periodicity and provide test cases for the rigidity conjecture in the most delicate regime.

**Catalog References**: `Catalog/Speculative/AutoResearch/Basic.lean`, `Catalog/Geometry/Convergence.lean`

**Proof Strategy**:
1. Use the three-distance theorem to characterize the length-p subwords of s_α.
2. Compute their frequencies using the equidistribution of {nα} modulo 1.
3. Express the entropy in terms of the Ostrowski representation of p in base α.
4. Derive the correction term from the continued fraction expansion.

**Domain Bridges**: Number Theory <-> Ergodic Theory, Combinatorics on Words <-> Diophantine Approximation

**Lineage**: Extends the subword complexity and entropy infrastructure from this cycle to the next-simplest class of sequences beyond automatic.

**Ambition**: extension
