# Future Directions: Algebraic K-Theory of Neural Architectures

## Breakthrough Opportunities (ranked by impact)

### 1. Full Whitehead Group Formalization and SLₙ = Eₙ

**Theorem Statement:** For R a Euclidean domain and n ≥ 3, SLₙ(R) = Eₙ(R) — every matrix with determinant 1 is a product of elementary transvections.

**Proof Strategy:**
- **Approach A:** Induction on n. Base case n=2 uses the Euclidean algorithm on the first column. Inductive step uses column reduction to achieve a (1,0,...,0)ᵀ first column, then apply the inductive hypothesis to the (n-1)×(n-1) minor.
- **Approach B:** Smith normal form over Euclidean domains. Factor through diagonal matrices, then show each diagonal SLₙ matrix is elementary.
- **Key lemma:** `gcd_achievable_by_elementary` — for a Euclidean domain, the gcd of any two elements can be achieved by elementary column operations.

**Why This Is Revolutionary:** Completes the adversarial certification framework: every integer-weight neural network layer with det(W)=1 is provably certifiable. This is the "completeness theorem" for K₁-based adversarial robustness.

**Catalog Leverage:** Builds on `isElementaryCertified_det_one`, `certified_invertible`

**Research Mode:** prove
**Estimated Depth:** 4

### 2. Milnor K₂ Formalization and Matsumoto's Theorem

**Theorem Statement:** K₂(F) for a field F is generated by Steinberg symbols {a, b} subject to bilinearity and the Steinberg relation {a, 1-a} = 0. Matsumoto's theorem states this presentation is complete.

**Proof Strategy:**
- **Approach A:** Construct K₂^M(F) as the quotient of F× ⊗ F× by the Steinberg relation. Prove it equals Milnor K₂.
- **Approach B:** Use tame symbols and the product formula. Define the tame symbol ∂_v: K₂(F) → k(v)× at each discrete valuation v, prove {a,1-a} maps to 1 at every place.

**Why This Is Revolutionary:** Provides the algebraic foundation for the compositional depth bound theorem. Connects to class field theory and the Brauer group.

**Catalog Leverage:** Builds on `steinberg_compositional_relation`, `steinberg_depth_bound`

**Research Mode:** formalize
**Estimated Depth:** 5

### 3. Lipschitz-Constrained Architecture Search

**Theorem Statement:** For any certification budget B and accuracy target α, the optimal Steinberg-compliant architecture (d*, w*) satisfying d·w² ≤ B maximizes accuracy subject to certified robustness radius ≥ ε.

**Proof Strategy:**
- Formalize the optimization problem: max accuracy(d,w) s.t. d·w² ≤ B
- Prove existence of optimum by compactness (finite parameter space)
- Derive closed-form solution for special cases (uniform width)

**Why This Is Revolutionary:** First principled architecture search algorithm with certified robustness guarantees. Replaces empirical NAS with provably optimal design.

**Catalog Leverage:** Builds on `architecture_budget_constraint`, `efficient_architecture_exists`

**Research Mode:** prove

### 4. Topological K-Theory for Quantum Neural Networks

**Theorem Statement:** For a quantum neural network on d qubits, the feature space is classified by K⁰(S²ᵈ) ≅ ℤ, with the Chern character providing a computable invariant.

**Proof Strategy:**
- Define quantum feature bundles as complex vector bundles over the Bloch sphere
- Use the Chern character ch: K⁰(X) → H*(X; ℚ) to compute invariants
- Connect to existing Mathlib topology (fundamental groups, covering spaces)

**Why This Is Revolutionary:** Extends K-theoretic classification to quantum architectures. The 2^d quantum feature space has finite K-theoretic classification despite exponential dimension.

**Catalog Leverage:** Builds on `quantum_feature_dimension`, `quantum_compression_advantage`

**Research Mode:** discover

### 5. K₁ Cryptographic Hash Functions

**Theorem Statement:** The map W ↦ det(W) mod p is a collision-resistant hash under standard lattice assumptions. Two matrices with different K₁-classes cannot be adversarial perturbations of each other.

**Proof Strategy:**
- Formalize the determinant as a K₁-invariant
- Connect collision resistance to the hardness of finding W₁, W₂ with det(W₁) = det(W₂) and W₁ ≠ W₂ in Eₙ(ℤ/pℤ)
- Reduce to Shortest Vector Problem on the SLₙ lattice

**Why This Is Revolutionary:** Creates post-quantum hash functions from K-theory. Security follows from lattice hardness, which is believed quantum-resistant.

**Catalog Leverage:** Builds on `k1_hash_separation`, `lattice_dimension_bound`

**Research Mode:** discover
**Estimated Depth:** 4

## Under-explored Territory

### Regulator Maps and Network Capacity
The Borel regulator K₂ₙ₋₁(ℤ) → ℝ maps K-theoretic invariants to real-valued capacity measures. This could provide K-theoretic VC-dimension bounds.

### Algebraic K-Theory of Ring Spectra
For networks with non-commutative weight structures (attention mechanisms), K-theory of ring spectra provides invariants beyond the commutative case.

### Motivic K-Theory and Architecture Motives
The motivic viewpoint could unify K-theoretic invariants across different base rings, providing universal architecture classification.

## Cross-Domain Bridges

1. **K-theory ↔ Information Theory:** The certificate description length O(d·n²) matches the Steinberg interaction count, suggesting a deep information-theoretic interpretation of K₂.

2. **K-theory ↔ Thermodynamics:** The Steinberg relation a + (1-a) = 1 as energy conservation. Steinberg compliance = thermodynamic equilibrium. Certification cost = entropy.

3. **K-theory ↔ Topology:** Transfer equivalence as homotopy equivalence of feature bundles. K₀ as the Grothendieck group of vector bundles.

4. **K-theory ↔ Number Theory:** K₂(ℤ) ≅ ℤ/2 (Milnor). The 2-torsion in K₂ may classify binary feature interactions.

## Open Problems Encountered

1. **Exact complexity separation bounds:** We proved d·w² < w^d for large d, w but the precise crossover point requires careful case analysis. Is there a clean closed-form for the crossover?

2. **Matrix norm instances:** Mathlib lacks good norm instances for `Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℝ`, making spectral radius bounds harder to formalize. Need `NormedAlgebra` instance.

3. **Full K₁ formalization:** Defining K₁(R) = GL(R)/E(R) as a quotient group in Mathlib requires careful handling of the direct limit GL(R) = colim GLₙ(R).

4. **Steinberg relation as actual K₂ relation:** Our formalization uses a structural model. The actual K₂^M(F) quotient construction would require tensor product quotients not yet in Mathlib.
