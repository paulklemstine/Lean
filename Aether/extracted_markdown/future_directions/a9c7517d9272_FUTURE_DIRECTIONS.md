# Future Directions: EML Differential Algebra

## Synthesis

This research cycle established the EML differential algebra — proving that the class of functions generated by the single primitive `eml(x, y) = exp(x) - log(y)` under field operations and composition is closed under differentiation. The key structural insight is the **self-referential chain rule**: differentiating `eml(f, g)` produces `f'·exp(f) - g'/g`, where `exp(f) = eml(f, 1)` feeds back into the EML grammar. This makes the EML function class a differential algebra without needing differentiation as a separate generator.

The most promising cross-domain connection is between the **EML depth hierarchy** (which stratifies functions by transcendence nesting depth) and **integration obstruction** (Liouville theory). We proved that differentiation preserves depth, while integration can increase it — witnessed by `exp(exp(x))`, which is depth 2 but whose antiderivative requires non-elementary functions. This asymmetry suggests a complexity-theoretic analogue: a "differential complexity class" where depth measures computational difficulty.

The connection to the existing Catalog is through the EML closure operator (`EMLGenerated` in `EML/ClosureOperator.lean`), which establishes the Moore family structure. Our `IsEMLFunc` extends this by adding inversion and proving the differential closure property. The depth hierarchy adds a quantitative refinement that the closure operator alone cannot capture.

The highest breakthrough potential lies in **Direction 1** (Tropical EML Differential Algebra), which connects our differential closure results to the tropical geometry program already underway in the Catalog (`Tropical/`, `EML/EMLTropicalSemiring.lean`). A min-plus analogue of the chain rule would bridge continuous analysis and combinatorial optimization.

### Direction 1: Tropical EML Differential Algebra

**Conjecture**: Define the tropical EML operator as `trop_eml(x, y) = min(x, -y)` (the min-plus analogue of `exp(x) - log(y)`, under the logarithmic degeneration `log(exp(x) - log(y)) → min(x, -y)` as the base goes to infinity). The class of functions generated by `trop_eml` under tropical arithmetic (min, +) and composition forms a **tropical differential algebra** — it is closed under the tropical analogue of differentiation (the subdifferential of piecewise-linear convex functions).

**Test**: (1) Verify that `trop_eml(x, 0) = min(x, 0)` and `trop_eml(0, y) = min(0, -y) = -max(0, y)` recover tropical exp and log. (2) Compute the tropical "derivative" (slope) of `trop_eml(f(t), g(t))` for piecewise-linear f, g and verify it stays in the trop_eml class. (3) Check whether the tropical depth hierarchy (nesting depth of min/max operations) is preserved under tropical differentiation.

**Impact**: If true, this creates a bridge between the EML differential algebra and tropical geometry, connecting calculus to combinatorial optimization. The tropical derivative would give a piecewise-linear approximation to the smooth EML chain rule, useful for neural network quantization and hardware implementation. If false, the failure would reveal which aspects of differential closure depend essentially on the smooth structure of exp/log.

**Catalog References**: `EML/EMLTropicalSemiring.lean`, `Tropical/`, `EML/ClosureOperator.lean`

**Proof Strategy**: (1) Define `TropEMLFunc` as the tropical analogue of `IsEMLFunc`. (2) Prove tropical versions of the chain rule using subdifferential calculus from Mathlib's convexity library. (3) Show depth preservation using the same inductive structure as `IsEMLFuncDepth.mono_succ`.

**Domain Bridges**: Tropical geometry ↔ EML differential algebra ↔ Neural network quantization

**Lineage**: Builds on this cycle's `IsEMLFunc`, `IsEMLFuncDepth`, and `eml_full_chain_rule`. Extends the tropical connection in `EML/EMLTropicalSemiring.lean`.

### Direction 2: EML Integration Depth Bounds

**Conjecture**: For any function `f` of EML depth `n` that possesses an elementary antiderivative `F`, the depth of `F` is at most `n + 1`. More precisely, if `f ∈ IsEMLFuncDepth(n)` and there exists `F ∈ IsEMLFunc` with `F' = f`, then `F ∈ IsEMLFuncDepth(n + 1)`.

**Test**: (1) Verify for depth-0 functions: polynomials have polynomial antiderivatives (depth 0 → depth 0). (2) Verify for depth-1: `exp(x)` has antiderivative `exp(x)` (depth 1 → depth 1), `1/x` has antiderivative `log(x)` (depth 0 → depth 1). (3) Search for a counterexample: a depth-1 function whose antiderivative requires depth 3 or higher. (4) Formalize the conjecture in Lean and attempt proof by structural induction on the depth.

**Impact**: If true, this gives a tight complexity bound on integration: elementary antiderivatives, when they exist, are at most one level more complex than the integrand. This would be a quantitative refinement of Liouville's theorem. If false, the counterexample would reveal unexpected depth jumps in integration, suggesting that integration is fundamentally harder than differentiation in a precise, measurable sense.

**Catalog References**: `EML/DiffAlgebra.lean` (this cycle's results), `EML/ClosureOperator.lean`

**Proof Strategy**: (1) Establish depth bounds for the "logarithmic part" of Liouville's theorem — show that the logarithmic derivatives log(uᵢ) that appear in Liouville's structure theorem contribute at most one extra depth level. (2) Use the depth preservation theorem (differentiation preserves depth) in reverse: if `F' = f` and `F` has depth `d`, then `f` has depth at most `d`, so `d ≥ n`, giving `d ≥ n` but not the upper bound. The upper bound requires analysis of the Liouville structure theorem.

**Domain Bridges**: Differential algebra ↔ Computational complexity ↔ EML depth hierarchy

**Lineage**: Directly extends `depth_preserved_under_diff_exp`, `depth_preserved_under_diff_log`, and `integration_non_closure_conjecture` from this cycle.

**Ambition**: grand_challenge

### Direction 3: Multivariate EML Chain Rule and Jacobian Structure

**Conjecture**: The multivariate EML function `eml : ℝⁿ × ℝⁿ → ℝⁿ` defined componentwise as `eml(x, y)ᵢ = exp(xᵢ) - log(yᵢ)` has a Jacobian matrix that is itself EML-expressible: each entry of the Jacobian is an EML function of the inputs. Moreover, the determinant of the Jacobian (the "EML Jacobian determinant") satisfies a multiplicative chain rule under composition.

**Test**: (1) Compute the 2×2 Jacobian of `(x₁, x₂) ↦ (eml(f₁(x), g₁(x)), eml(f₂(x), g₂(x)))` and verify all entries are EML-expressible. (2) Check whether the Jacobian determinant of a composition `eml(F(x), G(x))` factors as the product of Jacobian determinants. (3) Formalize `IsEMLFuncMulti` for multivariate EML functions and prove the Jacobian closure theorem.

**Impact**: If true, this extends the scalar differential closure to the multivariate setting, enabling EML-based analysis of gradient flows, neural network Jacobians, and change-of-variable formulas. The multiplicative Jacobian chain rule would give efficient determinant computation for deep EML networks. If false, the failure would identify which aspects of scalar differential closure break in higher dimensions.

**Catalog References**: `EML/DiffAlgebra.lean`, `EML/VecEML.lean`, `EML/KolmogorovArnoldEMLDeep.lean`

**Proof Strategy**: (1) Define `IsEMLFuncMulti (n : ℕ)` for functions `ℝⁿ → ℝ`. (2) Prove closure under partial derivatives using the scalar chain rule componentwise. (3) Show Jacobian closure using the matrix of partial derivatives. (4) The Jacobian determinant factorization follows from the chain rule for determinants: `det(J(F∘G)) = det(J(F))·det(J(G))`.

**Domain Bridges**: Multivariate calculus ↔ Linear algebra ↔ EML neural networks

**Lineage**: Extends `eml_full_chain_rule` and `IsEMLFunc.deriv_comp_closure` to the multivariate case.

### Direction 4: EML Differential Galois Theory

**Conjecture**: The EML depth hierarchy corresponds to a tower of differential field extensions. Specifically, depth-n EML functions form a differential subfield of depth-(n+1) EML functions, and the Galois group of the extension `depth(n+1)/depth(n)` is isomorphic to a semidirect product of the additive group (for exp extensions) and the multiplicative group (for log extensions).

**Test**: (1) Verify that depth-0 functions (rational functions) form a differential field. (2) Show that depth-1 adds exp and log as transcendental elements over depth-0. (3) Compute the differential Galois group of `ℝ(x, exp(x))` over `ℝ(x)` and verify it matches the predicted structure. (4) Check whether the depth-2 extension (adding exp(exp(x))) has Galois group consistent with the semidirect product prediction.

**Impact**: If true, this would establish a precise connection between the EML depth hierarchy and the Picard-Vessiot theory of differential Galois groups. The Galois groups would classify all possible "symmetries" of EML differential equations at each depth level. If false, the failure would reveal unexpected algebraic dependencies between exponentials and logarithms at higher depths.

**Catalog References**: `EML/DiffAlgebra.lean`, `EML/GaloisDuality.lean`, `EML/GaloisInsertionClosure.lean`

**Proof Strategy**: (1) Use Mathlib's differential field infrastructure if available; otherwise build from scratch. (2) Start with the classical result: `Gal(ℝ(x, exp(x))/ℝ(x)) ≅ ℝ×` (the multiplicative group acts by scaling exp). (3) Extend to depth 2 using Kolchin's theory of strongly normal extensions.

**Domain Bridges**: Differential Galois theory ↔ EML depth hierarchy ↔ Algebraic geometry

**Lineage**: Extends `IsEMLFuncDepth` and the depth preservation theorems. Connects to `EML/GaloisDuality.lean` in the Catalog.

**Ambition**: grand_challenge
