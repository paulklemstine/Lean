# Future Directions: Tropical Spectral Causality

This document outlines breakthrough research opportunities opened by the formalization of tropical spectral causality — the identification of tropical eigenvectors as invariant causal directions for min-plus dynamics.

---

## 1. Tropical Causal Cone Theorem

**Goal:** Generalize from invariant eigen-*rays* to invariant eigen-*cones*, characterizing the full causal structure of tropical linear maps.

**Hypothesis:** For a matrix $A$ with tropical eigenvalue $d$ and eigenspace $V_d = \{v : A \otimes v = v + d\}$, the tropical cone $\text{conv}_{⊕}(V_d)$ — the min-plus convex hull of the eigenspace — is forward-invariant under $A$, and the causal preorder restricted to this cone decomposes into a product of one-dimensional ray preorders.

**Proof Strategy:**
- Prove that the tropical eigenspace $V_d$ is a tropical convex set (closed under tropical linear combinations).
- Show that `tropMatVecMul A` maps $V_d$ to itself (up to the additive shift by $d$).
- Extend the `eigenvector_causal_invariance` theorem from single vectors to tropical convex combinations.
- Characterize the tropical spectral projector $\Pi_d$ and show it commutes with the causal preorder.

**Cross-Domain Impact:** In discrete event systems, this characterizes all stable timing profiles, not just individual ones. In network science, it identifies the full set of coherent propagation modes.

## 2. Collatz–Wielandt Causality Principle

**Goal:** Prove that the tropical spectral radius is the sharp causal Lipschitz constant for min-plus linear maps with respect to the Hilbert projective metric.

**Hypothesis:** For an irreducible $n \times n$ tropical matrix $A$ with spectral radius $\lambda^*(A) = \min_v \max_i (A \otimes v - v)(i)$, the quantity $\lambda^*(A)$ equals the optimal contraction rate:
$$\lambda^*(A) = \inf \{ c \in \mathbb{R} : d_H(A \otimes x, A \otimes y) \leq d_H(x, y) + c \text{ for all } x, y \}$$
where $d_H$ is the Hilbert projective metric.

**Proof Strategy:**
- Formalize the tropical Hilbert metric: $d_H(x, y) = \max_i (x_i - y_i) - \min_i (x_i - y_i)$.
- Prove that tropical linear maps are nonexpansive in $d_H$ (this is the tropical Birkhoff theorem).
- Show the spectral radius provides the exact Lipschitz constant via the Collatz–Wielandt minimax characterization.
- Connect to the iterate drift theorem: $A^k \otimes v = v + k \lambda^*$ shows $\lambda^*$ is the asymptotic drift rate.

**Cross-Domain Impact:** This would unify tropical Perron–Frobenius theory with metric fixed-point theory, providing a tropical analogue of the Power–Birkhoff contraction theorem for positive matrices.

## 3. Nonlinear Dynamic Programming Causality (Tropical Weak KAM)

**Goal:** Extend the spectral causality framework from matrices to monotone additively homogeneous operators, establishing a tropical weak KAM future-preservation theorem.

**Hypothesis:** Let $T : (\mathbb{R}^n, \text{min}) \to (\mathbb{R}^n, \text{min})$ be monotone (order-preserving) and additively homogeneous ($T(v + c) = T(v) + c$). If $v$ is a fixed point of $T - \lambda$ (i.e., $T(v) = v + \lambda$), then:
1. The ray $\{v + t : t \in \mathbb{R}\}$ is invariant under $T$.
2. $v$ is a viscosity solution of the tropical Hamilton–Jacobi equation $T(u) - u = \lambda$.
3. The causal preorder generated by $T$ restricts to an exact translation on the eigen-ray.

**Proof Strategy:**
- Abstract the key property used in our theorems: additive homogeneity (`tropMatVecMul_const_add`).
- Formalize the Lax–Oleinik semigroup in the min-plus setting.
- Prove the analogue of `eigenray_iterate_drift` for general additively homogeneous operators.
- Connect to viscosity solutions via the Crandall–Lions framework in the idempotent setting.

**Cross-Domain Impact:** This connects tropical spectral theory to optimal control, Hamilton–Jacobi PDEs, and Aubry–Mather theory. The eigen-ray becomes a KAM torus in the tropical setting.

## 4. Projective Geodesics and Tropical Busemann Functions

**Goal:** Identify tropical eigen-rays with geodesic rays in the tropical projective space equipped with the Hilbert metric, and define tropical causal boundaries via Busemann functions.

**Hypothesis:** In the tropical projective space $\mathbb{TP}^{n-1} = \mathbb{R}^n / \mathbb{R} \cdot \mathbf{1}$, the eigen-ray $[v + t] = [v]$ is a single point (since constant shifts are quotiented out). The correct geometric object is the *orbit* $\{[A^k \otimes v]\}_{k \geq 0}$. When $A$ is irreducible, this orbit converges to the tropical spectral projector, and the associated Busemann function $b(x) = \lim_{k \to \infty} (d_H(x, A^k \otimes v) - k\lambda^*)$ characterizes the causal boundary.

**Proof Strategy:**
- Formalize the tropical projective space as a quotient type.
- Define the Hilbert metric on $\mathbb{TP}^{n-1}$ and prove it is a complete metric space.
- Show that the action of $A$ on $\mathbb{TP}^{n-1}$ is a contraction when $A$ is irreducible (tropical Birkhoff theorem).
- Define Busemann functions and prove they characterize the "ideal boundary" of the tropical projective space.

**Cross-Domain Impact:** This creates a bridge between tropical geometry and geometric group theory / CAT(0) geometry. The Busemann function becomes an entropy function for the discrete event system.

## 5. Network Timing Semantics and Stable Delay Profiles

**Goal:** Interpret tropical eigen-rays as stable delay profiles in weighted directed graphs and prove graph-theoretic criteria for causal invariance.

**Hypothesis:** Consider a weighted directed graph $G$ with adjacency matrix $A$, where $A_{ij}$ represents the delay/cost of traversing edge $(i, j)$. A vector $v \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is a stable delay profile if and only if $(d, v)$ is a tropical eigenpair, where $d$ is the minimum cycle mean. The causal invariance theorem then says: if the network operates synchronously with delay profile $v$, then after one clock tick every node's delay shifts by exactly $d$.

**Concrete Criteria:**
- $(d, v)$ is an eigenpair if and only if $v$ assigns delays to nodes such that every edge $(i, j)$ satisfies $A_{ij} + v_j \geq v_i + d$, with equality for at least one outgoing edge per node.
- The critical graph (edges achieving equality) determines the eigenspace structure.
- Causal invariance holds along the eigen-ray if and only if the critical graph is strongly connected.

**Proof Strategy:**
- Formalize the graph-theoretic characterization of tropical eigenpairs via critical graphs.
- Prove Karp's theorem: the minimum cycle mean equals the tropical eigenvalue.
- Show that `eigenray_iterate_drift` specializes to the synchronous network timing theorem.
- Derive conditions under which the network stabilizes to the eigen-ray from arbitrary initial conditions.

**Cross-Domain Impact:** This provides formal verification tools for real-time scheduling systems, network timing protocols (e.g., IEEE 1588 PTP), and manufacturing flow-shop scheduling. The eigenvalue $d$ is the throughput of the system, and the eigenvector is the optimal timing profile.
