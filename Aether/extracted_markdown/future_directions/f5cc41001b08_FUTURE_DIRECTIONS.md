# Future Directions: Canonical Kernel Theory on Metric Graphs

## Synthesis

The canonical kernel framework established in this work — connecting discrete Laplacian theory, tropical Jacobian computation, Dirichlet energy, and pendant-edge rigidity — opens five major research avenues that span from foundational tropical geometry to computational physics. The unifying theme is **canonicality**: the existence of unique normalized harmonic representatives that are intrinsic to the metric graph structure. Each direction below extends this canonicality principle to a new mathematical domain, with concrete testable predictions and connections to the formally verified catalog results.

The theorems proved here (pendant-edge rigidity, energy non-negativity, normalized kernel uniqueness, S-equivalence structure, energy bilinear form descent) serve as the finite-model anchors for each direction. Every conjecture below can be attacked by first reducing to the finite vertex model (where our Lean proofs apply) and then taking appropriate limits or quotients.

## Direction 1: Tropical Hodge Decomposition on Metric Graphs

**Conjecture:** Every piecewise-linear 1-form on a compact metric graph admits a unique decomposition into exact, coexact, and harmonic components, where the harmonic component is represented by a canonical kernel generator. The harmonic space has dimension equal to the first Betti number β₁, and the Hodge inner product coincides with the Dirichlet energy bilinear form.

**Test:** Construct explicit PL 1-forms on cycle and theta graphs. Compute the Hodge projection onto the harmonic subspace via the canonical kernel basis. Verify that the residual is exact + coexact by checking d and d* conditions. A failure of the decomposition (e.g., non-zero residual that is neither exact nor coexact) would falsify the conjecture.

**Impact:** A formal tropical Hodge theorem would establish metric graphs as a genuine testing ground for Hodge theory, potentially guiding extensions to higher-dimensional tropical varieties. It would also provide a computational framework for tropical cohomology.

**Catalog References:**
- `Pythagorean/TropicalBridge/MetricCanonicalForms/Advanced.lean` — energyBilin_symm, energyBilin_psd
- `Pythagorean/TropicalBridge/MetricCanonicalForms/Theorems.lean` — harmonic_everywhere_implies_constant, energy_nonneg

**Proof Strategy:** Define PL 1-forms as functions on oriented edges. Define d and d* via the boundary and coboundary operators. Show that the kernel of the Hodge Laplacian dd* + d*d coincides with the span of canonical kernel generators. Use the energy bilinear form as the Hodge inner product.

**Domain Bridges:** Algebraic topology, de Rham cohomology, tropical intersection theory, spectral graph theory.

**Lineage:** Extends the energy bilinear form (Theorem 7) from functions to forms, building on energy non-negativity (Theorem 2) and the uniqueness theorem (Theorem 4).

**Ambition:** Grand challenge — would establish the first complete Hodge theory for a non-trivial class of tropical varieties.

**"The key insight is..."** that the canonical kernel generators already span the harmonic subspace, so the Hodge decomposition reduces to projecting onto a known finite-dimensional space.

**"Why now?"** The formal verification of energy bilinear form properties (symmetry, positive semidefiniteness, shift invariance) provides the rigorous foundation needed for a Hodge inner product. Previous approaches lacked verified canonicality.

## Direction 2: Arithmetic Tropical Jacobians via Non-Archimedean Skeleta

**Conjecture:** For an algebraic curve C over a non-Archimedean field K with semistable reduction, the canonical kernel generators on the Berkovich skeleton Σ(C) correspond to normalized Néron differentials, and the canonical kernel matrix encodes the component group of the Néron model of the Jacobian variety J(C).

**Test:** Compute canonical kernel matrices for the skeleton of a genus-2 curve over Q_p with known reduction type. Compare the lattice generated by kernel generator Laplacians with the known component group. A mismatch falsifies the conjecture.

**Impact:** Would provide a concrete computational bridge between tropical geometry and arithmetic geometry, enabling computation of Tamagawa numbers and component groups via linear algebra on graphs.

**Catalog References:**
- `Pythagorean/TropicalBridge/MetricCanonicalForms/Advanced.lean` — SEquiv_equivalence, s_principal_deg_zero
- `Pythagorean/TropicalBridge/Defs.lean` — graphLaplacian, rootedSubsetDivisor

**Proof Strategy:** Use the retraction map from the Berkovich analytification to the skeleton. Show that harmonic functions on the skeleton lift to analytic functions on the curve. Identify the S-equivalence quotient with the component group via the specialization map.

**Domain Bridges:** Number theory, p-adic Hodge theory, Néron models, Berkovich spaces.

**Lineage:** Extends the S-supported Jacobian quotient (Theorems 5-6) from abstract metric graphs to arithmetic skeleta.

**Ambition:** Grand challenge — would connect formally verified tropical computations to deep arithmetic invariants.

**"The key insight is..."** that the S-equivalence relation on divisors (Theorem 6) is the tropical shadow of the algebraic equivalence relation on divisors of the algebraic curve, and the canonical kernel uniqueness theorem (Theorem 4) corresponds to the uniqueness of Néron models.

**"Why now?"** The formal verification of the S-equivalence relation as a genuine equivalence (reflexivity, symmetry, transitivity) and the principal divisor lattice structure ensures that the tropical quotient is well-defined.

## Direction 3: Tropical Gaussian Free Fields and Canonical Covariance Coordinates

**Conjecture:** The canonical kernel matrix K on a metric graph M with support set S defines a natural coordinate system for the S-marginal of the Gaussian free field on M. Specifically, the covariance matrix of (GFF(s))_{s∈S} is expressible as a polynomial in K, and the canonical kernel generators are the principal modes of the field.

**Test:** For cycle and theta graphs, compute the covariance matrix of the GFF (via L⁺) restricted to S. Compare with the canonical kernel matrix. Check whether the covariance is a polynomial function of K entries. Test on random metric graphs.

**Impact:** Would provide a tropical coordinate system for network Gaussian fields, potentially simplifying correlation computations and enabling new sampling algorithms.

**Catalog References:**
- `Pythagorean/TropicalBridge/MetricCanonicalForms/Advanced.lean` — energyBilin_psd, energyBilin_shift_invariant
- `Pythagorean/TropicalBridge/MetricCanonicalForms/Theorems.lean` — energy_nonneg, twice_energy_eq_sum_sq_diff

**Proof Strategy:** Express L⁺ in terms of the canonical kernel basis. Use the spectral decomposition of the Laplacian and the fact that kernel generators span the non-constant eigenspaces (restricted to S). Identify the covariance matrix with the Gram matrix of kernel generators under the energy inner product.

**Domain Bridges:** Statistical mechanics, probability theory, random matrix theory, Gaussian processes.

**Lineage:** Extends energy bilinear form (Theorem 7) to a statistical-mechanics covariance interpretation.

**Ambition:** Solid extension — builds directly on verified energy positivity and bilinearity.

**"The key insight is..."** that the energy bilinear form B(f,g) is simultaneously the Jacobian polarization (tropical geometry), the power dissipation coupling (electrical networks), and the inverse covariance kernel (Gaussian free fields), so canonical coordinates for one are canonical for all three.

**"Why now?"** The verified descent of the energy form to equivalence classes (Theorem 9) guarantees that the covariance coordinates are well-defined modulo constants.

## Direction 4: Certified Tropical Abel–Jacobi Computation

**Conjecture:** For any compact metric graph Γ with genus g and any effective divisor D of degree g, the tropical Abel–Jacobi image μ(D) ∈ J(Γ) can be computed as the S-equivalence class of D for any support set S that meets every cycle, using only O(|S|³) arithmetic operations. Moreover, the computation is certified: the output includes a machine-verifiable proof that the computed class is correct.

**Test:** Implement the algorithm for genus-2 and genus-3 metric graphs with known Jacobian structure. Compare with direct Abel–Jacobi computation via path integrals. Measure the computational speedup from pruning and from restricting to small S.

**Impact:** Would provide the first certified algorithm for tropical Abel–Jacobi maps, enabling verified computation of tropical theta functions and intersection products.

**Catalog References:**

**Proof Strategy:** Reduce the Abel–Jacobi computation to solving the canonical kernel system. Use pendant-edge rigidity (Theorem 1) to justify pruning. Use normalized kernel uniqueness (Theorem 4) to guarantee well-definedness. Package the output with a Lean-verified certificate.

**Domain Bridges:** Computational algebraic geometry, cryptography (via Jacobian group structure), coding theory.

**Lineage:** Directly combines pendant pruning (Theorem 8), normalized uniqueness (Theorem 4), and S-principal lattice closure (Theorem 5).

**Ambition:** Solid extension — highly practical, builds on all established infrastructure.

**"The key insight is..."** that pendant-tree pruning reduces the Jacobian computation to the cycle core without loss of information (Theorem 8), and the kernel uniqueness theorem (Theorem 4) guarantees that the computation is canonical.

**"Why now?"** The complete formal verification of the S-principal divisor lattice (closure under +, −, ·) and the S-equivalence relation provides the mathematical infrastructure for certifiable output.

## Direction 5: Quantum Graph Spectral Invariants from Canonical Kernels

**Conjecture:** The canonical kernel matrix K of a metric graph M encodes the spectral determinant of the quantum graph Laplacian up to a correction term depending only on the total edge length. Specifically, log det(K_reduced) differs from log ζ_Γ(0) by a computable function of the total length L(Γ) and the genus g.

**Test:** Compute canonical kernel determinants for quantum graph families (cycles with irrational length ratios, theta graphs, complete graphs K₄) where the spectral determinant is known analytically. Compare. A discrepancy disproves the conjecture.

**Impact:** Would provide a finite-dimensional algebraic window into the infinite-dimensional spectral theory of quantum graphs, potentially useful for quantum chaos and inverse spectral problems.

**Catalog References:**
- `Pythagorean/TropicalBridge/MetricCanonicalForms/Advanced.lean` — energy_nonneg, energyBilin_self_eq_energy
- `Pythagorean/TropicalBridge/MetricKernel/Theorems.lean` — weightedLaplacian_psd

**Proof Strategy:** Use the trace formula connecting the spectral zeta function to periodic orbit sums. Express periodic orbit contributions in terms of canonical kernel entries via the harmonic extension formula. Identify the determinant of K with the regularized product of non-zero eigenvalues.

**Domain Bridges:** Quantum mechanics, spectral geometry, trace formulas, quantum chaos.

**Lineage:** Extends energy non-negativity (Theorem 2) and positive semidefiniteness (Theorem 7) to a spectral interpretation.

**Ambition:** Grand challenge — connects verified finite-dimensional algebra to infinite-dimensional spectral theory.

**"The key insight is..."** that the canonical kernel matrix is a finite-dimensional projection of the infinite-dimensional Green's operator, and its determinant captures the same information as the spectral zeta function at s=0.

**"Why now?"** The formal verification of energy positivity and the bilinear form structure ensures that the kernel matrix is non-degenerate (after removing the constant mode), which is the essential condition for the determinant formula to hold.
