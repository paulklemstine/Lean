# Future Directions: Weyl Algebra and the Jacobian–Dixmier Bridge

## Synthesis

The formalization of the Jacobian–Dixmier bridge through the first Weyl algebra opens five interconnected research directions spanning noncommutative algebra, symplectic geometry, deformation quantization, polynomial dynamics, and computational algebra. The central achievement — certifying that CCR-preserving filtered endomorphisms induce Keller maps via the Poisson bracket / Jacobian determinant identity — provides a reusable foundation for each direction. The grand challenges (Directions 1 and 4) target the full Tsuchimoto–BKK equivalence and a formal deformation quantization stack, while the extensions (Directions 2, 3, 5) build directly on the current theorems to deepen coverage of Weyl algebra structure, symplectic automorphism theory, and computational verification.

---

## Direction 1: Full Tsuchimoto–BKK Equivalence for Aₙ

**Conjecture:** Every algebra endomorphism φ of the n-th Weyl algebra Aₙ(K) is filtered with respect to the Bernstein filtration (after possible regrading), and the induced map on gr(Aₙ) ≅ K[x₁,...,xₙ,ξ₁,...,ξₙ] is a Keller map. Combined with JC(2n), this implies the full Dixmier Conjecture.

**Test:** Formalize the Bernstein filtration for Aₙ, prove that endomorphisms preserve filtration (the key step requires showing the image of each generator has finite order in the filtration — provable using the faithful representation on K[x₁,...,xₙ]). Then compute the induced graded map for explicit endomorphisms of A₂(ℚ) and verify the Keller condition computationally for a family of test endomorphisms.

**Impact:** Would complete the formal proof of JC ⟹ DC for all dimensions, not just A₁. This is one of the deepest cross-domain implications in algebra.

**Proof Strategy:** (1) Define Aₙ as a quotient of the free algebra or via Ore extension tower. (2) Prove the Bernstein inequality for Aₙ-modules. (3) Use the GK-dimension argument to show endomorphisms are filtered. (4) Extend the symbol map computation to n dimensions. (5) Apply JC(2n) to conclude.

**Domain Bridges:** Noncommutative algebra ↔ Algebraic geometry ↔ Representation theory (GK-dimension)

**Lineage:** Tsuchimoto (2005), Belov-Kanel–Kontsevich (2007), this work (A₁ case)

**Ambition:** Grand challenge — would be a landmark in formal mathematics

## Direction 2: PBW Theorem and Ore Extension Formalization

**Conjecture:** The Weyl algebra A₁(K) is isomorphic to the Ore extension K[x][d; d/dx], and the PBW basis {x^i d^j : i,j ≥ 0} is a K-linear basis. Furthermore, the Ore multiplication rule d · f(x) = f(x) · d + f'(x) gives a constructive normal ordering algorithm with certified correctness.

**Test:** Formalize the Ore extension construction in Lean, prove the universal property, and show the natural map from the Ore extension to any K-algebra containing a Weyl pair is an isomorphism onto its image. Verify computationally that the Ore multiplication agrees with our normal ordering algorithm for all monomials up to degree 10.

**Impact:** Would provide a canonical, constructive model of A₁ in Mathlib, enabling all subsequent Weyl algebra formalization to work with a concrete type rather than abstract axioms.

**Catalog References:**
- `Catalog/Pythagorean/WeylAlgebra.lean` — IsWeylPair, WeylPoly, evalWeylMonomial

**Proof Strategy:** (1) Define the Ore extension K[x][d; δ] for a derivation δ. (2) Prove the diamond lemma / Bergman's confluence theorem for the rewriting system DX → XD + 1. (3) Deduce PBW as a corollary. (4) Prove the faithfulness of the polynomial representation.

**Domain Bridges:** Ring theory ↔ Rewriting systems ↔ Formal language theory

**Lineage:** Ore (1933), PBW theorem, Bergman (1978), this work

**Ambition:** Solid extension — infrastructure for the research community

## Direction 3: Symplectic Automorphism Group of gr(A₁)

**Conjecture:** The automorphism group of the Poisson algebra (K[x,ξ], {·,·}) is exactly the group of polynomial symplectomorphisms — polynomial maps F : K² → K² with {F(x), F(ξ)} = 1. For n = 1, this group is generated by symplectic linear maps (SL₂) and symplectic shears (x ↦ x, ξ ↦ ξ + f(x)).

**Test:** Verify computationally that every quadratic symplectomorphism of K[x,ξ] decomposes as a product of at most 3 symplectic linear maps and shears. Search for a cubic symplectomorphism that does NOT decompose, which would falsify the "finite generation by tame maps" conjecture (polynomial analog of the Tame Generators Conjecture for polynomial automorphisms).

**Impact:** Would connect the Dixmier bridge to the structure theory of symplectic automorphisms, potentially constraining the possible forms of Weyl endomorphisms.

**Catalog References:**
- `Catalog/Pythagorean/WeylAlgebra.lean` — poissonBracket₂, poissonBracket_coords, poissonBracket_leibniz, poissonBracket_jacobi, filtered_end_keller_condition

**Proof Strategy:** (1) Formalize the symplectic automorphism group Aut_Poisson(K[x,ξ]). (2) Prove SL₂ and shear maps are Poisson automorphisms. (3) Use the Jung–van der Kulk theorem (polynomial automorphisms of K² are tame) to decompose. (4) Verify Poisson compatibility of tame decompositions.

**Domain Bridges:** Symplectic geometry ↔ Polynomial automorphism theory ↔ Group theory

**Lineage:** Jung (1942), van der Kulk (1953), Makar-Limanov, this work

**Ambition:** Solid extension

## Direction 4: Formal Deformation Quantization Stack

**Conjecture:** The Weyl algebra A₁(K) is a formal deformation of K[x,ξ] in the sense of Kontsevich's formality theorem: there exists a star product ⋆ on K[x,ξ][[ℏ]] such that f ⋆ g = fg + ℏ{f,g}/2 + O(ℏ²), and A₁ is isomorphic to (K[x,ξ][[ℏ]], ⋆) modulo ℏ = 1. Furthermore, the symbol map σ : A₁ → K[x,ξ] is the ℏ → 0 limit of this deformation.

**Test:** Define the Moyal star product on K[x,ξ][[ℏ]] as f ⋆ g = Σ (ℏ/2)^n/n! · Σ (−1)^k C(n,k) ∂^k f/∂x^k ∂^(n-k) f/∂ξ^(n-k) · ∂^(n-k) g/∂x^(n-k) ∂^k g/∂ξ^k. Verify associativity to order ℏ³ computationally. Verify that x ⋆ ξ − ξ ⋆ x = ℏ. Then formalize the ℏ = 1 specialization to recover A₁.

**Impact:** Would create the first formal deformation quantization infrastructure, with applications to mathematical physics, string theory, and noncommutative geometry.

**Catalog References:**
- `Catalog/Pythagorean/WeylAlgebra.lean` — IsWeylPair, poissonBracket₂, principalSymbol

**Proof Strategy:** (1) Define formal power series K[x,ξ][[ℏ]] in Lean. (2) Define the Moyal product. (3) Prove associativity by showing it's isomorphic to A₁[[ℏ]] via the Weyl quantization map. (4) Verify the semiclassical limit: lim_{ℏ→0} [f,g]_⋆ / ℏ = {f,g}.

**Domain Bridges:** Deformation quantization ↔ Mathematical physics ↔ Noncommutative geometry ↔ Homological algebra

**Lineage:** Moyal (1949), Bayen–Flato–Fronsdal–Lichnerowicz–Sternheimer (1978), Kontsevich (2003), this work

**Ambition:** Grand challenge — paradigm-shifting

## Direction 5: Verified Computational Algebra for Weyl Algebras

**Conjecture:** The normal ordering algorithm for Aₙ can be implemented with verified correctness in Lean, with O(D^{2n}) time complexity for multiplying two degree-D elements, and used to decide the word problem for Aₙ in polynomial time. Furthermore, the Gröbner basis theory for the Bernstein filtration can be formalized, enabling automated membership testing in filtration levels.

**Test:** Implement and verify in Lean a normal ordering function `normalOrder : WeylWord → WeylPoly K` together with a correctness theorem `normalOrder_correct : ∀ w, evalWord x d w = evalWeylPoly x d (normalOrder w)`. Benchmark against the Python implementation for words up to length 20. Test Gröbner basis algorithms for left ideals of A₁ on a standard benchmark set.

**Impact:** Would provide the first verified computational algebra system for noncommutative polynomial rings, with applications to automated theorem proving in operator algebras and symbolic computation for quantum mechanics.

**Catalog References:**
- `Catalog/Pythagorean/WeylAlgebra.lean` — WeylMonomial, WeylPoly, evalWeylMonomial

**Proof Strategy:** (1) Define `normalOrder` using the Leibniz formula for d^b · x^c. (2) Prove correctness by showing the Leibniz formula preserves evaluation. (3) Define Gröbner bases for the Bernstein filtration. (4) Prove the Buchberger algorithm terminates and produces a Gröbner basis.

**Domain Bridges:** Computer algebra ↔ Verified computation ↔ Automated reasoning

**Lineage:** Buchberger (1965), Galligo (1985) (noncommutative Gröbner bases), this work

**Ambition:** Solid extension — directly actionable
