# Future Directions: Categorical Physics and the Cobordism Hypothesis

The file `CobordismHypothesis.lean` makes rigorous the one-dimensional,
linear-algebraic *shadow* of the slogan "a topological theory of everything is a
fully dualizable object in a symmetric monoidal higher category." It proves that
the value-on-the-point of a 1d TQFT is a dualizable object (the snake identity
`point_value_dualizable`), that the circle partition function is its dimension
(`tqftCircle_apply_one`, `tqftCircle_eq_trace_id`), that the theory is monoidal
(`tqft_disjoint_union_mul`, `tqft_disjoint_union_trace_mul`) and cyclic
(`tqft_circle_cyclic`), and that it is classified by one computable invariant
(`tqft_classification_finrank`, `tqft_partition_computable`) — i.e. it contains
no oracle information. These results give the analytic content that the catalog
stub `Theorems.lean` (`two_infinity_necessity`, `two_infinity_achievable`) only
postulates. Below are five testable, falsifiable directions that extend it.

## 1. The 1d cobordism hypothesis as a genuine universal property

Right now dualizability is captured by the zig-zag identity on the *fixed*
witnesses `coevaluation`/`contractLeft`. The cobordism hypothesis asserts more:
the space of *all* symmetric-monoidal functors `Bord_1 → Vect_K` is equivalent to
the *groupoid of dualizable objects*. The concrete falsifiable claim is: for the
free rigid symmetric monoidal category on one (self-dual) generator, the functor
"evaluate at the generating object" is an equivalence onto finite-dimensional
`Vect_K`. **The key insight is** that in dimension one the cobordism category is
freely generated by a single dualizable object, so the universal property reduces
to the statement that `tqftCircle` is the *unique* extension of `id` on the point
compatible with the snake identity — a uniqueness theorem provable by the same
basis expansion already used in `tqftCircle_apply_one`. **Why now?** Mathlib's
`coevaluation`/`contractLeft` zig-zag API (used here) plus `LinearMap.trace`
already supply both halves; only the free-category bookkeeping remains, and it can
be modeled finitely without any (∞,n)-category machinery.

## 2. Dimension is the *complete* monoidal invariant, and it is a semiring map

We proved `dim` is multiplicative for `⊗`. Extend this to an honest morphism of
semirings `(K0-class of dualizable objects, ⊕, ⊗) → ℕ` and prove it is injective
on isomorphism classes (so the Grothendieck semiring of the 1d theory is exactly
`ℕ`). The falsifiable statement: `dim (V ⊕ W) = dim V + dim W` together with
`tqft_disjoint_union_mul` and `tqft_classification_finrank` exhibit
`V ↦ dim V` as a semiring iso onto `ℕ`. **The key insight is** that closing
`dim` under both monoidal operations turns the ad hoc classification of section 5
into a structural statement: the 1d theory of everything *is* the semiring `ℕ`.
**Why now?** `Module.finrank_directSum`/`finrank_prod` and `finrank_tensorProduct`
are both in Mathlib, so the semiring homomorphism is immediate; only injectivity
needs the classification lemma we already proved.

## 3. Higher traces and the Euler-characteristic obstruction (2d shadow)

Move one dimension up by replacing "trace of an endomorphism" with the *graded*
(super)trace on a bounded complex, so that the circle invariant becomes the Euler
characteristic `χ = Σ (-1)^i dim Hᵢ`. The falsifiable claim: the graded analogue
of `tqftCircle_eq_trace_id` holds, `χ(V•) = str(id)`, and `χ` is still
multiplicative under `⊗` but can now be *negative*, obstructing the naive
"dimension ≥ 0" picture. **The key insight is** that allowing a `ℤ/2`-grading is
the minimal categorification that distinguishes a 2d shadow from the 1d one, and
the sign is exactly the first place where a higher category with duals departs
from plain `Vect`. **Why now?** Mathlib has homology, Euler characteristics for
finite complexes, and the supertrace API; this is the smallest concrete step
toward the "(2,∞)" in the project's title that is provable today.

## 4. Computability boundary: where the oracle would have to enter

We showed the 1d theory is decidable because its invariant lives in `ℕ`. Make the
*converse* boundary precise: exhibit a sequence of would-be theories whose
invariant is not finitely computable unless the underlying object is dualizable —
formalize that for non-finite-dimensional `U`, `dim U = 0`
(`tqft_circle_degenerate_of_infinite`) is the *only* extension of `dim` to a
multiplicative, isomorphism-invariant `ℕ`-valued function, so no "oracle-valued"
refinement exists. **The key insight is** that requiring a single computable
multiplicative invariant *forces* the dualizable (finite-dimensional) restriction;
oracle information could only appear by abandoning multiplicativity or finiteness.
**Why now?** The degeneracy lemma is already proved, and the uniqueness of `dim`
as a multiplicative invariant is a short rigidity argument over Mathlib's
`finrank` API — turning a philosophical "computable vs. oracle" question into a
sharp theorem.

## 5. Field-dependence and the role of characteristic

All our invariants are stated over an arbitrary field `K`, but the *circle value*
`dim V` is an integer reduced mod `char K` when viewed inside `K`. The falsifiable
direction: prove `tqftCircle K V 1 = (dim V : K)` collapses to `dim V mod p` when
`char K = p`, so that two non-isomorphic theories can share a circle partition
function in positive characteristic even though `tqft_classification_finrank`
(an equivalence over the *same* `K`) still separates them. **The key insight is**
that the classification invariant `dim ∈ ℕ` and the *observable* invariant
`(dim : K)` decouple exactly in positive characteristic, predicting a concrete
"partition-function degeneracy" absent over `ℚ` or `ℝ`. **Why now?** Both sides of
the equality are already in the file (`tqftCircle_apply_one`), so the
characteristic-`p` collapse is a `ZMod`/`CharP.cast` computation that immediately
sharpens the computability story of direction 4.
