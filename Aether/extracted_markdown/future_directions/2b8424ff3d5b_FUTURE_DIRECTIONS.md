# Future Directions — Multivariate Tropical Canonical Forms

## Synthesis

This cycle lifted the catalog's univariate canonical-form theory
(`Catalog/Tropical/Canonical/Basic.lean`, theorem `canonical_tropical_poly_unique`,
which sorts pieces by slope over `ℝ`) into a genuinely **multivariate, real-exponent**
setting over `ℝⁿ = Fin n → ℝ`. A tropical polynomial is modeled as a nonempty `Finset`
of affine pieces `(coeff, exp)` evaluating to `max (coeff + ⟨exp, x⟩)`, and a piece is
*essential* (irredundant) when it is the unique strict maximizer at some point. The
central result, `TropPoly.canonical_unique`, is the *Fundamental Theorem of Tropical
Canonical Forms*: two canonical polynomials that agree as functions are equal as sets of
pieces. The structural payoff is `TropPoly.canonical_eval_eq_iff` — for canonical forms,
the finite set of pieces is a complete combinatorial fingerprint for tropical equivalence.

The decisive insight that unlocked the general-`n` proof is **local affine rigidity**
(`TPiece.eq_of_le_ball_eq_center`): if one affine function lies below another on a ball and
they meet at the center, they coincide. This replaced the naive plan, which needed a
Baire-category or measure-theoretic "the achiever dominates on an open set" argument. By
taking the *achiever of `Q` at the strict-max center of an essential piece of `P`*, rigidity
forces that achiever to equal the essential piece — so essential pieces are invariants of
the function, not the presentation (`TropPoly.essential_mem_of_eval_eq`). This is exactly
why the same single lemma handles every dimension `n` uniformly, including `n = 0`.

The Critic's boundary result `canonical_needed_for_unique` shows the canonicality
hypothesis is sharp: `{(0,0)}` and `{(0,0),(-1,0)}` compute the same function yet have
different piece-sets, because the second harbors a redundant (non-essential) monomial. What
this cycle did **not** reach is the Newton-polytope side of the concept (Theorems 2 and 3):
identifying essential pieces with upper-hull vertices, and the face-lattice / domain-poset
isomorphism. These need a polytope/convex-hull bridge that Mathlib supports only partially,
and are the natural targets below.

## Results Summary

- `TPiece.eq_of_eval_eq`: proved — an affine piece is determined by its values everywhere (coeff + exponents recoverable by probing the origin and axis points).
- `TropPoly.piece_le_eval`: proved — every piece lies pointwise below the polynomial's max.
- `TropPoly.exists_achiever`: proved — at every point some piece attains the max.
- `TPiece.eq_of_le_ball_eq_center`: proved — local affine rigidity, the workhorse lemma.
- `TropPoly.strict_max_ball`: proved — a unique strict maximizer persists on a neighborhood.
- `TropPoly.eval_eq_on_strict_max_ball`: proved — the max equals the dominating piece on that neighborhood.
- `TropPoly.essential_mem_of_eval_eq`: proved — essential pieces occur in any functionally-equal polynomial.
- `TropPoly.canonical_unique`: proved — **main theorem**: canonical + functionally equal ⟹ equal piece-sets.
- `TropPoly.canonical_eval_eq_iff`: proved — canonical piece-sets are a complete fingerprint for tropical equivalence.
- `canonical_needed_for_unique`: proved (disproof of naive uniqueness) — canonicality is a necessary hypothesis.

## Research Directions

### Direction 1: Canonicalization existence (essential pieces reconstruct the function)
**Hypothesis**: For every `TropPoly n` there is a canonical `TropPoly n` with the same
evaluation, namely the sub-Finset of essential pieces; equivalently
`(filter Essential P).eval = P.eval` whenever at least one piece is essential.
**Test**: Prove `∀ x, P.eval x = (P.essentialPart).eval x`. The hard inequality is
`P.eval x ≤ essential.eval x`: the achiever at a generic `x` may be non-essential, so one
must show the upper envelope is generated by its strict-maximizer pieces (a convexity /
exposed-face argument; in 1-D this is the `Set.Infinite` pigeonhole used in `Basic.lean`).
**Why now**: `essential_mem_of_eval_eq` already proves uniqueness of the canonical form;
only *existence* is missing, and `eval_eq_on_strict_max_ball` provides the local model.
**If true**: every tropical polynomial gets a computable normal form, completing the
"canonical form is well-defined" half and enabling a decision procedure for equivalence.
**If false**: there is a polynomial whose max genuinely needs a non-strict-maximizer piece,
which would reshape the very definition of "essential."

### Direction 2: Newton-polytope vertex characterization (Concept Theorem 2)
**Hypothesis**: A piece `(c, α)` is essential in `P` iff the point `(α, c)` is a vertex of
the upper hull of `conv {(α', c') : (c',α') ∈ P.pieces}` ⊂ `ℝⁿ × ℝ`.
**Test**: Build the bridge to `Mathlib`'s convexity (`convexHull`, `extremePoints`) and
prove essential ⇔ `(α,c) ∈ extremePoints (upper hull)`. Start with `n = 1`, where the upper
hull is a chain of segments and vertices correspond to slope breakpoints.
**Why now**: `Essential` is now a precise, proved-useful predicate; pairing it with
`extremePoints` is the missing combinatorial half and connects Tropical to the
Convex/Polyhedral catalog domains.
**If true**: the canonical form becomes purely combinatorial data (a polytope), giving the
"Newton polytope determines canon(p)" theorem and a cross-domain Tropical↔Convex bridge.
**If false**: essential pieces would distinguish more than the polytope vertices, exposing a
gap between tropical and polyhedral equivalence.

### Direction 3: Domain-of-linearity poset ≅ face lattice (Concept Theorem 3)
**Hypothesis**: The poset of *regions of linearity* of `P` (maximal sets where one piece is
the strict max), ordered by closure-inclusion, is isomorphic to the face lattice of the
Newton polytope; the number of full-dimensional regions equals the number of essential
pieces.
**Test**: Define `Region p = {x | ∀ q ≠ p, q.eval x < p.eval x}` and prove regions are open
convex, are nonempty exactly for essential pieces, and partition a dense subset; then match
their adjacency to polytope faces.
**Why now**: `strict_max_ball` already shows each region is open, and `canonical_unique`
gives the region↔piece bijection on the top stratum.
**If true**: completes the trilogy, yielding `#regions = #essential pieces = #vertices`.
**If false**: the lattice anti-isomorphism (a known subtlety: lower hull vs. upper hull, or
min-plus vs. max-plus) would be pinned down precisely.

### Direction 4: Tropical product and the additivity of Newton polytopes
**Hypothesis**: For the tropical product `(P ⊗ Q).pieces = {p+q}` (Minkowski sum of pieces),
essential pieces of `P ⊗ Q` are sums of essential pieces, and `Newt(P⊗Q) = Newt(P) +
Newt(Q)` (Minkowski sum). In particular `canon` is a monoid homomorphism up to equivalence.
**Test**: Define `TropPoly.tmul` (the `Finset` image of `(+)`), prove `eval (P⊗Q) = eval P +
eval Q`, then track essentiality through the sum using `eq_of_le_ball_eq_center`.
**Why now**: the univariate file already has `tmul`; porting it to the `Finset` model is
mechanical, and rigidity transfers strict-maximizer structure across the product.
**If true**: gives a tropical analogue of the classical "Newton polytope of a product is a
Minkowski sum," a genuinely algebraic-geometric statement.
**If false**: cancellation among cross-terms would reveal that tropical multiplication is not
"polytope-additive," a notable obstruction.

### Direction 5: Integer-exponent specialization and the lattice-width bound
**Hypothesis**: Restricting `exp : Fin n → ℕ` (the classical tropical setting), the number of
essential pieces of a degree-`d` tropical polynomial in `n` variables is bounded by the
number of lattice points of the `d`-dilated simplex, with equality detecting "generic"
coefficients.
**Test**: Add an `IntegralTPiece` predicate, specialize the main theorems (they hold verbatim
since `ℕ ⊂ ℝ`), and prove the counting bound via `Newt(P) ⊆ d·Δⁿ`.
**Why now**: the whole development was deliberately built over real exponents, so the integer
case is a free specialization; only the counting layer is new.
**If true**: connects tropical canonical forms to Ehrhart theory and gives effective size
bounds for ReLU-network canonicalization (cf. `relu_network_has_canonical_tropical_rational`).
**If false**: a super-Ehrhart blow-up would quantify the cost of tropical normal forms.
