# Future Directions: Categorical Physics

## 1. Frobenius Algebra Classification of 2D TQFTs

The cobordism algebra formalized here captures the *abstract* algebraic structure of cobordism categories. The next step is to formalize the **Dijkgraaf–Abrams–Kock classification theorem**: every 2D TQFT over a field k is equivalent to a commutative Frobenius algebra over k. This requires defining Frobenius algebras as algebras with a non-degenerate trace form, constructing the 2-cobordism category (at least at the level of generators and relations), and showing the two structures are in bijection.

The key insight is that the cobordism category of oriented surfaces is generated by a finite set of elementary cobordisms (pants, copants, cap, cup) subject to finitely many relations, and these generators/relations are *exactly* the axioms of a commutative Frobenius algebra. Why now? Our `CobordismAlgebra` structure already encodes half of this — the algebra side. The missing piece is the topological generators-and-relations presentation, which can be formalized purely algebraically.

**Testable conjecture**: A commutative Frobenius algebra structure on a finite-dimensional k-vector space V can be recovered from any CobordismAlgebra morphism from the free 2-cobordism algebra to `End(V)`.

## 2. Higher Cobordism Algebras and the Extended TQFT Hierarchy

Our cobordism algebra is a 1-categorical structure (monoid with involution). Physics demands *higher* categorical structure: an (∞,n)-category of cobordisms where n-manifolds can be cut along codimension-1 submanifolds, those submanifolds cut along codimension-2, and so on. The formalization should define a **graded cobordism algebra** — a sequence of cobordism algebras at each codimension level, connected by "boundary" maps satisfying compatibility conditions.

The key insight is that the Baez–Dolan cobordism hypothesis (proved by Lurie) says fully extended n-TQFTs are determined by a single datum: the value on a point, which must be a "fully dualizable" object. Our inner product symmetry theorem (`innerProduct_symm`) is the 1-dimensional shadow of full dualizability — formalizing the full hierarchy would show how our result generalizes. Why now? The product construction (`CobordismAlgebra.prod`) already shows how to combine theories at one level; extending to a tower of levels is a natural next step.

**Testable conjecture**: A "2-cobordism algebra" (with an additional layer of 2-morphisms satisfying interchange and coherence) admits a decomposition theorem: every 2-morphism factors through elementary pieces corresponding to handle attachments.

## 3. Non-Commutative Cobordism Algebras and Oriented TQFTs

Our `IsCommutative` predicate distinguishes oriented from unoriented theories. The non-commutative case is rich: non-commutative Frobenius algebras classify *open-closed* TQFTs and appear in string topology. Formalizing the non-commutative theory requires weakening commutativity to a *center condition* and studying the resulting Hochschild (co)homology.

The key insight is that our `innerProduct_symm` theorem holds even without commutativity — the inner product is *always* symmetric, but the underlying multiplication need not be. This separation between "inner product symmetry" (a topological/physical property) and "multiplication commutativity" (an algebraic property) is the formal version of the distinction between closed strings (commutative) and open strings (non-commutative). Why now? The current formalization already handles the non-commutative case in its main theorems; we just need to develop the consequences.

**Testable conjecture**: For any cobordism algebra C, the set {a : C.Carrier | ∀ b, C.mul a b = C.mul b a} (the center) is closed under mul, star, and the inner product restricts to a non-degenerate form on it if and only if the original form is non-degenerate.

## 4. Computability of TQFT Invariants

Our formalization shows that cobordism algebra operations are algebraically tractable. A deeper question: given a finite presentation of a cobordism algebra (generators and relations), is the word problem decidable? This connects categorical physics to computability theory. For finite-dimensional Frobenius algebras the answer is yes (linear algebra is decidable), but for infinite-dimensional theories or theories defined by generators and relations, the answer is open.

The key insight is that the composition law (`Morphism.comp`) and inner product preservation (`preserves_innerProduct`) are *constructive* — they produce explicit morphisms from explicit data. This suggests that the *finite* theory is fully computable, while the passage to infinite dimensions (needed for QFT on non-compact manifolds) is where computability may break. Why now? The proofs in our formalization use no classical axioms (as verified by `#print axioms`), which is a strong signal that the finite theory is constructively valid.

**Testable conjecture**: For any cobordism algebra C with `Fintype C.Carrier` and `DecidableEq C.Carrier`, the function `C.innerProduct` is computable (definable without `noncomputable`), and the question "does there exist a non-trivial morphism from C to D?" is decidable.

## 5. Monoidal Structure and Tensor Products of Theories

The product construction `CobordismAlgebra.prod` models *non-interacting* physical systems. Interacting systems require a *tensor product* — a cobordism algebra C ⊗ D where the two factors can interact through their traces. Formalizing this requires defining a universal property for the tensor product of cobordism algebras and showing it satisfies the expected monoidal category axioms (associativity, unitality, symmetry).

The key insight is that the trace on C ⊗ D should be τ(a ⊗ b) = τ_C(a) · τ_D(b) (multiplicative, not additive as in our product). This multiplicative trace corresponds physically to the *partition function of independent systems being multiplicative*. The additive trace in our `prod` gives the "free energy" (logarithm of partition function), so both constructions are physically meaningful but algebraically distinct. Why now? Our product construction and its commutativity theorem provide the template; the tensor product is the natural multiplicative analogue.

**Testable conjecture**: There exists a cobordism algebra `tensorProd C D` with τ(a ⊗ b) = τ_C(a) · τ_D(b) such that `(tensorProd C D).IsCommutative` whenever both `C.IsCommutative` and `D.IsCommutative`, and the canonical morphism from `prod C D` to `tensorProd C D` (sending (a, b) to a ⊗ b with appropriate identification) preserves the star operation.
