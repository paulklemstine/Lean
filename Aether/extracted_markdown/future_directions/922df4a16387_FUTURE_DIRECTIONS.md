# Future Research Directions

## Synthesis

This research cycle established a rigorous, machine-verified bridge between Arrow's impossibility theorem and the positive curvature of the Fisher information manifold. The core results formalized are: (1) the square-root embedding p ↦ √p maps the probability simplex isometrically to the unit sphere, with inner products equal to Bhattacharyya coefficients; (2) the squared Hellinger distance equals half the squared Euclidean distance between sphere points; (3) the concavity of cosine on [0, π/2] — expressing the curvature contraction of the sphere — is the analytical engine of midpoint contraction; and (4) ultrafilters on finite sets are principal, giving the algebraic route to Arrow's dictator.

The most promising cross-domain connection is between **quantitative curvature bounds and the severity of Arrow-type impossibilities**. The polarization index π (average pairwise Hellinger distance) provides a computable bridge: high π means the curvature contraction is large, Arrow's constraints are tight, and any aggregation rule must sacrifice fairness significantly. Low π means Arrow's theorem is vacuously satisfied. This connection has the highest breakthrough potential because it could transform Arrow's impossibility from a binary impossibility into a quantitative theory with testable predictions about real elections. The key insight from this cycle — that the AM-GM inequality (√(pq) ≤ (p+q)/2) is the microscopic building block of both the Bhattacharyya bound (BC ≤ 1) and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality on the sphere — suggests that the bridge can be sharpened into exact quantitative bounds.

### Direction 1: Quantitative Arrow Theory via Polarization Bounds

**Conjecture**: For m voters with preference distributions p₁,...,pₘ over n ≥ 3 alternatives, any social welfare function F satisfying IIA and approximate Pareto (F(P).rank(a,b) agrees with unanimous ranking with probability ≥ 1-ε) must agree with some single voter's ranking with probability ≥ 1 - C·ε/π, where π is the polarization index and C is a universal constant depending only on n.

**Test**: For n=3 alternatives and m=3 voters, enumerate all possible SWFs satisfying IIA on a discretized simplex (e.g., probabilities in multiples of 0.1). Compute the maximum fraction of profiles where F disagrees with the nearest dictator, and check whether this fraction is bounded by C·ε/π for some C ≤ 10.

**Impact**: If true, this transforms Arrow's impossibility from a qualitative binary result into a quantitative spectrum, parameterized by the curvature-derived polarization index. It would provide the first rigorous bound connecting voter disagreement (measured geometrically) to the degree of dictatorial behavior required.

**Catalog References**: `Algebra/ArrowCurvatureBridge/Arrow.lean`, `Algebra/ArrowCurvatureBridge/Geometry.lean`

**Proof Strategy**: Start with the Bhattacharyya-AM-GM bound (already verified). Define an ε-approximate SWF formally. Use the curvature contraction inequality (cos_midpoint_ge_avg) to bound how much the aggregation map can differ from a projection. The key lemma would be: if F satisfies unanimity and IIA, the image of F on the sphere must lie in a geodesic ball around some vertex (voter), with radius controlled by π.

**Domain Bridges**: Social Choice Theory ↔ Information Geometry ↔ Approximation Theory

**Lineage**: Builds on this cycle's formalization of the Hellinger-sphere isometry and the polarization index definition.

**Ambition**: grand_challenge

### Direction 2: Lin-Lu-Yau Curvature on the Permutohedron

**Conjecture**: The Lin-Lu-Yau Ricci curvature on the Cayley graph of the symmetric group Sₘ (generated by adjacent transpositions) is positive for all edges when m ≥ 3. Specifically, κ_LLY(e) ≥ 2/(m(m-1)) for every edge e.

**Test**: Compute Lin-Lu-Yau curvature (using the optimal transport definition with the lazy random walk at idleness parameter α → 0) for all edges in Cayley(S₃, {(12),(23)}) and Cayley(S₄, {(12),(23),(34)}). Check if all edge curvatures are positive. For S₃ this is a graph on 6 vertices; for S₄, on 24 vertices.

**Impact**: If true, this provides a discrete-geometric proof of Arrow's theorem: positive discrete Ricci curvature on the preference permutohedron creates the same obstruction as continuous positive curvature on the Fisher-Rao sphere. This would unify the algebraic (ultrafilter) and geometric (curvature) perspectives at the discrete level. Prior work falsified this for standard Ollivier-Ricci curvature, so success with Lin-Lu-Yau would identify the correct notion of discrete curvature for social choice.

**Catalog References**: `Algebra/ArrowCurvatureBridge/Geometry.lean`

**Proof Strategy**: (1) Implement Lin-Lu-Yau curvature computation in Python using the optimal transport characterization. (2) Verify computationally for m = 3, 4, 5. (3) If positive, seek a proof by analyzing the structure of geodesic balls in the Cayley graph and the Wasserstein distance between uniform measures on neighborhoods. The key difficulty is that Cayley(Sₘ) is not vertex-transitive under the action of Sₘ on itself, so edge curvatures vary.

**Domain Bridges**: Discrete Geometry ↔ Combinatorial Group Theory ↔ Optimal Transport

**Lineage**: Builds on this cycle's falsification of Ollivier-Ricci for the permutohedron and the successful continuous curvature formalization.

**Ambition**: grand_challenge

### Direction 3: Negative Curvature and Preference Divergence

**Conjecture**: On a statistical manifold with negative sectional curvature (e.g., the space of Gaussian distributions with fixed mean and varying variance, which has K = -1/2), there exist non-dictatorial aggregation rules satisfying IIA and a modified Pareto condition. Specifically, the geometric mean of Gaussians provides such a rule.

**Test**: Define the space of 1D Gaussians N(0, σ²) parametrized by σ > 0. Verify that the Fisher metric gives ds² = 2dσ²/σ² (curvature K = -1/2). Define the "geometric mean" aggregation: given σ₁,...,σₘ, output σ* = (∏σᵢ)^(1/m). Check that this satisfies IIA and unanimity but is non-dictatorial.

**Impact**: If true, this establishes that Arrow's impossibility is specifically a *positive* curvature phenomenon, not a universal feature of curved spaces. This would sharpen the curvature-impossibility bridge and potentially identify new domains where non-dictatorial aggregation is possible.

**Catalog References**: `Algebra/ArrowCurvatureBridge/Geometry.lean`, `Catalog/Algebra/YamabeNonCompact.lean`

**Proof Strategy**: The key insight is that on a negatively curved space, geodesic midpoints are *farther* from reference points than flat-space averaging predicts (the opposite of contraction). Formally, the cosine inequality reverses: cos((θ₁+θ₂)/2) ≤ (cos θ₁ + cos θ₂)/2 when the space has negative curvature. This allows non-trivial aggregation. Define the relevant framework, check IIA, and verify non-dictatorship computationally and then formally.

**Domain Bridges**: Hyperbolic Geometry ↔ Statistical Estimation ↔ Social Choice Theory

**Lineage**: Motivated by the positive curvature results of this cycle; explores the complementary regime.

**Ambition**: extension

### Direction 4: Computational Polarization Index for Real Elections

**Conjecture**: The polarization index π computed from ranked-choice voting data (converted to Plackett-Luce probability distributions) for U.S. presidential primaries exceeds 0.15 in contested primaries and falls below 0.05 in uncontested ones.

**Test**: Obtain ranked-choice polling data for at least 3 elections (e.g., 2020 Democratic primary, 2016 Republican primary, a local uncontested race). Convert each voter's ranking to a Plackett-Luce distribution. Compute π. Check the 0.15/0.05 threshold.

**Impact**: If the threshold holds, the polarization index becomes a practical early-warning indicator for elections where Arrow-type impossibilities will be most problematic — where the choice of voting system matters most. This connects the abstract mathematical theory to actionable political science.

**Catalog References**: `Algebra/ArrowCurvatureBridge/Arrow.lean` (polarization index definition)

**Proof Strategy**: This is primarily empirical. (1) Implement the Plackett-Luce conversion from rankings to probabilities. (2) Apply the polarization index formula. (3) Collect data from FairVote or similar organizations. The mathematical component is verifying that the Plackett-Luce model places voter preferences on the correct simplex and that the Hellinger distance is the right metric for comparing these specific distributions.

**Domain Bridges**: Political Science ↔ Information Geometry ↔ Applied Statistics

**Lineage**: Direct application of the polarization index defined in this cycle.

**Ambition**: extension

### Direction 5: Tropical Arrow's Theorem

**Conjecture**: Arrow's impossibility theorem has an analogue in tropical geometry: on the tropical simplex (the space of probability vectors under the max-plus algebra), there is no non-dictatorial aggregation rule satisfying tropical analogues of IIA and Pareto.

**Test**: Define the tropical simplex as {x ∈ ℝⁿ : max(x₁,...,xₙ) = 0}. Define tropical IIA as: the tropical social ranking of a vs b (i.e., the sign of F(P)_a ⊕ F(P)_b in max-plus) depends only on each voter's tropical ranking. Check whether the "tropical median" (coordinate-wise median) satisfies tropical IIA for n = 3 alternatives and m = 3 voters.

**Impact**: If true, this extends Arrow's impossibility to the tropical world, connecting it to the rich theory of tropical convexity, tropical linear algebra, and combinatorial optimization. The tropical simplex has zero curvature in a piecewise-linear sense, which would challenge the "curvature causes impossibility" narrative and suggest that the impossibility has deeper roots in the algebraic structure of aggregation itself.

**Catalog References**: `Catalog/Tropical/` (existing tropical algebra infrastructure), `Algebra/ArrowCurvatureBridge/Arrow.lean`

**Proof Strategy**: (1) Formalize the tropical simplex and tropical preference orderings. (2) Define tropical IIA and Pareto. (3) Enumerate all possible aggregation rules for small cases (n=3, m=3) and check which satisfy the axioms. (4) If no non-dictatorial rule exists, seek a structural proof using tropical convexity theory.

**Domain Bridges**: Tropical Geometry ↔ Social Choice Theory ↔ Combinatorial Optimization

**Lineage**: Novel direction suggested by the cross-domain nature of this cycle's bridge construction.

**Ambition**: extension
