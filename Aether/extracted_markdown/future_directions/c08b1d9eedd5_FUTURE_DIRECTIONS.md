# Future Directions

## 1. Replace Surrogate Profiles by Genuine Hyperbolic Translation Lengths

The current displacement profile is simply the orbit triple (a, b, c). A more
geometric invariant would be the **hyperbolic translation length** of the associated
Lorentz transformation, computed via the Hilbert metric or the trace formula:

```
ℓ(M) = 2 · arcosh(tr(M) / 2)
```

for SL(2,ℝ) elements, or an analogous formula for O(2,1). Formalizing this requires
embedding ℤ-matrices into ℝ-matrices and working with `Real.arcosh`. The key theorem
would be that translation lengths separate generators (a weaker but more geometric
version of our sector separation theorem).

## 2. Prove Freeness of the Positive Lorentzian Berggren Semigroup

Our orbit injectivity theorem (berggren_orbit_injective) proves that different words
produce different triples. The stronger statement — that the semigroup generated by
A, B, C is **free** (no non-trivial relations between generators) — follows from
orbit injectivity together with the fact that the orbit map is faithful (which we
have). Formalizing this connection would establish the Berggren semigroup as a
free product in the category of monoids.

## 3. Transfer to SL₂(ℤ) / Modular Geodesic Model

There is a classical correspondence between 3×3 Lorentz matrices preserving the null
cone and 2×2 matrices via the spin representation. The three Berggren generators
correspond to specific elements of PSL(2,ℤ), and the Berggren tree structure can be
interpreted as geodesics on the modular surface. Formalizing this correspondence
would connect our rigidity theorem to the rich theory of modular forms and hyperbolic
surfaces.

## 4. Certified Decoding Algorithm from Noisy Profiles

The decoding algorithm we've formalized assumes exact integer input. A natural
extension is to handle **approximate** or **noisy** inputs: given a vector v near
the null cone, find the closest Berggren triple and decode it. This requires:
- Error bounds on the sigma functionals
- A certified nearest-neighbor search on the null cone
- Formal analysis of the algorithm's sensitivity to perturbations

This would be relevant to applications in signal processing and error correction.

## 5. Connect to Hidden-Subsemigroup Recovery in Post-Quantum Cryptography

The Berggren semigroup is a discrete, infinite-index subsemigroup of O(2,1;ℤ).
The orbit rigidity theorem shows that semigroup elements can be uniquely recovered
from their action on a single vector. This is a prototype for the **hidden
subsemigroup problem**: given oracle access to a semigroup action, recover the
generating structure. Formalizing the complexity of this recovery (polynomial-time
via our decoding algorithm vs. potential hardness for other semigroups) would
connect to post-quantum cryptographic security models.

## 6. Extend to Quaternary and Higher Trees

The Berggren tree is a ternary tree. Analogous constructions exist for other
quadratic forms and higher-dimensional null cones. For instance:
- Markov triples (x² + y² + z² = 3xyz) admit a similar tree structure
- Apollonian gaskets correspond to orbit problems for Schottky groups
- Higher-dimensional Pythagorean-like equations may admit Lorentzian treatments

Formalizing these extensions would test the generality of our sector separation method.

## 7. Formalize Primitiveness Preservation

While we proved that generators preserve positivity and the null cone condition,
we did not formally verify that they also preserve **primitiveness** (gcd = 1).
The proof is straightforward: since det(G) = ±1, the inverse G⁻¹ has integer
entries, so if d | Gv then d | G⁻¹(Gv) = v. This would complete the chain to
show that the Berggren tree produces exactly all **primitive** Pythagorean triples.

## 8. Visualize the Hyperbolic Geometry

The positive null cone is diffeomorphic to the hyperbolic plane H² via
projectivization. Under this identification, the Berggren generators become
hyperbolic isometries, and the tree structure corresponds to a tessellation
of H². Creating an interactive visualization (in the Poincaré disk or
upper half-plane model) would make the geometric content of our rigidity
theorem visually accessible.
