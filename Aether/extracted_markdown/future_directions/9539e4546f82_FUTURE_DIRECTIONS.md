# Future Directions: Berggren Voronoi–CVP Duality

## Direction 1: Infinite-Height Berggren Voronoi Limits and Asymptotic Cell Geometry

**Goal:** Extend the finite Voronoi construction to the full infinite Berggren tree and study the limiting cell geometry.

**Approach:**
- Define a locally finite Voronoi complex on the countable set of all primitive Pythagorean triples.
- Prove that the finite-H Voronoi cells converge (in a suitable Hausdorff metric) as H → ∞.
- Characterize the asymptotic volume of Voronoi cells as a function of triple height, leveraging the density theorem |S_H| ~ H/(2π).
- Study whether the limiting complex has self-similar or fractal structure reflecting the ternary Berggren branching.

**Expected Impact:** This would establish the Berggren-Voronoi tessellation as a bona fide infinite geometric object, not just a finite approximation. The asymptotic cell volumes would give a geometric interpretation of the prime-counting-like density of Pythagorean triples.

**Technical Challenges:**
- Need locally compact topology on ℝ³ minus accumulation points.
- Cell boundaries in the infinite case may degenerate.
- Requires careful treatment of the three-way branching and its interaction with Euclidean geometry.

## Direction 2: Uniqueness/Stability Radii for Decoding Under Arithmetic Noise

**Goal:** Characterize the stability radius of the Berggren CVP decoder as a function of the triple's position in the Berggren tree.

**Approach:**
- For each triple t at Berggren depth d, compute the exact stability radius r(t) — the largest perturbation under which decoding to t is guaranteed.
- Relate r(t) to the height c(t) and the Berggren tree depth d(t).
- Prove bounds of the form r(t) ≥ C · c(t)^α for constants C, α depending on the tree structure.
- Define "arithmetic noise" as noise whose components are integers or rational multiples of triple components, and analyze decoding under this restricted noise model.

**Expected Impact:** Explicit stability radii would enable the Berggren decoder to be used as a practical error-correcting code with known correction capability. The arithmetic noise model is natural for number-theoretic applications.

**Concrete Next Steps:**
1. Compute r(t) numerically for all triples up to H = 10,000.
2. Fit the relationship r(t) vs. c(t) empirically.
3. Prove the conjectured bound in Lean.

## Direction 3: Extension to Markov Triples and Apollonian Circle Packings

**Goal:** Generalize the Voronoi-CVP framework from Pythagorean triples to other arithmetic orbits generated by matrix actions.

**Markov Triples:** Solutions (a, b, c) to a² + b² + c² = 3abc, generated by Vieta involutions from (1, 1, 1). These form a binary tree with a different arithmetic flavor but the same recursive structure.

**Apollonian Packings:** Circle packings generated by Descartes' theorem, where four mutually tangent circles satisfy a quadratic relation on curvatures. The Apollonian group is generated by four inversions.

**Approach:**
- Define abstract `ArithmeticTreeVoronoiData` generalizing `BerggrenVoronoiData`.
- Instantiate for Markov triples with defect based on the Markov quadratic form.
- Instantiate for Apollonian packings with defect based on curvature distance.
- Prove the Voronoi-CVP duality in the abstract setting, obtaining all three instances simultaneously.

**Expected Impact:** This would show that Voronoi-CVP duality is a general phenomenon of arithmetic orbits, not special to Pythagorean triples. Each arithmetic family yields a different certified decoder with different hardness characteristics, potentially providing a rich source of cryptographic primitives.

## Direction 4: Hardness Assumptions for Berggren-CVP Decoding Primitives

**Goal:** Analyze the computational complexity of the Berggren CVP problem and explore its viability as a cryptographic primitive.

**Key Questions:**
1. **Worst-case hardness:** Is Berggren-CVP NP-hard? The structured nature of the lattice may make it easier than generic CVP, but the Berggren tree's branching structure may introduce its own hardness.
2. **Average-case hardness:** Under natural distributions on targets, how hard is Berggren-CVP? This is critical for cryptographic applications.
3. **Quantum hardness:** Can quantum algorithms exploit the Berggren tree structure to solve Berggren-CVP faster than classical algorithms?

**Approach:**
- Reduce known hard lattice problems to Berggren-CVP, or vice versa.
- Analyze the geometry of numbers in the Berggren lattice (shortest vector, covering radius, etc.).
- Implement and benchmark lattice reduction attacks (LLL, BKZ) on Berggren lattices.
- Define a Berggren-CVP-based encryption scheme and analyze its security.

**Expected Impact:** If Berggren-CVP is sufficiently hard, it could provide a new post-quantum cryptographic primitive with arithmetic structure. If it is easy, the explicit structure would make it a useful pedagogical example of structured lattice problems.

## Direction 5: Spectral and Quantum Walks on the Berggren Delaunay Graph

**Goal:** Study the spectral properties of the Delaunay adjacency graph and define quantum walks on it.

**Approach:**
- Compute the adjacency matrix and Laplacian of the Berggren Delaunay graph for increasing H.
- Analyze the spectral gap, which controls mixing time of random walks and expansion properties.
- Define continuous-time quantum walks on the Delaunay graph and study their spreading behavior.
- Relate spectral properties to the Berggren tree structure (depth, branching, matrix type).
- Investigate whether the Delaunay graph is an expander family as H → ∞.

**Expected Impact:** Expander properties of the Delaunay graph would imply efficient mixing and potentially support cryptographic hash functions. Quantum walks on the graph could provide quantum speedups for search problems on Pythagorean triples.

**Concrete Milestones:**
1. Compute spectra for H = 50, 100, 200, 500, 1000.
2. Plot spectral gap vs. H and test for expansion.
3. Implement quantum walk simulation and compare with classical random walk mixing.
4. Formalize the spectral gap bound in Lean if a pattern emerges.

## Cross-Cutting Themes

All five directions share common infrastructure:
- The abstract `BerggrenVoronoiData` framework, which should be generalized to an `ArithmeticTreeVoronoiData`.
- The certified decoding paradigm, which applies uniformly across arithmetic families.
- Machine verification in Lean 4, ensuring that all claimed results are rigorous.

**Priority ordering:** Direction 2 (stability radii) is most immediately tractable and would validate the practical utility of the framework. Direction 3 (other arithmetic families) would have the broadest mathematical impact. Direction 4 (hardness) is the most speculative but has the highest potential payoff for cryptographic applications.
