# Future Directions: Testable Hypotheses for Formal Class Field Theory

This document identifies five falsifiable scientific hypotheses that build on the formally verified class field theory infrastructure developed in this cycle. Each hypothesis is precise enough to succeed or fail definitively.

---

## Hypothesis 1: Ray Class Precursor Hypothesis

**Conjecture.** The surjective Artin map extends from ordinary class groups to ray class groups modulo a finite modulus 𝔪, with codomain the Galois group of the maximal abelian extension unramified outside 𝔪. Specifically, for any number field K and modulus 𝔪, there exists a structure `IsRayClassField K L 𝔪` such that:
- Gal(L/K) ≅ Cl_𝔪(𝓞_K), and
- the natural projection Cl_𝔪(𝓞_K) → Cl(𝓞_K) induces restriction Gal(L/K) → Gal(H/K).

**Test.** Formalize a `RayClassGroup K 𝔪` structure for a finite modulus 𝔪 over an imaginary quadratic field K = ℚ(√-5) with 𝔪 = (2). Construct the ray class group as a quotient of fractional ideals coprime to 𝔪 modulo principal ideals generated by elements ≡ 1 mod 𝔪. Prove that the natural surjection Cl_𝔪 → Cl factors through the Artin maps of the corresponding class fields.

**Success criterion.** A sorry-free proof that `Nat.card (RayClassGroup K 𝔪) ≥ Nat.card (ClassGroup (𝓞 K))` and that the projection is a surjective group homomorphism.

**Failure criterion.** The formalization reveals that Mathlib's ideal infrastructure cannot express the coprimality condition cleanly, requiring a fundamentally different approach to moduli.

**Impact.** Ray class fields generalize Hilbert class fields to all abelian extensions, completing the formal skeleton of abelian class field theory. This is the gateway to conductor-discriminant formulas and explicit reciprocity laws.

## Hypothesis 2: Capitulation Detection via Transfer Map

**Conjecture.** For cyclic unramified extensions L/K of prime degree p, the capitulation kernel equals the image of the transfer (Verlagerung) map Ver : Gal(L/K)^{ab} → Cl(𝓞_K). Specifically, if L/K is the subextension of the Hilbert class field corresponding to a cyclic quotient of Cl(𝓞_K) of order p, then:

ker(Cl(𝓞_K) → Cl(𝓞_L)) = Ver(Gal(H/L))

where H is the Hilbert class field of K.

**Test.** Define the group-theoretic transfer map for a subgroup H ≤ G in Lean as:

```
Ver : G^{ab} → H^{ab}
Ver(gH) = ∏ᵢ (g·sᵢ·s_{σ(i)}⁻¹)
```

For G = Cl(𝓞_{ℚ(√-23)}) ≅ ℤ/3ℤ and the unique index-3 subgroup, compute Ver explicitly and verify it matches the capitulation kernel.

**Success criterion.** A sorry-free proof of the transfer-capitulation equality for at least one explicit cyclic extension of prime degree.

**Failure criterion.** The transfer map formalization requires coset representatives whose construction depends on unformalized choice principles, making the proof unfeasible without additional Mathlib infrastructure.

**Impact.** The transfer map is the group-theoretic mechanism behind the Principal Ideal Theorem. Formalizing it opens the path to Furtwängler's theorem and the systematic study of capitulation kernels.

## Hypothesis 3: CM Generation for Heegner Discriminants

**Conjecture.** For each Heegner discriminant D ∈ {-3, -4, -7, -8, -11, -19, -43, -67, -163}, the splitting field of the certified Hilbert class polynomial H_D(x) over ℚ(√D) is formally provable to satisfy `IsHilbertClassField`.

For D with h(D) = 1, this amounts to proving that K itself is a Hilbert class field of K (trivial extension), which requires only showing the Galois group is trivial when the class number is 1.

**Test.** For D = -4 (K = ℚ(i)):
1. Prove `Fintype.card (ClassGroup (𝓞 ℚ(i))) = 1` (i.e., ℤ[i] is a PID).
2. Construct an instance of `IsHilbertClassField ℚ(i) ℚ(i)` where the algebra structure is the identity.
3. Derive that the Artin map is the trivial homomorphism.

For D = -20 (K = ℚ(√-5), h = 2):
1. Construct the extension L = K(i) as a degree-2 extension.
2. Prove `IsGalois K L` and commutativity.
3. Prove `ClassGroup (𝓞 K) ≅ ℤ/2ℤ ≅ Gal(L/K)`.
4. Instantiate `IsHilbertClassField`.

**Success criterion.** Sorry-free instantiation of `IsHilbertClassField` for at least 3 of the 9 Heegner discriminants, including at least one with h > 1.

**Failure criterion.** The computation of explicit class groups (e.g., showing `Fintype.card (ClassGroup (𝓞 ℚ(i))) = 1`) requires decision procedures not currently available in Mathlib's `ClassGroup` API.

**Impact.** Explicit CM generation is the constructive heart of Hilbert's 12th problem for imaginary quadratic fields. Formal verification of even one non-trivial case would be a landmark in computational algebraic number theory.

## Hypothesis 4: Functoriality as Natural Transformation

**Conjecture.** The Artin map defines a natural transformation between two functors on the category of unramified abelian extensions of K:
- The "class group quotient" functor sending L to the quotient of Cl(𝓞_K) corresponding to the extension L/K.
- The "Galois group" functor sending L to Gal(L/K).

Concretely, for K ⊆ M ⊆ L with both M/K and L/K unramified abelian:

```
Art_{L/K}
Cl(𝓞_K) ────────→ Gal(L/K)
    │                   │
  proj              res
    │                   │
    ↓                   ↓
Cl(𝓞_K)/N ──────→ Gal(M/K)
Art_{M/K}
```

commutes, where N = ker(Art_{L/K} → Gal(M/K)).

**Test.** Prove commutativity for one nontrivial tower. Use the cyclotomic tower ℚ ⊂ ℚ(ζ₃) ⊂ ℚ(ζ₉), where:
- Art₉ : (ℤ/9ℤ)× → Gal(ℚ(ζ₉)/ℚ) is the identity on units.
- Art₃ : (ℤ/3ℤ)× → Gal(ℚ(ζ₃)/ℚ) is the identity.
- The restriction Gal(ℚ(ζ₉)/ℚ) → Gal(ℚ(ζ₃)/ℚ) corresponds to reduction mod 3.

Verify: `levelRaiseChar` (from existing catalog infrastructure) witnesses functoriality, and `AlgEquiv.restrictNormalHom` provides the restriction map.

**Success criterion.** A sorry-free proof that the diagram commutes for at least one cyclotomic tower, formalized as equality of MonoidHom compositions.

**Failure criterion.** The restriction map `AlgEquiv.restrictNormalHom` is not compatible with the concrete `CyclotomicGaloisGroup` representation, requiring a compatibility lemma that involves deep Galois theory infrastructure.

**Impact.** Functoriality is what separates "bag of lemmas" from "coherent theory." Formalizing it means the Artin map becomes a computable natural transformation — the first step toward categorical class field theory.

## Hypothesis 5: Abelian Langlands Shadow — Hecke Character Correspondence

**Conjecture.** For a number field K with finite class group, there is a bijection between:
- Finite-order Hecke characters of K unramified everywhere (i.e., characters χ : Cl(𝓞_K) → ℂˣ), and
- One-dimensional Artin representations (characters ρ : Gal(H/K) → ℂˣ where H is the Hilbert class field).

This bijection is given by the character transport `artinCharacterTransport` defined in this cycle.

**Test.** For K = ℚ(√-5) with Cl(𝓞_K) ≅ ℤ/2ℤ:
1. Enumerate all characters χ : ℤ/2ℤ → ℂˣ (there are exactly 2: the trivial and the sign character).
2. Transport each through the Artin isomorphism.
3. Verify that the resulting Galois characters are distinct and exhaust all characters of Gal(H/K) ≅ ℤ/2ℤ.

Prove: `Fintype.card (ClassGroup (𝓞 K) →* ℂˣ) = Fintype.card ((L ≃ₐ[K] L) →* ℂˣ)`.

**Success criterion.** A sorry-free proof that `artinCharacterTransport` is a bijection (not just surjective) on character groups, with explicit verification for at least one imaginary quadratic field.

**Failure criterion.** The character groups `G →* ℂˣ` are not finite types in Lean (since ℂˣ is uncountable), requiring a reformulation using roots of unity or finite target groups.

**Impact.** This is the GL(1) abelian Langlands correspondence in its most explicit form. Formalizing the bijection — not just the surjection proved in this cycle — would establish the complete abelian Langlands theorem for unramified characters, opening the path to ramified characters, higher-rank groups, and eventually non-abelian Langlands.

## Priority Ordering

1. **Hypothesis 3** (CM Generation): Most immediately tractable with current infrastructure. Even partial results (e.g., trivial class number cases) are valuable.
2. **Hypothesis 4** (Functoriality): Builds directly on existing `levelRaiseChar` and `AlgEquiv.restrictNormalHom` infrastructure.
3. **Hypothesis 5** (Hecke Correspondence): Extends `artinCharacterTransport_surjective` to bijectivity.
4. **Hypothesis 1** (Ray Class Fields): Requires significant new infrastructure but has the highest long-term impact.
5. **Hypothesis 2** (Transfer Map): Most technically demanding, requiring group-theoretic constructions not yet in Mathlib.
