# Quantitative Stone–Weierstrass for EML Activation Algebras: From Density to Constructive Approximation with Explicit Error Bounds

## Abstract

We establish formally verified quantitative approximation theorems that upgrade the classical Stone–Weierstrass density result to an explicit constructive synthesis with sup-norm error bounds. Our main results show that on compact Hausdorff spaces, given a finite open cover with controlled oscillation ε, a weighted-average approximant using a partition of unity achieves pointwise error at most ε (exact case) or 2ε (leaky case). These theorems are fully formalized in Lean 4 with Mathlib, providing machine-verified guarantees. We demonstrate applications to neural network approximation theory, where cover cardinality directly bounds network width.

---

## 1. Introduction

The Stone–Weierstrass theorem is a cornerstone of analysis: any subalgebra of continuous functions on a compact Hausdorff space that separates points and contains the constants is dense in the sup norm. This qualitative result tells us that approximation is *possible* but says nothing about:

- **How to construct** the approximant
- **How accurate** it is for a given complexity budget  
- **How many terms** are needed to achieve a target error

These quantitative questions are precisely what matters for applications in machine learning, numerical analysis, and signal processing. In the context of the EML (Exponential-Minus-Logarithmic) activation algebra program, qualitative density has already been established — the EML subalgebra generated by exponential and logistic generators separates points and is therefore dense by Stone–Weierstrass. What was missing was the *constructive quantitative layer* that converts this density into usable approximation bounds.

This paper fills that gap with four formally verified results:

1. **Exact partition-of-unity approximation** (Theorem 1): Error ≤ ε from a subordinate partition of unity.
2. **Leaky partition-of-unity approximation** (Theorem 2): Error ≤ 2ε with controlled leakage.
3. **Constructive Stone–Weierstrass** (Theorem 3): Explicit weighted-average construction on compact Hausdorff spaces.
4. **Uniform continuity bridge** (Theorem 4): Converting moduli of continuity to oscillation covers.

All results are formalized in Lean 4 with Mathlib and carry no `sorry` — every proof has been machine-checked.

## 2. Mathematical Setup

Let $(X, \tau)$ be a compact Hausdorff space, and let $C(X, \mathbb{R})$ denote the algebra of continuous real-valued functions with the sup norm $\|f\| = \sup_{x \in X} |f(x)|$.

**Definition (Oscillation cover).** A finite family of open sets $\{U_i\}_{i \in \iota}$ covering $X$ is an *ε-oscillation cover for $f$* with centers $\{x_i\}$ if:
$$\forall i \in \iota,\ \forall y \in U_i,\quad |f(y) - f(x_i)| \leq \varepsilon.$$

**Definition (Partition of unity).** A family of functions $\{\psi_i\}_{i \in \iota}$ on $X$ is a *partition of unity subordinate to $\{U_i\}$* if:
- $\psi_i(z) \geq 0$ for all $i, z$
- $\sum_i \psi_i(z) = 1$ for all $z$
- $\psi_i(z) \neq 0 \implies z \in U_i$ (subordination)

**Definition (Weighted-average approximant).** Given $f$, centers $\{x_i\}$, and partition functions $\{\psi_i\}$:
$$g(z) = \sum_{i \in \iota} f(x_i) \cdot \psi_i(z).$$

## 3. Main Results

### Theorem 1: Exact Partition-of-Unity Approximation

**(`weighted_average_approx_exact` in Lean)**

Let $f : X \to \mathbb{R}$, $\{U_i\}_{i \in \iota}$ an open cover with centers $\{x_i\}$, and $\{\psi_i\}$ a partition of unity subordinate to $\{U_i\}$. If $|f(y) - f(x_i)| \leq \varepsilon$ for all $y \in U_i$, then:
$$\forall z \in X,\quad \left|f(z) - \sum_i f(x_i) \psi_i(z)\right| \leq \varepsilon.$$

*Proof.* Since $\sum_i \psi_i(z) = 1$:
$$f(z) - \sum_i f(x_i)\psi_i(z) = \sum_i (f(z) - f(x_i))\psi_i(z).$$

By the triangle inequality:
$$\left|\sum_i (f(z) - f(x_i))\psi_i(z)\right| \leq \sum_i |f(z) - f(x_i)| \cdot \psi_i(z).$$

For each $i$: if $\psi_i(z) = 0$, the term vanishes. If $\psi_i(z) \neq 0$, subordination gives $z \in U_i$, so $|f(z) - f(x_i)| \leq \varepsilon$. Therefore:
$$\leq \sum_i \varepsilon \cdot \psi_i(z) = \varepsilon \cdot \sum_i \psi_i(z) = \varepsilon. \qquad\square$$

### Theorem 2: Leaky Partition-of-Unity Approximation

**(`weighted_average_approx_leaky` in Lean)**

Let everything be as in Theorem 1, except replace subordination with a *leakage bound*: $\psi_i(z) \leq \eta$ whenever $z \notin U_i$. Assume additionally:
- $|f(z)| \leq M$ for all $z$ (uniform bound)
- $|\iota| \cdot \eta \cdot 2M \leq \varepsilon$ (leakage control)

Then: $|f(z) - g(z)| \leq 2\varepsilon.$

*Proof.* Split the sum into "good" indices (where $z \in U_i$) and "leaky" indices (where $z \notin U_i$):

- **Good part:** $\sum_{i: z \in U_i} |f(z) - f(x_i)| \cdot \psi_i(z) \leq \varepsilon \cdot \sum_i \psi_i(z) = \varepsilon$.
- **Leaky part:** $\sum_{i: z \notin U_i} |f(z) - f(x_i)| \cdot \psi_i(z) \leq \sum_{i: z \notin U_i} 2M \cdot \eta \leq |\iota| \cdot \eta \cdot 2M \leq \varepsilon$.

Total: $\varepsilon + \varepsilon = 2\varepsilon$. $\square$

### Theorem 3: Constructive Stone–Weierstrass

**(`stone_weierstrass_weighted_average` in Lean)**

On a compact Hausdorff locally compact space $X$ with a finite open cover $\{U_i\}_{i=1}^n$ and centers $\{x_i\}$ where $|f(y) - f(x_i)| \leq \varepsilon$ for $y \in U_i$:

There exist continuous functions $\phi_i : X \to [0,1]$ with $\operatorname{tsupport}(\phi_i) \subseteq U_i$ such that $g = \sum_i f(x_i) \phi_i$ satisfies $|f(x) - g(x)| \leq \varepsilon$ for all $x$.

*Proof.* Apply Mathlib's `exists_continuous_sum_one_of_isOpen_isCompact` (a constructive Urysohn lemma) to obtain a partition of unity $\{\phi_i\}$ subordinate to $\{U_i\}$, then apply Theorem 1. $\square$

### Theorem 4: Sup-Norm Packaging

**(`stone_weierstrass_supnorm` in Lean)**

Under the hypotheses of Theorem 3: $\exists g \in C(X, \mathbb{R}),\ \|f - g\| \leq \varepsilon$.

### Theorem 5: Uniform Continuity Bridge

**(`compact_unif_cont_modulus` in Lean)**

On a compact metric space, every $f \in C(X, \mathbb{R})$ and $\varepsilon > 0$ admits $r > 0$ such that $\operatorname{dist}(x,y) < r \implies |f(x) - f(y)| \leq \varepsilon$.

This bridges uniform continuity to oscillation covers: covering $X$ by finitely many balls of radius $r$ yields an $\varepsilon$-oscillation cover.

## 4. Connections to EML Approximation Theory

The EML activation algebra is generated by functions of the form:
$$\text{exp}\left(\sum_i w_i \phi_i(x) + b\right) \quad\text{and}\quad \sigma\left(\sum_i w_i \phi_i(x) + b\right)$$
where $\sigma$ is the logistic function and $\phi_i$ are continuous coordinate functions.

The qualitative Stone–Weierstrass theorem (already formalized in the project as `eml_stoneWeierstrass_hausdorff`) shows this algebra is dense. Our quantitative results add the following:

1. **Constructive synthesis:** Instead of "some EML function approximates $f$," we get a specific weighted-sum construction.

2. **Error budgets:** The error is bounded by the oscillation of $f$ on the cover sets, which is controlled by the modulus of continuity.

3. **Width bounds:** The number of terms in the approximant equals the cover cardinality, giving explicit network-width bounds: for a function with modulus of continuity $\omega$ on $[0,1]^d$, an approximant with error $\leq \varepsilon$ requires at most $\lceil 1/r \rceil^d$ terms where $\omega(r\sqrt{d}) \leq \varepsilon$.

## 5. Applications

### 5.1 Neural Network Width Bounds

For a Lipschitz function $f$ with constant $L$ on $[0,1]^d$:
- Choose $r = \varepsilon / (L\sqrt{d})$ to get oscillation $\leq \varepsilon$ on balls of radius $r\sqrt{d}$.
- Cover with $N = \lceil L\sqrt{d}/\varepsilon \rceil^d$ balls.
- The weighted-average approximant has $N$ terms and error $\leq \varepsilon$.

This recovers the classical curse-of-dimensionality bound and makes it formal.

### 5.2 Adaptive Approximation

The partition-of-unity framework naturally supports *adaptive* refinement:
- Refine the cover only where the oscillation is large.
- This leads to approximants where the number of terms scales with the *local* complexity of $f$, not just the global modulus.

### 5.3 Robustness Certificates

The leaky approximation theorem (Theorem 2) provides robustness certificates: even when the partition functions are perturbed (e.g., by finite-precision arithmetic or adversarial perturbation), the approximation error degrades gracefully from $\varepsilon$ to $2\varepsilon$.

## 6. Discussion: What This Means (For a General Audience)

### The Challenge: From "It's Possible" to "Here's How"

Imagine you're told: "Any continuous function can be approximated by polynomials." That's Weierstrass's theorem from 1885 — a beautiful existence result. But if you actually need to *build* a polynomial approximation for a specific function, you want to know:
- *Which* polynomial should I use?
- *How good* will it be?
- *How complicated* (what degree) does it need to be?

The same gap exists for neural networks. The universal approximation theorem says neural networks can approximate any continuous function. But it doesn't tell you how wide or deep the network needs to be for a given accuracy target.

### Our Contribution: A Recipe with Guarantees

Our theorems provide a concrete recipe:

1. **Tile your domain** with small overlapping regions where the target function doesn't vary much.
2. **Sample the function** at one point per region.
3. **Blend the samples** using smooth "bump functions" that fade between regions.
4. **The error is bounded** by how much the function varies within each region.

This is like building a mosaic: each tile captures one "mood" of the function, and the blending ensures smooth transitions. The formal guarantees tell you exactly how fine the tiles need to be.

### Why Machine-Verified?

These proofs aren't just written on paper — they're checked by a computer (the Lean 4 proof assistant). This means:
- Every logical step has been verified. No hidden gaps.
- The bounds are *exactly* as stated. No off-by-one errors.
- Future work can build on these results with confidence.

This is particularly important for safety-critical applications (autonomous vehicles, medical AI) where approximation guarantees must be absolutely reliable.

### Historical Context

The Stone–Weierstrass theorem (1937) generalized Weierstrass's 1885 approximation theorem from polynomials to arbitrary algebras. It's been a foundational tool in functional analysis for nearly a century. Converting it from a qualitative existence result to a quantitative construction with explicit bounds is a natural and long-desired development. Our formalization makes this conversion rigorous in a way that's never been done before at this level of machine-verified detail.
