## Assignment: Algebra–Pythagorean–Geometry Gravitational Tropical Lens Rigidity via Berggren Height Potentials and Canonical Factorization Caustics

**Mode:** prove

Build a genuinely new formal bridge between three worlds that almost never meet in one theorem:  
1. the **Berggren tree** of primitive Pythagorean triples,  
2. **tropical/min-plus optics** on weighted trees, and  
3. **arithmetic reconstruction** of factorization signatures from geometric caustic data.

The breakthrough is not “another invariant on triples.” The breakthrough is to show that **factorization information can be encoded as a tropical optical profile on the Berggren tree and then rigidly recovered from its caustics**. If successful, this opens a new formal research direction: **tropical arithmetic lensing**.

You should aim for three linked theorem families:

1. **Functoriality and monotonicity of tropical lens profiles** under Berggren child maps and valuation refinements.
2. **Caustic rigidity**: equality of canonical lens/caustic profiles forces equality of prime-support signatures, with exact recovery in the squarefree regime.
3. **Certified reconstruction algorithm**: a finite caustic certificate yields a finite candidate factor set together with a formal soundness theorem.

The point is to turn “factorization from arithmetic data” into “factorization from tropical geometry on a canonical arithmetic tree.”

## Proof Strategy Architecture

You must not pursue a single route. Develop at least three proof paths and decide early which one is the formal backbone.

### Strategy A: Direct min-plus tree dynamics + separating test primes
**Most promising for formalization.**

1. **Define a finite-depth admissible window family** on the Berggren tree and prove every profile value is realized by a finite path. This avoids infinite minimization issues.
2. **Show child maps increase or control height** using existing Berggren monotonicity / valuation lemmas. Push this to path sums and then to profile monotonicity.
3. **Construct prime-separating windows**: for each prime `p` in the support of `n`, build a depth/window where the caustic contribution of triples with `p`-interaction is uniquely visible. Then equality of profiles forces equality of prime supports.

Why this is promising:
- It is combinatorial, finite, and Lean-friendly.
- It leverages `Finset`, path sums, monotonicity, and divisibility lemmas.
- It avoids difficult abstract tropical geometry while still delivering the breakthrough theorem.

### Strategy B: Min-plus matrix / dynamic programming formulation
Potentially more elegant and stronger algorithmically.

1. Encode Berggren child transitions as a weighted directed graph or finite-depth automaton.
2. Express the lens profile as a **min-plus transfer matrix** or shortest-path semiring computation.
3. Use matrix factorization / path decomposition to prove canonicity of the caustic profile and derive reconstruction from exposed minimizers.

Why it is powerful:
- Gives an actual certified algorithm almost for free.
- Connects directly to tropical linear algebra and automata theory.
- May scale to asymptotic complexity statements later.

Why it is harder:
- Requires more infrastructure around min-plus matrices and graph shortest paths.
- If the catalog lacks tropical matrix lemmas, this may cost time.

### Strategy C: Valuation sheaf / support-detection abstraction
Most visionary, but likely second-phase.

1. Treat prime-support interaction as a valuation observable on Berggren states.
2. Show the caustic profile is the lower envelope of a family of valuation-affine functions.
3. Prove equality of envelopes implies equality of exposed valuation supports, hence equality of prime-support signatures.

Why it matters:
- This is the conceptual route to a future “tropical arithmetic microlocal analysis.”
- It reframes the result as rigidity of lower envelopes, not just a tree combinatorics trick.

Why it is risky:
- Harder to make computationally explicit in Lean on the first pass.

**Recommendation:** Build the formal core with Strategy A, use Strategy B for the extraction algorithm, and frame Strategy C in FUTURE_DIRECTIONS.

def tropMul {n : ℕ} (hn : 0 < n) (A B : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℝ) :
    Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℝ :=
  fun i j => Finset.univ.inf' (univ_nonempty_iff.mpr ⟨⟨0, hn⟩⟩)
    (fun k => A i k + B k j)

theorem tropMul_entry_le {n : ℕ} (hn : 0 < n) (A B : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℝ)
    (i j k : Fin n) : tropMul hn A B i j ≤ A i k + B k j :=
  Finset.inf'_le _ (Finset.mem_univ k)

theorem tropMul_exists_witness {n : ℕ} (hn : 0 < n) (A B : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℝ)
    (i j : Fin n) : ∃ k, tropMul hn A B i j = A i k + B k j := by
  obtain ⟨k, _, hk⟩ := Finset.exists_mem_eq_inf' (univ_nonempty_iff.mpr ⟨⟨0, hn⟩⟩)
    (fun k => A i k + B k j)
