### Exact Breakthrough Target

Let `T` be the Berggren-generated family of primitive Pythagorean triples, truncated by height bound `H`. Assume each triple `t` is assigned a symmetric integral Gram form `G_t` and an embedded vector `v_t` in a quadratic space compatible with the already verified Lorentz product structure. Define a canonical quadratic defect/height functional
\[
\delta_H(x,t) := Q_t(x-v_t) + \lambda \,\mathrm{ht}(t),
\]
for a fixed canonical normalization `λ ≥ 0` chosen so that Berggren descendants have controlled defect growth.

The breakthrough theorem is that, for finite Berggren families, **Voronoi membership, Delaunay adjacency, and certified closest-vector decoding are all equivalent manifestations of the same arithmetic geometry**.

### Precise Theorem Statement

You should formalize a theorem of the following shape, with the finite-height truncation made explicit and all finiteness hypotheses stated constructively:

> **Theorem (Berggren Voronoi–CVP Duality).**  
> Let `H : ℕ`. Let `S_H` be the finite set of primitive Pythagorean triples of Berggren height at most `H`. Suppose:
> 1. each `t ∈ S_H` is assigned a symmetric positive-definite integral Gram form `G_t`,
> 2. each `t ∈ S_H` is assigned an embedding vector `v_t` in a common quadratic lattice `Λ`,
> 3. Berggren parent/child maps act by integral affine isometries or uniformly controlled quasi-isometries on these embedded data,
> 4. the quadratic defect functional `defect x t` is induced by `G_t` and `v_t`.
>
> Then:
> 1. the Voronoi cells
>    \[
>    \mathrm{Vor}(t)=\{x \in \Lambda_\mathbb{R} : \forall s\in S_H,\ defect(x,t)\le defect(x,s)\}
>    \]
>    form a locally finite Berggren-equivariant polyhedral decomposition of the convex hull of the embedded family;
> 2. if `Vor(t)` and `Vor(s)` share a codimension-1 boundary, then `t` and `s` are related by a bounded elementary Berggren mutation;
> 3. for every target `x`, membership `x ∈ Vor(t)` is equivalent to `t` minimizing `defect x ·` over `S_H`;
> 4. this minimization is equivalent to a closest-vector problem in the lattice generated by the triple Gram data, and the minimizer carries a deterministic correctness certificate given by the Voronoi inequalities.

The key point is not merely existence of a minimizer in a finite set, but a **functorial equivalence** between:
- Berggren combinatorics,
- Voronoi/Delaunay geometry,
- and certified CVP decoding.

### Proof Strategy Architecture

#### Strategy A: Finite Voronoi-by-Argmin Formalization
Most promising for Lean-first execution.

1. **Constructive finite family and argmin theorem**  
   Use `berggren_certified_enumeration_depth_bound` to define `berggrenFamilyUpTo H` as a `Finset`. Prove that for any `x`, the function `t ↦ tripleDefect x t` attains a minimum on this finite set.

2. **Define Voronoi cells by universal inequalities**  
   Define `berggrenVoronoiCell H t` directly as the set of points where `t` is defect-minimizing. Then the theorem
   `x ∈ berggrenVoronoiCell H t ↔ isCertifiedNearest H x t`
   becomes almost tautological, but crucially formal and reusable.

3. **Extract Delaunay adjacency and CVP interpretation**  
   Define adjacency by nonempty intersection of equality loci or shared minimizing sets. Then show that minimizing defect is equivalent to minimizing distance in the embedded Gram lattice. This gives a deterministic decoder plus certificate.

Why this is promising: it aligns perfectly with Lean’s strengths—finite combinatorics, ordered argmin reasoning, and explicit certificates—while still delivering a conceptually new theorem.

#### Strategy B: Quadratic-Form Geometry from Lorentz Invariants
Best for stronger mathematics after the first theorem lands.

1. **Derive explicit defect identities**  
   Use `berggren_triple_product_lorentz` to express `tripleDefect x t` via Lorentz/Gram invariants. Show that pairwise defect comparisons reduce to affine half-space inequalities.

2. **Prove polyhedrality and local finiteness**  
   Because each pairwise comparison `defect x t ≤ defect x s` is affine/quadratic with controlled sign, Voronoi cells become intersections of finitely many half-spaces in the truncated family. This yields the Delaunay complex.

3. **Relate codimension-1 faces to elementary Berggren moves**  
   Show that only bounded mutations can produce adjacent cells, because larger Berggren distance forces a strict invariant gap in defect or height.

Why this matters: this is the route to a truly geometric theorem, not merely a finite-search equivalence.

#### Strategy C: Certified Robustness as Decoding Correctness
Most powerful for cryptographic interpretation.

1. **Winner-gap implies stable decoding**  
   Define the defect margin
   \[
   \mathrm{gap}(x,t)=\inf_{s\neq t} (\mathrm{defect}(x,s)-\mathrm{defect}(x,t)).
   \]
   Show `gap(x,t) > 0` implies uniqueness and local stability of the decoded triple.

2. **Use Lipschitz control to certify perturbation tolerance**  
   Apply the robustness theorem pattern from the deep-learning bridge: if the defect map is Lipschitz and the margin is positive, decoding is stable under bounded perturbation.

3. **Package as a cryptographic primitive**  
   The decoder outputs not just a nearest triple but a proof witness: a finite family of inequalities certifying correctness and perturbation tolerance.

Why this is revolutionary: it reframes CVP-style decoding as certified geometric classification on arithmetic data.
