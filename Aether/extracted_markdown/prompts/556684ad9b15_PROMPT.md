## Core Vision

A Lorentzian polynomial is not merely “stable under perturbation”; it has an intrinsic **conditioning profile** determined by the spectral geometry of its quadratic leaves. If this profile can be extracted into a computable invariant `κ(f)`, then:

1. **Certified robustness** becomes quantitative: coefficient perturbations below `1 / κ(f)` preserve Lorentzianity.
2. **Sampling complexity** becomes geometric: Markov chains associated to the induced strongly log-concave distribution mix at a rate controlled by `κ(f)`.
3. **Uniform matroids become the calibration point**: the known `m^2` radius should emerge as the canonical finite-dimensional norm-conversion case of the general theory.

This would open a new field: **condition-number theory for Lorentzian combinatorics**. The breakthrough is not another stability lemma; it is a unifying principle that lets algebraic structure predict algorithmic behavior.

## Precise theorem statements to target

Below are theorem-level targets. You do not need to use exactly these names, but the statements should be this precise.

### Theorem 1: Certified perturbation radius from condition number

**Mathematical statement.**  
Let `f` be a Lorentzian polynomial with positive quadratic leaf gaps. Let `κ(f)` be any certified upper bound on the ratio
\[
\max_\alpha \frac{\|H_\alpha\|_{\mathrm{op}}}{\mathrm{gap}(H_\alpha)}.
\]
Then every coefficient perturbation `δ` with sufficiently small induced Hessian operator norm preserves Lorentzianity; in particular, if the coefficient-to-Hessian map is bounded by `C`, then
\[
\|\delta\|_\infty < \frac{1}{C\,\kappa(f)}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
f+\delta \text{ is Lorentzian.}
\]

**Lean 4 type signature sketch.**
```lean
theorem lorentzian_perturbation_radius_of_condition
  {n : ℕ}
  (f δ : MvPolynomial (Fin n) ℝ)
  (κ C : ℝ)
  (hκ : CertifiedLorentzianConditionNumber f ≤ κ)
  (hC : HessianCoeffLInfBound f ≤ C)
  (hsmall : coeffLInfNorm δ < (C * κ)⁻¹)
  (hLor : IsLorentzian f) :
  IsLorentzian (f + δ)
```

If the exact notions above are not yet available, formulate a theorem for the catalog’s existing certified stability predicate and prove a bridge theorem showing that your new condition number implies the catalog certification hypotheses.

### Theorem 3: Condition number controls discrete curvature / one-step contraction surrogate

A full mixing-time theorem may be too ambitious immediately in Lean, but you should at minimum prove a rigorous theorem connecting `κ(f)` to a certified contraction or curvature lower bound for a local update chain or for the Hessian geometry of `log f`.

**Mathematical statement.**  
For a Lorentzian polynomial `f` with certified condition number `κ`, the negative Hessian of the log-density on a positive slice admits a lower bound of order `1/κ`; equivalently, a curvature surrogate used in Bakry–Émery-type arguments is bounded below by `1/κ`.

This can be formalized as a continuous geometric theorem first, then interpreted algorithmically.

**Lean 4 type signature sketch.**
```lean

  {n : ℕ}
  (f : MvPolynomial (Fin n) ℝ)
  (κ : ℝ)
  (hLor : IsLorentzian f)

  ∀ x ∈ positiveOrthantDomain f,
```


and prove

## Strongest proof architecture: 3 candidate strategies

You must include 2–3 proof approaches in your work notes and choose one as primary. Here are the ones most worth pursuing.

### Strategy A: Leafwise Hessian perturbation + spectral gap transfer
**Most promising.**

1. Define the quadratic leaf Hessian `Hα(f)` and show that coefficient perturbations induce Hessian perturbations bounded by a linear operator:
   \[
   \|H_\alpha(f+\delta)-H_\alpha(f)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
   \le C_\alpha \|\delta\|_\infty.
   \]
   Use the catalog theorem `hessian_entry_bound_from_coeff_perturbation` and convert entrywise bounds to operator norm bounds via dimension factors.

2. Prove a spectral-gap stability lemma:
   if `‖ΔH‖op < gap(H)`, then the signature/Lorentzian property of the quadratic leaf is preserved. This is where multi-step `calc`, `by_contra`, and matrix norm inequalities should appear.

3. Take the supremum over leaves to obtain a global certified radius. Then instantiate to uniform matroids and recover the `m^2` scale.

**Why this is best:** it is structurally aligned with the catalog, computable, and naturally yields an algorithm.

### Strategy B: Direct certificate refinement via existing `certifyStability_sound`
1. Reverse-engineer the hypotheses needed by `certifyStability_sound`.
2. Prove that your new condition number implies those hypotheses through a chain of lemmas converting:
   coefficient norm → Hessian entry norm → operator norm → leaf spectral gap preservation.
3. Package this into a reusable theorem:
   `CertifiedLorentzianConditionNumber f ≤ κ → certifyStability ...`.

**Why useful:** maximizes code reuse and reduces foundational burden.  
**Why secondary:** it may obscure the conceptual heart unless you also prove independent mathematical lemmas.

### Strategy C: Log-concavity curvature route
1. Define a positive-domain log-density from coefficients or evaluations of `f`.
2. Express the Hessian of `log f` in terms of derivatives of `f`, then bound it using leafwise Hessian data.
3. Deduce a curvature lower bound or a contraction surrogate relevant to Glauber-type or heat-bath chains.

**Why exciting:** this is the true algorithmic bridge.  
**Why risky:** formalizing analytic derivatives and MCMC in Lean may be heavier than the perturbation theory itself. Best as a theorem on a surrogate curvature quantity, with full mixing-time consequences stated in `RESEARCH_PAPER.md`.
