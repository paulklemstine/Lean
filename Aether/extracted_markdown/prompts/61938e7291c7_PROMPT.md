### Research Direction

**Conjecture (β-Class Structural Canonicity):** For every pair of closed well-typed terms `t, u : A` with `BetaEq t u`, there exists a uniform depth `d₀` depending only on `max(normDepth t, normDepth u)` such that for all `d ≥ d₀`, the strong bisimulation quotients of `toFTS d t` and `toFTS d u` are **isomorphic as labeled transition systems** — not merely equal in cardinality. This is the λ-calculus analogue of the Myhill–Nerode theorem: finite observation structures completely and canonically characterize equivalence.

**Precise Theorem Statement (Lean 4 type signature):**

```lean
-- New structure: LTS isomorphism (not just cardinality equality)
structure LTSIso (S₁ S₂ : LTS Label) where
  stateEquiv : S₁.states ≃ S₂.states
  transitionComm : ∀ s₁ lbl s₁',
    S₁.step s₁ lbl s₁' →
    S₂.step (stateEquiv.toFun s₁) lbl (stateEquiv.toFun s₁')

-- Main theorem: β-equivalent terms yield isomorphic bisimulation quotients
-- at sufficient depth
theorem betaEq_iso_canonicalQuotient {A : Ty} {t u : Term}
    (ht : Closed t) (hu : Closed u)
    (hAt : Typed [] t A) (hAu : Typed [] u A)
    (heq : BetaEq t u) :
    ∃ d₀ : ℕ, ∀ d ≥ d₀,
      Nonempty (LTSIso
        (bisimQuotient (toFTS d t ht hAt))
        (bisimQuotient (toFTS d u hu hAu)))
```

**Key Intermediate Lemma (Reduction DAG Shape Invariance):**

```lean
-- The shape of the reduction DAG to the normal form is β-invariant
-- This is the crucial bridge from cardinality to structure
theorem reductionDAG_shape_invariant {A : Ty} {t u : Term}
    (ht : Closed t) (hu : Closed u)
    (hAt : Typed [] t A) (hAu : Typed [] u A)
    (heq : BetaEq t u) (d : ℕ) (hd : d ≥ max (normDepth t) (normDepth u) + 1) :
    LTS.similarity
      (toFTS d t ht hAt)
      (toFTS d u hu hAu)
```

where `LTS.similarity` denotes strong bisimilarity (not just bisimulation quotient size equality).

**Test:** Exhaustive enumeration of all closed well-typed terms of type depth ≤ 3 and term size ≤ 12. For each β-equivalence class, compute the bisimulation quotient at `d = max(normDepth) + 2` and verify structural isomorphism using a canonical labeling algorithm (e.g., nauty-based canonical form). The test must verify that *isomorphic* quotients arise, not merely equinumerous ones.

**Impact:** This upgrades our current result (quotient size equality for normal forms) to full structural canonicity for arbitrary terms. It establishes that bisimulation quotients are **complete invariants** of β-equivalence at sufficient depth — the λ-calculus analogue of the Myhill–Nerode characterization of regular languages, where minimal DFAs are unique up to isomorphism. This opens the door to: (1) decidability of β-equivalence via FTS comparison at bounded depth, (2) canonical representatives for β-equivalence classes, and (3) a coalgebraic foundation for observational equivalence in type theory.

### Proof Strategy (Three Paths)

**Strategy A: Normal Form Anchor + Confluence (Most Promising)**
1. By strong normalization (Church–Rosser), every β-equivalence class has a unique normal form `nf`. Both `t` and `u` reduce to `nf`.
2. At depth `d ≥ max(normDepth t, normDepth u) + 1`, the FTS of each term captures the **complete reduction DAG** from that term to `nf`, including all intermediate redexes.
3. The key lemma: confluence ensures that any two reduction paths to `nf` yield **bisimilar** intermediate structures. Formally, parallel reduction (Takahashi-style) commutes with FTS construction: if `t ⇒ t'` via parallel reduction, then `toFTS d t` simulates `toFTS d t'` and vice versa.
4. Transitivity of simulation then gives `LTS.similarity (toFTS d t) (toFTS d u)`, which implies isomorphic bisimulation quotients.
5. **Why most promising:** We already have `betaEq_typed_same_observations` and `betaEq_preserves_canonicalQuotientSize` in the catalog. The extension from "same observation count" to "same observation structure" is a natural strengthening that reuses the confluence infrastructure.

**Strategy B: Final Coalgebra Characterization**
1. The category of finitely-branching LTS has a final coalgebra `ν(L)` — the "universal observable behavior."
2. The bisimulation quotient `bisimQuotient (toFTS d t)` is the image of `toFTS d t` under the unique morphism to `ν(L)`.
3. If `BetaEq t u`, then both terms map to the same element of `ν(L)` at sufficient depth (because β-equivalence is contained in observational equivalence).
4. Finality forces the quotient structures to be isomorphic, not merely equinumerous.
5. **Challenge:** Requires building coalgebraic infrastructure for LTS in Lean; high upfront cost but yields a more conceptual proof.

**Strategy C: Direct Structural Induction on Reduction Sequences**
1. Define a "reduction shape" function `shape : Term → LTS Label` that captures the tree of possible reductions up to depth `d`.
2. Prove that `shape` is invariant under parallel reduction: `t ⇒ t' → shape t ≅ shape t'` (bisimulation isomorphism).
3. Compose invariance along the reduction sequence from `t` to `nf` and from `u` to `nf`.
4. **Challenge:** The induction on reduction sequences is subtle because reduction can duplicate subterms; requires careful treatment of substitution.

**Recommended approach:** Start with Strategy A, using `betaEq_preserves_canonicalQuotientSize` from `Pythagorean/BisimMinimization.lean` as a lemma. The key new ingredient is proving that **simulation** (not just same-size quotient) holds between `toFTS d t` and `toFTS d u`, which then upgrades to isomorphism of quotients by the standard result that bisimilar LTS have isomorphic minimal realizations.

    (∀ s₁, ∃ s₂, R s₁ s₂)

def betaClassDepth {A : Ty} (t u : Term)

### Falsifiable Conjecture

**Conjecture (Tight Depth Bound):** The minimal sufficient depth `d₀` for isomorphic quotients equals `max(normDepth t, normDepth u) + 1`, and this bound is tight: there exist β-equivalent terms `t, u` where `d = max(normDepth t, normDepth u)` yields merely equinumerous (not isomorphic) quotients.

**Computational test:** Enumerate all β-equivalent pairs of terms with `normDepth ≤ 3` and `size ≤ 15`. For each pair, compute:
- `Q_d = bisimQuotient (toFTS d t)` for `d = max(normDepth t, normDepth u)` and `d = max + 1`
- Check if `Q_d` at `d = max` is merely equinumerous but not isomorphic
- Check if `Q_d` at `d = max + 1` is always isomorphic

A single counterexample at `d = max + 1` (non-isomorphic quotients) falsifies the main conjecture. A single example at `d = max` (non-isomorphic but equinumerous quotients) confirms tightness.
