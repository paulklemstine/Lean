## Assignment: Key Lemma / Breakthrough Direction

Prove a **structural uniqueness theorem for cherry pairs from the metric alone**, and then use it as the combinatorial spine for a **stability theorem for noisy reconstruction**. The point is not merely to show that some tree exists realizing `D`, but that the first nontrivial combinatorial feature of the realization — its cherries — is already encoded in `D` itself. This is the tree-metric analogue of the statement that a point in the relative interior of a maximal cone of a tropical moduli space determines a unique combinatorial type.

This should be attacked as a **field-opening bridge theorem** between:

- finite tree metrics / phylogenetic reconstruction,
- tropical geometry of `Gr(2,n)` and tropical moduli,
- metric rigidity / identifiability,
- and eventually robust reconstruction under perturbation.

The theorem below is the right fulcrum: if cherry pairs are intrinsic to `D`, then reduced-tree uniqueness becomes a metric theorem, not a representation theorem.

## Primary Target: Cherry pairs are determined by `D` alone

### Mathematical Statement

Let `D : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℝ` be a tree metric, and let `T₁`, `T₂` be two reduced leaf-labeled trees realizing `D`. Then the set of cherry pairs in `T₁` equals the set of cherry pairs in `T₂`.

A stronger and more formalizable version is:

> For any pair of leaves `a ≠ b`, whether `a,b` form a cherry is expressible purely in terms of the metric `D`; hence it is invariant across all reduced realizations of `D`.

This is the theorem that upgrades cherry detection from “there exists a cherry somewhere” to “every cherry is metrically canonical.”

### Precise Lean 4 Target

You will need to adapt names to the actual tree API, but aim for a theorem of this shape:

```lean
/-- Being a cherry pair is determined by the realized distance matrix alone. -/
theorem cherry_pair_metric_invariant
    {n : ℕ}
    (D : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℝ)
    (T₁ T₂ : LBTree (Fin n))
    (hred₁ : T₁.Reduced)
    (hred₂ : T₂.Reduced)
    (hreal₁ : realizes_distance_matrix T₁ D)
    (hreal₂ : realizes_distance_matrix T₂ D) :
    ∀ {a b : Fin n}, a ≠ b →
      (is_cherry_pair T₁ a b ↔ is_cherry_pair T₂ a b)
```

A set-theoretic corollary should also be produced:

```lean
/-- Any two reduced realizations of the same tree metric have the same cherries. -/
theorem cherry_pairs_unique_of_reduced_realization
    {n : ℕ}
    (D : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℝ)
    (T₁ T₂ : LBTree (Fin n))
    (hred₁ : T₁.Reduced)
    (hred₂ : T₂.Reduced)
    (hreal₁ : realizes_distance_matrix T₁ D)
    (hreal₂ : realizes_distance_matrix T₂ D) :
    cherryPairSet T₁ = cherryPairSet T₂
```

If the current library is not yet organized around `cherryPairSet`, formulate the pointwise equivalence theorem first and derive set equality by extensionality.

## Proof strategy architecture

You should pursue **two main proof routes in parallel**, and a third fallback route if the API is awkward.

### Strategy A: Metric characterization of cherries
Most promising if the current library has enough matrix/algebra support.

**Goal:** Prove a criterion `cherry_metric_criterion D a b` depending only on `D`, then show:
```lean
is_cherry_pair T a b ↔ cherry_metric_criterion D a b
```
for any reduced realization `T` of `D`.

A classical shape of such a criterion is that for a cherry `{a,b}`, the quantity
`D a k + D b l - D a b - D k l`
has special behavior, or equivalently that the “limb length difference”
`D a k - D b k`
is independent of `k` away from `a,b`. Another common formulation is:
for all distinct `k,l` not equal to `a,b`,
```text
D a k + D b l = D a l + D b k
```
or a suitable inequality/equality pattern derived from the four-point condition.

#### Concrete steps
1. **Extract a metric-only cherry criterion.**  
   Formalize a predicate on `D,a,b` that is necessary for `a,b` to be a cherry in any realization.
2. **Prove sufficiency in reduced trees.**  
   Show that in a reduced realization, the criterion forces `a,b` to share a parent/internal attachment pattern.
3. **Transfer across realizations.**  
   Since the criterion depends only on `D`, cherryhood is invariant.

**Why this is promising:**  
It gives the strongest theorem and directly sets up the noisy version: once cherryhood is characterized by inequalities with margin, perturbation arguments become possible.

---

### Strategy B: Recursive leaf-pruning + uniqueness of reduction data
Most promising if `cherry_pair_exists` is already strong and the tree API supports pruning.

**Idea:**  
A cherry can be pruned, producing a smaller reduced tree and a smaller distance matrix / induced metric. If two reduced realizations share one metrically detectable cherry, prune it in both trees and induct on `n`.

#### Concrete steps
1. **Show existence of a metrically detectable cherry pair.**  
   Use `cherry_pair_exists` together with a metric lemma showing that if `{a,b}` is a cherry in one realization, then the distances in `D` encode the corresponding reduction step.
2. **Define and compare reduced metrics after pruning.**  
   Construct the induced metric on `Fin (n-1)` or on a subtype of remaining leaves. Show both trees reduce to realizations of the same smaller metric.
3. **Induct and lift back.**  
   Apply the induction hypothesis to the pruned trees, then reintroduce the pruned cherry to recover equality of cherry sets in the original trees.

**Why this is promising:**  
This aligns with phylogenetic reconstruction algorithms and can scale to full uniqueness/isomorphism results. It also uses `cherry_pair_exists` in the most mathematically meaningful way.

---

### Strategy C: Isomorphism-first route
Most promising if the existing tree isomorphism infrastructure is already mature.

**Idea:**  
Prove that any two reduced leaf-labeled realizations of the same metric are isomorphic, then derive cherry invariance as a corollary.

#### Concrete steps
1. **Establish a canonical reconstruction from `D`.**  
   Even a partial canonical decomposition suffices.
2. **Show both `T₁` and `T₂` match this canonical decomposition.**
3. **Transport cherries through the isomorphism.**

**Why this may be best in the long run:**  
It gives a much stronger theorem than requested. But it may be too expensive for the current cycle unless the isomorphism API is already robust.

## Secondary Target: Noisy Stability Theorem

The noisy theorem in the draft is too vague as stated. Make it precise. The mathematically meaningful version is not just existential closeness, but **combinatorial stability under a metric gap condition**.

### Precise theorem statement

Let `D₀` be a tree metric realized by a reduced tree `T₀`. Assume every true cherry/non-cherry decision in `D₀` is separated by a positive margin `δ > 0` in the metric characterization from Strategy A. If `D` is entrywise `ε`-close to `D₀` with `ε < δ / C(n)` for an explicit constant, then:
- the cherry pairs inferred from `D` agree with those of `T₀`, and
- hence a reduced realizing tree for `D` (or an approximate reconstruction from `D`) has the same first combinatorial layer.

A Lean-facing skeleton:

```lean
/-- Stability of cherry detection under small perturbations of a separated tree metric. -/
theorem noisy_cherry_stability
    {n : ℕ}
    (D D₀ : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℝ)
    (ε δ : ℝ)
    (T₀ : LBTree (Fin n))
    (hreal₀ : realizes_distance_matrix T₀ D₀)
    (hred₀ : T₀.Reduced)
    (hclose : ∀ i j, |D i j - D₀ i j| ≤ ε)
    (hsep : cherry_separation_margin D₀ δ)
    (hε : ε < δ / 4) :
    ∀ {a b : Fin n}, a ≠ b →
      (stable_cherry_predicate D a b ↔ is_cherry_pair T₀ a b)
```

If exact realization of `D` is too strong under noise, weaken the conclusion to stable recovery of the cherry predicate rather than exact realization by a tree. That is already powerful and formalizable.

### Why this matters

This is the robust version of tropical combinatorial rigidity: points sufficiently deep inside a cone retain their combinatorial type under perturbation. In applications, this is what makes reconstruction meaningful with empirical distances.

## Cross-domain connections to emphasize in the development

### 1. Tropical geometry
Tree metrics are the tropicalization of `Gr(2,n)` / phylogenetic tree space phenomena. Cherry invariance corresponds to **combinatorial type rigidity on cone interiors**. The noisy version corresponds to **distance from the tropical discriminant / cone walls**.

A future theorem here is:

> If a tree metric lies in the relative interior of a maximal cone, then its reduced combinatorial type is unique.

Your cherry theorem is the first formal step toward that statement.

### 2. Moduli spaces
Reduced trees model points of tropical moduli spaces `M_{0,n}^{trop}`. Cherries are codimension-sensitive local combinatorial features. Proving they are metric-determined means the moduli point has intrinsic local coordinates visible from pairwise distances.

### 3. Algorithmic phylogenetics
Cherry detection is the atomic step in recursive reconstruction. A formal proof of metric invariance gives certified correctness for broad classes of reconstruction algorithms.

### 4. Robust statistics / inverse problems
The noisy theorem reframes phylogenetic reconstruction as a **stable inverse problem**. The separation margin plays the role of identifiability gap, analogous to condition numbers in numerical analysis and margin theory in learning theory.
