### Theorem 1: Finite-cylinder factorization on maximal compact
For every finite set `s : Finset ι` and local events `A : ∀ i, Set (G i)` with `A i ⊆ K i` on `i ∈ s`, the measure of the corresponding finite-coordinate event inside the maximal compact factors as the product of local normalized measures.

### Suggested Lean 4 statement shape
You may need to adapt names to the actual API, but the target should look structurally like:

```lean
theorem measure_inter_basicCylinder_eq_prod
    {ι : Type*} [DecidableEq ι]
    {G : ι → Type*}
    (K : ∀ i, Set (G i))
    (μ : Measure (RestrictedProduct G K))
    (s : Finset ι)
    (A : ∀ i, Set (G i))
    (hA : ∀ i, i ∈ s → A i ⊆ K i)
    (hmeas : ∀ i, MeasurableSet (A i)) :
    μ ((maximalCompact K) ∩ ⋂ i ∈ s, coordPreimage K i (A i))
      = ∏ i in s, localProb K μ i (A i)
```

Here `localProb K μ i (A i)` should be the normalized local mass `μ_i(A_i) / μ_i(K_i)` or its counting-measure analogue in the finite setting. If the current catalog is purely finite/cardinality-based, then formalize the counting version first:

```lean

    {ι : Type*} [DecidableEq ι]
    {G : ι → Type*} [∀ i, Fintype (G i)]
    (K : ∀ i, Finset (G i))
    (s : Finset ι)

      ∀ i ∈ s, coord x i ∈ A i}

    ∏ i in s, (A i).card
```

together with the normalization identity
```lean
theorem prob_maximalCompact_inter_basicCylinder_eq_prod
    ...
    :
    ((Fintype.card {x // ...}) : ℚ) / ((Fintype.card (maximalCompactType K)) : ℚ)
```

This is the theorem that turns restricted-product combinatorics into probability.

## Proof architecture: 3 serious strategy paths

You must not rely on trivial simplification. Use induction on finite supports, `rcases` on membership in cylinders, explicit decomposition of the restricted product, and multi-step `calc` chains. At least 3 theorems should require real proof structure.

### Strategy A: Induction on the finite support `s` of constrained coordinates
This is the most promising route in the finite/cardinality setting.

1. **Base case `s = ∅`:**
   Show the event is exactly `maximalCompact K`, hence has normalized probability `1`.
   This should require a real set-theoretic argument, not `rfl`: rewrite the empty intersection, identify the event, and use the normalization theorem.

2. **Inductive step `insert i s`:**
   Decompose the constrained event into a product of:
   - the condition at coordinate `i`,
   - the event on remaining coordinates `s`.
   
   Use the restricted-product structure on maximal compact to split cardinalities or measures. If the catalog theorem `finite_product_card` gives a cardinality formula for finite products, use it as the engine for the multiplicative step.

3. **Normalization step:**
   Divide by the cardinality/measure of maximal compact and use `field_simp`, nonvanishing of `(K i).card`, and `Finset.prod_insert` to obtain the product formula.

Why this is strongest: it mirrors the actual probabilistic factorization and scales cleanly from counting measure to Haar measure.

This route reveals the deep reason independence holds: **inside the maximal compact, the restricted product forgets its “restrictedness” and becomes an honest product law**.

Why it matters: this equivalence is likely to become a foundational lemma for later arithmetic-statistics formalization.
