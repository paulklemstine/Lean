## Concept

**Title**: Tropical VC dimension bounds from arithmetic-height valuation cells
**Domain**: Bridges
**Mathematical framing**: Work over rational points equipped with an arithmetic-height map and a finite tuple of valuation-like observables encoded through `TropicalValuationObject`. Define tropical halfspaces by pairwise comparison of affine tropical forms and define tropical concept classes as finite intersections/unions of such halfspaces. First prove normal-form lemmas reducing formulas to comparisons among finitely many affine valuation expressions. Next prove a finite cell decomposition theorem: on any finite sample, membership in each concept is determined by the induced weak order on a bounded set of valuation expressions. Then count the number of possible labelings by counting weak orders/cells, deducing a polynomial shatter bound. Finally package this as a bridge theorem from arithmetic-height-controlled tropical definability to statistical complexity. If formalization goes well, one can also derive monotonicity under coordinate projection and product bounds for combined tropical concept classes.
**Concept description**: The key insight is that the existing arithmetic-height measure and tropical valuation object infrastructure can be fused to give a genuinely new bridge theorem: finite families of tropical threshold sets defined by valuation inequalities should admit explicit VC-dimension upper bounds controlled by the number of valuation coordinates and the combinatorial complexity of their tropical cell decomposition. Why now: `Bridges/ArithmeticVCDimension.lean` already provides the arithmetic-height side (`ArithHeightMeasure`, `ratArithHeight`, positivity lemmas), while `Bridges/CategoricalTropicalUltrametric.lean` already provides tropical valuation objects (`TropicalValuationObject`, `TropObj`, `UltraNormObj`), and the catalog explicitly identifies Bridges <-> Tropical as a high-potential missing bridge. The concrete target is to define a class of subsets of rational points cut out by formulas of the form `v_i(x) + a_i ≤ v_j(x) + b_j` (or finite tropical minima comparisons), prove that each such family refines into finitely many valuation cells, and then prove a Sauer–Shelah style polynomial shatter bound implying a finite VC dimension estimate. A sharper theorem should show that if a family is generated by at most `k` tropical linear inequalities in `d` valuation coordinates, then its shatter function is bounded by a polynomial in `n` of degree at most `d`, hence VC dimension is at most a function of `d` and `k`. This is falsifiable: the cell-count lemma may fail without the right normal form, and the VC bound may require extra definability restrictions. If successful, it yields an algorithmic pipeline for learning/controlling tropical decision classes via arithmetic height certificates, rather than a one-off existence theorem.
