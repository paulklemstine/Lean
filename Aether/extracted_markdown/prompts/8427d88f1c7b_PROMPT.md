## Central Vision

Let `L` be a labeled transition system. Consider the presheaf/nerve object generated by finite traces of `L`, and the lattice of subobjects/sieves on this trace category up to bounded depth. The breakthrough theorem should show that:

1. **Deterministic branching** forces the subobject logic to be Boolean/classical.
2. **Nondeterministic branching** creates a witness subobject with no complement.
3. The corresponding **Lawvere–Tierney topology of bisimilarity collapse** is trivial exactly in the deterministic case.

This is the process-theoretic analogue of:
- Boolean logic ↔ classical observables,
- non-Boolean logic ↔ branching superposition of futures.

This opens a program in which **operational semantics is classified by internal logic**, with possible extensions to concurrency, modal logic, coalgebra, and even quantum-style semantics of branching systems.

### 3. Booleanity / complementedness of the observable lattice
A direct and Lean-friendly definition:
```lean

  ∀ S : NerveSubobject L, ∃ T : NerveSubobject L,
    IsCompl S T
```
or if you build an actual lattice instance on bounded-depth subobjects:

If `IsBooleanAlgebra` is too heavy for your representation, define:
```lean

  ∀ S : NerveSubobject L, ∃ T : NerveSubobject L,
    S ⊓ T = ⊥ ∧ S ⊔ T = ⊤
```

This is mathematically faithful and often easier to prove.

### 4. Identity topology associated with bisimilarity

Then state the topology theorem with this closure operator. If you can formalize an actual nucleus/Lawvere–Tierney operator on a Heyting algebra of subobjects, even better.

## Proof Architecture: 3 Viable Strategies

## Strategy A — Modal-lattice route from catalog theorems
**Most promising.**

1. Use `lts_diamond_conj_of_det` to show deterministic systems satisfy a distributive interaction law between modal operators and meets.
2. Upgrade this to a complementedness result for the lattice of bounded trace subobjects/sieves.
3. Use `det_of_diamond_conj` for the converse: if the lattice is Boolean/classical, then the relevant modal conjunction law holds, forcing determinism.
4. Use `sieve_nonBoolean` to produce the explicit obstruction in the nondeterministic case.

Why this is strongest:
- It directly leverages the catalog.
- It yields a theorem that is conceptually elegant: **classical internal logic = deterministic branching**.
- It naturally connects to the Lawvere–Tierney statement.

---

## Strategy B — Explicit witness construction from branching forks
1. Assume nondeterminism: obtain `s, a, t₁, t₂` with two distinct `a`-successors by `rcases` on `¬ FullyDeterministic L`.
2. Define a subobject `S` that selects traces/futures passing through one branch but not canonically through the other.
3. Prove by contradiction (`by_contra`) that no complement `T` can exist:
   - one branch forces `S ⊔ T = ⊤`,
   - the shared predecessor and action force overlap or incompleteness,
   - derive contradiction using stability/prefix closure.
4. In the deterministic case, construct complements recursively by induction on trace length.

Why this is valuable:
- It gives a **canonical obstruction object**.
- It makes the theorem computational and inspectable.
- It is ideal for the finite exhaustive test over systems with ≤ 4 states and ≤ 2 actions.

---

## Strategy C — Closure-operator / topology route
1. Define a bisimulation-induced closure/nucleus on bounded nerve subobjects.
2. Show deterministic systems have no nontrivial identification of futures, so closure is pointwise identity.
3. Show any branching fork induces a nontrivial closure expansion, hence the topology is not identity.
4. Deduce Booleanity/classicality from triviality of the closure operator, or prove the equivalence triangle:
   - determinism ⇒ identity closure,
   - identity closure ⇒ excluded middle,
   - excluded middle ⇒ determinism.

Why this matters:
- This is the most conceptually ambitious route.
- It upgrades the result from “lattice combinatorics” to **topos semantics of process theory**.
- It is the best bridge to future work on sheaves, concurrency, and semantics of observation.
