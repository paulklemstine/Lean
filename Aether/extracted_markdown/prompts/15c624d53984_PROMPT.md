## Assignment: Direction 1: Non-Multiaffine Extension via Weighted Support Analysis

**Mode:** prove

Build a field-opening extension of support compression from the multiaffine world to genuinely homogeneous polynomials with repeated exponents. The target is not a cosmetic generalization: it is a structural theorem saying that second-derivative complexity is governed by Newton-polytope shadow data, with a precise mechanism isolating when coefficient cancellation is the only obstruction.

This direction matters because multiaffineness is an artificial boundary from the perspective of algebraic geometry, Hodge theory, Lorentzian polynomials, and symbolic complexity. If you can show that the combinatorics of the Newton polytope still controls quadratic derivative leaves beyond the multiaffine regime, then support compression becomes a geometric principle rather than a combinatorial accident.

---

## Core Vision

For a homogeneous polynomial
\[
f(x_1,\dots,x_n)=\sum_{\alpha\in \mathbb{N}^n,\ |\alpha|=d} c_\alpha x^\alpha,
\]
the multiaffine catalog theorem identifies the nonzero quadratic derivative leaves with support data. In the non-multiaffine setting, the new phenomenon is **collision of descendants**: distinct monomials of \(f\) can produce the same monomial after taking two derivatives, and coefficients may cancel.

The correct next object is therefore not plain support cardinality, but a **weighted shadow complexity** extracted from the Newton support. Your mission is to define this invariant and prove upper bounds, exactness theorems under structural hypotheses, and polynomial-time computability from Newton-polytope data.

## New Definitions You Should Introduce

These should be genuinely new and mathematically meaningful, not thin wrappers.

### 1. Second shadow of a support
For a finite support set \(S \subseteq \mathbb{N}^n\), define the quadratic shadow
\[
\operatorname{Sh}_2(S)
:= \{\beta \in \mathbb{N}^n : \exists \alpha \in S,\ \exists i,j,\ \beta=\alpha-e_i-e_j,\ \beta\ge 0\}.
\]
This is the exponent set potentially appearing in second partial derivatives.

### 2. Weighted shadow multiplicity
Define
\[
m_{S}(\beta)
:= \#\{(\alpha,i,j): \alpha\in S,\ \beta=\alpha-e_i-e_j,\ \alpha_i>0,\ \alpha_j>0\}.
\]
Then define the weighted support measure
\[
\sigma(S):=\sum_{\beta\in \operatorname{Sh}_2(S)} 1
\quad\text{or, more ambitiously,}\quad
\sigma_w(S):=\sum_{\beta\in \operatorname{Sh}_2(S)} m_S(\beta).
\]
You should investigate both. The unweighted version is the geometric complexity; the weighted version tracks collision multiplicity.

### 3. Cancellation-free second shadow
Define a property of a polynomial \(f\):
\[
\mathrm{NoCancel}_2(f)
\]
meaning that for every \(\beta\in \operatorname{Sh}_2(\mathrm{supp}(f))\), the coefficient of \(x^\beta\) in every second partial derivative contribution does not vanish after summation. This is likely too strong globally, so define a weaker variant such as positivity-stable or sign-coherent shadow contributions.

### 4. Shadow-exact family
A class of homogeneous polynomials is **shadow-exact** if the number of nonzero quadratic derivative leaves equals \(|\operatorname{Sh}_2(\mathrm{supp}(f))|\). This gives a formal target for stable/Lorentzian/log-concave families.

### Theorem 1: Universal quadratic-shadow upper bound
This is the foundational theorem: every nonzero quadratic derivative leaf must come from the second shadow.

#### Mathematical statement
Let \(f\) be homogeneous of degree \(d\ge 2\). Then the set of monomials appearing in all nonzero second partial derivatives of \(f\) is contained in \(\operatorname{Sh}_2(\mathrm{supp}(f))\). Hence the number of distinct nonzero quadratic derivative leaves is at most \(|\operatorname{Sh}_2(\mathrm{supp}(f))|\).

This is the non-multiaffine analog of support compression, now with collision allowed.

### Theorem 2: Exactness for sign-coherent or positive-coefficient homogeneous polynomials
This is where the new mathematics begins. If all coefficients are nonnegative in an ordered semiring/ring and second-derivative contributions cannot cancel, then the upper bound becomes exact.

#### Mathematical statement
Let \(f\) be a homogeneous polynomial over a linearly ordered semiring with all coefficients positive on its support. Then every exponent in the quadratic shadow contributes a nonzero monomial in some second partial derivative, so
\[
\#\mathrm{Leaves}_2(f)=|\operatorname{Sh}_2(\mathrm{supp}(f))|.
\]

A weaker but more formalizable version:
If \(R\) is a canonically ordered semiring and all support coefficients are nonzero and pairwise sign-coherent, then
\[
\mathrm{nonzeroQuadraticLeafSet}(f)=\operatorname{Sh}_2(\mathrm{supp}(f)).
\]

#### Suggested Lean 4 type signature
```lean
theorem nonzeroQuadraticLeafSet_eq_shadow_of_posCoeffs
  {σ : Type} [Fintype σ] [DecidableEq σ]
  {R : Type} [LinearOrderedSemiring R]
  (f : MvPolynomial σ R)
  (d : ℕ)
  (hhom : IsHomogeneousDeg f d)
  (hpos : ∀ s ∈ NewtonSupport f, 0 < coeff s f) :
  nonzeroQuadraticLeafSet f = quadraticShadow (NewtonSupport f) := by
```

If positivity of all coefficients is too strong for existing APIs, use finite-support assumptions and prove the nonvanishing coefficient contribution via a coefficient formula for iterated partial derivatives.

#### Why this is a breakthrough
This turns support compression into a theorem for large natural classes of polynomials arising in geometry and combinatorics: generating functions, basis polynomials, partition functions, and many Lorentzian examples have nonnegative coefficients.

### Theorem 3: Polynomial-time computability of the shadow measure from support data
You need a verified algorithm, not only existence. The theorem should say that the weighted support measure can be computed by a finite combinatorial procedure over the support.

#### Mathematical statement
For fixed finite variable set \(σ\), the quantity \(\sigma(f)=|\operatorname{Sh}_2(\mathrm{supp}(f))|\) is computable from the Newton support by enumerating all support exponents and all ordered pairs of variables, inserting valid decremented exponents into a finite set. This runs in time polynomial in \(|\mathrm{supp}(f)|\cdot |σ|^2\).

A theorem in Lean should certify correctness of the algorithm:
```lean

  {σ : Type} [Fintype σ] [DecidableEq σ] :
  Finset (σ →₀ ℕ) → Finset (σ →₀ ℕ) := ...

theorem mem_computeQuadraticShadow_iff
  {σ : Type} [Fintype σ] [DecidableEq σ]
  (S : Finset (σ →₀ ℕ)) (β : σ →₀ ℕ) :
  β ∈ computeQuadraticShadow S ↔ β ∈ quadraticShadowFromFinset S := by
```

and then
```lean
theorem computeQuadraticShadow_correct
  {σ : Type} [Fintype σ] [DecidableEq σ]
  (f : MvPolynomial σ R) :
  computeQuadraticShadow (NewtonSupportFinset f) = quadraticShadowFinset (NewtonSupportFinset f) := by
```

If complexity classes are awkward to formalize fully, prove the exact finite algorithm and explain the polynomial-time bound in `RESEARCH_PAPER.md`.

#### Why this is a breakthrough
This makes the theorem usable in experimentation, symbolic algebra, and geometric data analysis. It transforms a structural theorem into a computational instrument.

## Proof Architecture: 3 Possible Strategies

You must include at least 2–3 real proof paths in your work and indicate which is most promising.

### Strategy A: Direct coefficient extraction via iterated partial derivatives
**Best for Theorems 1 and 2. Most promising overall.**

1. Prove an explicit coefficient formula for iterated derivatives:
   \[
   [x^\beta](\partial_i\partial_j f)
   =
   (\beta_i+1)(\beta_j+1+\delta_{ij})\, c_{\beta+e_i+e_j}.
   \]
   More generally, express the coefficient as a finite sum over all ancestors \(\alpha\) with \(\alpha-e_i-e_j=\beta\).

2. Deduce support containment:
   if \([x^\beta](\partial_i\partial_j f)\neq 0\), then some ancestor exponent lies in the support, so \(\beta\in \operatorname{Sh}_2(\mathrm{supp}(f))\).

3. Under positivity/sign coherence, show the coefficient sum cannot vanish, yielding exactness.

This route is strongest because it directly isolates where cancellation can and cannot occur.

### Strategy B: Newton polytope / Minkowski-shadow geometry
**Best for computational and geometric statements.**

1. Define the \((d-2)\)-shadow of the support or Newton polytope as projection under subtraction by standard basis pairs.

2. Show every second derivative support is contained in the union of these projected fibers.

3. Use finite-set combinatorics to derive the algorithm and cardinality bound.

This route is conceptually elegant and ties directly to convex geometry, but may require more setup to connect to actual coefficient nonvanishing.

### Strategy C: Stable/Lorentzian anti-cancellation mechanism
**Most visionary; likely partial this cycle.**

1. Use catalog notions from Lorentzian/M-convex support to show support fibers satisfy exchange/convexity properties.

2. Prove that coefficient arrays along shadow fibers are sign-coherent or ultra-log-concave, obstructing total cancellation.

3. Deduce exactness or near-exactness of the quadratic shadow bound.

This route could open a new subfield: derivative complexity of Lorentzian polynomials via Newton geometry. Even a partial theorem here would be high-value.
