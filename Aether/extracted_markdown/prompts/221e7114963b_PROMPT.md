### I. THE GRAND VISION

The Connes-Kreimer Hopf algebra H_CK of rooted trees is the algebraic engine behind perturbative renormalization in quantum field theory. Its coproduct encodes the combinatorics of subdivergences; its antipode implements the Birkhoff decomposition (the algebraic heart of the Zimmermann forest formula); its universal property identifies it as the *free* commutative Hopf algebra on the 1-cocycle B₊. Yet no proof assistant has ever verified the *coassociativity* of Δ, the *free Hopf universal property*, or the *β-function fixed-point equation* — the three pillars that make H_CK a dynamical system on the character group Gₐ, i.e., the renormalization group.

We will prove all three. In doing so, we forge the first machine-verified bridge between **algebraic renormalization** (Hopf algebra cohomology), **quantum field theory** (RG flow as a dynamical system), and **computational fixed-point theory** (contractive maps on character groups with explicit Lipschitz bounds). **Strategy A: Direct Combinatorial Induction (RECOMMENDED for Coassociativity).**
- Define `admissibleCuts : CKTree → Finset (Cut CKTree)` enumerating all admissible cuts of a tree.

- By induction on `vertexCount t`, reduce coassociativity on `t = B₊(forest)` to coassociativity on subtrees.
- **Why most promising**: This mirrors the original Connes-Kreimer proof and makes the combinatorics explicit, enabling extraction of O(n²) algorithms for coproduct computation.

**Strategy B: Algebraic Descent from the Universal Property.**
- First prove the universal property (Theorem 4) using the recursive definition of φ.
- Then deduce coassociativity as a *consequence*: since H_CK is free on B₊, and B₊ is a 1-cocycle for the coproduct, the coproduct is uniquely determined and must be coassociative (because the universal target Hopf algebra has a coassociative coproduct, and φ is a Hopf morphism).
- **Why risky**: Requires developing the full universal property first, which is harder. But if successful, coassociativity becomes a one-line corollary.

**Strategy C: Rota-Baxter Fixed-Point Iteration (for β-function).**
- Define the RG iteration operator `T(β) = -φ(B₊(β · φ₊⁻¹))`.
- Show T is a contraction using the Rota-Baxter identity with Lipschitz constant `1/(1 + |λ|)`.
- Apply Banach's fixed-point theorem (constructively, via iterating T from β₀ = 0).
- The unique fixed point satisfies the β-function equation.
- **Why most promising for β-function**: Gives both existence and uniqueness, plus explicit convergence rate O(log(1/ε)/|λ|).
