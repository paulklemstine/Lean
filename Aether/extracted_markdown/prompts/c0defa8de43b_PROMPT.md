## Assignment: Direction 1: Characterization of Lorentzian Ground-State Families

**Mode:** prove

Prove a genuinely new structural theorem at the interface of Lorentzian polynomials, stoquastic quantum many-body systems, and transfer-matrix statistical mechanics. The ambition is not to verify isolated examples, but to isolate a mathematically sharp mechanism by which positivity-preserving quantum ground states generate Lorentzian coefficient families. If successful, this creates a new bridge between Brändén–Huh Lorentzian geometry and the algorithmic theory of quantum state preparation.

The central scientific target is to characterize when the occupation-basis ground-state amplitude vector of a stoquastic Hamiltonian canonically defines a Lorentzian polynomial, and to extract from that characterization a verified certificate-generation algorithm with nontrivial complexity guarantees.

## Core theorem targets

You should introduce a new formal structure capturing the passage from a nonnegative amplitude family to a multiaffine polynomial, and then prove at least the following three substantial theorems.

### New definition to introduce

Define a new concept, not already present in the catalog, expressing that a Hamiltonian ground-state amplitude family induces a Lorentzian polynomial.

Suggested concept:

- `GroundStateFamily`
- `AmplitudePolynomial`
- `IsLorentzianGroundStateFamily`

A mathematically natural model is:

For a finite spin system indexed by `Fin n`, let `ψ : (Fin n → Fin q) → ℝ` be the coefficient function of a normalized ground state in the occupation basis. Define the associated generating polynomial
\[
P_\psi(\{X_{i,a}\}) := \sum_{\sigma : \mathrm{Fin}\ n \to \mathrm{Fin}\ q}
\psi(\sigma)\prod_{i=0}^{n-1} X_{i,\sigma(i)}.
\]
For qubits (`q = 2`), this is the multiaffine polynomial
\[
P_\psi(x_0,\dots,x_{n-1},y_0,\dots,y_{n-1})
= \sum_{\sigma \in \{0,1\}^n}\psi(\sigma)\prod_{i:\sigma_i=0}x_i\prod_{i:\sigma_i=1}y_i.
\]

This is the right object because Lorentzianity is naturally a statement about coefficient families of homogeneous multiaffine polynomials, while the physics gives you a positive vector.

### Theorem 1: Transfer-matrix preservation of recursive Lorentzianity

For 1D nearest-neighbor stoquastic chains whose amplitudes factor through products of entrywise nonnegative `2 × 2` transfer matrices, prove that recursive Lorentzian certificates are preserved under extension by one site under explicit hypotheses.

A precise mathematical statement to target:

\[
\forall n,\ \forall \psi_n : \{0,1\}^n \to \mathbb R_{\ge 0},
\]
if `ψ_n` is recursively Lorentzian and if
\[
\psi_{n+1}(\sigma,b)=\sum_{a\in\{0,1\}} \psi_n(\sigma,a)\,T_n(a,b)
\]
for a nonnegative transfer matrix `T_n` satisfying a rank-two log-concavity preservation condition, then the amplitude polynomial of `ψ_{n+1}` is recursively Lorentzian.

This is the key structural step: it turns Lorentzianity from a miraculous global property into an inductive dynamical invariant of transfer evolution.

### Theorem 2: TFIM ferromagnetic chain yields Lorentzian ground-state family in a nontrivial parameter regime

For the transverse-field Ising chain with ferromagnetic coupling `J ≥ 0` and transverse field `h ≥ 0`, prove that in a rigorously specified regime (for example, a transfer-matrix-dominated regime or a high-field/low-field regime you can actually formalize), the unique Perron–Frobenius ground-state coefficient family defines a Lorentzian polynomial.

A realistic first sharp theorem is not “for all parameters,” but rather:

- for open chains,
- ferromagnetic nearest-neighbor coupling,
- and parameters in an explicit region where the transfer matrix is totally nonnegative or where an inductive Hessian bound closes.

This is already a breakthrough if stated and proved cleanly, because it identifies a concrete quantum phase region where Lorentzian geometry controls amplitudes.

### Theorem 3: Certificate complexity bound for chain-generated Lorentzian families

Using the catalog’s recursive certificate machinery, prove a nontrivial complexity theorem:

If a qubit chain ground-state family is generated by an inductive transfer-matrix scheme satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem 1, then there exists a recursive Lorentzian certificate of depth `O(n)` and verification complexity bounded by `O(n * polylog n)` or by a formally proved explicit recursive bound that asymptotically improves brute-force Hessian checking.

This theorem is what transforms structural mathematics into algorithmic science. It says the Lorentzian property is not just true; it is efficiently certifiable from local data.

The Lorentzian condition is one of the deepest modern forms of discrete concavity. Stoquastic ground states are one of the most algorithmically tractable corners of quantum many-body theory. A theorem linking them says:

- positivity in quantum mechanics is not merely order-theoretic,

This would open a new field: **Lorentzian quantum complexity**. It would suggest that quantum ground states can be organized not only by entanglement and spectral gap, but by a hierarchy of geometric concavity certificates. It also creates a mathematically precise interface between:

- Brändén–Huh Lorentzian geometry,
- Perron–Frobenius theory for positive operators,
- transfer-matrix methods in statistical mechanics,

If you can identify the exact transfer-matrix hypotheses under which Lorentzianity is preserved, you will have isolated the boundary between “positive but unstructured” and “positive with deep global concavity.” That is the kind of classification theorem that changes how people think.

## Proof strategies: 3 plausible routes

### Strategy A: Direct inductive transfer-certificate proof
**Most promising.**

1. Define the amplitude polynomial and show that a transfer step corresponds to a structured linear operator on multiaffine homogeneous polynomials.
2. Prove this operator preserves the recursive premises required by `IsRecursivelyLorentzian` under explicit nonnegativity / total-positivity hypotheses on the transfer matrix.
3. Induct on system size and extract recursive certificates with controlled depth.

Why this is promising: it matches the catalog API, avoids hard spectral formalization, and converts a physics recurrence into a proof-relevant combinatorial transformer.

### Strategy B: Hessian-based derivative analysis
1. For degree-2 derivatives of the amplitude polynomial, compute the Hessian after one transfer step.
2. Show that the “at most one positive eigenvalue” condition is preserved under the transfer operator.
3. Bootstrap from quadratic derivatives to recursive Lorentzianity.

Why this is valuable: it gets closer to the Brändén–Huh analytic characterization and directly mirrors the computational test.  
Why it is harder: eigenvalue-signature arguments over symbolic matrices in Lean can become technically heavy.

### Strategy C: Total positivity / stable polynomial route
1. Formalize a stronger hypothesis on transfer matrices, such as total nonnegativity or a variation-diminishing property.
2. Show the induced generating polynomial is strongly log-concave or stable in a sense implying Lorentzianity.
3. Deduce recursive Lorentzianity as a corollary.

Why this is conceptually profound: it would connect quantum transfer matrices to the theory of stable and Lorentzian polynomials.  
Why it is risky: Mathlib support for stability technology may be thinner than for direct combinatorial induction.

**Recommendation:** pursue Strategy A first, use Strategy B for a sharp lemma or sanity check, and reserve Strategy C as the long-range conceptual generalization.
