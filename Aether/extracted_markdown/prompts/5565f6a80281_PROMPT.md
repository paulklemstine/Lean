## Core Mathematical Vision

Let `P` be a finite type of proof states, `d : P → P → ℝ≥0∞` an ultrametric Lawvere distance, and `C : P → P` a compression operator. Think of `C` as one step of proof simplification or canonicalization. The system is “diagonally stable” if repeated compression does not create new off-diagonal distinctions beyond those already visible through self-consistent observers; formally, this should be encoded in a way compatible with your existing observer/minimality infrastructure.

Associate to `(P,d,C)` the semimodule of admissible potentials
\[
M(P) := \{\phi : P \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}\cup\{\infty\} \mid \forall x,y,\ \phi(x)\le d(x,y)+\phi(y)\},
\]
with pointwise idempotent addition (`inf`/`min`, depending on the chosen convention) and tropical scalar action by cost shift. The operator `C` induces an endomorphism by pullback:
\[
T_C(\phi) := \phi \circ C.
\]

Conversely, from a finitely generated separated idempotent semimodule `M` equipped with a contractive endomorphism `T`, construct an observer system `Obs(M)` whose points are extremal evaluation profiles / generator-supported observers, and whose induced distance is ultrametric. The central claim is that **finite diagonally stable ultrametric proof-compression systems are exactly those recognized by such semimodules**.

This is the right theorem because it converts speculative proof compression into something structurally classifiable:
- geometric side: finite ultrametric dynamical proof systems,
- algebraic side: finitely generated separated tropical semimodules with contractive endomorphism,
- algorithmic side: minimal compressor reconstruction by generator elimination.

## Proof Strategy Architectures

### Strategy A: Potential-Semimodule First, Then Realization, Then Ultrametric Identification
**Most promising.**

1. Define `M(P)` as 1-Lipschitz/Lawvere potentials and prove it is an idempotent semimodule.
2. Use finiteness of `P` to prove finite generation by distance-to-center potentials or a finite extremal basis extracted from representables `d(·,p)`.
3. Invoke `finite_separation_semimodule_realization_minimal`, then prove the resulting realization is ultrametric and equivalent to the original compression system.

Why this is promising: it reuses the strongest existing catalog machinery and isolates the novel work into the ultrametric identification lemma and dynamics transfer. It is the best route to minimizing sorry.

### Strategy B: Direct Observer Construction from Ultrametric Ball Tree
1. Show every finite ultrametric space corresponds to a rooted hierarchical cluster tree.
2. Construct observers from tree cuts / ball indicators / level potentials.
3. Prove these observers generate the potential semimodule and reconstruct the minimal compressor directly from the tree.

Why this matters: finite ultrametrics are secretly combinatorial. This could yield the cleanest cardinality = generator-rank theorem, because extremal generators may correspond exactly to nonredundant internal nodes or leaves of the compression dendrogram.

Why it is secondary: formalizing finite ultrametric tree representation in Lean may be heavier unless the infrastructure already exists.

### Strategy C: Quotient-and-Kan Extension View
1. Quotient `P` by observational zero-distance.
2. Show the potential semimodule on the quotient is the separated reflection of the original semimodule.
3. Characterize `MinComp` as a universal object among all nonexpansive compressor realizations, then derive minimality and uniqueness by universal property.

Why this is deep: it reframes the theorem categorically, with `MinComp` as a reflective hull. This is the conceptual endpoint.

Why it may be harder: universal-property proofs in Lean can be elegant but require more setup. Use if your category/quotient machinery is already mature.

Recommended plan: **A first, then import B-style combinatorics for rank/cardinality, and C for uniqueness packaging if feasible.**
