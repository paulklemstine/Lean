## Assignment: Direction 1: Higher-Order Negative Dependence Certificates via k×k Minor Perturbation

Work in **prove** mode.

Build a genuinely new higher-order perturbation theory for principal minors of symmetric PSD kernels, extending pairwise DPP certification to **k-wise negative dependence certificates**. This should not be a cosmetic generalization of the 2×2 case: the goal is an explicit, formal, algorithmically usable bound showing that **all k-point principal minors are Lipschitz in entrywise max norm with a polynomial constant in the entry bound**. This opens a route from pairwise diversity certification to **higher-order diversity guarantees** for k-DPPs, matroidal sampling, correlation inequalities, and finite-particle observables.

The mathematical breakthrough target is to turn the vague principle “determinants are stable under small perturbations” into a **uniform certified theorem for all principal k-minors**, with explicit constants, Lean-ready recurrence, and a computational certificate. The resulting theory should support not only proof but also a verified procedure that, given a kernel perturbation budget `η`, outputs certified bounds on all principal minors of size `k`.

### Core Breakthrough Theorem

Let `K K' : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℝ` be symmetric PSD matrices with entrywise bound
`|K i j| ≤ M`, `|K' i j| ≤ M`, and perturbation bound `|K i j - K' i j| ≤ η` for all `i j`.
For every subset `S : Finset (Fin n)` with `S.card = k`, let `K_S` and `K'_S` denote the corresponding principal submatrices. Prove that there exists an explicit polynomial `P : ℕ → ℝ → ℝ`, with `P k M` of degree `k-1` in `M`, depending only on `k`, such that

\[
\forall S,\ S.card = k \to
\bigl| \det(K_S) - \det(K'_S) \bigr| \le P(k,M)\,\eta.
\]

A strong explicit candidate is the factorial-scale bound

\[
P(k,M) = k!\, k\, M^{k-1}
\]

or any sharper recursively defined polynomial with a complete proof. If the sharp closed form is too difficult initially, first prove a recursive certificate:

\[
P(1,M)=1,\qquad
P(k+1,M) = (k+1)\bigl(P(k,M)\,M + M^k\bigr),
\]

and then derive a closed-form upper bound.

The theorem is significant because it converts **local kernel perturbation control** into **global higher-order correlation control**. In DPP language, principal minors encode inclusion probabilities; in statistical physics they encode finite-order correlation amplitudes; in quantum chemistry they model determinant-like structure in reduced density surrogates. A polynomial-in-`k` or factorial-explicit certificate makes higher-order certification computationally plausible.

## Theorem Package to Deliver

You must prove at least **3 nontrivial theorems** with genuine proof structure.

### Theorem A: Recursive perturbation bound for principal minors
Prove the recursive bound for `k×k` principal minors using cofactor expansion and induction on `k`.

Mathematical statement:
\[
\forall k \ge 1,\ \forall S,\ | \det(K_S)-\det(K'_S)| \le P_k(M)\eta.
\]

This theorem should require:
- induction on `k`,
- decomposition of the determinant difference into cofactor and entry perturbation terms,
- triangle inequality and multi-step `calc`,
- nontrivial combinatorial handling of principal submatrices.

### Theorem B: Explicit closed-form polynomial bound
From the recurrence, derive a simpler explicit bound such as
\[
P_k(M) \le k \cdot k! \cdot \max(1,M)^{k-1}
\]
or a sharper formula if you can prove it.

This is important because applications need a **human-readable certificate**, not just a recurrence.

Expected proof ingredients:
- induction on `k`,
- inequality algebra (`nlinarith`, `ring_nf`, `field_simp` where appropriate),
- monotonicity of powers / factorial estimates.

### Theorem C: Higher-order negative dependence stability / correlation stability
Show that if principal minors represent k-point inclusion weights or correlation observables, then these observables are stable under entrywise perturbation.

One precise form:
\[
\forall S,\ | \rho_k^K(S) - \rho_k^{K'}(S) | \le P_k(M)\eta,
\]
where \(\rho_k^K(S)=\det(K_S)\).

This is the theorem that crosses domains:
- **probability/combinatorics**: DPP inclusion probabilities,
- **statistical physics**: k-point correlation functions,
- **quantum chemistry**: determinant-based k-electron observables.

A fourth theorem would be excellent:

### Theorem D: Perturbative preservation of positivity margin
If \(\det(K_S) \ge \delta\) for all `|S| = k` and \(P_k(M)\eta < \delta\), then
\[
\det(K'_S) > 0
\]
for all such `S`.

This is the real certification theorem: a quantitative margin condition guarantees persistence of higher-order diversity under perturbation.

## Proof Strategy Architecture

You must pursue at least 2–3 proof paths and decide which one is most promising.

### Strategy 1: Induction via Laplace expansion along a row
1. For `k+1`, expand `det(K_S)` and `det(K'_S)` along the first row of the induced principal submatrix.
2. Write the difference as
   \[
   \sum_j (a_j c_j - a'_j c'_j)
   = \sum_j (a_j-a'_j)c_j + \sum_j a'_j(c_j-c'_j).
   \]
3. Bound entries by `η`, cofactors by induction, and determinant magnitudes by a Hadamard-style crude bound or by recursive `M^k` control.

Why promising: this mirrors the known `2×2` proof, is constructive, and naturally yields a recurrence suitable for Lean induction. This should be your **primary path**.

### Strategy 2: Multilinearity of determinant in rows
1. Regard determinant as a multilinear map in the rows.
2. Replace rows of `K_S` one at a time by rows of `K'_S`, telescoping:
   \[
   \det(R_1,\dots,R_k)-\det(R'_1,\dots,R'_k)
   = \sum_{t=1}^k \bigl[\det(R'_1,\dots,R'_{t-1},R_t,\dots,R_k)
   - \det(R'_1,\dots,R'_t,R_{t+1},\dots,R_k)\bigr].
   \]
3. Bound each term using rowwise multilinearity and uniform row-entry bounds.

Why promising: conceptually cleaner and may avoid some awkward principal-submatrix bookkeeping. If Mathlib’s determinant multilinearity lemmas are convenient, this could even supersede Laplace expansion.

### Strategy 3: Leibniz formula and permutation counting
1. Expand both determinants as sums over permutations.
2. Bound each product difference by telescoping products:
   \[
   \Big|\prod_i a_i - \prod_i b_i\Big|
   \le \sum_t |a_t-b_t| \prod_{u\neq t}\max(|a_u|,|b_u|).
   \]
3. Sum over `k!` permutations to get an explicit bound like `k·k!·M^(k-1)·η`.

Why promising: this may yield the cleanest explicit closed form. Why dangerous: Lean permutation bookkeeping can become heavy. Use this either for the closed-form theorem or as a companion derivation once the recursive theorem is established.

Recommended plan: prove Theorem A by **Strategy 1 or 2**, then derive Theorem B by **Strategy 3** or by induction from the recurrence.
