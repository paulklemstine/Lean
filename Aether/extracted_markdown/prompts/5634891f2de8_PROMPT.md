## Precise Theorem Target

You should define:

1. A type of pitch classes, preferably `ZMod 12`.
2. Major/minor triads as 3-element multisets or sorted triples:
   - major rooted at `r`: `{r, r+4, r+7}`
   - minor rooted at `r`: `{r, r+3, r+7}`
3. A quotient-compatible voice-leading distance `vlDist` between triads, defined as the minimum `L¹` or `L²` distance over all permutations / representatives in a sorted chamber. If quotient construction is too heavy at first, define the **sorted normal-form distance** and prove the theorem there first, then descend to the quotient.
4. PLR operations on major/minor triads.

Then prove a theorem of the following form:

### Core theorem
For each PLR transformation `T ∈ {P, L, R}`, and for every major/minor triad `c`,
`T c` is connected to `c` by a path whose length is at most `C * vlDist c (T c)`,
for some explicit universal constant `C`, ideally `C = 1` or `C ≤ 2`.

The strongest version is:

> **Exact geodesicity theorem:** For every triad `c` and every `T ∈ {P,L,R}`, the straight segment between suitable sorted representatives of `c` and `T c` projects to a minimizing geodesic in the quotient, except possibly when the segment crosses a singular stratum; in all cases the projected path length equals the quotient distance.

If exact geodesicity is too ambitious at first, prove the weaker but still nontrivial:

> **Uniform near-geodesicity theorem:** There exists a universal constant `C` such that for all `c` and `T ∈ {P,L,R}`,
> `pathLength (plrPath c T) ≤ C * vlDist c (T c)`,
> with an explicit constructive path `plrPath`.

A still-concrete and likely Lean-friendly intermediate theorem is:

> **Finite-case optimality theorem for triads:** For each `T ∈ {P,L,R}` and each major/minor triad `c`, the voice-leading displacement induced by `T` changes exactly one pitch class by one or two semitones in a suitable representative, and hence realizes a shortest or uniformly shortest path among all major/minor-triad transitions of that combinatorial type.

This is already a serious theorem because it identifies PLR as metric minimizers inside a nontrivial quotient geometry.

## Mathematical Content You Should Actually Prove

### Theorem A: PLR realizes minimal single-voice displacement
For every root `r : ZMod 12`,
- `P` maps the major triad `{r,r+4,r+7}` to the minor triad `{r,r+3,r+7}` and vice versa by moving exactly one note by one semitone.
- `R` maps major to relative minor / minor to relative major by moving exactly one note by two semitones.
- `L` maps major to leading-tone-related minor / minor to corresponding major by moving exactly one note by one semitone.

Then prove this realizes the minimum possible voice-leading distance among all triads of the target PLR class.

### Theorem B: Sorted-chamber line segments are geodesics
In the sorted chamber of ordered triples in `ℝ³` modulo permutation, geodesics are Euclidean or `L¹` line segments. Prove this in the formal model you choose.

### Theorem C: Projection to quotient preserves near-optimality for PLR moves
When the straight segment between sorted representatives crosses a singular stratum, the quotient projection may fold, but for PLR endpoints the fold cannot increase path length by more than a universal factor; ideally show no increase at all in the triad cases.

This theorem is the real conceptual bridge. It says symbolic harmonic moves are controlled by the polyhedral/orbifold geometry, not merely embedded into it.

## 2–3 Proof Strategies

### Strategy 1: Finite classification + direct metric computation
**Most promising for Lean and for a first breakthrough.**

1. Define major/minor triads explicitly as normalized pitch-class triples.
2. Define `P`, `L`, `R` by formulas on roots and quality.
3. Compute the induced displacement vector in a canonical representative.
4. Prove by finite case analysis over all candidate target triads of the same quality class that PLR gives minimal distance.

Why this is promising:
- `ZMod 12` is finite.
- Major/minor triads are completely classifiable.
- Minimality can be reduced to finite arithmetic and permutation checks.
- This yields a theorem with strong mathematical meaning and little analytic overhead.

This route is especially attractive if you want a theorem that is fully verified with almost no sorry.

---

### Strategy 2: Sorted chamber / Weyl chamber geometry
1. Represent triads by sorted lifts in `ℤ³` or `ℝ³`.
2. Prove that in the sorted chamber, the segment between representatives is a geodesic.
3. Show that PLR endpoints differ by a sparse displacement vector with minimal norm among all admissible transitions.
4. Descend this statement to the quotient under `S₃`.

Why it matters:
- This reveals the geometry behind PLR.
- It connects directly to orbifolds, Coxeter/Weyl chamber methods, and polyhedral metric geometry.
- It is conceptually deeper and scales to larger chord types.

This is the right route if you want the paper-level theorem, not just a finite verification.

---

### Strategy 3: Tropical/polyhedral reformulation
1. Encode chord classes as points in a min-plus or polyhedral parameter space, using sorted coordinates or interval vectors.
2. Interpret PLR transformations as adjacency moves on a tropical/polyhedral complex.
3. Prove these moves are edge-geodesic or quasi-geodesic in the induced path metric.
4. Relate the tropical metric to the voice-leading metric on triads.

Why this is visionary:
- It would connect neo-Riemannian theory to tropical geometry.
- It creates a formal bridge from musical transformation theory to polyhedral combinatorics and metric geometry.
- It may let you reuse or analogize from catalog tropical lemmas, especially symmetry and distributive identities, even if only structurally.

This is the boldest path. It may require introducing new definitions, but if successful it opens a new formal research program.

## Cross-Domain Connections You Should Explicitly Lean Into

### 1. Orbifolds and configuration spaces
The quotient of ordered triples by permutation is a configuration-space orbifold. PLR then becomes a motion-planning primitive in a singular quotient. This is not just music theory; it is geometric group action on configuration spaces.

### 2. Tropical geometry and polyhedral complexes
The sorted chamber is a polyhedral cone, and quotienting by permutations gives a chamber complex reminiscent of tropical fans. If PLR edges are geodesic or near-geodesic there, you are building a tropicalized harmonic geometry.

### 3. Robotics and motion planning
Voice leading is a shortest-path problem under symmetry and collision-like singularities. This is analogous to robot arms modulo joint relabeling or formation control in quotient spaces.

### 4. MIR and computational auditory scene analysis
A verified metric theorem gives principled harmonic distance features for chord tracking, segmentation, and harmonic similarity.

### 5. Generative composition and constrained optimization
If PLR moves are certified geodesic or near-geodesic, they become optimal local moves in algorithmic composition systems.

### 6. Coxeter/Weyl chamber geometry
Sorted representatives live in a fundamental chamber for a symmetric group action. This is mathematically elegant and potentially generalizable from triads to `n`-note chords.
