## Core Mathematical Vision

Let
\[
e_r(x_1,\dots,x_n)=\sum_{|I|=r}\prod_{i\in I}x_i
\]
be the basis generating polynomial of the uniform matroid \(U_{r,n}\). The Lorentzian property is detected on degree-2 derivatives:
for every multi-index \(\alpha\) with \(|\alpha|=r-2\), the quadratic polynomial
\[
\partial^\alpha e_r
\]
must have Hessian with exactly one positive eigenvalue on the positive orthant, equivalently satisfy the standard quadratic Lorentzian signature condition.

The central insight is that for \(U_{r,n}\), all quadratic leaves are symmetry-equivalent, so the true stability radius should be governed by the **smallest spectral gap to signature change** among these leaves under coefficient perturbation. This is the first place where the abstract existence theorem from the catalog should collapse to a concrete exact formula.

This would be a breakthrough because it turns Lorentzian stability from a qualitative compactness statement into a **spectral perturbation theory** for matroid polynomials. That opens a new program: certified robustness for strongly log-concave sampling, optimization under combinatorial uncertainty, and symmetric-function stability theory.

### Theorem 1: Existence of a symmetry-reduced exact leaf gap for the uniform matroid
For \(2 \le r \le n-2\), every quadratic leaf of \(e_r\) is permutation-conjugate to every other, hence the minimum normalized eigengap is attained by a single canonical leaf.

Mathematical statement:
\[
\forall n,r,\ 2\le r \le n-2 \implies
\exists g_{r,n}>0,\ 
\forall \alpha,\ |\alpha|=r-2,\ 
\mathrm{gap}(\partial^\alpha e_r)=g_{r,n}.
\]

    ∃ g : ℝ, 0 < g ∧
      ∀ α : Fin n → ℕ,
        (∑ i, α i = r - 2) →
        normalizedLeafGap (uniformMatroidPoly n r) α = g := by

If summation over functions is awkward, replace by a finite-support multi-index type already available in Mathlib.

### Theorem 2: Lower bound — spectral margin implies Lorentzian stability under coefficient perturbation
If a perturbation \(f = e_r + \delta\) has coefficient sup-norm less than an explicit constant times the canonical leaf gap, then \(f\) remains Lorentzian.

Mathematical statement:
\[
\exists C_{r,n}>0,\ 
\|\delta\|_\infty \le C_{r,n}\, g_{r,n}

## Proof architecture: 3 viable strategies

### Strategy A: Symmetry reduction + explicit Hessian spectral computation
Most promising.

1. Show every quadratic leaf of \(e_r\) is permutation-equivalent.
   - Reduce all leaf analysis to one canonical derivative, e.g. \(\partial_{1}\cdots\partial_{r-2} e_r\).
2. Compute the resulting quadratic polynomial explicitly.
   - It should be a scalar multiple of \(e_2\) on the remaining \(n-r+2\) variables.
3. Compute its Hessian exactly.
   - The matrix should be of the form \(b(J-I)\) or equivalent rank-one perturbation.
4. Extract the eigengap and identify the smallest perturbation needed to cross the Lorentzian signature boundary.
5. Use matrix perturbation inequalities to derive lower and upper bounds matching up to an explicit constant, ideally equality.

Why this is best:
The uniform matroid is maximally symmetric; if one cannot get an exact spectral answer here, then the generic stability theorem has not yet been truly understood.

### Strategy B: Build directly from catalog perturbation theorems
Conservative but robust.

1. Start from `residual_gap_of_perturbation` and instantiate it for `uniformMatroidPoly n r`.
2. Prove that the residual gap in the uniform case simplifies to a closed-form symmetric quantity.
3. Strengthen the generic `lorentzian_stability_radius_exists` existence result into an explicit formula by supplying the exact leaf Hessian norm and exact perturbation operator norm.

Why it may work:
It leverages vetted infrastructure and minimizes foundational overhead. This is ideal if the existing catalog already contains enough derivative/Hessian machinery.

### Strategy C: Representation-theoretic route via Johnson scheme / symmetric functions
Higher risk, potentially deepest.

1. Identify quadratic leaves with \(S_m\)-invariant quadratic forms on the remaining variables.
2. Use the decomposition of the permutation representation into trivial plus standard representation.
3. Read off the two eigenvalues directly from irreducible decomposition.
4. Translate the resulting spectral formula into Lorentzian stability and perturbation thresholds.

Why it matters:
This route would expose the conceptual reason for the exact answer and create a reusable template for non-uniform but highly symmetric matroids, paving the way to partition matroids, paving matroids, and Schur-positive deformations.

## Concrete mathematical subgoals

You should aim to prove at least these intermediate facts, with real proofs using induction / `rcases` / contradiction / `field_simp` / multi-step `calc`:

1. **Canonical leaf formula**:
   a quadratic leaf of \(e_r\) is a scalar multiple of \(e_2\) in fewer variables.

2. **Explicit Hessian entries**:
   diagonal entries vanish and off-diagonal entries are constant, or an equivalent exact formula.

3. **Signature theorem**:
   the leaf Hessian has one positive eigenvalue and the rest nonpositive, with exact gap.

4. **Perturbation transfer lemma**:
   coefficient sup-norm perturbation induces an operator-norm perturbation on every quadratic leaf Hessian.

5. **Instability witness construction**:
   a signed perturbation aligned with the smallest-gap eigenspace breaks Lorentzianity at threshold scale.

At least one of these should visibly connect to another domain.
