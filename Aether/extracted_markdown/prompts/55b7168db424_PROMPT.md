## New Definitions You Should Introduce

At least one genuinely new concept is mandatory. I recommend introducing all of the following.

### 1. Parabolic index weight
For a composition `c = (n₁, …, n_k)` of `n`, let `P_c ≤ GL_n(𝔽_q)` be the corresponding standard parabolic subgroup. Define the **parabolic index weight**
\[
w_q(c,n) := \log [\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb F_q) : P_c].
\]

This is the arithmetic entropy cost of forcing a partial flag type.

### 2. Parabolic pressure
Define
\[
\Pi^{\mathrm{par}}_{n,q}(\beta) := \sum_{c \vDash n} \exp(-\beta\, w_q(c,n)),
\]
where the sum runs over all compositions of `n`.

This is a tractable proxy for full subgroup pressure and already encodes Gaussian-binomial thermodynamics.

### 3. Composition energy density
Define
\[
E_q(c,n) := \frac{1}{n^2}\, w_q(c,n),
\]
or alternatively the `1/n` normalized free energy
\[
F^{\mathrm{par}}_{n,q}(\beta) := \frac{1}{n}\log \Pi^{\mathrm{par}}_{n,q}(\beta).
\]

This normalization choice is mathematically meaningful: the index of parabolics naturally scales quadratically in `n`, while the number of compositions scales exponentially in `n`. The competition between those scales is the phase diagram.

### 4. q-deformed extensivity defect
Define a defect measuring failure of direct-product extensivity:
\[
\Delta_q(m,n;\beta)
:= \log \Pi^{\mathrm{par}}_{m+n,q}(\beta)
 - \log \Pi^{\mathrm{par}}_{m,q}(\beta)
 - \log \Pi^{\mathrm{par}}_{n,q}(\beta).
\]

This is the exact quantity that tells you how far parabolic thermodynamics is from the catalog’s direct-product theory.

### Theorem 5: Cross-domain theorem — entropy functional on compositions

You are required to bridge domains. Here is the right bridge:

For a composition `c` of `n`, define empirical proportions `p_i = n_i / n`. Then
\[
\frac{1}{n^2}w_q(c,n)
\approx
\frac{\log q}{2}\left(1-\sum_i p_i^2\right).
\]

This is a **Tsallis-2 entropy law**:
\[
H_2(p)=1-\sum_i p_i^2.
\]

So prove a theorem making that precise, with explicit finite-`n` error:
\[
\left|
\frac{1}{n^2}w_q(c,n)
-\frac{\log q}{2}H_2(p)
\right|
\le \frac{C_q}{n}.
\]

#### Lean-facing theorem shape
```lean

  1 - p.foldr (fun x s => x^2 + s) 0
```

## Proof Strategy Architecture

Do not give Aristotle one route. Give him multiple attack paths.

### Strategy A: Exact `q`-combinatorial reduction via flags
1. Identify standard parabolics with stabilizers of partial flags of type `c`.
2. Count such flags using iterated Gaussian binomial coefficients / `q`-multinomials.
3. Convert the count into logarithmic energy bounds using product formulas
   \[
   \binom{n}{k}_q = \prod_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{q^{n-i}-1}{q^{k-i}-1}.
   \]
4. Derive asymptotics by comparing `log(q^a - 1)` with `a log q + O_q(1)`.

**Why promising:** This is the most canonical route and gives exact formulas, explicit constants, and a direct bridge to arithmetic geometry via flag varieties.

### Strategy B: Root-subgroup / Bruhat-energy decomposition
1. Express the parabolic index through dimensions of unipotent radicals or positive roots omitted by the Levi.
2. Show the leading term is the number of cross-block root pairs:
   \[
   \sum_{i<j} n_i n_j.
   \]
3. Bound correction terms using uniform estimates on finite-field point counts.

**Why promising:** This reveals the structural reason behind the quadratic energy. It is the conceptual proof and scales to other reductive groups beyond `GL_n`.

### Strategy C: Thermodynamic subadditivity through composition concatenation
1. Define concatenation of compositions of `m` and `n` to get one of `m+n`.
2. Compare `q`-multinomials under concatenation.
3. Prove near-multiplicativity of weights up to boundary error.
4. Apply a Fekete-style argument to normalized logarithms.

**Why promising:** This is the route to free-energy limits and the exact analogue of extensivity in the catalog. It is the bridge from combinatorics to thermodynamics.

**Most promising overall:** Start with **Strategy A**, because it gives the exact formula and explicit estimates with the least infrastructure risk. Then use **Strategy C** to obtain near-extensivity and asymptotic consequences. Strategy B should appear in the paper as the conceptual explanation and the roadmap to general reductive groups.
