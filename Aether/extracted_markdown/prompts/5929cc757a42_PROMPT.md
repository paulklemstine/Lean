## Synthesis

This cycle established two complementary bodies of work in tropical mathematics. First, we formalized the foundational tropical valuation algebra on `WithTop ℕ`, proving all semiring identities including the non-trivial distributivity law `a + min(b,c) = min(a+b, a+c)`. Second, and more substantially, we proved that every univariate ReLU network computes a continuous piecewise-linear (CPL) function — a theorem that required developing significant new infrastructure.

The proof of `univReluNet_is_cpl` demanded solving a key technical challenge: showing that `max(f, 0)` preserves the CPL property. The obstacle was demonstrating that the "nonconstant zeros" of a CPL function form a finite set. We resolved this through a counting injection argument: mapping each nonconstant zero to the number of breakpoints below it, and using a connectedness-based contradiction (`no_adjacent_nonconstant_zeros`) to prove injectivity. The connectedness argument itself required proving that a continuous function that is locally affine on an open interval must be globally affine — a clean application of the topology of connected sets via `isPreconnected_Ioo`.

The tropical algebra work connects to the CPL theory through the interpretation of tropical polynomials as pointwise maxima of affine functions. The distributivity `a ⊕ (b ⊙ c) = (a ⊕ b) ⊙ (a ⊕ c)` (in tropical notation) is exactly the algebraic identity that makes tropical polynomial evaluation well-defined as a piecewise-linear function.

### Direction 1: Tropical Fundamental Theorem of Differential Algebra
**Hypothesis**: The tropicalization of a differential ideal equals the tropical differential ideal generated by the tropicalization of the generators. Formally: if I is a differential ideal in K{y} (differential polynomials over a valued field K), then trop(V(I)) = V(trop(I)) where trop maps solutions to their valuations and V denotes the solution set.
**Test**: Formalize tropical differential polynomials as min-plus expressions with derivative operators acting on valuation sequences, prove the theorem for the case of first-order linear ODEs y' = a·y.
**Why now**: We have the tropical semiring infrastructure and the key connectedness lemma for piecewise-linear functions. The linear case reduces to showing that the valuation of a solution to y' = a·y satisfies the tropical constraint v(y') ≥ min(v(a) + v(y)), which our derivative valuation bound partially addresses.
**If true**: Opens the door to computational tropical methods for solving differential equations via Newton polygon methods.
**If false**: Would indicate that tropicalization does not commute with differential operations, suggesting the need for a modified tropicalization functor.

### Direction 2: Multivariate ReLU Networks and Tropical Geometry
**Hypothesis**: Every multivariate ReLU network ℝⁿ → ℝ computes a continuous piecewise-linear function whose breakpoint locus is a tropical hypersurface (a codimension-1 polyhedral complex).
**Test**: Formalize multivariate CPL functions, define the breakpoint locus as a polyhedral complex, and prove the correspondence for 2-layer networks.
**Why now**: The univariate case (`univReluNet_is_cpl`) provides the inductive base case and key technical machinery (finiteness of nonconstant zeros, connectedness arguments). The key insight is that the injection argument generalizes: in ℝⁿ, the "nonconstant zeros" of a CPL function form a polyhedral complex of codimension 1.
**If true**: Provides a rigorous foundation for the empirical observation that ReLU network decision boundaries are tropical hypersurfaces, connecting deep learning theory to tropical algebraic geometry.
**If false**: The breakpoint locus might be more complex than a tropical hypersurface, suggesting that ReLU networks access a strictly richer class of piecewise-linear functions.

### Direction 3: Newton Polygon Method for Tropical Power Series
**Hypothesis**: For a polynomial f(y) = Σ aᵢ yⁱ over a valued field, the valuations of the roots of f are exactly the negatives of the slopes of the lower convex hull of {(i, v(aᵢ))} (the Newton polygon). This can be formalized and proved for polynomials over ℚₚ or formal Laurent series.
**Test**: Define the Newton polygon as the lower convex hull of the coefficient-valuation pairs. Prove that for a polynomial of degree 2, the slopes determine the root valuations. Extend to degree n using the factorization f = Π(y - rⱼ) and the multiplicativity of valuations.
**Why now**: We proved `polyVal_mul_eq_add` (multiplicativity of the tropical valuation, partially), and the tropical semiring distributivity provides the algebraic framework. The key insight is that the Newton polygon slopes are exactly the tropical roots of the tropicalized polynomial.
**If true**: Gives a complete formalization of the classical Newton polygon method, connecting p-adic analysis to tropical geometry.
**If false**: Would indicate subtle issues with multiplicity or characteristic of the residue field.
