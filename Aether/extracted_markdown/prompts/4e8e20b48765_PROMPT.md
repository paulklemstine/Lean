**Title**: Tropical closure rank from closure-stable probes and valuation depth
**Domain**: Bridges
**Mathematical framing**: Work in a `ClosureSemimoduleSystem` with a `ProbeFamily`. For finite probe supports `S`, define a closure complexity functional `ccost S` by minimizing a min-plus combination of `ValuationDepthMeasure` over all coefficient data/witnesses that certify generation of elements from `S`. Main theorems to target: (1) `S ⊆ T -> ccost S ≤ ccost T` or the order-reversed variant depending on convention; (2) `ccost (S ∪ T) ≤ ccost S + ccost T` in tropical/min-plus form; (3) if `x` is generated from `S` by a witness `w`, then `cost x ≤ ccost S ⊗ vdepth w`; (4) closure-stable probes imply the functional descends to closed supports and is invariant under replacing `S` by its closure. The proof strategy should use existing closure-stability axioms to transport witnesses through closure, and `vdepth_sum_le` to control cost under composition/sums of witnesses. If possible, formulate a finite-support certificate extraction theorem giving an explicit bound for closure membership. This is a Bridges concept with a genuine cross-domain link to Computation.
**Concept description**: The key insight is that the closure machinery already formalized in `Bridges/AlgebraEMLClosureComputation.lean` can be paired with the quantitative valuation-depth functional from `Computation/PadicValuationDepth.lean` to produce a genuine bridge theorem: every closure-stable probe family induces a monotone tropical rank/energy on finite probe supports, and this rank satisfies subadditivity and certificate bounds for closure generation. Why now: the catalog already contains `ClosureSemimoduleSystem`, `ProbeFamily`, and `ClosureStableProbe` on the bridge side, and `ValuationDepthMeasure` with lemmas such as `vdepth_const_eq_zero` and `vdepth_sum_le` on the computation side, so the missing work is not building a new framework but proving a new interaction theorem between two existing ones. Concretely, define for a finite family of probes a tropical cost given by the infimum/min-plus aggregate of valuation depths of coefficients witnessing membership in the generated closure; then prove monotonicity under inclusion of supports, a tropical subadditivity law under union of supports, and a certificate theorem saying that if an element lies in the closure generated by a support then its closure cost is bounded by the support cost plus the valuation depth of a witness. This yields an algorithmic pipeline: finite supports get assigned a computable tropical complexity profile, and closure-generation proofs can be quantitatively compared. The direction is distinct from the in-flight tropical Galois-connection project because it does not study closed-set/support adjunctions; instead it studies a valuation-sensitive complexity invariant on closure certificates. A falsifiable target theorem is that closure-stability is exactly the hypothesis needed for the induced tropical cost to be monotone and union-subadditive, and counterexamples should exist without closure-stability.
