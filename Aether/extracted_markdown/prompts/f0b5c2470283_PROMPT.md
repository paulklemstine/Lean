## 1. Multivariate Tropical Polynomial Convexity and Amoeba Structure

The natural next step is extending our one-variable tropical polynomial convexity result to the multivariate setting. A tropical polynomial in n variables f(x₁,...,xₙ) = max_i(aᵢ + Σⱼ nᵢⱼ · xⱼ) is the pointwise supremum of affine functions ℝⁿ → ℝ, hence convex. The tropical hypersurface in ℝⁿ — the locus where the maximum is achieved by ≥2 terms — forms a polyhedral complex. The key insight is that convexity of the tropical polynomial forces the complement of the tropical hypersurface to decompose into convex polytopes on which the polynomial is affine, and this decomposition is dual to the Newton polytope subdivision. Why now? Our formalized one-variable framework (Finset.sup', ConvexOn.sup induction) generalizes directly to ℝⁿ using Mathlib's ConvexOn over vector spaces, and the polyhedral complex structure can be captured via Finset-indexed decompositions of ℝⁿ.

## 2. Quantitative Maslov Dequantization: Convergence Rates and Tropical Limits of Integrals

Our Maslov dequantization theorem shows h·log(exp(a/h) + exp(b/h)) → max(a,b) as h→0⁺. A quantitative refinement would establish the convergence rate: the error is O(h·exp(-|a-b|/h)) when a ≠ b, and O(h·log 2) when a = b. More ambitiously, the Maslov dequantization of integrals — showing that h·log(∫ exp(f(x)/h) dμ(x)) → sup f as h→0⁺ (Laplace's method in disguise) — would connect tropical geometry to large deviations theory. The key insight is that the Laplace method is precisely the infinite-dimensional Maslov dequantization, and our two-term proof strategy (squeeze between bounds that collapse) extends to integrals via the Dominated Convergence Theorem. Why now? Mathlib has both the measure theory (MeasureTheory.Integral) and the Laplace-type asymptotics infrastructure needed to formalize this bridge.

## 3. Ronkin Function Convexity via Jensen's Formula

The classical Ronkin function N_f(x) = (2πi)⁻ⁿ ∫_{|zⱼ|=eˣʲ} log|f(z)| dz₁/z₁ ∧...∧ dzₙ/zₙ is convex on all of ℝⁿ and affine on each connected component of the amoeba complement. Formalizing this requires complex integration, which is partially available in Mathlib. The key insight is that Ronkin function convexity follows from Jensen's formula applied fiber-by-fiber: the integral of a subharmonic function (log|f|) over a torus is a convex function of the log-radii. Why now? Mathlib's Cauchy integral formula and basic complex analysis infrastructure are maturing, and our tropical polynomial convexity theorem provides the "tropical shadow" that the Ronkin function converges to under Maslov dequantization, giving a clear verification target.

## 4. Tropical Discriminants and Secondary Polytopes

The tropical discriminant of a family of tropical polynomials characterizes when the tropical hypersurface becomes singular (i.e., when the polyhedral complex develops a vertex of higher valence). This connects to the theory of secondary polytopes and regular subdivisions. The key insight is that the tropical discriminant can be computed as the tropical resultant — a purely combinatorial object defined via mixed subdivisions of Minkowski sums — and our Finset-based framework for tropical polynomials can encode mixed subdivision data. Why now? The formalization of Minkowski sums of polytopes is tractable using Mathlib's convexity library, and the connection between regular subdivisions and convex piecewise-linear functions (which we have formalized) provides the bridge.

## 5. Berkovich Analytification and the Tropical Map

In non-Archimedean geometry, the tropicalization map is the composition of the Berkovich analytification with the "val" map to ℝⁿ. The image is the Berkovich skeleton, which is a tropical variety. Formalizing even the basic setup — valued fields, Berkovich spaces as multiplicative seminorm spaces, and the tropicalization map — would create foundational infrastructure for formal tropical geometry. The key insight is that the Maslov dequantization we proved is the Archimedean shadow of this non-Archimedean construction: as the valuation becomes "infinitely non-Archimedean" (h→0), classical complex geometry degenerates to tropical geometry. Why now? Mathlib has the theory of valuations and valued fields (Mathlib.RingTheory.Valuation), providing the algebraic foundation, and our Maslov dequantization theorem provides the motivating analytic example to guide the formalization.
