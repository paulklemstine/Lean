## Assignment: Direction 1: Full Wreath Product Phase Transition

Prove a genuinely new theorem package about random generation in imprimitive wreath products that goes beyond coordinate-defect heuristics and captures the full maximal-subgroup geometry of  
\[
W_{k,m} := S_k \wr S_m = S_k^m \rtimes S_m
\]
in product action. The target is not a cosmetic refinement of subgroup pressure, but a structural theorem showing that the semidirect symmetry contributes only lower-order pressure and therefore does not destroy the base-group phase transition mechanism.

This should be treated as a **prove + discover** project: prove rigorous upper/lower bounds that isolate the dominant pressure term, define at least one new pressure invariant adapted to wreath products, and produce a verified computational pipeline that tests the conjectural asymptotic law on small instances.

Build explicitly on:

- `Pythagorean/SubgroupPressure.lean`
  - pressure definition
  - product factorization principles
  - block/coordinate-defect pressure formulas
- any already-certified lemmas there concerning maximal subgroup contribution bounds, summability, or generation probability upper/lower estimates.

Your goal is to turn those tools into the first formal theorem saying: in the full wreath product, the phase transition is governed to first order by coordinate defects, while all non-coordinate maximal subgroups are pressure-subcritical.

## Central Mathematical Objective

Let
\[
P(W_{k,m}) := \sum_{M \in \mathrm{Max}(W_{k,m})} [W_{k,m}:M]^{-1}
\]
denote the full maximal subgroup pressure, and let
\[
P_{\mathrm{coord}}(W_{k,m})
\]
be the contribution from maximal subgroups arising from coordinate defects in the base group \(S_k^m\).

The breakthrough theorem should isolate a decomposition
\[
P(W_{k,m}) = P_{\mathrm{coord}}(W_{k,m}) + P_{\mathrm{noncoord}}(W_{k,m}),
\]
and show that \(P_{\mathrm{noncoord}}\) is asymptotically lower-order in \(m\) (for fixed \(k\), or in a specified scaling regime), so that the threshold for random generation is determined by the same first-order term as in the product/base model.

This would be the first rigorous “universality theorem” for generation phase transitions in a structured nontrivial permutation family: the semidirect coupling changes constants, but not the mechanism.

### Theorem 1: Pressure decomposition and dominance of coordinate defects

Mathematical statement:

For fixed \(k \ge 5\), there exists a function \(C_k : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}\) with \(C_k(m) = o(m)\) such that for all \(m \ge 1\),
\[
P(W_{k,m}) \le m \, P(S_k) + C_k(m),
\]
and
\[
P(W_{k,m}) \ge m \, P(S_k).
\]
Hence
\[
P(W_{k,m}) = m\,P(S_k) + o(m).
\]

Lean 4 target shape:

```lean
theorem wreath_pressure_sandwich
    (k : ℕ) (hk : 5 ≤ k) :
    ∃ C : ℕ → ℝ,
      (∀ m : ℕ, 0 ≤ C m) ∧
      (∀ m : ℕ, coordDefectPressure k m ≤ wreathPressure k m) ∧
      (∀ m : ℕ, wreathPressure k m ≤ coordDefectPressure k m + C m) ∧
      PressureSubcriticalInM C (fun m => (m : ℝ) * subgroupPressure (SymmetricGroup k)) := by
  ...
```

This is the conceptual heart of the project. It says the semidirect action contributes pressure, but not enough to change the first-order law.

### Theorem 2: Uniform sublinear bound for non-coordinate pressure

Mathematical statement:

Assume each non-coordinate maximal subgroup type of \(W_{k,m}\) has index at least \(F_k(m)\), and the number of such subgroups is at most \(N_k(m)\), where
\[
\frac{N_k(m)}{F_k(m)} = o(m).
\]
Then
\[
P_{\mathrm{noncoord}}(W_{k,m}) = o(m).
\]

Lean 4 target shape:

```lean

    (hcount : ∀ m : ℕ, 0 ≤ N m)

  ...
```

This theorem is robust and reusable: once you prove count/index estimates from O’Nan–Scott or computational classification, the asymptotic phase transition follows abstractly.

## Proof architecture: 3 possible strategies

You must include multi-step proofs using induction, `rcases`, `by_contra`, `field_simp`, and `calc`. No trivial `decide`-style closure.

### Strategy A: O’Nan–Scott type partition + index summation
**Most promising.**

1. Partition maximal subgroups of \(W_{k,m}\) into:
   - coordinate-defect type,
   - block/diagonal type induced by the \(S_m\)-action,
   - twisted/product-action types.
2. Prove lower bounds on subgroup indices for each non-coordinate class.
3. Prove upper bounds on the number of subgroups in each class.
4. Sum reciprocal indices and show the total is \(o(m)\).

Why this is strongest: it gives a conceptual theorem, not just a computational fit. It matches the conjectural mechanism and interfaces naturally with existing pressure lemmas from the catalog.

### Strategy B: Abstract pressure comparison via semidirect products
1. Develop a general theorem for semidirect products \(G^m \rtimes H\): if maximal subgroups not projecting to coordinate defects have sufficiently large index, then their pressure is subcritical.
2. Instantiate with \(G=S_k\), \(H=S_m\).
3. Recover the wreath product theorem as a corollary.

Why this matters: it opens a whole new field—phase transitions in structured semidirect products, not just wreath products. This is the “field-opening” route.

### Strategy C: Certified small-case classification + asymptotic bootstrapping
1. Use GAP to enumerate maximal subgroups for \(km \le 12\).
2. Discover empirical laws for non-coordinate pressure.
3. Formalize an asymptotic theorem under a clean combinatorial hypothesis suggested by the data.
4. Prove the hypothesis in the easiest infinite regime first (e.g. fixed \(k\), growing \(m\)).

Why useful: this de-risks the project and may reveal the correct invariant before the full proof is in place.

Pressure behaves like a rare-event union bound over maximal obstructions to generation.
