## Assignment: prove

Prove a genuinely new multivariate polynomial theorem in Lean 4: a **Schwartz–Zippel / footprint bound on arbitrary finite Cartesian products** over a field, not just the full grid `F^n` or `GF(q)^n`.

This is the right target because it upgrades the combinatorial Nullstellensatz worldview from uniform coordinate alphabets to **anisotropic finite geometries**. That is the mathematically natural setting for coding theory with unequal symbol sets, constrained interpolation, finite statistical mechanics on product state spaces, and algebraic learning on structured discrete domains.

The theorem is not a cosmetic generalization. It is the correct “coordinatewise degree versus support size” law for restricted product spaces, and it opens a path to:
- anisotropic Reed–Muller / evaluation code distance bounds,
- combinatorial nullstellensatz on non-uniform alphabets,
- finite-grid uncertainty principles,
- algebraic lower bounds for sparse predictors on product domains,
- tropical / entropy analogies where each coordinate has its own capacity.

## Precise theorem target

Let `F` be a field, let `σ : Fin n → Type*` index variables as `Fin n`, and let `S : Fin n → Finset F` be finite nonempty coordinate sets. Define for each coordinate
\[
g_i(X_i) := \prod_{a \in S_i}(X_i-a).
\]
A polynomial `f : MvPolynomial (Fin n) F` is **reduced relative to `S`** if, for every variable `i`, the degree of `f` in `X_i` is strictly less than `|S_i|`.

For a nonzero reduced polynomial `f`, if `e_i := \deg_{X_i}(f)` (or any upper bounds with `e_i < |S_i|`), then
\[
\#\{x \in \prod_i S_i : f(x)\neq 0\}
\;\ge\;
\prod_i (|S_i|-e_i).
\]

This is the finite-product version of the Alon–Füredi / footprint bound.

## Exact mathematical framing

You should explicitly aim at the following theorem schema.

### Main theorem (anisotropic footprint bound)
Let `F` be a field, `S_i ⊆ F` finite nonempty, and let
\[
A := \prod_{i=1}^n S_i.
\]
Let `f ∈ F[X_1,\dots,X_n]` be nonzero and reduced modulo the coordinate vanishing polynomials
\[
g_i(X_i)=\prod_{a\in S_i}(X_i-a).
\]
If for each `i` the exponent of `X_i` in every monomial of `f` is at most `e_i`, with `e_i < |S_i|`, then
\[
|\{x\in A : f(x)\neq 0\}| \ge \prod_{i=1}^n (|S_i|-e_i).
\]

### Stronger equivalent zero-set form
\[
|\{x\in A : f(x)=0\}| \le |A| - \prod_i (|S_i|-e_i).
\]

### Specialization
When all `S_i = F_q`, this recovers the classical footprint / Alon–Füredi style bound on `F_q^n`.

That specialization should be stated explicitly in comments/docstrings: the theorem is a strict conceptual extension of the uniform-grid case.

## Why this is a breakthrough

This theorem is the missing finite-geometry primitive for **nonuniform discrete algebra**. In modern language, it says:

> On a product space with coordinate capacities `|S_i|`, a polynomial with bounded coordinatewise complexity cannot vanish “too much” unless it is algebraically forced to.

That principle is foundational across several fields:

- **Coding theory:** minimum distance bounds for evaluation codes on unequal alphabets.
- **Additive combinatorics:** restricted-grid Nullstellensatz and incidence bounds.
- **Learning theory:** lower bounds on how often low-coordinate-degree predictors can be identically silent.
- **Statistical mechanics:** product state spaces with inhomogeneous local state counts.
- **Tropical / idempotent analogues:** coordinate capacities become tropical box constraints.

This is exactly the sort of theorem that creates a reusable bridge object in Mathlib: finite product sets + multivariate evaluation + degree control.

## Proof architecture: 3 viable strategies

### Strategy A: induction on the number of variables via fiberwise root counting
This is the most promising formal route.

#### Step A1
Write `f` as a polynomial in the last variable with coefficients in
`MvPolynomial (Fin (n-1)) F`, or equivalently use `Fin.cons`/`Fin.snoc` machinery to split one coordinate.

#### Step A2
For each fixed choice of the first `n-1` coordinates, obtain a univariate polynomial in the last coordinate. By the univariate root bound, either:
- that fiber polynomial is identically zero, or
- it has at most `e_last` zeros in `S_last`,
hence at least `|S_last| - e_last` nonzeros.

#### Step A3
Control the set of base points where the fiber polynomial is identically zero by showing its coefficient polynomials satisfy smaller coordinatewise degree bounds, then invoke the induction hypothesis on at least one nonzero coefficient / leading coefficient.

Why this is best:
- it uses robust existing Mathlib tools for univariate root counts,
- avoids Gröbner-basis-heavy ideal quotient arguments,
- naturally matches the product formula in the theorem.

The key conceptual lemma is:
> If many fibers vanish identically, then the leading coefficient polynomial on the remaining coordinates vanishes on many base points, contradicting the induction hypothesis.

This is the classical Alon–Füredi induction, and it is likely the cleanest Lean path.

---

### Strategy B: interpolation basis / tensor-product Lagrange expansion
This is conceptually elegant and may produce the strongest reusable API.

#### Step B1
Construct the tensor-product Lagrange basis on the grid:
\[
L_u(x)=\prod_i \ell_{u_i}^{(i)}(x_i), \quad u \in \prod_i S_i,
\]
where each `\ell_{a}^{(i)}` is the univariate Lagrange basis on `S_i`.

#### Step B2
Show every reduced polynomial has a unique interpolation representation from its values on the grid. Thus the evaluation map from reduced polynomials to functions on the grid is injective, in fact bijective onto all functions.

#### Step B3
Use a dimension / support argument or the explicit top-monomial coefficient formula to show that if `f` vanishes on too many grid points, then all coefficients in a certain “box” vanish, forcing `f = 0`.

Why this is powerful:
- it yields not only the theorem but also a formal **interpolation equivalence** for reduced polynomials on arbitrary grids,
- this becomes infrastructure for coding theory and discrete harmonic analysis.

Why it is harder:
- requires more setup for basis manipulations,
- coefficient extraction over `MvPolynomial` can be technically dense.

This is the most reusable long-term route, but maybe not the fastest first theorem.

---

### Strategy C: ideal-theoretic quotient and standard monomial footprint
This is the most algebraically canonical route.

#### Step C1
Consider the ideal
\[
I_S = \langle g_i(X_i) : i=1,\dots,n\rangle.
\]
Show reduced representatives modulo `I_S` are exactly those with coordinate exponents `< |S_i|`.

#### Step C2
Show the standard monomials form a basis of the quotient ring, with cardinality `∏ |S_i|`, and identify the quotient with the algebra of functions on the grid `∏ S_i`.

#### Step C3
Translate the support of a reduced polynomial into a footprint bound via monomial ideals or the combinatorial Nullstellensatz machinery.

Why this matters:
- this is the deepest conceptual proof,
- it aligns the theorem with Gröbner theory and finite algebraic geometry,
- it opens a road to arbitrary zero-dimensional complete intersections.

Why it is hardest:
- quotient-ring and ideal APIs in Lean may slow progress,
- proving the full algebra isomorphism may be substantial.

Use this if you want a field-opening library contribution, not just the theorem.

### 1. Coding theory

\[
\mathcal C(S,e)=\{(f(x))_{x\in \prod_i S_i} : \deg_{X_i}f\le e_i\},

your theorem gives the minimum distance lower bound
\[

This is the anisotropic Reed–Muller / affine Cartesian code bound.

### 2. Combinatorial Nullstellensatz
If the coefficient of `∏ X_i^{e_i}` is nonzero with `e_i < |S_i|`, then `f` cannot vanish everywhere on the grid. Your theorem quantitatively strengthens the existential CN conclusion.

### 3. Learning theory / VC-style algebraic complexity
A polynomial classifier on a finite product domain with low coordinatewise degree must activate on a substantial fraction of states unless zero. This suggests algebraic lower bounds for sparse-discrete models.

### 4. Statistical mechanics
The grid `∏ S_i` is a finite product configuration space with inhomogeneous local state counts. Your theorem becomes a rigidity law: low local algebraic complexity cannot suppress activity on too much of the phase space.

### 5. Tropical / idempotent analogy
The factors `|S_i| - e_i` behave like residual capacities per coordinate. This invites a tropicalized “capacity × support” principle and a future bridge to idempotent information theory.
