## Precise Theorem Targets

You should formulate and prove a finite-dimensional theorem first, with all hypotheses explicit and Lean-friendly.

### Theorem 1: Pressure equals logarithmic spectral radius

Let:
- `X` be a finite type,
- `Gen` be a finite type of basic closure correspondences,
- each generator `g : Gen` induce a relation `step g : X → X → Prop`,
- `w : Gen → ℝ` be a weight function,
- admissible traces of length `n` be sequences of generators/states compatible with closure and step rules,
- `Z_n = ∑_{admissible traces τ of length n} exp (∑_{g in τ} w g)`.

Define the transfer operator on observables `f : X → ℝ` by
\[
(T_W f)(x) = \sum_{g \in Gen}\ \sum_{y : X,\ step\ g\ y\ x}\exp(w(g))\, f(y),
\]
or the closure-compatible variant where only closure-admissible predecessor pairs are counted.

Then prove:
\[
P := \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log Z_n = \log \rho(T_W),
\]
where `ρ(T_W)` is the spectral radius of the finite matrix representing `T_W`.

A stronger finite result is preferable:

> If the weighted adjacency/transfer matrix `A_W` is nonnegative and nonzero, then
> \[
> P = \log(\max \{ |\lambda| : \lambda \text{ eigenvalue of } A_W\}),
> \]
> and if `A_W` is irreducible, the limsup is an actual limit.

This should be formalized through finite matrices first; only then lift to closure correspondences.

### Lean-oriented type signature target for Theorem 1

A plausible Lean 4 statement skeleton:

```lean
theorem pressure_eq_log_spectralRadius
  {X Gen : Type*} [Fintype X] [DecidableEq X] [Fintype Gen] [DecidableEq Gen]
  (step : Gen → X → X → Prop)
  [DecidablePred (fun p : Gen × X × X => step p.1 p.2.1 p.2.2)]
  (w : Gen → ℝ)
  (A : Matrix X X ℝ)
  (hA :
    ∀ x y, A x y =
      ∑ g : Gen, if step g y x then Real.exp (w g) else 0)
  (hnonneg : ∀ x y, 0 ≤ A x y)
  (hnonzero : A ≠ 0) :
  closurePressure step w = Real.log (spectralRadius A)
```

You may need to define `closurePressure` as a limsup over weighted path sums, and possibly prove an intermediate theorem identifying `Z_n` with a matrix norm / total mass of `A^n`.

If `spectralRadius` over `ℝ` matrices is inconvenient in current Mathlib, reduce to a finite-dimensional nonnegative matrix theorem already available or formalize a suitable `maxRoot` / Perron eigenvalue proxy for nonnegative irreducible matrices. If necessary, first prove:
```lean
closurePressure step w = Real.log (perronRoot A)
```
under irreducibility hypotheses, where `perronRoot A` is defined as the positive eigenvalue furnished by Perron–Frobenius.

### Theorem 2: Equilibrium states correspond to extremal normalized semiring characters

Construct a weighted correspondence semiring `S(C,W)` whose elements are finite closure correspondences modulo closure-congruence, with:
- addition given by union/superposition,
- multiplication given by composition,
- scalar weight filtration induced by `w`,
- a normalization at the identity / total mass.

Define a normalized semiring character to be a map
\[
\chi : S(C,W) \to \mathbb R_{\ge 0}
\]
such that
- `χ(0)=0`, `χ(1)=1`,
- `χ(a+b)=χ(a)+χ(b)` for disjoint/additively represented pieces, or an appropriate semiring-additive axiom,
- `χ(a*b)=χ(a)\chi(b)`,
- `χ` is constant on closure congruence classes,
- `χ` is extremal in the convex set of normalized positive characters.

Define equilibrium states/functionals on observables as maximizers of the thermodynamic functional
\[
\mathcal F(\mu)= h(\mu)+\int W\, d\mu
\]
or, in the finite setting if entropy formalization is too heavy, an equivalent transfer/eigenmeasure notion:
- `μ` is `T_W`-conformal or stationary after normalization,
- `μ` realizes the top eigenvalue,
- `μ` is extremal among invariant normalized positive functionals.

Then prove a bijection:
\[
\{\text{equilibrium states}\}
\cong
\{\text{extremal normalized closure-stable semiring characters}\}.
\]

### Theorem 3: Quotient–Equilibrium Galois correspondence

Let closure quotients be represented by closure-compatible congruences `≈` on correspondences or states. Let `EqFace(≈)` denote the face of equilibrium functionals factoring through the quotient. Let `Ker(μ)` be the largest closure congruence invisible to `μ` or its associated character.

Prove antitone maps:
\[
\Phi(\approx)=\{\mu : \mu \text{ equilibrium and factors through } X/{\approx}\},
\]
\[
\Psi(F)=\bigcap_{\mu\in F}\ker(\mu),
\]
and establish:
\[
\approx \subseteq \Psi(F) \iff F \subseteq \Phi(\approx).
\]

This is the actual Galois duality theorem. It says semantic coarse-grainings of closure dynamics are exactly reflected by thermodynamic degeneracies/equilibrium faces.

### Lean-oriented theorem signature target

```lean

  {Q : Setoid X}
  (hQ : ClosureRespecting Q)
  (F : Set (EquilibriumFunctional X)) :
  QuotientCongruenceLe (equilibriumFaceKernel F) Q ↔
    F ⊆ equilibriumFaceOfQuotient Q
```

You may need to reverse the order convention depending on how you encode the lattice.

## Minimal Formal Development Plan

A successful formal campaign should proceed in layers.

### Layer 1: Weighted finite path counting
Define weighted admissible paths/traces and prove:
- `Z_(m+n) ≤ Z_m * Z_n` or exact matrix-composition identities,
- pressure exists as limsup,
- in irreducible/primitive settings, limsup is limit.

### Layer 2: Matrix realization of transfer operator
Construct the weighted adjacency matrix `A_W` and prove:
- path weights equal entries or total mass of `A_W^n`,
- `Z_n` is a sum of entries / norm / sandwich expression involving `A_W^n`,
- therefore asymptotic growth is spectral.

### Layer 3: Semiring of closure correspondences
Define:
- closure correspondences,
- quotient by closure congruence,
- additive/multiplicative structure,
- weight filtration,
- positive normalized characters.

### Layer 4: Equilibrium ↔ character dictionary
Show:
- a positive eigenfunctional induces a multiplicative character on correspondence classes,
- an extremal character yields a stationary/eigenmeasure,
- these assignments are inverse under irreducibility/separation assumptions.

### Layer 5: Galois correspondence
Formalize the lattice of closure congruences and the convex face lattice of equilibria, then prove the antitone adjunction.

## 2–3 Proof Strategy Architectures

### Strategy A: Matrix thermodynamic formalism first, then algebraize
1. Encode weighted closure dynamics as a finite nonnegative matrix `A_W`.
2. Prove `Z_n` is exactly the total weighted mass of length-`n` paths, hence controlled by `A_W^n`.
3. Apply Perron–Frobenius to identify pressure with `log ρ(A_W)`.
4. Use left/right Perron eigenvectors to build equilibrium functionals.
5. Show multiplicativity on correspondences by passing from composition of relations to matrix multiplication; characters arise from evaluating correspondence operators against the eigenfunctional.
6. Prove quotient invariance by checking closure congruent correspondences act identically on quotient observables.

**Why this is promising:** it is the shortest route to a fully formal theorem because finite nonnegative matrices are the most Lean-accessible object. This should be your primary route.

### Strategy B: Subadditive/Fekete pressure route, then identify operator growth
1. Prove submultiplicativity or quasi-multiplicativity of `Z_n`.
2. Deduce existence of pressure via Fekete’s lemma or limsup control.
3. Independently show operator norms of `T_W^n` encode the same weighted trace growth.
4. Use Gelfand-type spectral radius formula in finite dimension to identify the asymptotic rate.
5. Build equilibrium states as maximizing normalized positive eigenfunctionals.

**Why this matters:** it is more conceptual and closer to thermodynamic formalism. Use it if matrix spectral lemmas are available but path counting identities are delicate.

### Strategy C: Semiring representation-theoretic route
1. Define `S(C,W)` as the semiring generated by weighted closure correspondences.
2. Construct a semiring representation on observables and identify the transfer operator as the image of a distinguished “one-step partition element.”
3. Show normalized extremal characters correspond to extremal positive eigenfunctionals of the represented semiring.
4. Recover pressure from logarithmic growth of powers of the distinguished semiring element.
5. Derive the quotient–equilibrium Galois connection at the semiring level.

**Why this is visionary:** this route reveals the true algebraic mechanism and could lead to a general theory beyond finite systems. It is likely harder in Lean, so treat it as the conceptual endgame after Strategy A.

## Cross-Domain Connections You Should Exploit

This project becomes field-opening only if you make the connections explicit in the formalization and theorem statements.

### 1. Symbolic dynamics / thermodynamic formalism
Your pressure theorem is a closure-dynamics analogue of the classical Perron–Ruelle pressure formula. But unlike subshifts of finite type, admissibility is generated by **closure semantics**, not by a graph alone. This makes EML closure systems a new host for thermodynamic semantics.

### 2. Idempotent and semiring algebra
The character side is a nonclassical analogue of the Gelfand spectrum, but for semirings of correspondences rather than commutative Banach algebras. This suggests a future **semiring spectral geometry of semantics**.

### 3. Universal algebra and Galois theory
The quotient–equilibrium correspondence is structurally analogous to classical Galois duality:
- congruences ↔ invariant substructures,
- equilibrium faces ↔ thermodynamic observables preserved under coarse-graining.
This is the right conceptual frame: not just “duality,” but **thermodynamic reconstruction via congruence spectra**.

### 4. Statistical mechanics
Nonuniqueness of extremal characters should be interpreted as **phase splitting**. The algebraic criterion for multiple extremal characters becomes a semantic phase transition detector.

### 5. Theoretical computer science / semantics
Closure quotients yielding canonical summaries of EML dynamics suggest applications to:
- state-space minimization,
- semantic compression,
- emergent language reconstruction,
- algorithmic detection of metastable semantic regimes.

### 6. Category theory
There is a latent functor:
\[
(\text{closure systems with weights}) \to (\text{character spaces / equilibrium polytopes}),
\]
contravariant on quotient maps. Even if not fully formalized now, phrase your results so that a categorical upgrade is natural.
