### Theorem 1: Symmetry and positivity of the cycle-period matrix

For a connected finite graph with positive edge lengths and a chosen integral cycle basis, the induced period matrix is symmetric positive definite on the cycle space.

**Mathematical statement.**
Let `G` be a connected finite graph, let `B` be an integral cycle basis of rank `g`, and let `L = diag(ℓ_e)` be the diagonal edge-length matrix with `ℓ_e > 0`. If `C` is the edge-by-cycle incidence matrix of the basis, define
\[
Q := C^\top L C.
\]
Then:
1. `Q` is symmetric;
2. for every nonzero `x ∈ ℝ^g`, one has `x^T Q x > 0`.

This is the metrized graph period form. It is the continuous analogue of the reduced Laplacian pairing.

**Lean 4 type signature target:**

### Theorem 2: Discrete-to-continuous energy correspondence

Show that the period form on cycles matches the weighted edge energy, making the continuous Jacobian computable from combinatorial representatives.

**Mathematical statement.**
With the notation above, for any `x ∈ ℝ^g`,
\[
x^\top Q x = \sum_{e \in E} \ell_e \left(\sum_{i=1}^g C_{e i} x_i\right)^2.
\]
This is the key identity from which positivity and stability flow.

**Lean 4 type signature target:**
```lean
theorem periodMatrix_quadratic_form
    {g : ℕ} (C : Matrix E (Fin g) ℤ) (ℓ : E → ℝ)
    (x : Fin g → ℝ) :
    let L : Matrix E E ℝ := Matrix.diagonal (fun e => ℓ e)
    let CR : Matrix E (Fin g) ℝ := fun i j => (C i j : ℝ)
    let Q : Matrix (Fin g) (Fin g) ℝ := (CRᵀ * L * CR)
    dotProduct x (fun i => ∑ j, Q i j * x j)
      = ∑ e, ℓ e * (∑ i, (C e i : ℝ) * x i)^2 := by
  sorry
```

**Why this matters.**
This is the exact bridge between tropical geometry and electrical network theory. It says the Jacobian metric is literally an energy functional. That opens connections to:
- resistor networks,
- Gaussian free fields on graphs,
- spectral graph theory,
- and tropical Hodge structures.

### Theorem 3: Stability under edge-length perturbation

Prove a Lipschitz-type or monotonicity theorem showing that the period form changes continuously under deformation of edge lengths.

**Mathematical statement.**
Let `ℓ, ℓ' : E → ℝ_{>0}` be two edge-length assignments. Let `Q(ℓ), Q(ℓ')` be the corresponding period matrices. Then for each `x`,
\[
|x^\top(Q(\ell)-Q(\ell'))x|
\le
\left(\max_e |\ell_e-\ell'_e|\right)
\sum_e \left(\sum_i C_{ei}x_i\right)^2.
\]
A matrix-norm corollary is also desirable.

**Lean 4 type signature target:**
```lean
theorem periodMatrix_stability_quadratic
    {g : ℕ} (C : Matrix E (Fin g) ℤ)
    (ℓ ℓ' : E → ℝ) (x : Fin g → ℝ) :
    let CR : Matrix E (Fin g) ℝ := fun i j => (C i j : ℝ)
    let Qof (w : E → ℝ) : Matrix (Fin g) (Fin g) ℝ :=
      CRᵀ * (Matrix.diagonal w) * CR
    |dotProduct x (fun i => ∑ j, (Qof ℓ i j - Qof ℓ' i j) * x j)|
      ≤
      (∑ e, |ℓ e - ℓ' e| * (∑ i, (C e i : ℝ) * x i)^2) := by
  sorry
```

A stronger version with `sup`/operator norm is even better if the required matrix norm machinery is manageable.

**Why this is revolutionary.**
This turns the slogan “SNF deforms continuously” into a rigorous theorem on the continuous side. Once stability is formalized, you can study wall-crossing, tropical moduli, and degeneration to the discrete Laplacian in a controlled way.

## Proof strategy architecture

You must pursue 2–3 proof avenues and explain which is most promising.

### Strategy A: Matrix-energy expansion via direct algebra
1. Define `Q = Cᵀ L C`.
2. Prove the quadratic-form identity by expanding matrix multiplication and using `Matrix.diagonal`.
3. Deduce symmetry from `Lᵀ = L`.
4. Deduce positivity from the energy sum and linear independence of cycle columns.

**Why promising:** This is the most Lean-native route. It reduces everything to finite sums, matrix identities, and positivity of squares. It should support `calc`, `rw`, `simp`, `field_simp` where needed, and multi-step algebraic reasoning.

### Strategy B: Bilinear form / Gram matrix route
1. Regard each cycle basis vector as an element of a weighted Euclidean space on edges.
2. Define a weighted inner product
   \[
   \langle u,v\rangle_\ell = \sum_e \ell_e u_e v_e.
   \]
3. Show `Q` is the Gram matrix of the basis under this inner product.
4. Invoke general facts: Gram matrices are symmetric; they are positive definite iff the vectors are linearly independent.

**Why promising:** Conceptually cleaner and mathematically deeper. If Mathlib’s inner product / bilinear form API cooperates, this gives elegant proofs and clearer future generalizations to tropical Hodge theory.

### Strategy C: Deformation from the discrete Laplacian
1. Start from discrete Laplacian pairings available through catalog structures.
2. Construct the cycle-space / harmonic representative dictionary.
3. Show the weighted period form is the continuous deformation of the discrete pairing under edge-length scaling.
4. Use catalog stability theorems to derive perturbation bounds.

**Why promising:** Best for the headline theorem and for explaining scientific significance, but likely heavier in Lean. Use this as the conceptual superstructure, with Strategy A or B supplying the formal core.

**Recommended plan:** Use **Strategy A** for the core formal theorems, **Strategy B** for elegance if the APIs permit, and **Strategy C** to frame the discrete-to-continuous bridge theorem and the paper narrative.
