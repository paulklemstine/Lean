## Concept

**Title**: Closure-stable probe compression via finite semimodule generators
**Domain**: Bridges
**Mathematical framing**: Work with a finite type α of states and a probe family P valued in an ordered algebraic codomain already supported by the closure-semimodule interface. Define, for each subset S ⊆ α, the reconstructed closure Cl_P(S) as the intersection of all probe-defined closed supersets certified by probes stable on S. Prove first that Cl_P is a `SetClosureOperator` using the existing reconstruction lemmas. Then define redundancy of a probe p ∈ P by extensional invariance of Cl under deletion: `redundant p :↔ Cl_(P \ {p}) = Cl_P`. Main theorem A: every finite probe family admits an irredundant subfamily P_min with `Cl_Pmin = Cl_P`; proof strategy is finite descent by deleting redundant probes, using extensional equality of closure operators and finiteness for termination. Main theorem B: characterize indispensability by witness closed sets or witness points x ∈ Cl_P(S) \ Cl_(P\{p})(S). Main theorem C: if the probe family is generated by a finite semimodule basis in the sense of `ClosureSemimoduleSystem`, then compression can be performed at the level of generators, yielding a computable upper bound on certificate size. Optional strengthening: uniqueness up to extensional equivalence classes of probes, not literal equality. This is a bridge result between Algebraic generation and closure reconstruction, producing an algorithm rather than a one-off theorem.
**Concept description**: The key insight is that the existing closure/probe formalism should admit a genuinely algorithmic compression theorem: when a closure system is represented by a semimodule-style family of probes, one can identify a finite generator subfamily that is extensionally equivalent for reconstruction on a prescribed finite support, and prove monotonicity/idempotence of the induced compressed closure operator. Why now: the catalog already contains a mature closure bridge in `Bridges/AlgebraEMLClosureComputation.lean` with `ClosureSemimoduleSystem`, `ProbeFamily`, and `ClosureStableProbe`, together with reconstruction infrastructure in `Bridges/AlgebraEMLReconstruction.lean` (`SetClosureOperator`, closed sets). This makes it realistic to move beyond existence of closures and prove a new finite compression pipeline linking Algebra-style semimodule generation to Bridges-style reconstruction. Concretely, target a theorem of the following shape: for a finite family of observables/probes over a finite carrier, define the closure extracted from all probes that vanish on a set, then show there exists a finite irredundant subfamily yielding the same closure on all subsets of the carrier; moreover prove that greedy deletion of redundant probes terminates and preserves the closure operator. A second theorem should characterize minimality: a probe is indispensable iff removing it strictly enlarges at least one closed set, giving a falsifiable certificate notion. This is different from the in-flight tropical closure-probe work because it stays in the general bridge/closure-semimodule setting and aims at compression/minimality theorems rather than tropical Helly reconstruction. The payoff is an explicit algorithmic pipeline for certificate extraction from large probe systems, with immediate downstream use in any domain that instantiates the closure framework.
