### Conjecture: asymptotic sharp constant
Let \(\gamma_n\) be the spectral gap of the bubble-rotation walk on \(S_n\). Then
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty} n^2 \gamma_n = \kappa
\]
for some \(\kappa \in (0,\infty)\), and in fact \(\kappa\) is determined by the standard representation of \(S_n\).

A stronger representation-theoretic prediction:
\[
\gamma_n = 1 - \lambda_{\max}\!\left(\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{s\in S}\rho_{\mathrm{std}}(s)\right),
\]
i.e. the gap is realized by the \((n-1)\)-dimensional standard irrep for all sufficiently large \(n\).

This is falsifiable: compute exact or high-precision eigenvalues for \(n=3,\dots,8\), compare the full regular-representation gap to the standard-representation prediction, and test stabilization of \(n^2\gamma_n\).

## Proof architecture: 3 viable strategies

You must discuss at least 2–3 strategies in the paper/comments and pursue the most promising one in Lean.

### Strategy A — Recursive symbol-placement canonical paths (most promising)
1. For each pair \((\sigma,\tau)\), route from \(\sigma\) to \(\tau\) by correcting the image/preimage of one symbol at a time.
2. Use powers of the long cycle to bring the target symbol into a convenient location.
3. Use adjacent transpositions to insert it into place; then recurse on the stabilizer of that symbol, identified with \(S_{n-1}\).
4. Prove inductively that path length is \(O(n^2)\), then count how many pairs can use a given edge to obtain congestion \(O(n^2 n!)\).

Why promising: it aligns with `CanonicalPathData`, naturally supports induction on `n`, and turns the long cycle into a global repositioning tool that sharply lowers congestion compared to pure bubble-sort routing.

### Strategy B — Comparison to known chains via Dirichlet-form domination
1. Compare the bubble-rotation walk to the adjacent-transposition walk and/or random-transposition walk.
2. Show that adding the long cycle improves the form enough to transfer a spectral estimate.
3. Use catalog spectral comparison lemmas if available, or prove a custom inequality showing each random transposition can be simulated by \(O(n)\) bubble-rotation moves.

Why it may work: less path-counting, more algebraic comparison.  
Why less promising in Lean: the comparison constants may become messy, and proving a clean simulation theorem for all transpositions may require more infrastructure than explicit routing.

### Strategy C — Representation-theoretic attack on the averaging operator
1. Express the Markov operator as convolution by the class-adjacent-plus-cycle measure.
2. Analyze eigenvalues on irreducible representations, especially the standard representation.
3. Derive explicit lower bounds on the spectral gap from character estimates.

Why scientifically exciting: this could identify the sharp constant and connect directly to the conjecture.  
Why risky for this cycle: Mathlib support for the full finite-group representation analysis may not yet be sufficient for an end-to-end formal proof.

**Recommendation:** pursue Strategy A as the main theorem-producing route, and use Strategy C in `RESEARCH_PAPER.md` and `demo.py` as the conjectural sharpness narrative.
