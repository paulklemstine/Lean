### Concrete proof strategy

1. **Define the forward invariant map and prove overlap compatibilities.**  
   Show that the edge valuations extracted from `f` agree with the rank-1 faces of both Levi marginals. This should be a direct consequence of how the Levi marginal/restriction is defined. If your Levi marginals are obtained by restriction to the `(1,2)` and `(2,3)` directions, prove lemmas of the form:
   ```lean
   theorem levi12_restrict_edge01 (f : TropicalHeckeFnGL3 B) :
     restrictEdge01 (levi12Marginal f) = simpleCorootEdgeVal01 f

   theorem levi23_restrict_edge12 (f : TropicalHeckeFnGL3 B) :
     restrictEdge12 (levi23Marginal f) = simpleCorootEdgeVal12 f
   ```
   and the common-face compatibility at the central ray/corner. These lemmas are the exact reason the codomain should be a structure of *compatible* data rather than an unrestricted product.

2. **Use the existing finite-test separation theorem for injectivity.**  
   The key move is: if two bounded-support tropical Hecke functions have identical edge and Levi data, then they agree on the finite separating family already constructed in the partial GL₃ development; by the separation theorem, they are equal. In Lean, avoid reproving any chamber combinatorics globally if the separation theorem already says equality follows from agreement on a specified finite family. The proof skeleton should be:
   ```lean
   intro f g hfg
   apply existing_separation_theorem B
   intro t ht
   -- extract equality of the relevant test value from hfg
   ```
   If the separating family consists of all rank-2 slices plus edge values, then `hfg : boundaryLeviMap B f = boundaryLeviMap B g` should destruct directly into the needed component equalities.

3. **Use the existing surjectivity theorem for compatible datasets.**  
   If your current surjectivity result is formulated for a tuple of boundary invariants satisfying overlap constraints, instantiate it with `d : CompatibleEdgeLeviData B`. Then package the produced `f` into a surjectivity proof for `boundaryLeviMap B`. If the existing theorem is only stated as existence of a preimage for separate edge/Levi pieces, prove a small adapter lemma converting the structure fields of `CompatibleEdgeLeviData B` into the hypotheses expected by that theorem.

4. **Package bijectivity into an equivalence and expose component formulas.**  
   Once injective and surjective are available, use `Equiv.ofBijective`. Then prove the component identities for the forward map by reflexivity/unfolding:
   ```lean
   change simpleCorootEdgeVal01 f = _
   rfl
   ```
   and for the inverse direction by applying `e.apply_symm_apply` or `e.symm_apply_apply` and then the already-proved forward characterization. This yields exact reconstruction formulas, not just abstract existence.

5. **Optional but valuable: prove an induction-style reconstruction lemma by dominant height.**  
   To make the theorem mathematically robust, prove a separate lemma that values are determined recursively by lower-height values plus the relevant Levi/rhombus data:
   ```lean
   theorem gl3_value_determined_by_boundary_and_levi
       {B : ℕ} {f g : TropicalHeckeFnGL3 B}
       (hedge01 : simpleCorootEdgeVal01 f = simpleCorootEdgeVal01 g)
       (hedge12 : simpleCorootEdgeVal12 f = simpleCorootEdgeVal12 g)
       (hlevi12 : levi12Marginal f = levi12Marginal g)
       (hlevi23 : levi23Marginal f = levi23Marginal g) :
       f = g
   ```
   Prove it by induction on a natural “height” such as `μ₁ - μ₃` or `μ₁ + μ₂ + μ₃`, using rank-2 rhombus/diamond relations to recover interior values from previously known boundary/slice values. Even if the final injectivity theorem is discharged abstractly by an existing separation result, this lemma gives the conceptual reason reconstruction works and may be reusable later for algorithmic inversion.

### Why this matters

This theorem is the right completion of the current GL₃ tropical Satake program on bounded support: not merely that edge/Levi data are separating, and not merely that some compatible datasets admit extensions, but that there is a *canonical finite reconstruction equivalence*. In other words, bounded dominant tropical Hecke data for `GL₃` can be encoded exactly by lower-rank marginals plus simple-coroot boundary values, with no loss and no redundancy beyond explicit overlap constraints.

That is important for three reasons:

1. It gives the first honest finite-coordinate model of bounded `GL₃` tropical Satake data, which is stronger than ad hoc injectivity tests or one-off extension lemmas.
2. It identifies the correct compatibility locus, clarifying what the true gluing conditions are between rank-1 and rank-2 pieces.
3. It sets up algorithmic inversion and future comparison theorems: once the data are packaged as an equivalence, one can transport structure, define reconstruction procedures, and compare tropical objects to classical or `p`-adic Satake data on finite windows.

So the goal is not just to prove a bijection abstractly, but to expose the exact finite boundary/Levi coordinates and show they reconstruct the full bounded-support `GL₃` tropical Hecke function uniquely.
