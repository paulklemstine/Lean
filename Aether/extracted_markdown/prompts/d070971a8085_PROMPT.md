## Research Direction
Formalize a mathematically meaningful fragment of tropical intersection theory in Lean 4 by representing tropical hypersurfaces and tropical cycles as weighted polyhedral data on `Finset`s / finite supports, and prove a **certified tropical Bézout theorem** in a finite-dimensional, explicitly computable setting.

The strategic breakthrough is not “define tropicalization vaguely.” It is:

1. **Define tropical polynomial support data** and the associated tropical hypersurface as a corner locus condition.
2. **Define stable intersection multiplicity** for transverse intersections of tropical hypersurfaces in low dimension first (`ℝ²`, then `Fin n → ℝ`).
3. **Prove an explicit Bézout bound**: the total tropical intersection multiplicity is bounded by the product of degrees, and in the generic/transverse case equals that product.
4. **Package the result as a preservation theorem** for a formalized tropicalization map on support/valuation data, at least for a class of polynomials over a valued semiformal coefficient system.

This would open a Lean-native route toward tropical enumerative geometry, certified polyhedral algorithms, and eventually a formal bridge to non-Archimedean geometry and combinatorial optimization.

## Exact Theorem Targets

Start with a rigorous theorem in dimension 2 where the geometry is concrete and Mathlib can support finite combinatorics.

### Core theorem A: Tropical Bézout upper bound in the plane
For tropical plane curves of degrees `d₁, d₂`, the sum of stable intersection multiplicities is at most `d₁ * d₂`.

A Lean-oriented target signature:

```lean
theorem tropical_bezout_bound_plane
  (f g : TropicalPoly2)
  (hf : 0 < f.degree)
  (hg : 0 < g.degree) :
  totalStableIntersectionMultiplicity f g ≤ f.degree * g.degree
```

Here `TropicalPoly2` should be a concrete structure, e.g. finite support in `ℕ × ℕ` with real coefficients:
```lean
structure TropicalMonomial2 where
  exp : ℕ × ℕ
  coeff : ℝ

structure TropicalPoly2 where
  terms : Finset TropicalMonomial2
  degree : ℕ
  degree_spec : ∀ m ∈ terms, m.exp.1 + m.exp.2 ≤ degree
```

Then define tropical evaluation by
```lean
def tropEval (f : TropicalPoly2) (x y : ℝ) : ℝ :=
  (f.terms.sup fun m => m.coeff + (m.exp.1 : ℝ) * x + (m.exp.2 : ℝ) * y)
```
or an equivalent `Finset.max'`-based definition with nonempty support assumptions.

The tropical hypersurface is the set of points where the maximum is attained at least twice. Stable intersection should first be defined only for a certified transverse finite intersection configuration.

### Core theorem B: Generic equality form of tropical Bézout
In the transverse case, the total stable intersection multiplicity is exactly the product of degrees.

```lean
theorem tropical_bezout_transverse_plane
  (f g : TropicalPoly2)
  (hf : 0 < f.degree)
  (hg : 0 < g.degree)
  (htrans : TransversePlaneCurves f g) :
  totalStableIntersectionMultiplicity f g = f.degree * g.degree
```

This is the theorem that matters scientifically. The upper bound is the safety theorem; the equality theorem is the field-opening one.

### Core theorem C: Intersection-number preservation under tropicalization, in a restricted formal regime
Do not overpromise full scheme-theoretic tropicalization immediately. Formalize a restricted theorem for finite-support bivariate polynomials over a coefficient type equipped with a valuation.

```lean
theorem tropicalization_preserves_intersection_number_plane
  (K : Type*) [Field K]
  (v : K → ℝ∞)
  (F G : LaurentPoly2 K)
  (hgeneric : AlgebraicallyTransverse F G)
  (hgood : TropicalizationWellBehaved v F G) :
```

Even if the full left-hand side is initially formalized as a combinatorial Newton-polytope mixed-area quantity rather than local algebra length, that is still substantial. A mixed-volume / Newton-polytope formulation is an excellent intermediate breakthrough.

## Proof strategy options

### Strategy A: Newton polygon / mixed area route
This is the most promising.

1. Associate to each tropical polynomial its Newton polygon as the convex hull of exponent support.
2. Show that the degree controls the polygon inside the standard degree simplex.
3. Prove that total stable intersection multiplicity is bounded by the mixed area of the Newton polygons, and that this mixed area is bounded by `deg f * deg g`.
4. In the generic homogeneous/full-support case, identify the mixed area exactly with `deg f * deg g`.

Why this is best: it converts analytic corner-locus complexity into finite convex combinatorics. Lean is much happier with finite support and determinant identities than with local analytic geometry.

### Strategy B: Dual subdivision route
1. Define the regular subdivision of the Newton polygon induced by tropical coefficients.
2. Show that stable intersection points correspond to mixed cells in the common refinement.
3. Sum local multiplicities as normalized areas of mixed cells.
4. Bound the sum by the total area budget of the Minkowski sum subdivision.

Why valuable: this is the conceptually canonical tropical proof and gives stronger structural output. It is heavier to formalize, but if successful it creates infrastructure for tropical cycles and balancing.

### Strategy C: Direct planar graph balancing route
1. Formalize tropical plane curves as weighted balanced embedded graphs.
2. Define stable intersection multiplicity via perturbation and local determinant counts.
3. Use balancing and degree data at infinity to prove the global count.

Why harder: graph embeddings in `ℝ²` and perturbation arguments are delicate in Lean. Use only if the polyhedral route stalls.

**Recommendation:** begin with Strategy A for the first certified theorem, then import pieces of Strategy B to strengthen from bound to equality.

## Cross-domain connections you must exploit

### 1. Convex geometry
The tropical Bézout theorem is secretly a mixed-volume theorem. Formalize this connection explicitly. If you prove a finite 2D mixed-area lemma in Lean, you are not just doing tropical geometry; you are creating reusable infrastructure for convex combinatorics.

### 2. Optimization / max-plus algebra
The corner locus of a tropical polynomial is where a max-plus linear form ceases to be uniquely optimal. This is the geometry of degeneracy in linear optimization. Stable intersections can be interpreted as certified degeneracy counts of coupled max-plus systems.

### 3. Non-Archimedean geometry
Even a restricted tropicalization-preserves-intersection theorem is a finite combinatorial shadow of deep Berkovich/non-Archimedean phenomena. State this motivation clearly in comments and ARTICLE.md even if the initial Lean theorem only handles a simplified valuation setup.

### 4. Enumerative geometry and computational algebra
A verified tropical Bézout theorem immediately suggests certified root-counting bounds, elimination procedures, and polyhedral homotopy sanity checks.

### Product-degree area bound
For Newton polygons inside degree simplices:
```lean
theorem mixedArea_le_degree_mul_degree
  (P Q : NewtonPolygon2)
  (hP : P ⊆ degreeSimplex d₁)
  (hQ : Q ⊆ degreeSimplex d₂) :
  mixedArea P Q ≤ d₁ * d₂
```

### Generic equality for full simplex supports
```lean
theorem mixedArea_degreeSimplex
  (d₁ d₂ : ℕ) :
  mixedArea (degreeSimplexPolytope d₁) (degreeSimplexPolytope d₂) = d₁ * d₂
```

### Stable intersection equals mixed area in the transverse case
```lean
theorem stableIntersection_eq_mixedArea
  (f g : TropicalPoly2)
  (htrans : TransversePlaneCurves f g) :
  totalStableIntersectionMultiplicity f g =
    mixedArea (newtonPolytope f) (newtonPolytope g)
```

This theorem is the hinge. If you get it, the Bézout theorem becomes a corollary.

## Formalization advice
Use concrete, finite, decidable structures. Avoid topological generality at first.

- Represent supports by `Finset`.
- Represent exponent vectors in dimension 2 by `ℕ × ℕ`, later generalize to `Fin n → ℕ`.
- Use `Matrix (Fin 2) (Fin 2) ℤ` or direct determinant formulas for local multiplicities.
- If convex hull machinery in Mathlib is too heavy, define the needed 2D polygon invariants combinatorially for lattice polygons first.
- Consider starting with a special class: **dense homogeneous tropical polynomials of degree `d`**. This may simplify Newton polytope arguments enough to prove the exact product formula.
