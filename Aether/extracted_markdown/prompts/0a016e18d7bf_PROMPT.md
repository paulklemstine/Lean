```lean
def laplacianDivisor {V} [Fintype V] [DecidableEq V]
    (G : SimpleGraph V) (f : V → ℤ) : GraphDivisor V :=
  ⟨fun v => ∑ w, if G.Adj v w then f v - f w else 0⟩

    (G : SimpleGraph V) (D E : GraphDivisor V) : Prop :=
```

## Precise theorem targets

You must prove at least **3 deep theorems**. The following list gives the target hierarchy. If full generality is too expensive, prove the strongest formally manageable version, but it must still be mathematically meaningful.

### Theorem 1: Degree is invariant under chip-firing
This is foundational and nontrivial because it certifies Laplacian moves as principal divisors.

**Lean target:**
```lean
theorem divisorDegree_laplacian_zero
    {V : Type _} [Fintype V] [DecidableEq V]
    (G : SimpleGraph V) (f : V → ℤ) :
    divisorDegree (laplacianDivisor G f) = 0
```

and hence

```lean
theorem LinearEquivalent.degree_eq
    {V : Type _} [Fintype V] [DecidableEq V]
    {G : SimpleGraph V} {D E : GraphDivisor V} :
    LinearEquivalent G D E → divisorDegree D = divisorDegree E
```

This should require genuine summation rearrangement and cancellation, not trivial automation.

### Theorem 2: Canonical divisor has degree `2g - 2`
This is the tropical shadow of the algebro-geometric canonical class formula.

**Lean target:**
```lean
theorem degree_canonicalDivisor
    {V : Type _} [Fintype V] [DecidableEq V]
    (G : SimpleGraph V) [FiniteConnectedGraph G] :
    divisorDegree (canonicalDivisor G) = 2 * genus G - 2
```

This theorem matters because it identifies the graph-theoretic canonical divisor with the Euler characteristic of the underlying curve. It is the combinatorial backbone of Riemann–Roch.

### Theorem 3: Baker–Norine / tropical Riemann–Roch on complete graphs
If full connected finite graphs are too ambitious in one cycle, do not retreat to toy lemmas. Instead, prove the theorem on `Kₙ`, where explicit chip-firing normal forms are accessible and still deep.

For the complete graph `K n` with `n ≥ 2`, define an explicit genus
`g = (n-1)(n-2)/2`, explicit canonical divisor, and prove:

**Lean target:**
```lean
theorem completeGraph_riemann_roch
    (n : ℕ) (hn : 2 ≤ n)
    (D : GraphDivisor (Fin n)) :
    divisorRank (completeGraph n) D
      - divisorRank (completeGraph n) (subDivisor (canonicalDivisor (completeGraph n)) D)
      = divisorDegree D - genus (completeGraph n) + 1
```

If `divisorRank : ... → ℤ` is too difficult to construct globally, prove the equivalent pair of inequalities and existence statements via `DivisorRankAtLeast`, but the mathematical content must clearly instantiate Riemann–Roch.

### Theorem 4: Explicit rank formula for complete-graph divisors
This is where the work becomes algorithmic and new.

For divisors of the form `D = k • [v]` on `Kₙ`, prove an explicit rank formula, e.g. a certified specialization of Baker–Norine’s complete-graph rank computation.

A plausible target:

```lean
theorem completeGraph_singleVertex_rank
    (n : ℕ) (hn : 2 ≤ n) (v : Fin n) (k : ℤ) :
    divisorRank (completeGraph n) (singleVertexDivisor v k)
      = explicitCompleteGraphRank n k
```

where `explicitCompleteGraphRank` is your verified arithmetic formula. If the full closed form is too ambitious, prove sharp upper and lower bounds that coincide on an infinite family.

### Theorem 5: Cross-domain theorem — chip-firing as discrete potential theory
You must include at least one theorem connecting tropical geometry to another domain. The best target here is **discrete harmonic analysis / physics**.

For example:

```lean
theorem chip_firing_preserves_total_charge
    {V : Type _} [Fintype V] [DecidableEq V]
    (G : SimpleGraph V) (f : V → ℤ) :
    divisorDegree (laplacianDivisor G f) = 0
```

interpreted as conservation of charge in a resistor network, and then strengthen it to:

```lean
theorem linear_equiv_iff_diff_in_laplacian_image
    {V : Type _} [Fintype V] [DecidableEq V]
    (G : SimpleGraph V) (D E : GraphDivisor V) :
    LinearEquivalent G D E ↔
    ∃ f : V → ℤ, ∀ v, E.coeff v - D.coeff v = -(laplacianDivisor G f).coeff v
```

This is simultaneously tropical geometry, algebraic graph theory, and discrete electrostatics.

A stronger cross-domain theorem, if feasible, is to connect divisor classes to the sandpile/critical group:
- prove that degree-zero divisor classes modulo chip-firing form a finite abelian group on finite connected graphs,

## Proof strategy architecture

### Strategy A: Combinatorial Laplacian first, Riemann–Roch second
Most promising.

1. **Build divisor calculus**
   - Define divisors, degree, effectiveness, subtraction, principal/Laplacian divisors, and linear equivalence.
   - Prove algebraic identities: degree additivity, degree of Laplacian zero, equivalence relation properties.

2. **Prove graph Euler/canonical identities**
   - Show `∑ deg(v) = 2|E|`.
   - Deduce `deg(K_G) = 2g - 2`.
   - This is the combinatorial analogue of Gauss–Bonnet / canonical degree.

3. **Specialize to complete graphs**
   - Use symmetry of `Kₙ` to normalize divisors.
   - Develop a reduced-divisor normal form and derive rank formulas.
   - Then prove Riemann–Roch on `Kₙ`.

Why this is best: it creates a reusable infrastructure and gives you theorem density early, while leaving the hardest rank arguments confined to a highly symmetric family.

### Strategy B: Reduced divisors and Dhar’s burning algorithm
Most algorithmically powerful.

1. Define `v`-reduced divisors and formalize a verified reduction procedure.
2. Prove uniqueness of the reduced representative in each divisor class.
3. Define rank by testing effectiveness after subtracting effective divisors and reducing.
4. Use the reduction algorithm to prove complete-graph rank formulas and then Riemann–Roch.

Why this is powerful: it gives you the required **verified algorithm**, not just existence proofs. This is the route toward a genuine computational tropical geometry engine.

### Strategy C: Sandpile/critical-group route
Most cross-domain and conceptually bold.

1. Identify degree-zero divisors modulo principal divisors with a cokernel of the Laplacian.
2. Prove structural group facts and compute examples.
3. Use critical-group structure plus canonical divisor identities to recover Riemann–Roch phenomena on special families.

Why it matters: this connects tropical geometry to statistical physics, Markov chains, and arithmetic combinatorics. Even partial success here is scientifically explosive.

Recommended priority: **A → B**, with **C** as the cross-domain theorem stream.
