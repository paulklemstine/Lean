## Central Mathematical Objective

Let \( G_q := \mathrm{Sp}_4(\mathbb F_q) \). Let \( B_q \) denote the spherical building of type \( C_2 \) associated to \( G_q \), and let \( T_q \) be a normalized spherical Hecke averaging operator on functions on the building (or on the chamber/vertex incidence space, depending on the formalization route you choose). Let \( S_q \subseteq G_q \) be a symmetric toral generating set, and let \( A_q \) be the normalized adjacency / averaging operator of the Cayley graph \( \mathrm{Cay}(G_q,S_q) \) acting on mean-zero functions on \( G_q \).

The breakthrough target is to prove a **comparison theorem** of the following shape:

\[
\exists C_1,C_2>0,\ \forall q \text{ odd prime power sufficiently large},\quad
C_1(|S_q|)\,\mathrm{gap}(T_q)\ \le\ \mathrm{gap}(A_q)\ \le\ C_2(|S_q|)\,\mathrm{gap}(T_q),
\]
where \(C_1,C_2\) depend only on the local combinatorial type / degree data of the generator family, not on \(q\).

This is stronger and more conceptual than a mere lower bound. It says the building Hecke operator is not just morally related to Cayley expansion — it is the correct asymptotic spectral model.

\Big(
\text{selfAdjoint}(A_D)\ \wedge\ \text{selfAdjoint}(T_D)\ \wedge\

c_1 \cdot \mathrm{gap}(T_D) \le \mathrm{gap}(A_D) \le c_2 \cdot \mathrm{gap}(T_D).

## Proof architecture: 3 viable strategy paths

You must not give only one hint. Pursue one route fully, but explain alternatives in your documentation.

### Strategy A — Rayleigh quotient transference via intertwining map
**Most promising for Lean.**

1. Define normalized averaging operators \(A_q\) and \(T_q\).
2. Construct a transfer map from building functions to group functions (or vice versa), preserving constants and controlling mean-zero subspaces.
3. Prove two inequalities comparing Dirichlet forms:
   \[
   \mathcal E_A(\Phi f) \asymp \mathcal E_T(f),\qquad
   \|\Phi f\|_2^2 \asymp \|f\|_2^2.
   \]
4. Deduce spectral-gap comparison by minimizing Rayleigh quotients.

**Why most promising:** this route is finite-dimensional, operator-theoretic, and compatible with Mathlib’s linear algebra and norm inequalities. It avoids deep representation-theoretic infrastructure while still capturing the main theorem.

### Strategy B — Representation decomposition through spherical / Iwahori components
1. Decompose the regular representation of \(G_q\) into isotypic pieces.
2. Identify the building Hecke operator with the spherical part of the convolution algebra.
3. Show toral Cayley averaging differs from the Hecke operator by an error operator controlled by character ratios.
4. Use operator norm bounds to compare second eigenvalues.

**Why powerful:** this best reflects the actual mathematics and explains the automorphic significance.  
**Why harder in Lean:** requires more infrastructure for finite group representations and explicit Hecke algebra actions.

### Strategy C — Comparison by perturbation from catalog certificates
1. Start from the certified gap bound already in `spectralGapBound`.
2. Define a building-side operator whose matrix differs from the Cayley operator by a sparse structured perturbation.
3. Use Weyl-type eigenvalue perturbation inequalities or direct quadratic-form estimates.
4. Deduce one-sided or two-sided gap control.

**Why useful:** this may produce a first theorem quickly and can be upgraded later.  
**Limitation:** less conceptual unless the perturbation is tied explicitly to geometry.
