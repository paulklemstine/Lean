## Core Vision

A finite min-plus attention mechanism on token sets `I, J` should determine, and be determined by, a finitely presented idempotent transport semimodule equipped with source/target evaluation maps. The indecomposable extremal generators of this semimodule should be exactly the irreducible attention heads. From this, derive a **certified compression theorem**:

- semimodule rank = minimal number of sparse heads,
- extremal rays classify heads up to permutation/tropical scaling,
- a separation margin yields combinatorial stability under perturbation,
- reconstruction is algorithmic via residuation and extremal enumeration.

If formalized cleanly, this opens a new algebraic theory of attention interpretability: **attention as tropical transport representation theory**.

## Mathematical Target

Work in a finite setting. Let `I, J, O` be finite types. A tropical attention kernel is a map

- `K : I → J → Tropical`,

where `Tropical` is a min-plus semiring such as `WithTop ℝ` or a finite tropical scalar type already supported by Mathlib-friendly order/algebra structures. The induced operator acts on score vectors by tropical matrix multiplication:

- `(T_K x) j = ⨅ i, (x i + K i j)`,

or dually on outputs, depending on which direction is easiest to formalize. Fix one convention and make all statements coherent with it.

A **multi-head tropical attention architecture** should be represented by a finite family of kernels
`K_h : I → J → Tropical` indexed by heads `h : H`, together with an idempotent aggregation law
(min over heads, or min-plus sum of head outputs), and a finite-support/sparsity condition ensuring each head corresponds to an extremal indecomposable generator.

Define the associated **transport semimodule** `M_K` as the finitely generated idempotent semimodule generated by the columns/rows/transport profiles of `K` together with distinguished evaluation maps from source and target coordinates. The key is to choose a definition that makes the realization theorem taut but not trivial: `M_K` should forget presentation-level redundancies while retaining the operator-theoretic content of attention.

## Proof Strategy Paths

### Strategy A: Finite Generator Geometry → Realization/Minimality
Most promising for Lean.

1. Define `M_K` concretely as the sub-semimodule generated by a finite set of transport profiles extracted from `K`.
2. Define extremality and indecomposability using finite generator inequalities.
3. Show every minimal head decomposition gives a minimal extremal generating family, and conversely every extremal generating family reconstructs a head decomposition.

Why this is promising: everything reduces to finite combinatorics over `Finset`, extensional equality of functions on finite types, and order-theoretic lemmas. It avoids heavy category theory while still proving the main theorem.

### Strategy B: Residuation / Galois Connection Approach
Likely best for reconstruction algorithm and uniqueness.

1. Define a residuated pair between source score functions and target outputs induced by `K`.
2. Show the transport semimodule is the image/fixed-point structure of this residuation.
3. Prove extremal generators correspond to irreducible residuation atoms, yielding a canonical decomposition.

Why this matters: residuation is the natural language of idempotent linear algebra and gives the algorithmic extraction theorem conceptual force. If manageable in Lean, this gives the cleanest route to certified reconstruction.

### Strategy C: Tropical Convexity / Polyhedral Fan Route
Best for stability and geometric significance.

1. Interpret heads as tropical convex generators and attention outputs as points in a tropical polytope.
2. Show separated architectures correspond to semimodules with simplicial/extremal combinatorics.
3. Prove perturbation below margin preserves the normal fan/cell decomposition, hence preserves irreducible heads and minimal count.

Why this is powerful: it gives the field-opening interpretation — attention compression is tropical polyhedral reduction. But it may be more cumbersome to formalize fully unless you keep it finite and combinatorial.

**Recommendation:** Use Strategy A as the backbone, Strategy B for the reconstruction theorem, and Strategy C only for the conceptual stability lemma if finite inequalities suffice.
