## Theorem 1: Semantic invariance of equivalence closure needs no convergence

This theorem should isolate the real engine behind extraction correctness.

### Statement
```lean
theorem eqvGen_semantics_preserved_of_step_sound
    {α : Type u} {σ : Type v}
    (denote : α → σ)
    (R : α → α → Prop)
    (hR : ∀ ⦃a b : α⦄, R a b → denote a = denote b) :
    ∀ ⦃a b : α⦄, Relation.EqvGen R a b → denote a = denote b
```

### Why this matters
This theorem says the semantic content of equality saturation lives in the **equivalence closure**, not in any normalization theorem. It is the formal statement of the slogan:

> **Confluence is sufficient for canonical forms, but unnecessary for semantic soundness.**

This should be proved directly and cleanly, likely by induction on `Relation.EqvGen`.

### Proof strategy options

**Strategy A (most promising): induction on `Relation.EqvGen`)**
1. Induct over the constructors of `Relation.EqvGen R a b`.
2. Handle:
   - reflexivity trivially by equality of denotation,
   - forward/backward step cases using `hR` and symmetry,
   - transitivity by chaining equalities with `calc`.
3. Package as a reusable lemma for all downstream theorems.

Why promising: this is structurally canonical and exposes the exact minimal assumptions.

**Strategy B: factor through a `Setoid`/quotient construction**
1. Show `Relation.EqvGen R` is contained in the kernel relation of `denote`.
2. Use the universal property of quotients or `Setoid`-style reasoning.
3. Deduce semantic invariance from factorization through the quotient.

Why valuable: this makes the theorem conceptually deeper and prepares categorical generalizations.

**Strategy C: prove closure-preservation for reflexive-symmetric-transitive closure abstractly**
1. Introduce a general closure-preservation lemma.
2. Specialize to `EqvGen`.
3. Reuse in later domains like lambda and circuits.

Why useful: it creates a portable theorem architecture for future work.

## Theorem 3: Context closure preserves semantic soundness in compositional languages

To move beyond abstract rewriting, formalize a compositional syntax where non-confluence naturally appears. This theorem connects rewriting with programming language semantics.

Define a simple term language, e.g. arithmetic expressions or a tiny lambda-free combinator language:

```lean
inductive Term
| var : Nat → Term
| const : Int → Term
| add : Term → Term → Term
| mul : Term → Term → Term
```

with denotation into an environment:

```lean
def Term.denote (ρ : Nat → Int) : Term → Int
```

Define one-step rewrites `R` and a notion of contextual closure, e.g. rewriting inside subterms.

### Statement
```lean
theorem contextual_eqvGen_semantics_preserved
    (ρ : Nat → Int)
    (R : Term → Term → Prop)
    (hR : ∀ ⦃a b : Term⦄, R a b → Term.denote ρ a = Term.denote ρ b) :
    ∀ ⦃a b : Term⦄, Relation.EqvGen (ContextClosure R) a b →
      Term.denote ρ a = Term.denote ρ b
```

where `ContextClosure R` is your new definition capturing closure under constructors.

### Why this matters
This theorem says soundness survives **compositional embedding into larger programs**. That is essential if the previous theorems are to justify real optimizers rather than isolated equation provers.

This is also where you satisfy the “novel definition” requirement if `ContextClosure` is new in your development.

### Proof strategy options

**Strategy A (induction on contextual closure, then induction on `EqvGen`)**
1. Prove a lemma: if `R` preserves denotation, then `ContextClosure R` preserves denotation.
2. The proof should use induction on the derivation of `ContextClosure R a b`, with recursive use of congruence of `denote`.
3. Then invoke Theorem 1 on `ContextClosure R`.

Why promising: clean separation between local congruence and global equivalence closure.

**Strategy B (define context closure as least congruence)**
1. Package the term constructors’ congruence laws.
2. Show semantic equality is a congruence.
3. Prove `ContextClosure R` is contained in semantic equality.

Why deeper: this starts to look like universal algebra and initial semantics.

**Strategy C (big-step denotation as a homomorphism)**
1. Treat denotation as an algebra homomorphism from syntax algebra to semantic algebra.
2. Prove any relation generated by sound equations and congruence lies in the kernel.
3. Deduce the result abstractly.

Why powerful: this gives a route to generic signatures and many-sorted algebras.
