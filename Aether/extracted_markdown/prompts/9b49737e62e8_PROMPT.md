Formalize the delooping correspondence between monoidal categories and one-object bicategories, and use it to characterize categorical 'causal loops'.

## Part 1: The Delooping Construction

Define a construction `Deloop` that takes a monoidal category C and produces a bicategory with one object. Concretely:
- The single object is Unit
- 1-morphisms from Unit to Unit are objects of C
- 2-morphisms between f and g are morphisms Hom_C(f, g)
- Composition of 1-morphisms is the tensor product ⊗
- Identity 1-morphism is the unit object I
- The associator for composition (f ∘ g) ∘ h ≅ f ∘ (g ∘ h) is the monoidal associator α_{f,g,h}
- The left/right unitors are λ and ρ
- Prove that the pentagon identity for the bicategory follows from the pentagon identity for the monoidal category
- Prove that the triangle identity for the bicategory follows from the triangle identity for the monoidal category

State and prove: `deloop_is_bicategory : Bicategory (Deloop C)`

## Part 2: The Loop Extraction

Define a construction `Loop` that takes a one-object bicategory B and extracts a monoidal category:
- Objects are 1-morphisms from the unique object to itself
- Morphisms are 2-morphisms
- Tensor product is composition of 1-morphisms
- Tensor unit is the identity 1-morphism
- Associator, left/right unitor come from the bicategory structure
- Prove this satisfies monoidal category axioms using bicategory coherence

State and prove: `loop_is_monoidal : MonoidalCategory (Loop B)`

## Part 3: The Correspondence

Prove that Deloop and Loop are inverse up to equivalence:
- `loop_deloop_equiv : Loop (Deloop C) ≌ C` (monoidal equivalence)
- `deloop_loop_equiv : Deloop (Loop B) ≅ B` (bicategorical equivalence for one-object B)

## Part 4: Causal Loops

Define a 'causal loop' in a bicategory as an endomorphism f: X ⟶ X such that there exist distinct n, m with f^(n+1) ≅ f^(m+1) as 1-morphisms (where powers are iterated composition, with the isomorphism mediated by associators).

Prove: In a one-object bicategory B, causal loops correspond precisely to objects X in Loop(B) such that X^⊗(n+1) ≅ X^⊗(m+1) for some n ≠ m in the monoidal category Loop(B).

Prove: The full subcategory of causal loops in Loop(B) forms a monoidal subcategory (closed under ⊗ and containing the unit).

Use Mathlib's existing CategoryTheory.Bicategory and CategoryTheory.Monoidal.Category modules. The key insight is that 'composition looping back' is literally the associator in a bicategory, and the coherence theorem guarantees this looping is controlled.
