## Precise Theorem Target

Work first in a finite-dimensional, purely combinatorial setting that Lean can support robustly.

Let `V` be a finite type of activation regions. Let `E = Sym2 V` or an equivalent oriented-edge model, and let `T` be the set of oriented triangles `(i,j,k)` with distinct vertices. Define:
- `C⁰ = V → ℝ`
- `C¹ = AltEdge V → ℝ` as alternating edge functions `ω i j = -ω j i`
- `C² = AltTriangle V → ℝ` as alternating triangle functions

Define the coboundary operators:
- `δ₀ : C⁰ → C¹` by `(δ₀ f) i j = f j - f i`
- `δ₁ : C¹ → C²` by `(δ₁ ω) i j k = ω j k - ω i k + ω i j`

Equip these spaces with the standard Euclidean inner product induced by finite summation (or a weighted inner product if you can carry weights cleanly).

Define the formal adjoints:
- `δ₀† : C¹ → C⁰`
- `δ₁† : C² → C¹`

Define the 1-Hodge Laplacian:
- `Δ₁ : C¹ → C¹ := δ₀ ∘ δ₀† + δ₁† ∘ δ₁`

### Main theorem
For finite `V`, prove the orthogonal direct sum decomposition
\[
C^1 = \operatorname{range}(\delta_0)\ \oplus\ \operatorname{range}(\delta_1^\dagger)\ \oplus\ \ker(\Delta_1).
\]
Moreover prove the characterizations
\[
\ker(\Delta_1)=\ker(\delta_1)\cap\ker(\delta_0^\dagger),
\]
and the orthogonality relations
\[
\operatorname{range}(\delta_0)\perp \operatorname{range}(\delta_1^\dagger),\quad
\ker(\Delta_1)\perp \operatorname{range}(\delta_0),\quad
\ker(\Delta_1)\perp \operatorname{range}(\delta_1^\dagger).
\]

### Breakthrough strengthening
Specialize to the complete simplex on `V`. Then prove:

- If `Fintype.card V ≥ 4`, then `ker Δ₁ = ⊥`, hence every inconsistency field admits the **unique orthogonal decomposition**
  \[
  \omega = \delta_0 f + \delta_1^\dagger \eta.
  \]
- If instead you work over a general clique complex built from an overlap graph, then `ker Δ₁` measures the first cohomological obstruction and is therefore the formal carrier of **irreducible adversarial inconsistency**.

This second statement is where the science-fiction becomes mathematics: harmonic inconsistency is not noise, but topology.

## Why This Is a Breakthrough

This theorem upgrades “adversarial inconsistency” from a heuristic geometric defect into a **decomposable field theory**. The three pieces have direct semantic meaning:

- **Gradient / coboundary**: inconsistency removable by reassigning scalar region potentials; this is the globally calibratable part.
- **Curl / coexact part**: local circulation around triples; this detects failure of pairwise margins to integrate to a global score.
- **Harmonic part**: globally persistent obstruction surviving both local and global correction; this is the topological core of adversarial pathology.

This opens a new field: **certified topological diagnostics for neural decision geometry**. Once formalized, one can ask whether robust models suppress harmonic energy, whether training minimizes coexact mass, and whether certified defenses can be phrased as projection onto exact fields.

## Proof Strategy Architecture

### Strategy A: Finite-dimensional Hilbert-space Hodge decomposition via orthogonal complements
This is the most promising route.

1. Realize `C⁰`, `C¹`, `C²` as finite-dimensional inner product spaces over `ℝ`.
   Use `PiLp`/finite product Euclidean structures if possible, or define custom finite-sum inner products.

2. Prove the general algebraic identity for linear maps between finite-dimensional inner product spaces:
   \[
   E = \operatorname{range}(A) \oplus \ker(A^\dagger), \qquad
   \ker(A^\dagger A) = \ker(A).
   \]
   Then apply this twice with `A = δ₀` and `A = δ₁`.

3. Use the cochain identity `δ₁ ∘ δ₀ = 0` to deduce
   \[
   \operatorname{range}(δ₀) \perp \operatorname{range}(δ₁^\dagger).
   \]
   Then show
   \[
   \ker Δ₁ = \ker δ₁ \cap \ker δ₀^\dagger
   \]
   by positivity:
   \[
   \langle Δ₁ω, ω\rangle = \|δ₀^\dagger ω\|^2 + \|δ₁ ω\|^2.
   \]
   This gives the orthogonal direct sum decomposition.

**Why most promising**: it reduces the problem to general finite-dimensional linear algebra, where Mathlib is strongest. The neural interpretation comes later, but the theorem itself becomes structurally clean and reusable.

---

### Strategy B: Coordinate-explicit graph proof on skew-symmetric matrices
This is less elegant but may be easier if adjoints become painful.

1. Model a 1-cochain as a skew-symmetric matrix `ω : V → V → ℝ`.
2. Define divergence explicitly:
   \[
   (δ₀^\dagger ω)(i) = \sum_j w_{ij}\,ω(i,j)
   \]
   in the weighted case, or plain sum in the unweighted case.
3. Define triangle-curl explicitly and compute
   \[
   \sum_{i,j} ω(i,j)^2
   \]
   decomposition identities by hand.
4. Solve for the gradient component via least squares over vertex potentials; define harmonic residual by projection.

**Why useful**: explicit formulas make the adversarial interpretation immediate and may avoid abstract adjoint infrastructure. This route is especially good if you want weighted overlap graphs early.

---

### Strategy C: General theorem for finite cochain complexes, then instantiate
This is the most ambitious and the most reusable.

1. Define a finite cochain complex `C⁰ → C¹ → C²` of inner product spaces with `d₁ ∘ d₀ = 0`.
2. Prove a generic Hodge decomposition theorem in degree 1.
3. Instantiate to graph/clique cochains.

**Why powerful**: this opens an entire library for discrete Hodge theory in Lean and would be a foundational contribution far beyond the immediate application. But it is more infrastructure-heavy and may increase sorry count unless carefully scoped.
