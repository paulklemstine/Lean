## Assignment: Closing the Single-Power Gap

You are not being asked for an incremental bound improvement. You are being asked to attack the exponent itself: determine whether certificate depth is the true asymptotic complexity law, or whether the current theory is still missing the correct structural invariant.

The central target is to close the “single-power gap” between known lower and upper bounds for worst-case descent length in depth-`k` exchange families. The breakthrough is not merely a sharper estimate; it is a structural dichotomy:

- either depth `k` already governs the exact exponent `d - k`,
- or there exists a finer obstruction beyond certificate depth, a new invariant controlling descent complexity.

Your task is to formalize and prove nontrivial theorems that make this dichotomy mathematically unavoidable.

---

## Core Vision

For each fixed `k : ℕ`, define `T(d,k)` to be the maximal worst-case descent length among all depth-`k` exchange families in ambient dimension `d`. Current theory leaves open whether
`T(d,k)` grows like `d^(d-k)` or only like `d^(d-k-1)`.

The decisive conjecture is:

> **Single-Power Gap Conjecture.**  
> For every fixed `k ≥ 0`, there exists `c_k > 0` such that for infinitely many `d`, there exists a depth-`k` exchange family in dimension `d` whose worst-case descent length is at least  
> `c_k * d^(d-k)`.

This would show that the known upper bound exponent is exactly sharp.

But do not stop at the conjecture. Build a framework that allows both outcomes to become mathematically meaningful:
1. a lower-bound machine that can certify `d^(d-k)`-scale constructions, and
2. a rigidity theorem showing that if such constructions fail, then a strictly finer invariant must exist.

That second direction is just as revolutionary as the first.

## Proof Strategy Architecture

You must provide multi-step proofs, not one-line proofs.

### Strategy A: Gadget amplification by products and induction
Most promising for lower bounds.

1. Construct explicit small adversarial exchange families in low dimensions with certified long descent chains.
2. Define a product or block-sum operation on exchange families and prove that descent lengths add or multiply under this operation.
3. Use induction on the number of blocks to produce families in dimension `d` whose worst-case descent length scales at least like an iterated product, then optimize the block sizes to approach `d^(d-k)`.

Why this is promising:
- It converts the asymptotic conjecture into a compositional theorem.
- It is highly formalizable in Lean.
- It mirrors hardness amplification and tensorization phenomena across combinatorics and complexity theory.

Key proof tools:
- induction on block count,
- `rcases` on chain decompositions,
- `calc` for asymptotic inequalities,
- careful monotonicity lemmas for powers and products.

### Strategy B: Gap-rigidity via contradiction and minimal counterexample
Most promising for the “certificate depth 2.0” branch.

1. Assume the exponent `d-k` is not attained infinitely often.
2. Extract a minimal family witnessing the largest discrepancy between depth and actual descent.
3. Prove, by contradiction, that such a minimal family induces a strictly finer monotone invariant dominating certificate depth but improving the upper bound exponent.

Why this is promising:
- It transforms a failed lower bound program into a structural theorem.
- It gives you publishable mathematics even if the original conjecture is false.
- It can likely be implemented in Lean with finite combinatorial surrogates instead of full asymptotic machinery.

Key proof tools:
- `by_contra`,
- well-founded minimality on `measure` or `dim`,
- `rcases` on extremal witnesses,
- `field_simp` and multi-step `calc` for normalized growth estimates.

### Strategy C: Entropy / partition-function bridge
Most promising for cross-domain impact.

1. Define path-count generating functions for descending chains.
2. Prove convolution bounds under product families.
3. Deduce subadditivity of logarithmic complexity, suggesting a thermodynamic formalism for descent.

Why this is promising:
- It creates a new language for the subject.
- It opens connections to information theory, statistical mechanics, and algorithmic complexity.
- Even partial formal results here could become the seed of an entirely new research area.

Key proof tools:
- finite sums over path decompositions,
- combinatorial counting lemmas,
- `Finset` convolution identities,
- `calc` chains converting counts into entropy inequalities.

3. fit

A negative outcome is scientifically valuable: it is direct evidence that certificate depth is not the full story.

Define the amplification profile `A_F(k) := certificateAmplificationProfile F k`. Conjecture:
```text

A_F(k) / d^(d-k)
```

This is stronger and more structural than merely tracking worst descent length.
