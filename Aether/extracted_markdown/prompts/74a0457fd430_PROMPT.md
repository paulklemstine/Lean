## Assignment: Direction 4: Voice-Leading as Discrete Optimal Transport

**Mode**: prove

Prove a genuinely new bridge theorem: in two-voice species-style counterpoint over discrete pitch space, the melodic/voice-leading objective is exactly a 1-Wasserstein transport functional on atomic measures on `ℤ`, and the global compositional optimization problem can be recast as a discrete dynamic optimal transport problem. This is not a cosmetic reformulation. If formalized cleanly in Lean, it opens a transport-theoretic foundation for algorithmic counterpoint, links music theory to discrete geometry and optimal transport, and creates a new interface between combinatorial composition, tropical methods, and variational optimization.

The right target is not “transport-inspired language.” The target is an exact theorem with explicit hypotheses and a reusable Lean API.

### Core theorem target

Let each sonority at time `i` be represented by a two-atom measure on pitch space:
- cantus pitch `cf i : ℤ`
- counterpoint pitch `cp i : ℤ`
- sonority measure `μ i = δ_(cf i) + δ_(cp i)`

Let the transport ground cost be the usual pitch distance:
- `c(x,y) = |x - y|`

Define the monotone voice-leading matching cost between consecutive sonorities by preserving voice labels:
- `VL(i) = |cf (i+1) - cf i| + |cp (i+1) - cp i|`

Then prove that this labeled voice-leading cost agrees with the 1-Wasserstein cost between the corresponding ordered atomic configurations, and that summing over time yields a discrete Benamou–Brenier-style action functional.

A mathematically sharp theorem to aim for is:

> **Theorem (two-voice ordered transport identity).**  
> For pitch pairs `(a₁,b₁), (a₂,b₂) : ℤ × ℤ` satisfying the order constraints
> `a₁ ≤ b₁` and `a₂ ≤ b₂`, let
> `μ = δ_{a₁} + δ_{b₁}` and `ν = δ_{a₂} + δ_{b₂}`.
> Then for the cost `c(x,y)=|x-y|`, the optimal transport cost between `μ` and `ν`
> equals
> `|a₁-a₂| + |b₁-b₂|`.
> Equivalently, the unique optimal matching is order-preserving.

This is the exact discrete monotone coupling theorem in the smallest musically meaningful case. It is the gateway result: once certified, you can derive the path-energy theorem for counterpoint.

### Breakthrough theorem beyond the identity

Do not stop at the pairwise equality. Prove a stability theorem with explicit modulus. A strong and realistic target is:

> **Theorem (Lipschitz stability of optimal counterpoint under cantus perturbation).**  
> Fix a finite horizon `n`. Suppose the admissible counterpoint set is nonempty and the optimization objective is
> `J(cf,cp) = Σ_i W₁(μ_i, μ_{i+1}) + H(cf,cp)`,
> where `H` is a harmonic penalty that is `K`-Lipschitz in the cantus argument for sup norm.  
> If `cp₁` minimizes `J(cf₁,·)` and `cp₂` minimizes `J(cf₂,·)`, then
> `|inf_cp J(cf₁,cp) - inf_cp J(cf₂,cp)| ≤ (nK + n) * ‖cf₁ - cf₂‖_∞`.
> Under a strict convexity / gap hypothesis on the discrete feasible set, deduce uniqueness and a finite-state argmin stability estimate.

This gives a certified robustness statement for compositional structure. It is exactly the sort of theorem that could seed an entirely new “stability of generated music” literature.

A Lean-friendly version:

```lean
def supNormFin {n : ℕ} (f g : Fin n → ℤ) : ℤ :=
  Finset.sup Finset.univ (fun i => |f i - g i|)

theorem transportAction_lipschitz_in_cantus
    (cf₁ cf₂ cp : Fin (n+1) → ℤ) :
    |pathCost cf₁ cp - pathCost cf₂ cp|
      ≤ (2 * n + 1) * supNormFin cf₁ cf₂
```

### Proof strategy A: Monge inequality on the line, then dynamic summation
Most promising.

1. **Prove the two-point monotonicity inequality**
   \[
   |a₁-a₂| + |b₁-b₂| \le |a₁-b₂| + |b₁-a₂|
   \]
   under `a₁ ≤ b₁` and `a₂ ≤ b₂`.
   This is the Monge property for absolute-value cost on a totally ordered space.

2. **Conclude order-preserving optimality**
   Since only two matchings exist, the minimum transport cost is the ordered one.

3. **Sum over time**
   For a sequence of ordered sonorities, the dynamic action is exactly the sum of these pairwise OT costs.

Why this is best: it is elementary, exact, and should formalize robustly in Lean with integer inequalities and `abs` lemmas. It also scales conceptually to `k` voices by rearrangement/monotone transport.

### Proof strategy B: Quantile/cumulative distribution formulation
Elegant and extensible.

1. Encode each two-atom sonority as a finite measure or multiset with total mass `2`.
2. On the line, identify `W₁` with the `L¹` distance between cumulative distribution functions, or equivalently quantile functions.
3. Compute the CDF/quantile formula explicitly for two ordered atoms and derive the same identity.

Why valuable: this creates the right abstraction for later extension to `k` voices and weighted chords. It is more conceptually powerful, but likely heavier in Lean unless Mathlib already gives enough infrastructure for finite measures and Wasserstein on ordered spaces.

### Proof strategy C: Finite bipartite assignment / Hungarian-minimality viewpoint
Good for algorithmic generalization.

1. Define the 2×2 assignment matrix with costs `|x_i - y_j|`.
2. Show the Monge property of the matrix induced by sorted pitches.
3. Deduce that the identity permutation is optimal; then interpret the dynamic problem as a shortest path / min-cost flow over time.

Why useful: this directly connects to computational counterpoint and scalable optimization. It is probably the best route if you want immediate generalization to `k` voices and dynamic programming.

### Strong extension target: k-voice monotone transport theorem

If the two-voice theorem lands cleanly, immediately attack the true field-opening statement:

> **Theorem (sorted matching optimality for chord transport on `ℤ`).**  
> Let `x,y : Fin k → ℤ` be nondecreasing. Then among all permutations `σ : Equiv.Perm (Fin k)`,
> \[
> \sum_i |x_i - y_i| \le \sum_i |x_i - y_{σ(i)}|.
> \]
> Hence the 1-Wasserstein cost between equal-mass `k`-atom measures on `ℤ` is realized by sorted matching.

Lean sketch:

```lean
theorem sorted_matching_optimal
    {k : ℕ} (x y : Fin k → ℤ)
    (hx : Monotone x) (hy : Monotone y)
    (σ : Equiv.Perm (Fin k)) :
    (∑ i, |x i - y i|) ≤ ∑ i, |x i - y (σ i)|
```

This would be a major theorem. It upgrades the project from a music-specific result to a certified discrete OT theorem specialized to linearly ordered atomic measures.
