$$\frac{a_k^2}{\binom{D}{k}^2} \geq \frac{a_{k-1}}{\binom{D}{k-1}} \cdot \frac{a_{k+1}}{\binom{D}{k+1}}$$

Equivalently: $a_k^2 \cdot \binom{D}{k-1} \cdot \binom{D}{k+1} \geq a_{k-1} \cdot a_{k+1} \cdot \binom{D}{k}^2$.

**Proof Strategy (3 Paths, ranked by promise):**

**Strategy A — Direct Binomial Inequality for Uniform Matroids (Most Promising for Initial Formalization):**
The shadow profile of $U(r,n)$ is $a_k = \binom{n}{k}$ for $k \leq r$. The ULC inequality becomes:
$$\binom{n}{k}^2 \cdot \binom{r}{k-1} \cdot \binom{r}{k+1} \geq \binom{n}{k-1} \cdot \binom{n}{k+1} \cdot \binom{r}{k}^2$$
*Proof plan:* (1) Cancel common factorial factors to reduce to a polynomial inequality in $n, r, k$. (2) Use `ring` and `linarith` on the simplified form. (3) The key algebraic identity is $\frac{n-k+1}{n-k} \cdot \frac{r-k}{r-k+1} \geq 1$ when $n \geq r$, which follows from $\frac{n-k+1}{n-k} \geq \frac{r-k+1}{r-k}$ since $n \geq r$. This is the cleanest path to a verified result.

**Strategy B — Lorentzian Polynomial / Differential Operator Route (Deepest Connection):**
Define $f_S(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{\alpha \in S} \mathbf{x}^\alpha$. For M-convex $S$, this is Lorentzian (Brändén–Huh). The shadow operator $\mathrm{Sh}_k$ corresponds to the $k$-th directional derivative $D_{\mathbf{e}}^k f$ evaluated at $\mathbf{1}$, where $\mathbf{e} = (1,\ldots,1)$. Lorentzian polynomials are closed under differentiation, so $D_{\mathbf{e}}^k f$ is Lorentzian, and its coefficients (the shadow profile) satisfy ULC by the Lorentzian $\Leftrightarrow$ ULC correspondence. *This is the "right" proof but requires building the Lorentzian polynomial theory first.*

**Strategy C — Probabilistic Negative Association Bridge (Novel Cross-Domain):**
Interpret $p_k = a_k / \binom{D}{k}$ as a probability distribution on $\{0, \ldots, D\}$. The Feder–Mihail theorem establishes negative association for matroid bases. Ultra-log-concavity of $p_k$ is equivalent to the distribution being "more concentrated than binomial" (Shepp–Olkin, 1981). *Strategy:* (1) Prove that M-convex shadow profiles induce negatively associated random variables. (2) Apply the Shepp–Olkin entropy concavity theorem. (3) Deduce ULC from maximum entropy characterization. *This opens a new bridge between combinatorial Hodge theory and information theory.*

**Recommendation:** Pursue Strategy A first for the uniform matroid case (it is the most concrete and yields a verified result immediately), then build toward Strategy B for the general M-convex case. Strategy C should be explored as a separate direction that could yield unexpected dividends.
