## Precise Mathematical Statement

### Theorem A: Finite Tropical Spectral Reconstruction

Let `S` be an idempotent, canonically ordered semiring with enough finite sup structure for finitely generated semimodules, and let `M` be a finitely generated `S`-semimodule. Let `T : M →ₛₗ[S] M` be order-preserving. Assume:

1. **Finite-generation / Noetherian observability:** every descending chain of `T`-stable observable congruences stabilizes.
2. **Idempotent-stable orbit closure:** for each generator `g` of `M`, the subsemimodule generated by the forward orbit `{T^n g | n ≥ 0}` is finitely generated.
3. **Spectral separation hypothesis:** distinct classes in the minimal observable quotient are separated by `S`-linear eigenfunctionals.

Then there exists a finite family of eigenfunctionals
`φ₁, ..., φₙ : M →ₛₗ[S] S`
with associated eigenvalues `λ₁, ..., λₙ : S` such that:

- the map
  `Obs : M → S^n`, `Obs(x) = (φ₁ x, ..., φₙ x)`
  factors through the minimal observable quotient `Q`,
- the induced map `Q → S^n` is injective,
- under this embedding, the quotient dynamics is conjugate to coordinatewise tropical scaling:
  `Obs(T x) = (λ₁ * φ₁ x, ..., λₙ * φₙ x)`,
- and `n` is minimal among all finite separating eigenfunctional families.

This identifies `n` as the **tropical observer dimension** or **spectral rank** of `(M,T)`.

## Proof Strategy Architecture

## Strategy A: Observable congruence + Noetherian minimization
**Most promising.**

1. Define the **minimal observable equivalence relation**:
   `x ~ y` iff every eigenfunctional takes the same value on `x` and `y`, or more constructively, iff no admissible observable can distinguish them.
2. Use finite-generation / Noetherianity to show the lattice of `T`-stable observable congruences has minimal separating representatives generated by finitely many tests.
3. Upgrade each test to an eigenfunctional, or select from a separating spectral class, producing a finite family.
4. Prove the induced map into `S^n` is injective on the quotient and conjugates `T` to diagonal tropical scaling.

Why this is promising:
- It matches the semantics story: quotient first, coordinates later.
- It avoids prematurely solving a full tropical Jordan theory.
- It interacts well with finite-generation assumptions and Lean setoid machinery.

---

## Strategy B: Finite orbit-envelope + tropical dual generation
1. Start from a finite generating set `G`.
2. Form the finitely generated `T`-stable orbit envelope `N`.
3. Study the dual semimodule `N →ₛₗ[S] S`; show that a finite subfamily of eigenfunctionals generates the observable dual needed for separation.
4. Extract coordinates from the finite dual generating family and prove minimality by cardinality comparison.

Why this is powerful:
- It is algorithmic from the start.
- It is closest to “certified reconstruction from generators and transition data.”
- It may align best with existing finite realization theorems, especially if the catalog theorem `finite_tropical_hecke_realization_duality` already provides a finite-realization/minimality pattern that can be abstracted.

Potential difficulty:
- The dual semimodule over idempotent semirings may require careful handling; not every classical linear-dual argument transfers directly.

---

## Strategy C: Closure-operator specialization first, then abstract generalization
1. Specialize to `T` idempotent (`T^2 = T`) or inflationary closure-like dynamics.
2. In that case, orbits stabilize immediately or after bounded height, making the observable quotient much more concrete.
3. Prove the spectral reconstruction theorem in this closure case.
4. Generalize to eventually stable or finitely generated orbit-envelope systems.

Why to consider it:
- It gives a clean first theorem with strong semantic meaning for EML.
- It may produce the right definitions for “observer dimension” and “minimal quotient.”
- The closure case is likely to be easier to formalize and could unlock the general theorem.

Best plan:
- Use Strategy C for the first implementation if general semimodule duality becomes heavy.
- But architect the definitions so Strategy A remains the final theorem path.

Classical Koopman theory linearizes nonlinear dynamics by moving to observables. Here, the idempotent/tropical version says:
- the observables are semiring-linear functionals,

- finite eigenfamilies encode all observable behavior.

This is a tropical analogue of finite-dimensional Koopman closure.

The minimal observable quotient is morally a tropical-semimodule version of the Myhill–Nerode quotient:
- states are equivalent iff all observables agree,
- the smallest separating family gives the observer dimension,

This connection is profound and should shape the language of the theorem.

Coordinatewise scaling by eigenvalues is a finite idempotent transfer-spectrum decomposition. This makes your theorem a discrete tropical analogue of:
- Perron–Frobenius spectral compression,

Closure operators and monotone updates are central in static analysis and semantics. Your theorem says:
- finite spectral observables certify the minimal semantic quotient,

That is a serious application direction.

### 5. Tropical systems identification / explainable ML
