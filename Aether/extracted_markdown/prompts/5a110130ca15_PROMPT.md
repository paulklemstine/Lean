## Primary Breakthrough Target

### Theorem 1: Four-voice compositional cost inequality
Define a four-voice voice-leading cost on absolute pitches:
- for `x y : Fin 4 → ℤ`,
- let
  `vlCost4 x y := sInf {c | ∃ σ : Equiv.Perm (Fin 4), c = ∑ i, |x i - y (σ i)| }`
or, more Lean-practically on a finite type,
- define it as the minimum over all permutations.

Then prove a compositional inequality:

### Precise mathematical statement
For all `x y z : Fin 4 → ℤ`,
\[
\mathrm{vlCost4}(x,z) \le \mathrm{vlCost4}(x,y) + \mathrm{vlCost4}(y,z).
\]

This is the foundational theorem that upgrades four-voice motion from a computational gadget to a genuine path geometry.

## Secondary Breakthrough Target

### Theorem 2: Permutation invariance of four-voice cost
The cost should not depend on labeling of voices.

### Precise mathematical statement
For all `x y : Fin 4 → ℤ` and permutations `τ₁ τ₂ : Equiv.Perm (Fin 4)`,
\[
\mathrm{vlCost4}(x \circ \tau₁, y \circ \tau₂) = \mathrm{vlCost4}(x,y).

### Why this matters

## Third Breakthrough Target

### Theorem 3: Sorted matching is optimal in one-dimensional pitch space
If `x` and `y` are monotone nondecreasing as functions on `Fin 4`, then the identity matching realizes minimal cost. This is a discrete Monge/rearrangement theorem specialized to four voices.

### Precise mathematical statement
Assume
\[
x(0)\le x(1)\le x(2)\le x(3), \qquad
y(0)\le y(1)\le y(2)\le y(3).
\]
Then
\[
\mathrm{vlCost4}(x,y) = \sum_i |x(i)-y(i)|.
\]

### Lean 4 type signature sketch
```lean
def MonotoneFin4 (x : Fin 4 → ℤ) : Prop :=
  ∀ ⦃i j : Fin 4⦄, i ≤ j → x i ≤ x j

theorem vlCost4_sorted_optimal
    (x y : Fin 4 → ℤ)
    (hx : MonotoneFin4 x)
    (hy : MonotoneFin4 y) :
    vlCost4 x y = ∑ i : Fin 4, Int.natAbs (x i - y i) := by
  ...
```

### Why this is revolutionary
This is the theorem that turns brute-force search into structure. It says optimal four-voice transport in 1D is canonical after sorting. That opens:
- efficient certified algorithms,
- canonical representatives for chord classes,
- direct links to optimal transport and Monge arrays,
- scaling from 4 voices to `Fin n` once the proof architecture is right.

This theorem is the bridge from musical combinatorics to discrete optimal transport.

## Proof Strategy Architecture

### Strategy A: Finite permutation minimization + explicit witness composition
Most promising for Theorem 1.

1. Define `vlCost4` as the minimum over the finite set of permutations of `Fin 4`.
2. Choose permutations `σ` and `τ` realizing the minima for `(x,y)` and `(y,z)`.
3. Use `τ * σ` as a witness permutation for `(x,z)` and prove
   \[
   \sum_i |x_i - z_{τ(σ(i))}| \le \sum_i |x_i - y_{σ(i)}| + \sum_i |y_{σ(i)} - z_{τ(σ(i))}|
   \]
   by pointwise triangle inequality and reindexing.

Why this is best:
- finite and constructive,
- aligns with Lean’s strengths,
- directly mirrors `tropPath_cost_compose_bound`,
- produces executable algorithms.

### Strategy B: Sort normal form + transport through canonical representatives
Most promising for Theorem 2 and Theorem 3.

1. Define a canonical sorted representative of a 4-voice chord.
2. Prove permutation invariance of the cost.
3. Reduce optimization to the sorted-sorted case and invoke a no-crossing lemma: swapping crossed assignments never decreases total absolute deviation.

Why this matters:
- conceptually cleaner,
- creates canonical quotient-level geometry,
- sets up generalization to `Fin n`.

### Strategy C: Matrix/assignment formulation
Potentially strongest for future scaling beyond 4.

1. Represent costs as a `4 × 4` matrix `C i j = |x i - y j|`.
2. Show this matrix satisfies a Monge property when `x,y` are sorted.
3. Deduce identity matching optimality from Monge assignment theory.

Why this is powerful:
- imports combinatorial optimization directly,
- opens algorithmic complexity results,
- gives a road to `n`-voice theory and Hungarian/Monge algorithms in Lean.
