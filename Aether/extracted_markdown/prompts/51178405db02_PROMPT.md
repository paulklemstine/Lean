## Core Vision

For a closed orientable triangulated surface, total discrete curvature is topologically rigid:
\[
\sum_{v} K(v)=2\pi \chi = 2\pi(2-2g).
\]
This means the space of curvature assignments lies in an affine hyperplane. On that hyperplane, the variance functional is a discrete curvature energy. Its unique minimizer is the constant curvature assignment. The real mathematical content is to formalize:

1. the optimization principle on the curvature hyperplane,
2. the induced lower bound for any realizable triangulation,
3. the rigidity statement that equality forces equicurvature,
4. existence mechanisms for realizable equicurvature triangulations in key families,
5. a computational search/verification framework.

This opens a discrete analogue of constant-scalar-curvature geometry for combinatorial surfaces.

## Stronger Theorem Statements You Should Actually Aim For

### Theorem 1: Quadratic curvature decomposition
For any finite set of vertices \(V\) and any curvature profile \(K : V \to \mathbb R\),
\[
\sum_{v}(K(v)-t)^2
=
\sum_{v}(K(v)-\bar K)^2
+
|V|(\bar K-t)^2.
\]
In particular, the unique minimizer over constant profiles \(t\) is \(t=\bar K\).

**Breakthrough value:** This converts curvature balancing into an exact energy identity, not merely an inequality.

### Theorem 2: Topological lower bound and rigidity
For any closed orientable triangulated surface \(T\) with \(n\) vertices and genus \(g\),
\[
\operatorname{Var}(K_T)\ge 0,
\]
with equality iff
\[
\forall v,\quad K_T(v)=\frac{2\pi(2-2g)}{n}.
\]

This is the formal discrete constant-curvature principle.

### Theorem 3: Cross-domain spectral/optimization theorem
Define the curvature defect vector
\[
\delta(v)=K(v)-\frac{2\pi(2-2g)}{n}.
\]
Then
\[
\sum_v \delta(v)=0.
\]
Interpret \(\delta\) as lying in the codimension-1 subspace orthogonal to constants. Prove that curvature variance is exactly the normalized squared Euclidean norm of the projection of \(K\) onto that subspace.

Suggested Lean target:

```lean
theorem curvatureVariance_eq_norm_sq_of_mean_zero_part
    {V : Type*} [Fintype V] (K : V → ℝ) :
    curvatureVariance K =
      ((∑ v, (K v - curvatureAverage K)^2) / Fintype.card V)
```

Then explain in `RESEARCH_PAPER.md` that this is a discrete Hodge-type decomposition into constant mode + fluctuation mode.

**Cross-domain connection:** spectral graph theory / statistical mechanics. Variance is an energy of fluctuations around equilibrium.

### Theorem 4: Jensen/Cauchy lower bound under pointwise constraints
If the angle lower bound \(\alpha_{\min}\) implies vertex-degree upper bounds or curvature bounds through local geometry, prove a nontrivial inequality constraining realizability of equicurvature. For example: if every face angle is at least \(\alpha_{\min}\), then at a vertex of degree \(d(v)\),
\[
K(v) \le 2\pi - d(v)\alpha_{\min}.
\]
Use this to derive a necessary condition for equicurvature:
\[
\frac{2\pi(2-2g)}{n} \le 2\pi - d(v)\alpha_{\min}
\]
for all vertices in any equicurvature realization.

This is where the angle bound enters in a mathematically serious way: not cosmetic regularity, but a realizability obstruction.

## Proof Strategy Architecture

You must provide at least 2–3 proof routes in the code comments and paper, and pursue the most promising one formally.

### Strategy A: Exact quadratic decomposition via algebraic expansion
**Most promising.**
1. Define the average curvature and variance on any finite type.
2. Prove the identity
   \[
   \sum (x_i-t)^2 = \sum (x_i-\bar x)^2 + n(\bar x-t)^2
   \]
   by expanding both sides, using
   \[
   \sum (x_i-\bar x)=0.
   \]
3. Deduce nonnegativity, uniqueness of minimizer, and the zero-variance iff constant theorem.
4. Combine with `discrete_gauss_bonnet` / `total_curvature_eq_genus` to identify the constant value.

**Why strongest:** it yields the cleanest rigidity theorem and avoids invoking heavy convex-analysis machinery absent from Mathlib’s current finite-dimensional API.

Tactics likely needed:
- `field_simp`
- multi-step `calc`
- `ring` / `nlinarith`
- `have hmean : ∑ v, (K v - curvatureAverage K) = 0 := ...`
- `by_contra` for uniqueness if needed

### Strategy B: Convexity / Jensen inequality route
1. Use strict convexity of \(x \mapsto x^2\).
2. Apply Jensen to the finite average under the fixed-sum constraint.
3. Show equality iff all terms are equal.

**Why valuable:** conceptually ties the theorem to optimization and information geometry.  
**Why less promising in Lean:** Jensen in the exact finite weighted form may require more infrastructure than direct algebra.

### Strategy C: Hilbert-space projection viewpoint
1. Regard functions \(V \to \mathbb R\) as a finite-dimensional inner product space.
2. Identify constants as a 1-dimensional subspace.
3. Show the average profile is the orthogonal projection onto constants.
4. Variance is the squared norm of the orthogonal complement.

**Why revolutionary:** connects discrete geometry to spectral theory and Hodge decomposition.  
**Why moderate risk:** depends on how much finite-dimensional inner-product infrastructure you want to invoke. But even a coordinate-level proof can be written and then interpreted spectrally in the paper.
