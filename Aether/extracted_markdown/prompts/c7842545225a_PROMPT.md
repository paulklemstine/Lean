## Exact theorem targets

You must include at least **3 substantial theorems** with real proof architecture. The following are the target statements.

### Theorem 1: Fisher matrix is symmetric positive semidefinite

Mathematical statement:

For any finite statistical model and parameter `θ`, the Fisher information matrix
\[
I_{ij}(\theta)=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega} p_\theta(\omega)\, s_i(\theta,\omega)\, s_j(\theta,\omega)
\]
is symmetric and positive semidefinite:
\[
I(\theta) = I(\theta)^\top,\qquad
\forall v,\; v^\top I(\theta) v \ge 0.
\]

Lean target shape:

```lean
theorem fisherMatrix_symmetric {n : ℕ} [Fintype Ω]
  (M : FiniteStatModel (Fin n → ℝ) Ω) (θ : Fin n → ℝ) :
  Matrix.IsSymm (fisherMatrix M θ)

theorem fisherMatrix_posSemidef {n : ℕ} [Fintype Ω]
  (M : FiniteStatModel (Fin n → ℝ) Ω) (θ : Fin n → ℝ) :
  ∀ v : Fin n → ℝ, 0 ≤
    ∑ i, ∑ j, v i * (fisherMatrix M θ i j) * v j
```

Why this matters: this is the first geometric layer. It converts inferential sensitivity into a certified quadratic form and creates the entry point for spectral algorithms, natural gradients, and lower bounds.

### Theorem 2: Score has mean zero

Under differentiability/normalization hypotheses, prove:
\[
\mathbb E_\theta[s_i(\theta,X)] = 0.
\]

For finite models, this should come from differentiating `∑ω pθ(ω)=1`.

theorem score_mean_zero {n : ℕ} [Fintype Ω]

### Theorem 3: Geometric Cramér–Rao inequality

For an unbiased estimator `T` of a scalar functional `g`, prove:
\[
\operatorname{Var}_\theta(T)\;\ge\; \nabla g(\theta)^\top I(\theta)^{-1}\nabla g(\theta)
\]
when `I(θ)` is invertible; or at minimum the one-dimensional/vector-directional version:
\[
\operatorname{Var}_\theta(T)\, I(\theta)[v,v] \ge (Dg_\theta[v])^2.
\]

A Lean-realistic version is the directional inequality avoiding full matrix inversion:

```lean
theorem cramérRao_directional {n : ℕ} [Fintype Ω]
  (M : FiniteStatModel (Fin n → ℝ) Ω)
  (hreg : RegularityHypotheses M)
  (T : Ω → ℝ) (g : (Fin n → ℝ) → ℝ)
  (hunb : UnbiasedFor M T g)
  (θ : Fin n → ℝ) :
  ∀ v : Fin n → ℝ,
    (directionalDeriv g θ v)^2 ≤
      varianceAt M θ T *
      (∑ i, ∑ j, v i * fisherMatrix M θ i j * v j)
```

If you can go further, prove a matrix-inverse corollary under positive definiteness:

```lean
theorem cramérRao_matrix {n : ℕ} [Fintype Ω]
  (M : FiniteStatModel (Fin n → ℝ) Ω)
  ...
  (hpd : PositiveDefinite (fisherMatrix M θ)) :
  grad g θ ⬝ (fisherMatrix M θ)⁻¹.mulVec (grad g θ) ≤ varianceAt M θ T
```

Why this is a breakthrough: it turns a classical statistical inequality into a certified geometric theorem in Lean, opening machine-checked lower bounds in estimation, experimental design, and scientific ML.

## Proof strategy architecture

### Strategy A: Finite-sum covariance route
Most promising for initial success.

1. **Define score and Fisher entrywise by finite sums.**
   Rewrite quadratic forms as
   \[
   v^\top I v = \sum_\omega p_\theta(\omega)\left(\sum_i v_i s_i(\omega)\right)^2.
   \]
   This gives positivity by sum-of-squares.

2. **Derive mean-zero score from normalization.**
   Differentiate the identity `∑ ω, p θ ω = 1` coordinatewise; use
   \[
   \partial_i p = p\,\partial_i \log p.
   \]

3. **Prove Cramér–Rao via Cauchy–Schwarz in weighted `L²(pθ)`.**
   Show covariance between `T - E[T]` and directional score equals `Dg[v]`.
   Then use
   \[
   \operatorname{Cov}(A,B)^2 \le \operatorname{Var}(A)\operatorname{Var}(B).
   \]

Why best: it avoids heavy manifold machinery and uses finite-dimensional linear algebra + finite sums + inequalities, which Lean handles relatively well.

---

### Strategy B: Convex-potential route for exponential families
Best for the deepest theorem.

1. **Define log-partition**
   \[
   \psi(\theta)=\log\sum_x \exp(\langle\theta,T(x)\rangle+k(x)).
   \]

2. **Compute first derivative as expectation of sufficient statistic.**
   \[
   \nabla\psi(\theta)=\mathbb E_\theta[T].
   \]

3. **Differentiate again to identify Hessian with covariance/Fisher.**
   \[
   \nabla^2\psi(\theta)=\operatorname{Cov}_\theta(T)=I(\theta).
   \]

Why powerful: it simultaneously proves positivity, convexity, and dual coordinates. It also links directly to Legendre duality and mirror descent.

---

### Strategy C: Coordinate-connection route for alpha-geometry
More ambitious; use if Strategy A/B land cleanly.

1. Define the Amari–Chentsov cubic tensor from third-order score moments.
2. Define alpha-Christoffel symbols by perturbing Levi-Civita symbols with the cubic tensor.
3. In exponential-family coordinates, prove vanishing of `+1` connection coefficients and duality with the `-1` connection relative to Fisher.

Why important: this is the true information-geometric payoff, but it may require more custom infrastructure. If full curvature-flatness is too much, prove coefficient vanishing and duality identities first.
