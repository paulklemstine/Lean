## Proof strategies

### Strategy A: Definitional reduction by operator extensionality
This is the most promising route.

1. Define `chargedWeight` so that `chargedWeight W A q i j = W i j + q * A i j` by `rfl`.
2. Expand both sides of `TropicalEinsteinMaxwell` and `TropicalEinsteinEquation`.
3. Use `simp [TropicalEinsteinMaxwell, TropicalEinsteinEquation, chargedWeight, add_assoc, add_left_comm, add_comm]`.

Why this is best:
- Minimal mathematical overhead.
- Produces a robust simplification lemma.
- Makes later automation easy with `[simp]`.

### Strategy B: Prove equality of Bellman operators first, then derive logical equivalence
1. Define `bellmanOp` and `maxwellBellmanOp`.
2. Prove pointwise equality:
   ```lean
   funext i; simp [bellmanOp, maxwellBellmanOp, chargedWeight, add_assoc, add_left_comm, add_comm]
   ```
3. Rewrite the equation predicates using the operator identity.

Why it matters:
- More conceptual than Strategy A.
- Gives a reusable theorem for fixed points, contraction arguments, and value iteration.
- Better for future abstractions into semiring-valued operators.

### Strategy C: Generalize immediately to arbitrary finite types and function weights
1. Work with `α → α → ℝ` instead of matrices.
2. Define finite Bellman equations using `Finset.univ.sup`.
3. Prove the theorem generically under `[Fintype α] [DecidableEq α]`.

Why this may be stronger:
- It is the right abstraction for graph algorithms and MDP-style systems.
- Easier to reuse than matrix-specialized statements.

Why it is not the first move:
- `Finset.sup` over `ℝ` can require more order-theoretic care than direct `iSup` on `Fin n`.
- Start concrete, then lift.

## Most promising route

Start with **Strategy B** on `Fin n` matrices. It gives a theorem of genuine conceptual value: the Maxwell-Bellman operator is literally the Bellman operator for the charged weight. Then derive the iff theorem as a corollary. After that, generalize to arbitrary finite types if time permits.
