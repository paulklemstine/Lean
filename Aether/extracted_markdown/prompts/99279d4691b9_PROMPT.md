## Assignment: Conjecture 4: Log-Linearization Characterizes Multiplicative Interactions

**Mode:** prove

Build a new formal bridge between multiplicative factorization on the positive quadrant and additive factorization after logarithmic coordinates. Do not treat this as a routine equivalence: elevate it into a structural theorem about interaction, identifiability, and coordinate geometry. The goal is to make “log-linearization” a mathematically certified detector of genuine multiplicative independence.

This direction is powerful because it turns a heuristic used across statistics, machine learning, information geometry, and thermodynamics into a theorem schema that can be reused: **interaction-free in log coordinates if and only if multiplicatively decoupled in original coordinates**. If formalized cleanly, this opens a reusable library for certifying when a bivariate positive law has no mixed interaction term.

## Core Breakthrough Target

### Main theorem (mathematical statement)

Let `f : ℝ → ℝ → ℝ` be intended to model a positive continuous function on the positive quadrant, with positivity hypothesis restricted to positive inputs. Prove that:

\[
\Big(\exists u\,v:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R,\ \forall x>0,\forall y>0,\ \log(f(x,y)) = u(\log x)+v(\log y)\Big)
\]
if and only if
\[
\Big(\exists \phi\,\psi:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R,\ \forall x>0,\forall y>0,\ \phi(x)>0 \land \psi(y)>0 \land f(x,y)=\phi(x)\psi(y)\Big).
\]

The nontrivial content is not just constructing witnesses by exponentiation/composition, but organizing the theorem so that it becomes a **characterization principle** and a basis for further invariants.

def LogAdditivelySeparableOnPos (f : ℝ → ℝ → ℝ) : Prop :=

### 3. Interaction defect / mixed ratio invariant
This is the concept that makes the project nontrivial and field-opening.

\[
D_f(x_1,x_2,y_1,y_2)
:= \frac{f(x_1,y_1)f(x_2,y_2)}{f(x_1,y_2)f(x_2,y_1)}.
\]

For multiplicatively separable functions, this defect is identically 1. This gives an algebraic interaction invariant, analogous to vanishing mixed second derivative in smooth settings.

```lean
def interactionDefect (f : ℝ → ℝ → ℝ)
    (x₁ x₂ y₁ y₂ : ℝ) : ℝ :=
  (f x₁ y₁ * f x₂ y₂) / (f x₁ y₂ * f x₂ y₁)
```

This definition is the real conceptual leap. It connects to odds ratios, rank-one kernels, and statistical independence.

### Theorem 3: Smooth mixed-derivative criterion

\[

Then additive separability follows from vanishing mixed derivative:
\[
\frac{\partial^2 G}{\partial s \partial t}=0.
\]

You may formalize a tractable version if full Fréchet smoothness is too heavy, but aim high. Even a one-dimensional iterated derivative statement on a defined pullback is excellent.

## Proof Strategy Architecture

You must present and pursue at least 2–3 proof avenues. Here are the best options.

### Strategy A: Direct logarithmic transport through `exp`/`log` (most promising for the main theorem)
1. Assume `LogAdditivelySeparableOnPos f` with witnesses `u, v`.
2. Define
   \[
   \phi(x)=\exp(u(\log x)), \quad \psi(y)=\exp(v(\log y))
   \]
   on positive inputs; extend arbitrarily outside the positive axis if needed.
3. Use `Real.exp_add` and `Real.exp_log` under positivity hypotheses to prove
   \[
   f(x,y)=\phi(x)\psi(y).
   \]
4. For the reverse implication, given `f(x,y)=φ(x)ψ(y)`, define
   \[
   u(s)=\log(\phi(e^s)), \quad v(t)=\log(\psi(e^t)).
   \]
   Then use `Real.log_mul`, `Real.log_exp`, positivity of `φ, ψ`.

**Why this is most promising:** It is conceptually canonical, uses standard Mathlib lemmas on `Real.log`/`Real.exp`, and naturally produces continuity statements by composition.

### Strategy B: Basepoint factorization via cross-ratio identity (most promising for the structural theorem)
1. Assume
   \[
   f(x_1,y_1)f(x_2,y_2)=f(x_1,y_2)f(x_2,y_1)
   \]
   for all positive points.
2. Fix a basepoint \((x_0,y_0)=(1,1)\) or any positive pair.
3. Rearrange:
   \[
   f(x,y)=\frac{f(x,y_0)f(x_0,y)}{f(x_0,y_0)}.
   \]
4. Define
   \[
   \phi(x):=\frac{f(x,y_0)}{f(x_0,y_0)},\qquad \psi(y):=f(x_0,y).
   \]
5. Use positivity to justify division and continuity to transfer regularity.

**Why this matters:** This proof does not depend on logs. It reveals the hidden rank-one tensor structure. This is the theorem that will connect your work to matrix theory, statistics, and kernel methods.

### Strategy C: Analytic/PDE route via vanishing mixed derivative
1. Define the log pullback \(G(s,t)=\log(f(e^s,e^t))\).
2. Show that if \(\partial_{st}G=0\), then for each fixed `s`, the derivative in `t` is independent of `s`, or vice versa.
3. Integrate to obtain
   \[
   G(s,t)=u(s)+v(t)+C.
   \]
4. Absorb the constant into one summand.
5. Transport back by exponentiation to recover multiplicative separability.

**Why this is visionary:** It reframes multiplicative factorization as a zero-curvature condition. This opens connections to thermodynamic potentials, statistical interaction models, and information geometry.

## Cross-Domain Connections You Must Explicitly Develop

At least one theorem and one discussion section must connect this work to another field. Use these aggressively.

### 1. Statistics / Information Geometry
The interaction defect
\[
D_f(x_1,x_2,y_1,y_2)
\]
is the continuous analogue of an odds ratio. `D_f = 1` means no interaction. This ties multiplicative separability to:
- independence models,
- log-linear models,
- contingency table geometry,
- exponential family factorization.

A formal theorem here is natural: multiplicative separability implies defect \(=1\), and conversely under continuity/positivity.

### 2. Tensor algebra / Rank-one kernels
A positive kernel \(f(x,y)\) is multiplicatively separable iff it is rank one in the multiplicative sense. The cross-ratio theorem is a continuous analogue of “all \(2 \times 2\) minors vanish implies rank one.”

This is the strongest algebraic interpretation of your result.

### 3. Thermodynamics / Statistical mechanics
If \(f(x,y)\) is a partition-like positive observable, then \(\log f\) is a free-energy-like quantity. Additive separability in log coordinates means decoupled subsystems. The mixed derivative criterion is literally “no coupling term.”

This is the physics connection: **vanishing mixed free-energy curvature = no interaction.**

### 4. Machine learning / Feature interaction detection
In representation learning, one tries to detect whether a response decomposes as separate contributions of two features. Your theorem gives an exact symbolic certificate:
- in original coordinates: multiplicative factorization,
- in log coordinates: additive decomposition,
- via invariant test: cross-ratio equals 1.

This suggests an algorithmic testable pipeline.
