## Primary Theorem Target: Uniform Spectral Expansion

### Mathematical Statement
Let `μ` be the uniform probability measure on the three Berggren generators acting on the relevant mod-`q` state space associated to primitive Pythagorean data. Let `berggrenSecondEigenvalue q` denote the operator norm of the averaging operator on the orthogonal complement of constants.

Prove a theorem of the following form:

\[
\exists \rho < 1,\ \forall q \text{ prime},\ q \ge 5 \to \lambda_2(q)\le \rho.
\]

But do not stop there. The real target is the sharp candidate:

\[
\forall q \text{ prime},\ q \ge 5 \to \lambda_2(q)\le \frac{1}{\sqrt 3}.
\]

And if exact equality can be exhibited for infinitely many `q`, then formalize that as well.

## Direction 2: Deterministic Extractor from Thin-Orbit Dynamics

Once you have even a coarse uniform spectral gap, pivot immediately to a second theorem: prove that Berggren orbit dynamics acts as a condenser or extractor for arithmetic weak sources.

### Mathematical Vision
Suppose `μ` is a distribution on a Berggren orbit slice modulo `q` with min-entropy at least `k`. Let `T_q` be the Berggren averaging operator. Then after `t` steps, the output distribution should have significantly improved Renyi-2 entropy, and under suitable quantitative hypotheses, become close to uniform.

The scientifically bold theorem is:

\[
H_\infty(\mu)\ge k
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\|T_q^t \mu - u_q\|_2 \le \rho^t \|\mu-u_q\|_2,
\]
hence
\[
H_2(T_q^t\mu)\ \text{increases linearly in } t
\]
until saturation.

### Lean Target
```lean
theorem berggren_l2_mixing_of_weak_sources
    (ρ : ℝ)
    (hρ : ρ < 1)
    (hexp : ∀ q : ℕ, q.Prime → q ≥ 5 →
      berggrenSecondEigenvalue q ≤ ρ) :
    ∀ q : ℕ, q.Prime → q ≥ 5 →
    ∀ μ, isWeakSource q μ →
      ∀ t : ℕ,
        berggrenL2DistanceToUniform q (berggrenIterate q t μ)
          ≤ ρ^t * berggrenL2DistanceToUniform q μ
```

### Stronger aspirational extractor statement

## What to Watch For

1. **Symmetry defect**: the Berggren semigroup is not obviously a group. If self-adjointness fails, use singular values / normality / symmetrized operators instead of naive eigenvalue arguments.
2. **State-space choice matters**: choose the mod-`q` object so that constants form the unique trivial representation and the averaging operator is well-defined and irreducible.
3. **Exact `1/√3` may encode a hidden relation**: if the proof repeatedly lands at `2√2/3`, you are seeing generic 3-regular tree behavior; to reach `1/√3`, you likely need extra algebraic cancellation or a spinorial decomposition.
4. **Prime restriction is a feature, not a bug**: use field structure mod prime `q` aggressively.
