## Core Vision

The breakthrough is to identify a **Helly number for representable generation**: a finite bound `h(P)` determined by a probe family `P` such that if a presheaf is representably finitely generated on every full subcategory of size at most `h(P)`, then it is globally representably finitely generated.

This would be a paradigm shift because it says that **finite generation is not merely a global algebraic property but a local combinatorial one**, detectable on tiny windows controlled by a separating measurement system. That is the categorical analogue of Helly’s theorem, Mayer–Vietoris descent, and locality principles in physics.

The conjectural sharp bound is `|P| + 1`, but you should formalize a robust theorem in the strongest form you can actually prove. If the exact Helly bound is too optimistic, prove a weaker but still nontrivial theorem with an explicit bound, and then isolate the obstruction.

## New Definitions to Introduce

You must define at least one genuinely new concept. The following are strongly recommended.

### 1. Local representable finite generation at radius `k`
For a finite category `C`, a presheaf `F`, and natural number `k`, define:

- `LocallyRepFinGen (P : ProbeFamily C) (k : ℕ) (F : Cᵒᵖ ⥤ Type _) : Prop`

meaning: for every full subcategory `S ⊆ Ob(C)` with `|S| ≤ k`, the restriction of `F` to `S` is representably finitely generated.

This is the exact local hypothesis needed for a Helly principle.

### 2. Probe-Helly number
Define a numerical invariant:

- `probeHellyNumber (P : ProbeFamily C) : ℕ`

Even if you cannot fully characterize it, defining it cleanly opens a new line of research.

### 3. Minimal obstruction
Define:

- `IsMinimalRepFinGenObstruction (P : ProbeFamily C) (F : Cᵒᵖ ⥤ Type _) : Prop`

meaning: `F` is not globally representably finitely generated, but every restriction to a proper small full subcategory is.

This is scientifically important: if the conjecture fails, the obstruction becomes the real object of study.

## Proof Strategy Architecture

You must pursue at least 2–3 proof avenues and record which one succeeds, fails, or partially succeeds.

### Strategy 1 — Descent via probe signatures
Most promising.

1. Use `repFinGen_of_probe_separation` as the global endpoint: reduce the problem to showing that the probe family already controls all global elements through signatures.
2. For each element of `F(X)`, extract its measurement/profile on probes. Show that this profile is determined by restrictions to small full subcategories containing `X` and the relevant probes.
3. Use local representable generators on these windows to build a finite global generating set, then invoke probe separation to prove surjectivity onto all elements.

**Why promising:** it directly leverages the catalog theorem rather than rebuilding finite generation from scratch.

---

### Strategy 2 — Minimal counterexample / obstruction compression
Likely essential if the exact Helly number is subtle.

1. Assume `F` is a minimal counterexample by number of objects.
2. For every proper small full subcategory, use local representable finite generation.
3. Show that probe separation forces a redundancy among objects/probes, allowing one to compress the obstruction and contradict minimality.

Key tactics should include `by_contra`, cardinal induction, and careful `rcases` on finite subsets.

**Why promising:** Helly theorems are often proved by minimal bad families; this approach may also produce the obstruction theorem if the positive theorem fails.

---

### Strategy 3 — Nerve / Čech-type gluing
Most conceptually ambitious.

1. Cover `C` by probe-adapted full subcategories.
2. Choose finite generating families on each patch.
3. Prove compatibility on pairwise overlaps and glue via a finite descent argument.

This may require introducing a finite combinatorial gluing lemma for generating families.

**Why promising:** this reveals the sheaf-theoretic meaning of representable generation and creates a bridge to topology and quantum locality, even if the full theorem only works under stronger compatibility hypotheses.
