## Precise Theorem Target

Let `α` be a finite type. Let `cl : Set α → Set α` be a closure operator, i.e.

- extensive: `s ⊆ cl s`,
- monotone: `s ⊆ t → cl s ⊆ cl t`,
- idempotent: `cl (cl s) = cl s`.

Assume additionally that `cl` is **finitarily generated of rank `r`**, meaning there is a finite implication presentation
\[
A \Rightarrow x \quad (A \subseteq α,\ |A| \le r,\ x \in α)
\]
such that
\[
x \in cl(S) \iff \exists \text{ derivation from implications of arity }\le r.
\]

Define the residual profile of an element `x : α` by
\[
\rho_x(S) := \mathbf{1}_{x \in cl(S)},
\]
viewed as a monotone Boolean functional on `Set α`.

Define residual equivalence:
\[
x \sim y \iff \forall S,\ x \in cl(S) \leftrightarrow y \in cl(S).
\]

Define the dependency semimodule `M_cl` as the finite idempotent semimodule generated by dependency patterns, with addition given by join/union and order induced by entailment of residual profiles.

## Strategic Proof Architecture

### Strategy A: Finite implicational closure → canonical minimal supports → circuit extraction
This is likely the most promising route.

**Step 1.** Prove every rank-bounded finite closure operator admits finite minimal support sets for each output `x`.
- For each `x`, define
  \[
  \mathcal M_x := \{A \subseteq α \mid x \in cl(A),\ \forall A' \subsetneq A,\ x \notin cl(A')\}.
  \]
- Use finiteness of `α` to show each `𝓜_x` is finite.
- Use rank boundedness to show every minimal support has size `≤ r` or is generated by a bounded-width derivation scheme.

**Step 2.** Define the canonical basis `B` as the set of all irredundant residual generators `(A, x)` with `A ∈ 𝓜_x`.
- Prove join-primality: if a residual profile is generated by a union/join, then irredundancy forces one component to already generate it.
- Prove basis uniqueness by extensionality on residual functionals.

**Step 3.** Reconstruct the circuit.
- For each `x`, build
  \[
  C_x := \bigvee_{A \in \mathcal M_x} \bigwedge_{a \in A} \mathrm{input}(a).
  \]
- This is a monotone DNF computing `x ∈ cl(S)`.
- Prove minimality among circuits respecting residual equivalence by showing every gate computing `x` must dominate at least one irredundant residual generator.
- Then `size = number of irredundant generators` up to your chosen size model.

**Why Strategy A is strongest:** it avoids prematurely formalizing heavy semimodule algebra and instead derives the semimodule from finite closure combinatorics. It gives a clean Lean path to a strong theorem and leaves the algebraic packaging as a corollary.

---

### Strategy B: Finite join-semilattice / Birkhoff-style representation of closure systems
This is more conceptual and may yield a more canonical theorem.

**Step 1.** Identify closed sets under `cl` as a finite Moore family, ordered by inclusion.
- Prove this forms a finite lattice or at least a finite meet-semilattice.
- Analyze join-irreducibles in the lattice of closed sets or in the entailment semilattice of residuals.

**Step 2.** Show residual profiles correspond to lattice morphisms into `Bool` or to prime filters of the dependency semilattice.
- The residual basis is then the finite set of join-prime generators.
- The “semimodule” becomes an idempotent semiring-semimodule object where addition is join and scalar attenuation is entailment restriction.

**Step 3.** Extract circuits from join-irreducible decompositions.
- Each join-irreducible gives a gate.
- Minimality follows from irredundant join decomposition.

**Why this matters:** this route makes the theorem feel like true algebra, not just combinatorics. If it works, it opens the door to infinite/generalized versions and categorical dualities. It is harder in Lean but scientifically deeper.

---

### Strategy C: Abstract residual equivalence à la Myhill–Nerode
This is the most visionary route.

**Step 1.** Define residual equivalence on targets or supports:
\[
A \equiv_{\mathrm{res}} B \iff \forall x,\ x \in cl(A) \leftrightarrow x \in cl(B).
\]
or dually on outputs:
\[
x \sim y \iff \forall S,\ x \in cl(S) \leftrightarrow y \in cl(S).
\]

**Step 2.** Prove finite index of this equivalence under bounded rank.
- Show equivalence classes admit canonical minimal representatives.
- Show these classes form the states/gates of the minimal computational object.

**Step 3.** Reconstruct the circuit as the transition algebra of residual classes.
- This mirrors DFA minimization, but for monotone closure propagation rather than language recognition.
- Minimality is then a quotient universal property.

**Why this is revolutionary:** this would be the first convincing “Nerode theorem for closure-generated monotone computation.” If feasible, it is the conceptual crown jewel. It may be harder to land fully, but even a finite restricted version would be a major statement.

## Minimality: Be Precise About the Notion

You must define a size measure that Lean can reason about. Suggested options:

1. **Basis size minimality**
   - easiest and mathematically cleanest,
   - prove no residual-equivalent presentation can use fewer irredundant residual generators.

2. **Gate count minimality up to congruence**
   - stronger, but requires a DAG representation and quotienting by shared subcircuits.

3. **DNF-clause minimality**
   - very tractable if your reconstructed circuits are monotone DNF,
   - theorem: the canonical basis yields the unique irredundant monotone DNF for the closure indicator family.

I recommend first proving **minimality in the DNF/basis sense**, then stating gate-level minimality modulo residual equivalence as a corollary or later extension.
