## Core Theorem Package

### Theorem 1: Positive-trace surjectivity and uniqueness of logarithm coordinates

Let `𝓗₀` denote the real vector space of traceless Hermitian `2 × 2` matrices. Define the normalized quantum exponential map
\[
\operatorname{qEMLnorm} : \mathcal H_0 \to \mathrm{SU}(2),
\]
with the intended meaning that for traceless Hermitian `H`, `qEMLnorm H` is the special unitary obtained by the normalized exponential construction already present in the project context.

For every target \(U_* \in \mathrm{SU}(2)\) with \(\operatorname{tr}(U_*) > 0\), there exists a unique \(H_* \in \mathcal H_0\) in the principal ball \(\|H_*\| < \pi\) such that
\[
\operatorname{qEMLnorm}(H_*) = U_*.
\]

This is the geometric heart of the story: the positive-trace region is the image of a single logarithm chart, so the optimization problem is globally identifiable there.

### Theorem 2: Global characterization of minimizers of the Frobenius loss

Define
\[
L_{U_*}(H) := \| \operatorname{qEMLnorm}(H) - U_* \|_F^2
\]
on traceless Hermitian matrices. Then for \(U_*\in \mathrm{SU}(2)\) with \(\operatorname{tr}(U_*)>0\):

1. \(L_{U_*}(H)\ge 0\) for all \(H\),
2. \(L_{U_*}(H)=0\) iff \(H=H_*\) within the principal ball,
3. every critical point in the principal ball is the global minimizer \(H_*\).

This upgrades identifiability into a **strict landscape theorem**: no spurious stationary points in the physically relevant chart.

  ‖qEMLnorm H.val - U‖^2

    (U : Matrix (Fin 2) (Fin 2) ℂ) (H : TracelessHermitian2) :
    0 ≤ frobeniusLoss U H

### Theorem 3: Exponential convergence of gradient flow in principal coordinates

Let \(H_*\) be the unique principal logarithm of \(U_*\). Prove a quantitative contraction statement for the gradient flow
\[
\dot H(t) = - \nabla L_{U_*}(H(t)).
\]
A fully formal ODE theorem may be ambitious, so formulate a theorem at one of the following levels, ordered by desirability:

#### Strongest target
For all trajectories staying in the principal ball,
\[
\|H(t)-H_*\| \le C e^{-\lambda t}\|H(0)-H_*\|
\]
for some explicit \(C,\lambda>0\) depending only on \(\operatorname{tr}(U_*)\).

#### Discrete verified surrogate
For gradient descent
\[
H_{n+1}=H_n-\eta \nabla L_{U_*}(H_n),
\]
for sufficiently small \(\eta\),
\[
\|H_{n+1}-H_*\| \le \rho \|H_n-H_*\|,\qquad 0<\rho<1.
\]

#### Energy-decay fallback
Show a Polyak–Łojasiewicz or strong-gradient-domination inequality in the principal ball:
\[
L_{U_*}(H) \le c \|\nabla L_{U_*}(H)\|^2,
\]
which implies exponential energy decay for the flow and linear convergence for small-step descent.

## Recommended Proof Strategies

## Strategy A: Pauli-coordinate reduction to radial Euclidean geometry
**Most promising.**

Exploit the classical fact that every traceless Hermitian `2 × 2` matrix has the form
\[
H = x\sigma_x + y\sigma_y + z\sigma_z,
\]
and `SU(2)` elements with positive trace can be written as
\[
U = \cos r\, I + i \sin r\, \hat n \cdot \sigma,\qquad 0 \le r < \pi/2 \text{ or more generally } r<\pi.
\]
Then `qEMLnorm` becomes a radial map from `ℝ^3` to the 3-sphere model of `SU(2)`. The loss against a fixed target becomes a function of radial distance and angular alignment. From there:

1. **Classification step:** prove positive-trace elements admit unique principal coordinates `(r, n)` with `r < π`.
2. **Loss decomposition step:** show \(L(H)\) can be rewritten in closed form in terms of `r(H)`, `r_*`, and the dot product \(n(H)\cdot n_*\).
3. **Coercivity/contraction step:** derive strict convexity or PL inequality in the principal chart, then infer uniqueness and exponential convergence.

Why this is best: it reduces matrix analysis to explicit real-variable inequalities in dimension 3, which Lean handles much more reliably than abstract manifold ODE theory.

---

## Strategy B: Lie-theoretic logarithm chart plus Hessian positivity
Work directly on `SU(2)` as a compact Lie group.

1. Prove the positive-trace region is a geodesically convex normal neighborhood of the identity.
2. Define the principal logarithm \(U \mapsto \log(U)\) there and set \(H_* = \log(U_*)\).
3. Show the pullback loss \(H \mapsto \|\exp(H)-U_*\|_F^2\) has positive-definite Hessian on the principal chart, hence unique critical point and exponential convergence.

This route is more conceptually elegant and connects directly to Riemannian optimization, but may require more differential geometry than is currently convenient in Mathlib.

---

## Strategy C: Algebraic identity route via trace formulas
Use explicit `2 × 2` matrix identities:
\[
\|A-B\|_F^2 = \operatorname{tr}((A-B)^*(A-B)),
\]
and for unitary matrices,
\[
\|U-V\|_F^2 = 4 - 2 \Re \operatorname{tr}(U^*V).
\]
Then:

1. Rewrite the loss entirely in terms of trace interactions.
2. Express `qEMLnorm(H)` using trigonometric matrix identities for traceless Hermitian matrices.
3. Use scalar inequalities on cosine and sine to prove uniqueness and quantitative decay.

This may be the best fallback if the Pauli basis infrastructure is incomplete.

## Concrete Mathematical Sublemmas to Target

These are likely the real proof skeleton.

1. **Pauli decomposition lemma**
   Every traceless Hermitian `2×2` matrix is uniquely determined by three real coordinates.

2. **Closed-form exponential lemma**
   For traceless Hermitian `H`,
   \[
   \exp(iH)=\cos(\|H\|)I + i\frac{\sin(\|H\|)}{\|H\|}H
   \]
   when \(H\neq 0\), with the correct zero-case handled separately.

3. **Positive-trace characterization**
   For \(U=\cos r\,I+i\sin r\,N\), positivity of trace is equivalent to \(\cos r>0\), hence \(r\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)\) modulo conventions; adapt carefully to your normalization.

4. **Frobenius loss identity**
   For unitary matrices,
   \[
   L(H)=4-2\Re \operatorname{tr}(U_*^*\,\operatorname{qEMLnorm}(H)).
   \]

5. **Unique minimizer lemma**
   The trace expression is maximized uniquely at the principal logarithm.

6. **Gradient domination lemma**
   On the principal chart, the loss controls the squared gradient by an explicit constant depending on the target radius.

These lemmas should require real proof work: `rcases` on matrix entries or Pauli coordinates, `field_simp` in explicit norm formulas, `by_contra` for uniqueness, and multi-step `calc` chains for trace identities.
