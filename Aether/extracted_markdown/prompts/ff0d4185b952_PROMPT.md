### Strategy A: Direct ultrametric-to-laminar combinatorics
1. Prove that finite ultrametric balls are pairwise nested or disjoint.
2. Restrict to diagonally stable balls and show closure properties needed for a finite lattice.

Why promising: this is the most concrete and likely the easiest to formalize robustly in Lean. Ultrametric ball laminarity is a strong structural fact, and finite `Finset` reasoning should work well.

### Strategy B: Birkhoff-style representation via finite distributive lattices
1. Show stable regions form a finite distributive lattice.
2. Apply finite distributive lattice representation ideas: every finite distributive lattice is determined by its poset of join-irreducibles.
3. Interpret join-irreducibles as prime-congruence observer cells and reconstruct the rooted tree from the poset.

Why promising: this gives the cleanest conceptual theorem. If Mathlib support for distributive lattices, finite ideals, and join-irreducibles is sufficient, this route could yield the strongest and most elegant duality statement.

1. Define the semimodule over an idempotent/tropical Boolean semiring.
2. Show observer contraction thresholds act idempotently and preserve laminar generators.
3. Derive representation and reconstruction from finite tropical decomposition plus ultrametric compatibility.

Why promising: this is the most visionary path and best aligned with the stated semimodule language. It is also the riskiest because semiring/semimodule infrastructure may be heavier in Lean than the combinatorial core. Best used after Strategy A has established the finite skeleton.

/-- Bridge: connects semiring congruence geometry to neural proof compression

structure FiniteProofObserverFamily (S : Type u) [Add S] [Mul S] where
  /-- Number of observers -/
  n : ℕ
  /-- The family of ring congruences, indexed by `Fin n` -/

  ∀ ⦃x y : S⦄, x ∈ T → y ∈ T → x ≠ y → ∃ i : Fin F.n, ¬ (F.cong i) x y
