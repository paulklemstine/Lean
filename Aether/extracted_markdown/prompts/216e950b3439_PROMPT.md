## Core theorem target

### Main breakthrough theorem
For each `n ≥ 1`, construct an explicit homogeneous quartic polynomial
\[
P_A(z,w,x_1,\dots,x_n) \in \mathbb{Q}[z,w,x_1,\dots,x_n]
\]
such that for every symmetric rational matrix \(A \in \mathrm{Sym}_n(\mathbb{Q})\),

\[
\mathrm{Lorentzian}(P_A)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
A \text{ has at most one positive eigenvalue}.
\]

Moreover, the coefficients of \(P_A\) must be computable in time polynomial in the bit-size of \(A\).

The intended shape is not arbitrary. You should aim for a quartic of the form
\[
P_A(z,w,x)= z^2\,Q_A(x) + M\, z w \sum_i x_i^2 + N\, w^2 \sum_i x_i^2
\]
or a nearby homogeneous variant, where \(Q_A(x)=x^\top A x\), and where the constants \(M,N\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\) are chosen so that a specific degree-2 leaf has Hessian block structure exposing \(A\) while the remaining directions are regularized just enough to preserve equivalence.

The theorem is only interesting if the “if and only if” is genuinely sharp, not merely one implication.

### Lean-oriented theorem signature target
You should formalize a theorem morally of the following shape (adapt names as needed to match Mathlib and catalog definitions):

```lean

  (n : ℕ) :
  ∃ (P : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℚ → MvPolynomial (Fin (n+2)) ℚ),
    (∀ A, A.IsSymm → IsHomogeneous (P A) 4) ∧

```

If `Lorentzian`, `IsHomogeneous`, or `PolynomialTimeComputable` are not yet available in exactly this form, define a precise surrogate structure and prove the equivalence against the catalog’s existing recognition predicates. The new definition itself is part of the contribution.

### Theorem 1: Explicit Hessian embedding theorem
Construct a new polynomial family `spectralEmbed` and prove that a designated degree-2 leaf has Hessian equal to a block extension of `A`.

Suggested formal target:

```lean
def spectralEmbed (A : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℚ) :
    MvPolynomial (Fin (n+2)) ℚ := ...

theorem hessian_degreeTwoLeaf_spectralEmbed
  (A : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℚ) (hA : A.IsSymm) :
  ∃ B : Matrix (Fin (n+2)) (Fin (n+2)) ℚ,
    hessianMatrix (degreeTwoLeaf (spectralEmbed A)) = B ∧
    EmbeddedPrincipalBlock A B
```

Here `EmbeddedPrincipalBlock` should be your new definition expressing that `A` appears as a principal block, Schur complement, or congruent block of the leaf Hessian. This is a **novel definition requirement**: do not merely reuse an existing matrix inclusion notion if the catalog lacks the exact concept you need.

**Why it matters:** this theorem is the algebraic heart of the reduction. It says the Lorentzian leaf is not merely analogous to a spectral object; it literally contains the matrix spectrum being tested.

### Theorem 2: Positive-direction obstruction theorem
Prove that if `A` has at least two positive eigenvalues, then the constructed polynomial is **not** Lorentzian.

Suggested target:

```lean

  (hpos : HasAtLeastTwoPositiveEigenvalues A) :
```

This should proceed by extracting a 2-dimensional positive subspace for the relevant Hessian block and contradicting the catalog’s Lorentzian leaf negativity principle. This is where `by_contra` is especially appropriate.

**Why it matters:** this gives the hard direction and creates the reduction from spectral infeasibility to Lorentzian failure.

### Theorem 3: Lorentzian synthesis theorem

**Why it matters:** this completes the equivalence and upgrades the construction from obstruction gadget to exact spectral certificate.

## Proof architecture: 3 viable strategies

### Strategy A: Direct Hessian block embedding
1. Define `spectralEmbed A` so that differentiating twice in the two auxiliary variables produces a quadratic leaf whose Hessian contains `A` exactly.
2. Prove a block-inertia lemma: the regularization terms contribute at most one positive direction beyond the embedded block, or are neutralized by a Schur complement argument.
3. Invoke the catalog’s Lorentzian recognition theorem on degree-2 leaves.

**Why this is promising:** it is the cleanest route from the existing `hessianMatrix` infrastructure. It aligns perfectly with the assignment’s “reverse recognition” philosophy.

---

### Strategy B: Quadratic-form/congruence route
1. Replace eigenvalue counting by a quadratic-form notion: “at most one positive eigenvalue” becomes “every 2D subspace contains a nonpositive direction.”
2. Show your embedded Hessian is congruent to a matrix of the form
   \[
   \begin{pmatrix}
   * & * \\
   * & A
   \end{pmatrix}
   \]
   with congruence preserving positive index.
3. Translate Lorentzianity into a statement about signatures of these quadratic forms.

**Why this is promising:** formalizing inertia over `ℚ` can be easier through congruence and subspace positivity than through spectral decomposition. This may be the best Lean path if eigenvalue APIs are awkward.

---

### Strategy C: Semidefinite duality route
1. Express “at most one positive eigenvalue” as a feasibility/infeasibility statement for a semidefinite cone slice.
2. Build `P_A` so its Lorentzian leaf inequalities are equivalent to that SDP condition.
3. Derive the equivalence by dual certificates.

**Why this is visionary:** this creates a direct bridge to optimization and could generalize beyond symmetric matrices to hyperbolic/Lorentzian cones.  
**Why it is riskier:** likely heavier to formalize unless the catalog already contains enough SDP infrastructure.

**Recommendation:** pursue **Strategy A first**, with **Strategy B as the formal fallback** if spectral language becomes brittle.
