## Assignment: Direction 2 — Robust Certificate Compilation for Approximate Lorentzianity

**Mode:** prove

Build a mathematically serious robustness theory for certificate compilation beyond the exact Lorentzian regime. The goal is not to repackage continuity folklore, but to establish a quantitatively sharp bridge between **approximate combinatorial log-concavity / Lorentzian structure** and **stable quantum state preparation**. This would open a genuine interface between Lorentzian polynomials, approximation theory, and quantum algorithm certification.

You should treat the exact compilation results as the zeroth-order theory, and prove the first nontrivial perturbative theory.

## Core Vision

Exact certificate compilation says: if the coefficient data comes from a certified Lorentzian source, then the compiled preparation tree produces the correct normalized coefficient state. That is powerful but brittle. Real coefficient data — from physics, counting, inference, or noisy combinatorial models — will almost never be exactly Lorentzian.

The breakthrough target is to prove that **approximate Lorentzianity is enough**: if the coefficient vector is close to one admitting an exact certificate, then the compiled state remains close in fidelity, with a quantitatively controlled loss. This upgrades certificate compilation from a symbolic theorem to a robust scientific tool.

This is not merely “continuity of normalization.” The deeper point is:

- Lorentzianity gives a **structured exact witness**,
- the compilation map should be shown to be **stable under perturbation of witness data**,
- and fidelity loss should be related to a mathematically natural distance such as total variation or ℓ¹ distance on nonnegative coefficients.

That is a certificate-compression theorem for approximate structure.

## Proof Architecture: 3 viable strategies

### Strategy A: direct perturbation of normalized coefficient states
Most promising.

1. Prove a deterministic normalization perturbation lemma:
   \[
   \left\|\frac{w}{\|w\|}-\frac{v}{\|v\|}\right\|
   \le
   \frac{\|w-v\|}{\|w\|}
   +
   \|v\|\left|\frac{1}{\|w\|}-\frac{1}{\|v\|}\right|.
   \]
   Then bound the reciprocal difference by reverse triangle inequality.

2. Convert norm closeness into fidelity closeness using:
   \[
   \|x-y\|^2 = 2 - 2\langle x,y\rangle
   \]
   for unit vectors in the real nonnegative setting, hence
   \[
   \langle x,y\rangle \ge 1 - \frac12\|x-y\|^2.
   \]

3. Use `coeffState_unique` to identify the exact compiled output with the normalized exact coefficient state, so the perturbative estimate transfers to the compilation target.

Why this is most promising: it minimizes dependence on deep Lorentzian internals and instead leverages exact compilation as a black-box rigidity theorem. It should fit Lean well because every step can be broken into finite-dimensional inequalities.

### Strategy B: mass-coupling / total variation route
Good if ℓ¹ APIs are easier than Hilbert-space APIs.

1. Normalize nonnegative weights into probability vectors \(p,q\).
2. Use total variation control to bound Hellinger or Bhattacharyya overlap:
   \[
   \sum_i \sqrt{p_i q_i} \ge 1 - \mathrm{TV}(p,q).
   \]
3. Interpret the coefficient state overlap as Bhattacharyya coefficient, then square to get fidelity.

This is elegant and naturally probabilistic. It creates a deep bridge between **quantum fidelity** and **classical statistical distance**. If this route is available, it may produce cleaner constants than the raw Euclidean argument.

### Strategy C: certificate-map stability as a compositional theorem
Most ambitious.

1. Formalize the compilation tree as a compositional map from local branching ratios to global amplitudes.
2. Prove each local operation is Lipschitz on a compact positive domain.
3. Conclude the whole compiled-state map is Lipschitz, with constant obtained by inductive composition over the tree depth.

This is the strongest theorem conceptually because it controls perturbations in the **certificate itself**, not just in the final coefficient vector. It may be harder, but if the tree API in the catalog is explicit enough, this would be a major advance.

That is a bona fide condition-number theorem for certificate compilation.
