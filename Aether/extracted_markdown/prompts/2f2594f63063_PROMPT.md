This research cycle established a complete formal characterization of reversible elementary cellular automata (radius 1, binary alphabet): exactly 6 of the 256 rules are universally reversible, and they are precisely the **single-dependency rules** with bijective transforms. The key insight is that reversibility requires a local rule to depend on at most one of its three inputs — any genuine mixing of information from multiple neighbors inevitably creates collisions (multiple configurations mapping to the same output) for some lattice size.

The proof architecture decomposes cleanly into three pillars: (1) the **SingleDepRule** abstraction, which factors a local rule into a cyclic index permutation and a pointwise Boolean transform, enabling a clean bijectivity proof by composition of bijections; (2) **dependency analysis**, which classifies rules by which inputs they genuinely affect, connecting to the SingleDepRule structure; and (3) **explicit counterexamples** (XOR/Rule 90) demonstrating the mechanism of information loss when multiple inputs are combined.

The most promising cross-domain connection is between the **algebraic structure of reversible CAs** (S₃ × ℤ/2ℤ for elementary rules) and the **automorphism group of the full shift** in symbolic dynamics.
