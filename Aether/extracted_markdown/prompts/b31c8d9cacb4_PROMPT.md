## Core Vision

The restricted product
\[
\prod\nolimits'_i (X_i, K_i)
\]
sits exactly at the crossroads of:

- **probability theory**: projective systems and Kolmogorov consistency,
- **harmonic analysis**: Haar measure on non-Archimedean and adelic groups,
- **ergodic theory**: inverse limits and infinite-volume states,
- **mathematical physics**: thermodynamic/infinite-volume Gibbs limits,
- **number theory**: restricted products over local fields and finite residue systems.

The classical Kolmogorov extension theorem is usually stated for full products of standard Borel spaces. Your task is to show that the **restricted product itself admits an intrinsic cylinder-based extension principle**, and that in countable standard-Borel settings this recovers the expected Borel measure on the restricted product topology.

This would open a formal route to **constructive Haar measure on adelic objects**, replacing black-box existence arguments with a finite-level compatible system.

## Precise Mathematical Target

Let `ι` be an index type, and for each `i : ι`, let `X i` be a measurable space with a distinguished measurable subset `K i` (“default compact/open support”). Let the restricted product consist of families `x : ∀ i, X i` such that `x i ∈ K i` for all but finitely many `i`.

For each finite set `S : Finset ι`, suppose we are given a probability measure
\[
\nu_S \text{ on } \prod_{i \in S} X_i
\]
such that for all `S ⊆ T`, the marginal of `ν_T` to coordinates `S` is `ν_S`.

The fundamental theorem to formalize is:

### Theorem A: Cylinder premeasure on restricted products
There exists a canonically defined cylinder premeasure on basic restricted-product cylinders, independent of the choice of finite support used to present the cylinder.

This should use the catalog lemmas directly.

### Theorem B: Countable additivity on the cylinder algebra
Under suitable countability / standard-Borel hypotheses on the component spaces, the cylinder premeasure is countably additive on the algebra generated by basic restricted cylinders.

This is the hard theorem: it is where the projective consistency is upgraded to a true measure-theoretic object.

### Theorem C: Extension and uniqueness
The cylinder premeasure extends uniquely to a measure on the Borel σ-algebra of the restricted product, and this extension has the prescribed finite-dimensional marginals.

### Theorem D: Haar reconstruction as a corollary
When each `X i` is a locally compact group with normalized measure assigning mass `1` to `K i`, and `ν_S` is the finite product of local measures over `S`, the extension coincides with the restricted-product Haar measure.

This is the conceptual payoff: **Kolmogorov consistency reconstructs Haar measure on restricted products**.

## Proof Strategy Architecture

### Strategy A: Cylinder algebra → Carathéodory extension
**Most promising.**

1. **Define a cylinder content**
   using finite-dimensional marginals and the restricted-support condition.

2. **Prove representation invariance**
   by enlarging supports and applying:
   - `basicCylinder_independent_of_disjoint`
   - `basicCylinder_measure_support_enlarge`

3. **Prove algebraic additivity**
   by refining finitely many cylinders to a common support and reducing to finite-dimensional measure additivity.

4. **Upgrade to a premeasure**
   on the cylinder algebra; then invoke a Carathéodory extension theorem to obtain the global measure.

5. **Prove uniqueness**
   from agreement on a generating π-system or measurable rectangles/cylinders.

**Why this is most promising:** it matches the catalog infrastructure directly, uses the existing restricted-product cylinder formulas as the core local lemma, and avoids needing to first identify the restricted product as a standard Borel space in full generality.

---

### Strategy B: Embed restricted product into full product as an eventually-in-`K` measurable subset
1. View the restricted product as a measurable subset of the full product \(\prod_i X_i\).
2. Apply a version of Kolmogorov extension on the full product to obtain a measure.
3. Prove concentration on the “eventually in `K`” subset using normalization / support assumptions.
4. Restrict the full-product measure to the restricted product and identify the finite-dimensional marginals.

**Why it is attractive:** conceptually elegant and closer to classical probability texts.

**Why it is harder in Lean:** the measurable structure and “eventually in `K`” subset can be technically delicate, especially if the restricted product topology/measurable structure is encoded intrinsically rather than as a subtype.

---

### Strategy C: Standard Borel coding by countable disjoint union of finite-support sectors
1. Decompose the restricted product into sectors indexed by finite subsets \(S\), where outside \(S\) coordinates lie in `K i`.
2. Code each sector by a finite product times a tail constrained to `K`.
3. Use countable union / measurable coding arguments to prove standard-Borel behavior.
4. Then apply a projective-limit theorem in a coded setting.

**Why it matters:** this strategy is the right one if you want the strongest theorem about standard Borel structure itself.

**Why it is secondary:** it is more ambitious and may be better as a second-stage theorem after the cylinder premeasure construction is complete.

## Exact Mathematical Strength to Aim For

A particularly strong target theorem is:

> **Restricted Kolmogorov Extension Theorem.**
> Let `ι` be countable. For each `i : ι`, let `X i` be a standard Borel space and `K i ⊆ X i` a measurable set. Suppose `F` is a compatible family of probability measures on finite coordinate products. Assume the induced cylinder content is concentrated on restricted cylinders compatible with eventual membership in `K`. Then there exists a unique Borel probability measure `μ` on the restricted product `RestrictedProduct X K` such that for every finite `S`, the pushforward of `μ` to the `S`-coordinates is exactly `F.ν S`.

This is stronger and more original than a generic projective-limit restatement because it is **intrinsic to restricted products** rather than inherited passively from full products.
