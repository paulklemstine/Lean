## Assignment: 4. Certified Tropical Polynomial Normal Form

**Mode:** prove

Prove a genuinely new theorem establishing a **decidable, computable canonical normal form for tropical polynomials** and a **semantic completeness theorem** saying that equality of tropical polynomial functions is equivalent to equality of canonical forms. This should not be a syntactic cleanup lemma; it should be a field-opening bridge between tropical algebra, convex geometry, automata minimization, and neural-network verification.

The central vision is this: in ordinary algebra, polynomial normal forms make equality decidable by coefficient comparison. In tropical algebra, naive coefficient comparison fails because distinct min-plus expressions can define the same piecewise-linear function. The breakthrough is to formalize the correct replacement: **lower-envelope canonicalization**, where semantically redundant monomials are removed, yielding a unique representation of the induced concave piecewise-linear function. If done correctly, this becomes a certified equivalence checker for a broad class of tropical/ReLU models.

---

### Precise Theorem Target

Work over exponent vectors `σ : ι →₀ ℕ` for a finite index type `ι`, and coefficients in `ℚ` first (then, if smooth, generalize to `ℤ` or `ℝ`). A tropical monomial represents the affine map
\[
x \mapsto c + \sum_i \sigma(i)\,x_i,
\]
and a tropical polynomial is a finite infimum of such affine maps:
\[
p(x)=\min_{m\in M} (c_m + \langle \sigma_m,x\rangle).
\]

Define a monomial `m` to be **globally dominated** in a polynomial `P` if for every `x`, some other monomial in `P` has value `≤ m(x)`. Define `canonicalize P` to:
1. merge duplicate exponent vectors by keeping the smallest coefficient,
2. remove globally dominated monomials,
3. sort the remaining support by a fixed total order.

Then prove:

> **Semantic completeness of tropical canonical form**  
> For all tropical polynomials `P Q`,  
> `eval P = eval Q` as functions `((ι → ℚ) → ℚ)` iff `canonicalize P = canonicalize Q`.

This is the theorem that turns tropical polynomial equivalence into certified normalization.

### Proof Strategy Architecture

#### Strategy A: Direct semantic separation by asymptotic weight vectors
This is likely the most promising route for the core completeness theorem.

**Step 1.** Define duplicate-merge normalization.  
For each exponent vector `σ`, keep only the least coefficient among monomials with exponent `σ`. Prove this preserves evaluation pointwise.

**Step 2.** Characterize domination semantically.  
Show that a monomial is removable iff it never uniquely attains the minimum. Then prove removing a dominated monomial preserves evaluation.

**Step 3.** Prove uniqueness by separation.  
Suppose two irredundant normalized supports differ. Choose a monomial present in one but not the other. Construct a test point `x` making that monomial strictly minimal among all competitors by sending coordinates in directions that separate exponent vectors. This is the tropical analogue of coefficient extraction. Conclude semantic equality forces support and coefficients to coincide.

**Why promising:**  
It is elementary, constructive, and likely formalizable without a large convex-geometry apparatus. The key lemma is a finite-dimensional separation-by-growth argument over `ℚ`.

---

#### Strategy B: Convex-geometric lower-envelope route
This is more ambitious and mathematically deeper.

**Step 1.** Interpret each monomial as a lifted point `(σ,c)` in exponent-coefficient space.

**Step 2.** Prove that evaluation of the tropical polynomial is the support function / lower envelope determined by these lifted points.

**Step 3.** Show monomials are semantically redundant exactly when their lifted points lie strictly above the lower convex hull. Then canonicalization is lower-hull extraction, and uniqueness follows from uniqueness of the lower hull.

**Why promising:**  
This gives the strongest theorem and the most future leverage. It turns normalization into convex geometry, unlocking Newton polytope machinery.  
**Why harder:** formal convex hull infrastructure may be heavier than needed for the first pass.

---

#### Strategy C: Finite minimization / automata analogy
This is the most cross-domain and could yield elegant corollaries.

**Step 1.** Define a semantic preorder on monomials relative to a polynomial: `m₁ ≼ m₂` if `m₁(x) ≤ m₂(x)` for all `x`.

**Step 2.** Show canonical support is the antichain of minimal generators after quotienting duplicate exponents and removing universally dominated terms.

**Step 3.** Relate this to a finite-state minimization principle inspired by `tropical_recognizable_iff_finite_nerode`: semantic equivalence classes admit unique minimal representatives.

**Why promising:**  
Excellent for conceptual synthesis and future generalizations to weighted automata and tropical circuits.  
**Why secondary:** likely best used after Strategy A establishes the concrete theorem.

### Mathematical Insight You Should Exploit

The central nontrivial fact is this:

- If two monomials have the same exponent vector, only the smaller coefficient matters.
- If one monomial’s lifted point is not on the lower hull, then it is never a minimizer and is semantically invisible.
- Distinct lower-hull monomials can be separated by suitable evaluation points.

That last bullet is the tropical replacement for comparing coefficients in ordinary polynomial algebra. It is the true source of uniqueness.

In one variable, this is especially transparent: canonical monomials correspond to edges/vertices of the lower Newton polygon. In many variables, they correspond to lower faces of the lifted Newton polytope. Formalizing even a finite combinatorial shadow of this would be significant.
