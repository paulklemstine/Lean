## Grand Objective

Formalize and prove a breakthrough closure principle:

> **Edge-factor Lorentzian closure for ferromagnetic multiaffine partition polynomials.**
> Every finite graph with nonnegative edge couplings produces a multiaffine partition polynomial in external-field variables that is Lorentzian on the positive orthant, because it factors into elementary ferromagnetic edge interactions and Lorentzianity survives this multiplication/polarization process.

If established, this would be a decisive bridge from **Lee–Yang/real stability ideas** to **Brändén–Huh Lorentzian geometry** for Ising-type models. It would not merely certify log-concavity of selected coefficient sequences; it would identify the entire positive-sector partition polynomial as belonging to the strongest currently known geometric concavity class.

## Precise Mathematical Target

Let `V` be a finite vertex set, `E` a finite set of unordered edges, and let nonnegative couplings `J : E → ℝ≥0` and inverse temperature `β : ℝ≥0` be given. Define the **multiaffine ferromagnetic partition polynomial** in field variables `z_v` by

\[
Z_G(z) \;:=\; \sum_{S \subseteq V} a_G(S)\,\prod_{v\in S} z_v,
\]

where `a_G(S) ≥ 0` is the induced coefficient obtained from the ferromagnetic Ising partition function after rewriting in the standard multiaffine external-field coordinates. Equivalently, one may work with an explicitly factorized edge-product presentation if that is what the catalog already supports.

### Main Conjectural Theorem to Prove

> **Theorem (positive-orthant Lorentzianity of ferromagnetic partition polynomials).**  
> For every finite graph `G`, every nonnegative coupling assignment `J`, and every `β ≥ 0`, the multiaffine partition polynomial `Z_G` is Lorentzian in the positive-orthant directional sense: for every sequence of vectors
> \[
> u_1,\dots,u_{d-2}\in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{V},
> \]
> where `d = totalDegree Z_G`, the iterated directional derivative
> \[
> D_{u_1}\cdots D_{u_{d-2}} Z_G
> \]
> is a quadratic form with at most one positive eigenvalue.

This is the exact missing theorem suggested by the current program. The core novelty is not a one-graph computation; it is the **closure theorem under edge multiplication** robust enough to generate this for all finite ferromagnetic graphs.

## Proof Architecture: 3 viable strategies

You must attempt at least two routes conceptually, even if one becomes the main implementation path.

### Strategy A: Edge-factor induction on the graph
1. Write the partition polynomial as a product or iterated composition of edge factors.
2. Prove each atomic edge factor is positive-orthant Lorentzian via explicit quadratic Hessian analysis.
3. Induct on the number of edges using a closure-under-product theorem.

**Why promising:** matches the physical construction of the model and scales naturally to arbitrary finite graphs.

**Technical obstacle:** multiplication of multiaffine polynomials can leave the multiaffine category unless variables are polarized or edge-local coordinates are introduced first and then projected. You must handle this cleanly.

---

### Strategy B: Polarization + known Lorentzian closure machinery
1. Lift the partition polynomial to a polarized polynomial with disjoint variable blocks per edge or per interaction.
2. Use or extend catalog closure lemmas for Lorentzianity under product/anti-cancellation.
3. Push Lorentzianity back down under diagonal specialization or positive linear maps.

**Why this is most promising:** it aligns directly with the Brändén–Huh worldview: Lorentzianity is best behaved under homogenization, polarization, and positive linear transformations. It also interfaces naturally with anti-cancellation lemmas from `LorentzianAggregateAntiCancel.lean`.

---

### Strategy C: Real stability / Lee–Yang transfer
1. Formalize that the ferromagnetic partition polynomial is real stable or sector-stable in the relevant variables.
2. Invoke or prove that multiaffine stable polynomials with nonnegative coefficients are Lorentzian.
3. Specialize to the positive-orthant derivative formulation.

**Why powerful:** conceptually deepest, and would unify your theorem with classical Lee–Yang theory.

**Why risky:** may require more stability infrastructure in Lean than currently available. Use this if the catalog already contains substantial real-stability APIs; otherwise keep it as a future direction or a parallel lemma chain.

## Conjecture with falsifiable computational test

State and investigate:

> **Conjecture (full positive-sector Lorentzianity for ferromagnetic partition polynomials).**  
> For every finite graph `G`, every `β ≥ 0`, every nonnegative coupling assignment `J`, and every positive specialization of all but two field directions, the resulting quadratic form has at most one positive eigenvalue.

### Computational falsification protocol
Implement a search over:
- complete graphs `K_3` through `K_7`
- triangle-free graphs
- random graphs on up to at least 8 vertices
- multiple coupling profiles: constant `J`, sparse heterogeneous `J`, high-β and low-β regimes

For each instance:
1. build the partition polynomial,
2. specialize `n-2` variables to positive values,
3. compute the Hessian of the resulting quadratic,
4. numerically estimate eigenvalues,
5. report any case with **two positive eigenvalues**.

A single such instance refutes the conjecture. This is scientifically valuable even if the theorem ultimately fails.
