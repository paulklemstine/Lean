## Precise Theorem Targets

### Theorem 1: q-reduced divisors are exactly the unique energy minimizers in their chip-firing class

**Mathematical statement.**  
Let `G` be a finite connected graph, `q` a sink vertex, and let `D` be a sink-normalized divisor. Define
\[
E_q(D) := D^\top (L^{(q)})^{-1} D,
\]
where `L^{(q)}` is the reduced Laplacian. Then for every linear equivalence class of sink-normalized divisors, there exists a unique q-reduced representative, and this representative is the unique minimizer of `E_q` on that class.

More explicitly:
\[
\forall D,\ \exists! D_r,\ \big(D_r \sim D \land \mathrm{qReduced}(D_r)\big),
\]
and
\[
\forall D_r,\ \mathrm{qReduced}(D_r) \to
\forall D',\ D' \sim D_r \to E_q(D_r) \le E_q(D'),
\]
with equality iff `D' = D_r`.

### Suggested Lean 4 type signature
```lean
theorem qReduced_iff_unique_energy_minimizer
  {V : Type} [Fintype V] [DecidableEq V]
  (G : SimpleGraph V) (q : V)
  (hconn : G.Connected) :
  ∀ (D : V → ℤ),
    D q = 0 →
    ∃! Dr : V → ℤ,
      Dr q = 0 ∧
      Dr ∼[G,q] D ∧
      IsQReduced G q Dr ∧
      (∀ D' : V → ℤ,
        D' q = 0 →
        D' ∼[G,q] D →
        reducedEnergy G q Dr ≤ reducedEnergy G q D') ∧
      (∀ D' : V → ℤ,
        D' q = 0 →
        D' ∼[G,q] D →
        reducedEnergy G q Dr = reducedEnergy G q D' → D' = Dr)
```

If the pseudoinverse is too heavy to formalize directly, replace it with the inverse of the reduced Laplacian on sink-normalized divisors and prove equivalence later.

### Theorem 2: Energy drop under nontrivial legal firing

**Mathematical statement.**  
If `D' = D - Lf` for a nonzero legal firing script `f` away from the sink, then
\[
E_q(D') = E_q(D) - 2\langle f, D\rangle_q + \langle f, L^{(q)} f\rangle,
\]
and in particular, for any step toward the q-reduced representative, energy strictly decreases unless the firing script is zero.

This is the engine theorem. It turns reducedness into a strict Lyapunov principle.

### Suggested Lean 4 type signature
```lean
theorem reducedEnergy_sub_laplacian
  {V : Type} [Fintype V] [DecidableEq V]
  (G : SimpleGraph V) (q : V)
  (hconn : G.Connected) :
  ∀ (D : V → ℤ) (f : V → ℤ),
    f q = 0 →
    D q = 0 →

    reducedEnergy G q D
```

### Theorem 4: Cross-domain bridge — spectral gap controls energy relaxation

This is where the project becomes visionary.

**Mathematical statement.**  
Let `P` be the chip-firing Markov operator on stable configurations with sink `q`. Then the one-step expected energy decay toward equilibrium is bounded below by the Fiedler eigenvalue `λ₂` of the graph Laplacian:
\[
\mathbb E[E_q(D_{t+1}) - E_q(D_\ast)\mid D_t]
\le
(1 - c\,\lambda_2)\,(E_q(D_t)-E_q(D_\ast)),
\]
for some graph-dependent normalization constant `c > 0`, where `D_\ast` is the critical representative of the class.

Even a weaker deterministic theorem is valuable:
\[
\lambda_2 \|x\|^2 \le x^\top L x
\quad \text{for sink-free } x,
\]
combined with energy descent to show that **spectral geometry governs sandpile relaxation**.

### Suggested Lean 4 type signature
```lean
theorem fiedler_lower_bound_laplacianQuadratic
  {V : Type} [Fintype V] [DecidableEq V]
  (G : SimpleGraph V) (q : V) :
  ∀ x : V → ℝ,
    x q = 0 →
    orthogonalToConstants x →
    fiedlerValue G * euclideanNormSq x ≤ laplacianRealQuadratic G x
```

Then connect this to energy descent:
```lean
theorem spectral_gap_controls_energy_relaxation
  {V : Type} [Fintype V] [DecidableEq V]
  (G : SimpleGraph V) (q : V)
  (hconn : G.Connected) :
  ∀ D : V → ℤ,
    D q = 0 →
    ∃ c : ℝ, 0 < c ∧
      energyDistanceToReduced G q D
        ≤ c * firingPotential G q D / fiedlerValue G
```

This theorem is the cross-domain bridge: combinatorial dynamics + spectral graph theory + statistical physics.

## Proof Strategy Architecture

### Strategy A: Reduced Laplacian as the primary variational object
This is the most promising path.

1. **Pass to sink-normalized coordinates.**  
   Replace divisors `D : V → ℤ` with vectors on `V \ {q}`. Define `Lq := reducedLaplacianMatrix G q`. Prove `Lq` is symmetric positive definite for connected `G`.

2. **Express chip-firing moves linearly.**  
   Show every divisor in the equivalence class has the form `D - Lq z` for some integer vector `z`. Expand
   \[
   E(D-Lqz)=E(D)-2\langle z,D\rangle + z^\top Lq z.
   \]
   Since `z^\top Lq z > 0` for `z ≠ 0`, minimization is strictly convex on the lattice orbit.

3. **Identify q-reducedness with first-order optimality.**  
   Prove that if `D` is not q-reduced, Dhar’s criterion gives a nonzero legal firing `z` with lower energy. Conversely, if `D` is q-reduced, every allowable nonzero `z` increases energy. This yields existence and uniqueness of the minimizer.

Why this is best: it avoids pseudoinverse technicalities and uses finite-dimensional positive definiteness, which is more Lean-friendly and more mathematically transparent.

---

### Strategy B: Potential theory / Green’s function route
This is conceptually elegant and ideal if resistance infrastructure already exists.

1. Define the Green potential `u = (L^{(q)})^{-1} D`.
2. Interpret energy as
   \[
   E(D)=\langle D,u\rangle = \sum_{v,w} D(v)G_q(v,w)D(w).
   \]
3. Show legal firings correspond to redistributing charge to lower electrostatic energy, and q-reducedness is the discrete obstacle-condition for equilibrium.

Why it matters: this makes the theorem read like a discrete Dirichlet principle and connects directly to electrical networks and resistance distance.

---

### Strategy C: Sandpile group and lattice reduction
This is strongest for the counting theorem.

1. Model chip-firing classes as the cokernel `ℤ^(V\{q}) / Im(Lq)`.
2. Prove each coset has a unique q-reduced/critical representative.
3. Use full rank of `Lq` and integer-lattice index theory to conclude
   \[
   |\mathrm{coker}(Lq)| = |\det Lq|.
   \]

Why this is useful: determinant counting may be easier through lattice index arguments than through direct recurrence enumeration.
