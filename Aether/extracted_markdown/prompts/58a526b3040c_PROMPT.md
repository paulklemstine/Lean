## What is mathematically new here

The obstacle is subtle: inversion permits identities like
\[
\exp(f)\cdot \exp(-f)=1,
\qquad
\frac{\exp(f)}{\exp(g)}=\exp(f-g),
\]
so naïve growth upper bounds fail. You need a stronger invariant than “largest visible exponent tower.” The right idea is to track **growth modulo cancellation**.

A promising new definition is one of the following:

### Option A: Growth Rank
Assign to each expression a natural number `growthRank`, recursively:
- input/constants have rank `0`,
- multiplication takes `max`,
- inversion preserves rank,
- exponentiation adds `1`.

Then prove that any expression of rank `d` is asymptotically controlled by a bounded combination of `iterExp d`, while `iterExp (d+1)` eventually dominates every such expression.

### Option B: Signed Exponential Profile
Associate to each DAG a pair `(u, ℓ)` of upper/lower tower-heights describing eventual bounds:
\[
\exp^{[\ell]}(c_1 x) \lesssim f(x) \lesssim \exp^{[u]}(c_2 x)
\]
for large positive `x`, with inversion swapping upper and lower behavior. Show that exact computation of `iterExp n` forces `u,ℓ ≥ n`.

### Option C: Log-Descent Complexity
Define the number of iterated logarithms required to reduce `f(x)` to a subexponential regime:
\[
\lambda(f) = \min\{k : \log^{\circ k}(f(x)) \text{ grows at most linearly/eventually moderately}\}.
\]
Then show:
- `λ(iterExp n) = n`,
- multiplication and inversion do not increase `λ`,
- exponentiation increases `λ` by at most `1`.

This is especially attractive because inversion becomes tame after taking logs on positive reals.

## Recommended proof strategies

You must present and try at least 2–3 approaches. Do not tunnel on one path too early.

### Strategy A: Asymptotic envelope induction on DAG structure
This is the most promising and the most Lean-compatible.

**Step 1.** Define a recursive invariant `HasEnvelope d G` meaning that both `G.eval` and its reciprocal are eventually bounded above by `iterExp d (C*x)` for some constant `C > 0`.

**Step 2.** Prove by structural induction on `G`:
- input/constants satisfy `HasEnvelope 0`,
- multiplication preserves `HasEnvelope (max d₁ d₂)`,
- inversion preserves the same `d`,
- exponentiation raises the envelope index by `1`.

**Step 3.** Show `iterExp (d+1)` cannot satisfy `HasEnvelope d`; derive contradiction if a depth-`< n` DAG computes `iterExp n`.

Why this is promising: it converts cancellation into a symmetric bound on both `f` and `1/f`, making inversion harmless rather than dangerous.

---

### Strategy B: Iterated logarithm descent
This is conceptually elegant and strongly connected to differential algebra.

**Step 1.** Define a predicate saying that after `d` iterated logs, a positive function becomes eventually bounded by an affine or polynomial function.

**Step 2.** Prove structural lemmas:
- multiplication corresponds to addition after logs,
- inversion contributes a sign change before logs but does not increase log-depth,
- exponentiation cancels one logarithm and thus increments complexity by exactly one.

**Step 3.** Show `iterExp n` requires exactly `n` logarithms to become tame. Any DAG of depth `< n` becomes tame too early.

Why this is promising: it matches the semantic nature of iterated exponentials almost perfectly. Why it is harder in Lean: eventual positivity and iterated-log domains need careful management.

---

### Strategy C: Differential-algebraic obstruction
This is more speculative but potentially revolutionary.

**Step 1.** Associate to each EML expression a differential complexity profile under repeated application of `x * d/dx` or logarithmic derivative.

**Step 2.** Show inversion and multiplication preserve a bounded differential class, while exponentiation increases class.

**Step 3.** Prove `iterExp n` has differential complexity exactly `n`.

Why this matters: if successful, it would connect arithmetic circuit depth with Liouvillian/differential transcendence ideas. Why it is less Lean-friendly: formal differential algebra over partial domains is likely heavy.

**Recommendation:** pursue Strategy A as the mainline proof, use Strategy B as a secondary route and source of intermediate lemmas, and record Strategy C in FUTURE_DIRECTIONS as a field-opening hypothesis.
