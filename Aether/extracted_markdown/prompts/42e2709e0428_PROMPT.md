## Theorem 1: Smith-normal-form extraction of torsion primes

Formalize the theorem that the torsion primes of homology are exactly the prime divisors of the non-unit invariant factors in the Smith normal form.

### Mathematical statement

Let \(C_{k+1} \xrightarrow{\partial_{k+1}} C_k \xrightarrow{\partial_k} C_{k-1}\) be a chain segment of finitely generated free abelian groups. If the induced homology group
\[
H_k = \ker(\partial_k)/\operatorname{im}(\partial_{k+1})
\]
has Smith decomposition
\[
H_k \cong \mathbb{Z}^r \oplus \bigoplus_i \mathbb{Z}/d_i\mathbb{Z},
\qquad d_i > 1,\ d_i \mid d_{i+1},
\]
then the torsion prime profile of \(H_k\) is exactly
\[
\bigcup_i \operatorname{PrimeFactors}(d_i).
\]

### Lean 4 target signature (schematic but precise)

```lean
theorem torsionPrimeProfile_eq_primeFactors_smithDiagonal
  {n m ℓ : ℕ}
  (d1 : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin m) ℤ)
  (d2 : Matrix (Fin m) (Fin ℓ) ℤ)
  (hchain : d1 * d2 = 0)
  (hsnf : SmithData d2) :
  torsionPrimeProfile (homologyOfBoundaryPair d1 d2 hchain) =
    hsnf.diagonal.toFinset.biUnion primeFactorsNat :
  ...
```

If the exact existing Smith API differs, adapt the statement to the available representation, but preserve the theorem’s content: **torsion primes are read directly from the SNF diagonal**.

### Why this is a breakthrough

This theorem upgrades `computeTorsionProfile_correct` from a correctness lemma about explicit moduli lists to a **structural extraction theorem** from canonical linear algebra data. It makes torsion profile computation compositional and portable across chain models.

### Proof strategies

**Strategy A: Classification-first approach**
1. Use the Smith normal form theorem to identify the cokernel / quotient as a direct sum of cyclic groups.
2. Characterize torsion in each summand \(\mathbb{Z}/d_i\mathbb{Z}\) by prime divisibility.
3. Reassemble via finite direct sums and extensionality of finite prime sets.

**Strategy B: Elementwise torsion detection**
1. Show that a prime \(p\) appears in the profile iff there exists \(x \neq 0\) killed by some \(p^r\).
2. Use the Smith basis to reduce to diagonal coordinates.
3. Prove equivalence with \(p \mid d_i\) for some invariant factor.

**Most promising:** Strategy A, because it aligns directly with the catalog theorem `computeTorsionProfile_correct` and avoids low-level element chasing.

**Deep proof tactics expected:** `rcases` on the Smith decomposition, multi-step `calc`, and a nontrivial finite-set extensionality proof.

## Theorem 2: Torsion detection via derived functors and prime profiles

Exploit the catalog bridge to derived algebra: the torsion profile should be equivalent to nonvanishing of a suitable Tor group.

### Mathematical statement

For a finitely generated abelian group \(A\) and a prime \(p\),
\[
p \in \mathrm{torsionPrimeProfile}(A)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{Tor}_1^{\mathbb{Z}}(A,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}) \neq 0.
\]
More generally, for homology \(H_k(K;\mathbb{Z})\),
\[
p \in \mathrm{torsionPrimeProfile}(H_k(K;\mathbb{Z}))
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{Tor}_1^{\mathbb{Z}}(H_k(K;\mathbb{Z}),\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}) \neq 0.
\]

### Lean 4 target signature

```lean
theorem prime_mem_torsionProfile_iff_Tor1_nontrivial
  (A : Type _) [AddCommGroup A] [Module ℤ A] [Module.Free ℤ A := by infer_instance]
  {p : ℕ} (hp : Nat.Prime p) :
  p ∈ torsionPrimeProfile A ↔
    ¬ Subsingleton (Tor₁ ℤ A (ZMod p)) 
```

or, if the Tor API is theorem-based rather than object-based, state it in terms of the catalog theorem:

```lean
theorem prime_mem_torsionProfile_iff_exists_p_torsion
  (A : Type _) [AddCommGroup A] [Module ℤ A]
  {p : ℕ} (hp : Nat.Prime p) :
  p ∈ torsionPrimeProfile A ↔
    ¬ noPTorsion A p
```

then derive it from:

- `Catalog/Algebra/Homology/DerivedFunctors/TorsionDetection.lean`
- `tor1_vanishes_iff_no_n_torsion`

### Why this is a breakthrough

This is the conceptual hinge: it connects **computational topology** to **derived algebra**. It says the arithmetic signature computed by your algorithm is not an ad hoc invariant—it is exactly the prime support seen by Tor. That gives arithmetic TDA a homological-algebraic foundation.

### Proof strategies

**Strategy A: Direct use of catalog theorem**
1. Rewrite “\(p\) in the profile” as existence of \(p\)-primary torsion.
2. Apply `tor1_vanishes_iff_no_n_torsion`.
3. Convert vanishing/nonvanishing into the desired biconditional.

**Strategy B: Through invariant factors**
1. Use Theorem 1 to identify profile primes with divisors of SNF diagonal entries.
2. Compute `Tor₁(ℤ/dℤ, ℤ/pℤ)` explicitly.
3. Extend over finite direct sums.

**Most promising:** Strategy A for elegance and reuse; Strategy B as a backup if API friction appears.

**Deep proof tactics expected:** `by_contra`, `rcases`, and a multi-step equivalence chain.

## Theorem 3: Degreewise union theorem for full simplicial torsion profile

Prove that the full arithmetic signature of a simplicial complex is the union of degreewise homological torsion signatures, and that this can be extracted from all boundary matrices alone.

### Mathematical statement

For a finite simplicial complex \(K\), define its full arithmetic signature
\[
\operatorname{ATS}(K)
=
\bigcup_{k \ge 0} \mathrm{torsionPrimeProfile}(H_k(K;\mathbb{Z})).
\]
Then \(\operatorname{ATS}(K)\) is exactly the union of the prime divisors of the non-unit Smith diagonal entries arising from the boundary operators of the integral chain complex of \(K\).

This should be expressed either degreewise or globally.

### Lean 4 target signature

```lean
def arithmeticPhaseSignature (K : SimplicialComplex V) : Finset ℕ :=
  ((Finset.range (K.topDimension + 1)).biUnion fun k =>
    torsionPrimeProfile (simplicialHomology ℤ K k))

theorem arithmeticPhaseSignature_eq_biUnion_smithBoundaryPrimes
  (K : SimplicialComplex V) [Fintype V] [DecidableEq V] :
  arithmeticPhaseSignature K =
    ((Finset.range (K.topDimension + 1)).biUnion fun k =>
      smithBoundaryPrimeProfile K k)
```

If top dimension is unavailable, package a finite chain range as explicit data.

### Why this is a breakthrough

This theorem turns local SNF computations into a **global arithmetic fingerprint** of a dataset. It is the formal mathematical foundation for an arithmetic TDA pipeline: from simplicial data to a finite prime signature.

### Proof strategies

**Strategy A: Degreewise reduction**
1. Expand the definition of the global signature as a biUnion over degrees.
2. Apply Theorem 1 degreewise.
3. use `Finset.biUnion_congr` and extensionality.

**Strategy B: Chain-complex packaging**
1. Define a finite-support arithmetic signature of a bounded chain complex.
2. Specialize to simplicial chain complexes.
3. Prove simplicial compatibility by unfolding the chain construction.

**Most promising:** Strategy A, unless the chain complex API is already strong enough to make Strategy B elegant.

**Deep proof tactics expected:** induction on degree range, `rcases` on membership in unions, and substantial `calc` reasoning.

## Theorem 4: Complexity-preserving reduction from torsion profile to Smith computation

You should formalize at least a mathematically precise reduction theorem, even if asymptotic complexity is encoded abstractly rather than in a full machine model.

### Mathematical statement

If a procedure returns the Smith diagonal of the relevant boundary matrices, then the torsion prime profile can be computed by factoring only the non-unit diagonal entries; hence torsion profile computation reduces to Smith computation plus a logarithmic-factor arithmetic post-processing stage.

A formal theorem can avoid full asymptotic infrastructure by proving a **correct reduction**:
- input: Smith diagonal data,
- output: exact torsion profile,
- post-processing depends only on prime factors of diagonal entries.

### Lean 4 target signature

```lean
theorem computeArithmeticPhaseSignature_fromSmith_correct
  (K : SimplicialComplex V) [Fintype V] [DecidableEq V] :
  computeArithmeticPhaseSignatureFromSmith K =
    arithmeticPhaseSignature K
```

and, if you introduce a cost model:

```lean
theorem cost_computeArithmeticPhaseSignatureFromSmith_le
  (K : SimplicialComplex V) :
  cost (computeArithmeticPhaseSignatureFromSmith K) ≤
    cost (computeAllSmithDiagonals K) + postProcessCost K
```

### Why this matters

This is the theorem that justifies the conjectured scalability. Even if full \(O(N^\omega \log N)\) asymptotics remain partly external, a verified reduction theorem is the decisive backbone.

## Conjecture with Testable Prediction

### Main conjecture

For any finite simplicial complex \(K\) with \(N\) simplices, the full arithmetic phase signature
\[
\operatorname{ATS}(K)=\bigcup_k \mathrm{torsionPrimeProfile}(H_k(K;\mathbb{Z}))
\]
can be computed in time \(O(N^\omega \log N)\), where \(\omega\) is the matrix multiplication exponent.

Equivalently: arithmetic TDA is asymptotically no harder than linear-algebraic TDA, up to logarithmic overhead from integer factorization of SNF diagonal entries.

### Falsifiable computational test

Implement the algorithm on random Vietoris–Rips or flag complexes with sizes \(N=100,1000,10000\). Measure:
- wall-clock time for Betti-number computation,
- wall-clock time for full torsion profile computation,
- ratio against \(\log N\).

**Falsification criterion:** if, across controlled families, torsion profile computation is empirically more than \(O(\log N)\) slower than Betti-number computation beyond plausible constant-factor effects, the conjecture is false or the reduction is not practically realized.
