## Assignment: Spectral–Polynomial Transfer for the Apollonian Semigroup

Prove a genuinely new bridge theorem connecting Apollonian dynamics, polynomial observables on Descartes quadruples, and certified spectral transfer from discrete group expansion to concrete decay of low-degree moments. Do not settle for a vague “spectral gap exists” statement: formalize a finite-dimensional transfer principle for degree-`k` observables and make the Descartes quadratic form the organizing invariant.

This is not an incremental exercise. The goal is to turn the Apollonian gasket from a geometric curiosity into a formally verified laboratory for **thin-orbit dynamics, representation theory, and polynomial mixing**.

### Research Direction

The Apollonian gasket is generated by the four standard Descartes reflections on curvature quadruples. These are integer `4 × 4` matrices preserving the Descartes quadratic form
\[
Q(b_1,b_2,b_3,b_4) := 2\sum_{i=1}^4 b_i^2 - \left(\sum_{i=1}^4 b_i\right)^2.
\]
The orbit of a root quadruple under the Apollonian group lies on the light cone `Q = 0`, and the four generators define a thin semigroup inside an arithmetic orthogonal group of signature `(3,1)`.

Your mission is to define the action on polynomial observables of bounded total degree and prove that any spectral gap on the generator action induces a strict contraction on the orthogonal complement of invariant observables. Then package this as a transfer theorem usable for counting, equidistribution, and low-degree statistic decay.

### Precise Theorem Targets

You should aim for a theorem of the following shape.

#### Target A: Descartes invariance of the generators
Define the Descartes quadratic form matrix
\[
J := 2 I_4 - \mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top,
\]
so that `Q(v) = vᵀ J v`. For each Apollonian generator `S_i : Matrix (Fin 4) (Fin 4) ℤ`, prove:
\[
S_i^\top J S_i = J.
\]

**Lean 4 target signature**
```lean
theorem apollonian_generator_preserves_descartes
  (i : Fin 4) :
  let S : Matrix (Fin 4) (Fin 4) ℤ := apollonianGen i
  let J : Matrix (Fin 4) (Fin 4) ℤ := descartesMatrix
  Sᵀ * J * S = J
```

A stronger orbit-level corollary should also be formalized:

```lean
theorem apollonian_word_preserves_descartes
  (w : List (Fin 4)) (v : Fin 4 → ℤ) :
  descartesQ (applyWord w v) = descartesQ v
```

This is the algebraic bedrock. Without it, the whole representation-theoretic story floats.

#### Target B: Finite-dimensional polynomial observable space is preserved
Define degree-`≤ k` observables on curvature vectors `v : Fin 4 → ℤ` as integer- or rational-valued polynomial functions generated by monomials
\[
v \mapsto \prod_{j=1}^4 v_j^{a_j}, \qquad \sum a_j \le k.
\]
Show that precomposition by an Apollonian generator preserves this finite-dimensional space.

**Lean 4 target signature**

This theorem converts the infinite geometric dynamics into a finite-dimensional linear operator problem.

#### Target C: Spectral transfer to degree-`k` observables
Let
\[
\mathcal{A}_k := \{ \text{polynomial observables of total degree } \le k \}.
\]
Define the averaging operator
\[
T_k := \frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^4 \rho_k(S_i),
\]
where `ρ_k` is the induced linear action on `\mathcal A_k`.

Prove a transfer theorem of the form:

> If `T_k` has a spectral gap `γ > 0` on the complement of the invariant subspace, then for every centered observable `f ∈ \mathcal A_k`,
> \[
> \|T_k^n f\| \le (1-\gamma)^n \|f\|.
> \]

This can be stated abstractly in finite-dimensional Euclidean spaces first, then instantiated for Apollonian observables.

**Lean 4 target signature**
```lean
structure FiniteSpectralGap (V : Type _) [NormedAddCommGroup V] [NormedSpace ℝ V] where
  T : V →L[ℝ] V
  gap : ℝ
  gap_pos : 0 < gap
  contract :
    ∀ v, v ∈ invariantComplement T →
      ‖T v‖ ≤ (1 - gap) * ‖v‖

theorem spectral_transfer_iterate_bound
  (V : Type _) [NormedAddCommGroup V] [NormedSpace ℝ V]
  (g : FiniteSpectralGap V) :
  ∀ n : ℕ, ∀ v, v ∈ invariantComplement g.T →
    ‖(g.T ^ n) v‖ ≤ (1 - g.gap)^n * ‖v‖
```

Then specialize:

```lean
theorem apollonian_degree_k_mixing
  (k n : ℕ) :
  ∀ f : ObservableSubspace ℝ k,
    f ∈ centeredObservableSubspace ℝ k →
    ‖(apollonianAvgOp k)^n f‖ ≤
      (1 - apollonianGap k)^n * ‖f‖
```

You may need to weaken this if full operator-norm infrastructure is too heavy. A matrix-norm version on a chosen finite basis is completely acceptable and may be the best first breakthrough.

### Proof Strategy Paths

#### Strategy A: Direct matrix-algebraic route
1. Define the four standard Apollonian generators explicitly and verify `Sᵀ J S = J` by `fin_cases` + `native_decide`/`ring`.
2. Show each generator sends coordinate linear forms to linear combinations of coordinates; deduce monomials of degree `≤ k` map to polynomials of degree `≤ k`.
3. Build the averaging operator on a finite monomial basis and prove an iterate contraction theorem from an abstract matrix spectral-gap hypothesis.

**Why promising:** this is the most formalization-friendly path. It keeps everything finite and algebraic. It is likely the shortest route to a real theorem in Lean.

#### Strategy B: Orthogonal-group representation route
1. Package generators as elements of an integral orthogonal group preserving the Descartes form.
2. Define the induced representation on symmetric tensors / homogeneous polynomials.
3. Derive the degree-`k` transfer theorem as a finite-dimensional representation-theoretic corollary of a spectral gap assumption on the group action.

**Why promising:** conceptually deepest and most extensible. This is the right architecture if you want future formalizations of orbit counting, automorphic shadows, or harmonic analysis on thin groups.

**Risk:** Mathlib support for orthogonal-group representations may be incomplete; you may need to build significant infrastructure.

#### Strategy C: Combinatorial word-growth route
1. Avoid abstract spectral theory initially; define the action of words on degree-`k` observables.
2. Prove recurrence bounds on coefficient growth and identify invariant/centered subspaces.
3. Show exponential decay assuming a one-step contraction estimate already verified on basis vectors.

**Why promising:** if operator-theoretic abstractions get stuck, this route still yields a nontrivial concrete theorem on bounded-degree moments under random generator application.

**Recommendation:** start with Strategy A, then lift pieces into Strategy B where worthwhile. Strategy C is the fallback that can still produce publishable finite-dimensional mixing estimates.
