            **Title**: Combinatorial species to tropical valuation profiles via coefficientwise valuation inequalities
            **Description**: The key insight is that the existing combinatorial-species exponential generating function machinery can be pushed into a genuinely new Applications–Tropical bridge by proving coefficientwise valuation inequalities for species operations, turning additive and multiplicative EGF structure into tropical min-plus bounds on valuation profiles. Why now: the catalog already has a near-complete species foundation in `Applications/CombinatorialSpecies.lean` and a mature tropical valuation-object interface in `Bridges/CategoricalTropicalUltrametric.lean`, while the currently in-flight jobs target a functorial bridge at a higher categorical level; a sharper, self-contained arithmetic core about valuations of EGF coefficients is different, more concrete, and can serve as the theorem package those broader programs need. The proposed objective is to formalize a precise theorem family of the following kind: for integer-valued coefficient sequences arising from species EGFs, the p-adic valuation profile of coefficientwise sums satisfies a tropical lower bound by coefficientwise minima, and the valuation profile of Cauchy/binomial convolutions induced by species product satisfies a tropical convolution lower bound. This should be instantiated first for the basic constructors already present in the species file—constant species, sum, product, and linear orders—so that one obtains an algorithmic pipeline sending a species expression to a computable tropical valuation profile certificate. The mathematical novelty is not merely to restate `v_p(a+b) >= min(v_p(a),v_p(b))`, but to transport that inequality through the specific EGF/binomial-convolution formulas of species and isolate a reusable certificate theorem for coefficient growth and divisibility. If successful, this creates the first concrete bridge from Applications to Tropical suggested by the catalog’s structural-opportunity analysis, and it can later feed cryptographic or asymptotic applications without duplicating the in-flight categorical work.

# Combinatorial Species as Functors and the Exponential Generating Function Bridge

This file formalizes a fragment of Joyal's theory of **combinatorial species** and the
classical bridge to **analytic functors / exponential generating functions (EGF)**.

A species is modeled (in skeletal form) as a functor from the *groupoid of finite sets*
to finite sets: a family `obj : ℕ → Type` of finite "structure types", together with a
functorial action of the symmetric group `Equiv.Perm (Fin n)` (relabelling) on each
`obj n`.  Its EGF is the formal power series

  `EGF F = ∑ₙ (|F[n]| / n!) Xⁿ`.

The central enumerative-combinatorics ↔ analysis dictionary established here is:

* **sum of species ↔ sum of EGFs**            (`egf_add`)
* **product of species ↔ product of EGFs**    (`egf_mul`, `egf_card_prodSpecies`)
* **species of sets `E` ↔ `exp`**             (`EGF_setSpecies`)
* **species of linear orders `L` ↔ 1/(1-X)**  (`egf_linearOrderSpecies`)

The product law is the heart of the bridge: the *structural* product of species (the
Day-convolution `(F·G)[n] = Σ_{S ⊆ [n]} F[S] × G[n∖S]`) has cardinality the **binomial
convolution** of the counting sequences, which is exactly the Cauchy product of the EGFs.

## Main results
* `egf_add`              — additivity of the EGF.
* `egf_mul`              — binomial convolution of counting sequences ↔ product of EGFs.
* `EGF_setSpecies`       — EGF of the species of sets equals `PowerSeries.exp ℚ`.
* `egf_linearOrderSpecies` — `(1 - X) · EGF(L) = 1`, i.e. EGF of linear orders is `1/(1-X)`.
* `card_prodSpecies`     — cardinality of the structural product is the binomial convolution.
* `egf_card_prodSpecies` — the full bridge: EGF of the structural product = product of EGFs.

### Deepening — the differential calculus of species (this cycle)
* `egf_injective`         — the EGF transform is injective on counting sequences.
* `binConv_comm`          — commutativity of the species product, via the analytic shadow.
* `egf_derivative`        — shift of a sequence ↔ formal derivative `derivativeFun`.
* `egf_pointing`          — multiplication by the index ↔ Euler operator `X·d/dX`.
* `EGF_derivativeSpecies` — `(F′).EGF = (F.EGF).derivativeFun` for the derivative species `F′`.
* `EGF_pointedSpecies`    — `(F•).EGF = X · (F.EGF).derivativeFun` for the pointed species `F•`.
