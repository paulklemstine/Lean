### The Deep Insight

Classical Hodge theory identifies the kernel of the Laplacian with the first homology: ker(Δ) ≅ H₁. This assignment proves the **tropical analogue**: the tropical kernel of the induced Laplacian on S is naturally isomorphic to the **relative tropical homology** of the pair (G[S∪{q}], {q}). This is not analogy—it is a structural theorem that makes the tropical Hodge decomposition theorem into a genuine homological statement, opening the door to tropical sheaf cohomology, tropical Poincaré duality, and computational topology over semirings.

### Definitions (Novel — Not in Catalog)

**Definition 1: Tropical Chain Complex of a Graph.** For a finite graph G = (V, E) with incidence matrix B (|V| × |E|), define the tropical chain groups:
- C₀^trop(G) := (Fin |V|) → WithTop ℕ (tropical 0-chains, i.e., functions on vertices)
- C₁^trop(G) := (Fin |E|) → WithTop ℕ (tropical 1-chains, i.e., functions on edges)

The tropical boundary map ∂₀^trop : C₁^trop(G) → C₀^trop(G) is defined by:
(∂₀^trop(φ))(v) = ⊕_{e incident to v} φ(e)

where ⊕ is tropical addition (min). This is the min-plus matrix-vector product ∂₀^trop(φ) = B ⊗_trop φ.

**Lean 4 signature:**
```lean
def tropicalBoundary {n m : ℕ} (B : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin m) ℕ) :
    (Fin m → WithTop ℕ) → (Fin n → WithTop ℕ) :=
  fun φ i => (Finset.univ : Finset (Fin m)).inf fun j => max (B i j) (φ j)

-- where max corresponds to + in the tropical semiring and inf to min (tropical addition)
```

**Definition 2: Relative Tropical Homology.** For a graph G and subgraph A (with A ⊆ G), the relative tropical homology is:
H₁^trop(G, A) := ker(∂₀^trop |_{C₁^trop(G,A)}) / im(∂₀^trop |_{C₀^trop(G,A)})

where C₁^trop(G, A) consists of tropical 1-chains vanishing on edges of A, and C₀^trop(G, A) consists of tropical 0-chains vanishing on vertices of A. The quotient is in the tropical sense: the tropical submodule generated by im(∂₀^trop) within ker(∂₀^trop).

**Definition 3: Tropical Semi-Module Isomorphism.** A tropical semi-module isomorphism φ : M₁ ≅ₜ M₂ between two tropical submodules of (WithTop ℕ)^n is a bijection that preserves tropical addition (φ(x ⊕ y) = φ(x) ⊕ φ(y)) and tropical scalar multiplication (φ(c ⊗ x) = c ⊗ φ(x)).

### Proof Strategies

**Strategy A: Direct Factorization (Most Promising).**
1. Prove tropical incidence factorization (Theorem 1): Show L_S = B_Sᵀ ⊗_trop B_S by expanding the min-plus matrix product entry-by-entry. Each entry (L_S)_{ij} is the minimum over edges e of max(B_{ie}, B_{je}), which equals the tropical path distance between i and j through q.
2. Apply the tropical first isomorphism theorem: The tropical kernel of B_S ⊗_trop · maps onto the tropical kernel of L_S ⊗_trop · via the map v ↦ B_Sᵀ ⊗_trop v.
3. Identify the image: Show that ker_trop(B_S ⊗_trop ·) consists exactly of tropical 1-cycles (elements of ker(∂₀^trop)), and that im(B_Sᵀ ⊗_trop ·) consists exactly of tropical boundaries from relative 0-chains. This gives ker_trop(L_S) ≅ ker(∂₀^trop) / im(∂₀^trop) = H₁^trop.

*Why promising:* This mirrors the classical proof of ker(Δ) ≅ H₁ and makes the tropical semiring structure explicit at each step. The key technical lemma is the tropical first isomorphism theorem, which can be established by constructing the quotient tropical module explicitly.

**Strategy B: Explicit Basis Correspondence.**
1. Compute explicit generators for ker_trop(L_S) using the existing catalog theorems on tropical kernel structure (cycle generators + component generators).
2. Compute explicit generators for H₁^trop(G[S∪{q}], {q}): cycle generators correspond to fundamental cycles of G[S], and relative generators correspond to paths from q to each connected component of G[S].
3. Construct the isomorphism by mapping each cycle generator to the corresponding tropical 1-cycle, and each component generator to the corresponding relative 1-boundary. Verify that this preserves tropical addition and scalar multiplication.

*Why viable:* This is the most constructive approach and directly leverages the catalog's existing decomposition of tropical kernels. However, it requires careful bookkeeping of the basis correspondence.

**Strategy C: Functorial/Natural Proof.**
1. Show that both ker_trop(L_·) and H₁^trop(·, {q}) are functors from the category of pairs (G, q) with morphisms being graph inclusions.
2. Prove that the conjectured isomorphism is a natural transformation between these functors.
3. Verify the isomorphism on the "generating" objects (trees, single cycles, and wedge sums thereof) and use naturality to extend to all graphs.

*Why ambitious:* This would be the most structural proof and would immediately yield functoriality of the Hodge decomposition. However, the category-theoretic infrastructure for tropical functors needs to be built first.

**Recommended approach:** Start with Strategy A for the core isomorphism (Theorem 2), use Strategy B to establish the explicit basis correspondence needed for the dimension formula in Theorem 3, and note that Strategy C provides the natural next step for future work.

### Conjecture with Testable Prediction

**Conjecture (Tropical Poincaré Duality for Graphs).** For any connected graph G with n vertices and m edges, and any vertex q:

β₀^trop(G, {q}) + β₁^trop(G, {q}) = m − n + 2

where β₀^trop(G, {q}) is the dimension of H₀^trop(G, {q}) and β₁^trop(G, {q}) is the dimension of H₁^trop(G, {q}).

**Computational test:** For all connected graphs on n ≤ 7 vertices, compute:
1. The tropical Betti numbers β₀^trop and β₁^trop using the tropical chain complex.
2. The quantity m − n + 2.
3. Verify β₀^trop + β₁^trop = m − n + 2 in all cases.
4. A single counterexample disproves the conjecture.

This is the tropical analogue of the Euler-Poincaré formula χ(G) = n − m, reformulated in terms of tropical Betti numbers. If true, it provides the first instance of Poincaré duality in tropical homological algebra.
