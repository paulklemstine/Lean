# Research Direction

## I. Degree-Growth Rigidity for Tame Keller Maps

### Breakthrough target
The central theorem should not merely assert existence of inverses for tame maps; it should quantify inverse complexity with a sharp algebraic bound that mirrors the global degree bound expected in the Jacobian Conjecture world.

### Precise theorem statement
Let `k` be a field, `n : ℕ`, and let `F : (Fin n → k[X Fin n]) →ₐ[k] (Fin n → k[X Fin n])` be the polynomial self-map associated to a tame automorphism, expressed as a finite composition of affine and elementary automorphisms. Define `polyDegreeMap F : ℕ` as the maximum total degree of its coordinate polynomials.

Prove:

> **Theorem (tame inverse degree bound).**  
> For every tame polynomial automorphism `F : k^n → k^n`, if `deg(F) = d`, then  
> `deg(F⁻¹) ≤ d^(n-1)`.

This should be formalized first for triangular/elementary compositions, then lifted to the tame class.

### Suggested Lean 4 type signature
A plausible target, to be adapted to the exact existing API:

```lean
theorem degree_inverse_le_pow_pred
    {k : Type*} [Field k]
    {n : ℕ}
    (F : PolyMap k n)
    (hF_tame : IsTameAutomorphism F) :
    polyMapDegree F.inverse ≤ (polyMapDegree F) ^ (n - 1)
```

A stronger intermediate theorem likely more tractable:

```lean
theorem degree_inverse_le_prod_elementary_degrees
    {k : Type*} [Field k]
    {n : ℕ}
    (L : List (PolyMap k n))
    (hL : ∀ G ∈ L, IsElementaryOrAffine G)
    (hcomp : IsPolyAuto (L.foldr PolyMap.comp PolyMap.id)) :
    polyMapDegree (IsPolyAuto.inv (L.foldr PolyMap.comp PolyMap.id))
      ≤ ∏ G in L, polyMapDegree G
```

and then derive the `d^(n-1)` bound by proving that only at most `n-1` coordinates can accumulate degree growth in the inverse of a tame composition after suitable normalization/conjugation.

### Why this is a breakthrough
This would convert the tame Jacobian landscape from existential algebra into complexity geometry. A formal degree bound on inverses gives:
- certified complexity bounds for symbolic inversion,
- a measurable notion of “distance from wildness,”
- a bridge from affine algebraic geometry to algebraic complexity theory,
- a possible template for separating tame from wild automorphisms by growth signatures.

This is exactly the kind of theorem that turns reduction theory into computable mathematics.

### Proof strategies

#### Strategy A: Triangular-factor induction via reversed composition
1. Formalize that the inverse of an affine or elementary map has the same degree.
2. Show that for a composition of triangular/elementary factors, inverse degree is bounded by the product of factor degrees by reversing the composition.
3. Prove a compression lemma: after conjugating a tame map into a suitable coordinate-adapted form, the product of factor degrees is bounded by `deg(F)^(n-1)`.

**Why promising:** This is closest to the existing infrastructure and should require the least new API.

#### Strategy B: Degree filtration on coordinate algebra
1. Define a filtration on `k[x₁,…,xₙ]` by total degree.
2. Show tame automorphisms act upper-triangularly on the associated graded under a suitable variable ordering.
3. Extract the inverse degree bound from the graded action, proving that at most `n-1` layers contribute multiplicatively.

**Why promising:** Conceptually deeper and may produce stronger invariants, such as monotone degree profiles rather than a single scalar degree.

#### Strategy C: Bass–Connell–Wright style recursive coordinate elimination
1. Prove a recursive inversion formula for elementary extensions.
2. Track degree growth coordinate-by-coordinate.
3. Establish that each elimination step contributes at most one multiplicative factor of `deg(F)`.

**Why promising:** This could yield an executable inverse algorithm in Lean, not just a bound.

**Most promising:** Strategy A first, then Strategy B if you want a field-opening generalization. A successful Strategy B could lead to a formal “degree spectrum” invariant for automorphisms.

### Cross-domain connections
- **Algebraic complexity theory:** inverse degree as circuit blowup measure.
- **Dynamical systems:** tame automorphisms as discrete polynomial flows with entropy-like degree growth.
- **Rewriting systems / compiler theory:** inversion by reversed elementary instructions.
- **Tropical geometry:** degree growth shadows tropical Lipschitz constants of piecewise-linearizations.

## II. Computational Nilpotence Detection for Cubic Jacobians

### Breakthrough target
Turn the Keller condition for cubic homogeneous perturbations of the identity into a finite nilpotence certificate. This would replace determinant computation by a matrix-power vanishing criterion, radically simplifying formal and computational verification.

### Precise theorem statement
Let `F = I + H`, where `H : k^n → k^n` is cubic homogeneous over a characteristic-zero field. Write `JH` for the Jacobian matrix of `H`.

Target theorem:

> **Theorem (Keller implies nilpotence for cubic homogeneous maps, low-dimensional formal regime).**  
> For `n ≤ N` (start with `N = 4`, then push upward), if `det(I + JH) = 1`, then `(JH)^n = 0`.

Sharper target:

> **Theorem (compressed nilpotence index).**  
> Under the same assumptions, `(JH)^(⌈n/2⌉ + 1) = 0`.

Do not overspeculate globally if the library is not ready; prove dimension-bounded versions first, but state the full conjectural program.

### Suggested Lean 4 type signature
A realistic finite-dimensional target:

```lean
theorem cubic_keller_implies_jacobian_nilpotent
    {k : Type*} [Field k] [CharZero k]
    {n : ℕ}
    (hn : n ≤ 4)
    (H : CubicHomogeneousMap k n)
    (hKeller : Matrix.det (1 + jacobianMatrix H) = 1) :
    (jacobianMatrix H) ^ n = 0
```

Sharper version:

```lean
theorem cubic_keller_implies_jacobian_nilpotent_sharp
    {k : Type*} [Field k] [CharZero k]
    {n : ℕ}
    (hn : n ≤ 4)
    (H : CubicHomogeneousMap k n)
    (hKeller : Matrix.det (1 + jacobianMatrix H) = 1) :
    (jacobianMatrix H) ^ (n / 2 + 1) = 0
```

If determinant-on-polynomial-ring is awkward, use coefficientwise equality of characteristic polynomial or trace identities.

### Why this is a breakthrough
This would transmute a nonlinear determinant constraint into a finite linear-algebraic vanishing test. If formalized even in small dimensions, it creates:
- a machine-checkable filter for candidate Keller maps,
- a route to exhaustive low-dimensional verification,
- a bridge between Jacobian conjecture reductions and nilpotent Lie theory,
- a new computational language for cubic maps.

This is not just a theorem; it is a compression principle.

### Proof strategies

#### Strategy A: Characteristic polynomial / trace identities
1. Use `det(I + t JH) = 1` for all scalar `t` induced by homogeneity.
2. Extract vanishing of all elementary symmetric functions in eigenvalues of `JH`.
3. Deduce nilpotence via Cayley–Hamilton.

**Why promising:** This is the mathematically canonical route and aligns with matrix identities already likely available in Mathlib.

#### Strategy B: Graded homogeneity and divergence recursion
1. Use cubic homogeneity to control the degree of entries of `JH`.
2. Show the Keller condition forces vanishing traces `tr((JH)^m)` for all relevant `m`.
3. In characteristic zero, conclude nilpotence from Newton identities.

**Why promising:** More flexible than determinant expansion and may generalize to higher-degree homogeneous perturbations.

#### Strategy C: Low-dimensional normal forms
1. Classify cubic homogeneous maps up to linear conjugacy for `n ≤ 3` or `4`.
2. Reduce Keller to a finite set of normal forms.
3. Verify nilpotence case-by-case, then package as a formal theorem.

**Why promising:** Best if symbolic determinant manipulation becomes too heavy in Lean.

**Most promising:** Strategy A for the conceptual theorem, Strategy C as fallback for initial formal victories.

### Cross-domain connections
- **Lie theory:** nilpotent Jacobians suggest exponential/unipotent structures.
- **Control theory:** nilpotence as finite-time truncation of nonlinear feedback.
- **Computational algebra:** determinant elimination in Gröbner-style workflows.
- **Mathematical physics:** nilpotent operators as algebraic analogues of constrained gauge generators.

### Application keywords
`Keller condition`, `nilpotent Jacobian`, `cubic homogeneous map`, `Cayley-Hamilton`, `trace identities`, `symbolic verification`, `computer-assisted proof`

## III. Stable-Equivalence Complexity Collapse to Drużkowski Form

### Breakthrough target
Do not merely restate Bass–Connell–Wright reduction. Sharpen it quantitatively. The real theorem is about dimensional blowup complexity under stable equivalence.

### Precise theorem statement
For cubic homogeneous Keller maps `F` in dimension `n`, prove that after stable lifting and affine conjugation, `F` becomes equivalent to a Drużkowski map in dimension bounded linearly in `n`, ideally `≤ 2n`.

> **Theorem (linear stable reduction to Drużkowski form).**  
> Every cubic homogeneous Keller map in dimension `n` is stably equivalent to a Drużkowski map in dimension at most `2n`.

Start with explicit formal statements in dimensions `2` and `3`, then isolate the general reduction lemmas.

### Suggested Lean 4 type signature
A likely target:

```lean
theorem cubic_keller_stably_equiv_druzkowski_bound
    {k : Type*} [Field k] [CharZero k]
    {n : ℕ}
    (F : PolyMap k n)
    (hF_cubic : IsCubicHomogeneous F)
    (hF_keller : IsKellerMap F) :
    ∃ m : ℕ, m ≤ 2 * n ∧
      StablyEquivalent F (someDruzkowskiMap k m)
```

For low-dimensional constructive versions:

```lean
theorem cubic_keller_dim3_stably_equiv_druzkowski
    {k : Type*} [Field k] [CharZero k]
    (F : PolyMap k 3)
    (hF_cubic : IsCubicHomogeneous F)
    (hF_keller : IsKellerMap F) :
    ∃ m : ℕ, m ≤ 6 ∧ StablyEquivalent F (someDruzkowskiMap k m)
```

### Why this is a breakthrough
A linear dimension bound would convert an abstract reduction principle into a complexity theorem. It would mean:
- the Jacobian Conjecture can be funneled into a sharply controlled search space,
- stable equivalence acquires a quantitative complexity theory,
- formal verification becomes feasible for bounded-dimension fragments,
- the reduction machinery itself becomes an object of study.

This is the difference between “reducible in principle” and “reducible with a measured computational budget.”

### Proof strategies

#### Strategy A: Explicit rank-factorization of the cubic tensor
1. Represent the cubic homogeneous part via symmetric trilinear data.
2. Factor it into sums of cubes of linear forms.
3. Introduce one auxiliary variable per rank component, then optimize the count to `≤ 2n`.

**Why promising:** This directly exposes where new variables are spent.

#### Strategy B: Formalize the BCW reduction with dimension accounting
1. Reconstruct each stable-lift and affine-conjugation step in Lean.
2. Attach a numerical invariant tracking ambient dimension.
3. Prove each reduction step increases dimension by at most a controlled amount, summing to `≤ 2n`.

**Why promising:** Most faithful to the classical literature and best aligned with `stableLift`.

#### Strategy C: Nilpotent Jacobian compression first
1. Use the nilpotence theorem above to place `JH` into a constrained normal form.
2. Show such constrained cubic maps admit Drużkowski realization with fewer auxiliary variables.
3. Deduce the linear stable bound.

**Why promising:** This would create a deep synthesis between the second and third directions, potentially giving stronger-than-classical reductions.

**Most promising:** Strategy B for formalization velocity; Strategy C if the nilpotence machinery lands.

### Cross-domain connections
- **Tensor rank / complexity theory:** Drużkowski reduction as structured tensor compression.
- **Category theory:** stable equivalence as a controlled enlargement in a localization of polynomial automorphisms.
- **Proof complexity:** reduction length and auxiliary-variable count as formal proof invariants.
- **Algorithm design:** bounded blowup means bounded state-space for exhaustive search.

### Application keywords
`stable equivalence`, `Drużkowski map`, `Bass-Connell-Wright reduction`, `dimension blowup`, `tensor rank`, `formal reduction complexity`
