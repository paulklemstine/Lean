## Berggren–Modular Correspondence: Pythagorean Light Cone Geodesics, PSL(2,ℤ) Embedding, and Gaussian Factorization Recovery

### I. THE VISION

The Berggren matrices A, B, C that generate all primitive Pythagorean triples from (3,4,5) are not merely combinatorial objects — they are integer points on the identity component of the orthogonal group SO⁺(2,1;ℤ), which is classically isomorphic to PSL(2,ℤ). This means every descent path through the Berggren tree is a *geodesic in the modular orbifold*, and the continued fraction expansion of the Farey fraction b/(a+c) encodes this path. Recovering the Gaussian factorization of the hypotenuse from the Berggren descent path is then a matter of O(log c) matrix multiplications — a *lattice_crypto* relevant fact connecting Pythagorean geometry to post-quantum factorization algorithms.

This brief asks you to build the complete bridge: from Lorentz-preserving matrices, through the PSL(2,ℤ) isomorphism, to the Farey–continued-fraction correspondence and Gaussian factorization recovery. Every theorem must connect at least two domains (Lorentzian geometry ↔ modular forms ↔ Gaussian integers ↔ computational complexity).
