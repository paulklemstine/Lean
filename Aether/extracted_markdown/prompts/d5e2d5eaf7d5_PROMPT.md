## Precise Target Theorem

Define a finite category of **closure-temporal idempotent semimodules**:

- `M` is a finitely generated semimodule over an idempotent semiring `R`,
- `≤` is the natural order induced by idempotent addition,
- `cl : M → M` is a closure operator: monotone, extensive, idempotent,
- `T : M → M` is a monotone temporal operator preserving closed elements, i.e.
  `IsClosed x := cl x = x`, and `IsClosed x → IsClosed (T x)`.

Define the **finite Priestley spectrum**
`Spec_pr(M)` as the type of prime order-congruences on `M`, equipped with:
- the Priestley topology generated by basic clopen up-sets induced by semimodule predicates,
- the specialization order,
- a temporal relation `R_T` induced by pullback of observables along `T`.

### Main theorem package

You should aim to formalize a theorem of the following shape:

1. **Finite Priestley representation**
   Every finite closure-temporal semimodule `M` determines a finite Priestley relational space
   `X(M) = (Spec_pr(M), τ, ≤, R_T)`.

2. **Contravariant duality**
   Semimodule morphisms preserving `cl` and `T` induce continuous order-preserving relational maps in the opposite direction, and conversely every such map arises from pullback on clopen up-sets stable under temporal structure.

3. **Reconstruction**
   If `M` is finitely generated and residually order-separated by clopen up-set observables, then `M` is isomorphic to the semimodule of temporal-stable clopen up-sets of `X(M)`.

4. **Certified minimal realization**
   For any finite ordered observation structure satisfying the Priestley-temporal axioms, the quotient by observational equivalence yields the **unique minimal** closure-temporal semimodule realizing the observations.

This should be stated in Lean as a cluster of definitions plus one or two capstone theorems.

## Proof architecture: 3 viable strategies

### Strategy A: Finite lattice-of-observables route
Most promising.

1. Define the algebra of **clopen up-sets stable under closure/temporal action** on the finite spectrum.
2. Show that evaluation `x ↦ { p | predicate(x) holds at p }` embeds `M` into this algebra.
3. Use residual order-separation to prove surjectivity, yielding reconstruction.
4. Prove minimality by identifying observational equivalence with equality of all stable clopen up-set observables.

Why this is promising:
- It localizes the difficult semantics into a finite distributive-lattice/Priestley layer.
- It is closest in spirit to classical Priestley duality, hence structurally robust.
- It should interact well with finite `Fintype` enumeration in Lean.

### Strategy B: Prime congruence quotient route
Potentially elegant, but definition-heavy.

1. Define prime order-congruences and prove existence/separation in the finite setting.
2. Build the spectrum functor directly from these congruences.
3. Show every element of `M` is determined by its truth set across prime order-congruences.
4. Characterize observational equivalence as intersection of all prime order-congruences.

Why it matters:
- This is the conceptually pure “ordered algebraic geometry” route.
- It gives the strongest foundational theorem and the cleanest statement of uniqueness/minimality.
- It could become a reusable Mathlib-scale framework for ordered semiring spectra.

Risk:
- Prime order-congruence definitions may be technically heavier than needed for the first milestone.

### Strategy C: Coalgebraic minimization via temporal observations
Best if you want algorithmic minimality first.

1. Treat `T` as a monotone transition semantics on closed states/observables.
2. Define observational equivalence by agreement on all finite monotone temporal formulas or stable predicates.
3. Prove the quotient carries a canonical closure-temporal semimodule structure.
4. Recover the Priestley space afterward as the dual of the quotient’s observable lattice.

Why this is attractive:
- It makes the minimization algorithm immediate and EML-relevant.
- It aligns with automata minimization, bisimulation collapse, and temporal logic semantics.
- It may let you prove the computationally strongest theorem first, then derive duality.

Recommended order:
- **Start with A**, because it gives a finite constructive duality core.
- Borrow the quotient/minimality punchline from **C**.
- Introduce the prime order-congruence language from **B** once the representation is stable.
