## Core Mathematical Vision

Let `μ` be a probability mass function on `{0,1}^n`, with multiaffine homogeneous generating polynomial
\[
g_\mu(z_1,\dots,z_n)=\sum_{S\subseteq [n]} \mu(S)\prod_{i\in S} z_i.
\]
Assume `g_μ` is strongly log-concave / Lorentzian with a certified spectral gap parameter `ε > 0` in the sense encoded by the catalog. Let `ν` be another measure with coefficient perturbation bounded by `δ`.

The goal is to prove that if `δ < C ε` for an explicit `C`, then:

1. `g_ν` remains Lorentzian / strongly log-concave;
2. the local exchange or Glauber-type Markov chain on `supp ν` inherits an explicit lower bound on functional inequality constants;
3. hence the chain has an explicit mixing time bound depending polynomially/logarithmically on `ε`, `δ`, and the state-space size.

This would create a formal pipeline:

\[
\text{Lorentzian gap} \Rightarrow \text{robust negative dependence} \Rightarrow \text{entropy/spectral contraction} \Rightarrow \text{mixing time certificate}.
\]

## Theorem 1: Quantitative Lorentzian Persistence Under Coefficient Noise

### Mathematical statement
Prove an explicit quantitative strengthening of the catalog’s existential stability radius.

A model statement:

> **Theorem (robust Lorentzian persistence).**  
> Let `μ` be a probability distribution on `Finset α` with homogeneous multiaffine generating polynomial `g_μ`. Assume `g_μ` is Lorentzian with certified gap `ε > 0`. Then there exists an explicit constant `C_μ > 0` such that for every distribution `ν`,
> \[
> \sum_{S} |\nu(S)-\mu(S)| < C_\mu \, \varepsilon
> \]
> implies `g_ν` is Lorentzian. Moreover, one may take `C_μ` to be computable from the Hessian-gap inequalities furnished by `reversed_cauchy_schwarz_of_gapped`.

A Lean-oriented signature sketch:

```lean
theorem lorentzian_persists_of_coeffDist_lt
    {σ : Type*} [DecidableEq σ] [Fintype σ]
    (R : RobustLorentzianData σ)
    (ν : Finset σ → ℝ)
    (hν_nonneg : ∀ S, 0 ≤ ν S)
    (hν_mass : (∑ S in Finset.powerset Finset.univ, ν S) = 1)
    (C : ℝ)
    (hC : 0 < C)
    (hnoise : coeffDist R.coeff ν < C * R.gap)
    (hCsmall : C ≤ explicitLorentzianConstant R) :
    IsLorentzianGeneratingFamily ν
```

You may need to define `IsLorentzianGeneratingFamily` in the exact representation best suited to Mathlib and the catalog.

### Why this is a breakthrough
The catalog gives existence of a stability radius. That is only the seed. The field-opening theorem is the conversion of this existence statement into a **usable perturbation certificate** for noisy data. In applications, one does not know whether a model is exactly Lorentzian; one knows only an estimated generating function. This theorem turns algebraic structure into a robust certification tool.

### Proof strategy options

**Strategy A: Direct reduction to the catalog radius theorem**
1. Use `lorentzian_stability_radius_exists` to obtain a certified radius `r > 0`.
2. Show that your `coeffDist` control implies the coefficient perturbation lies inside the catalog’s admissible neighborhood.
3. Transfer Lorentzianity of `μ` to `ν`.

Why promising: shortest path if the catalog theorem is already phrased in coefficient-norm language or can be bridged.

**Strategy B: Gap-preserving Hessian inequalities**
1. Translate the gap condition into reversed Cauchy–Schwarz inequalities on directional derivatives using `reversed_cauchy_schwarz_of_gapped`.
2. Show these inequalities are stable under sufficiently small coefficient perturbations by bounding the perturbation of derivative forms.
3. Deduce the perturbed polynomial remains in the Lorentzian cone.

Why promising: gives explicit constants and is conceptually deeper; likely the right route if you want quantitative downstream mixing estimates.

**Strategy C: Compactness/contradiction plus quantitative extraction**
1. Assume no explicit `C` works and construct a sequence `ν_k → μ` violating Lorentzianity.
2. Use closure properties of the Lorentzian cone and continuity of the gap witnesses to contradict the strict gap of `μ`.
3. Then extract a computable lower bound by tracing moduli of continuity in the finite-dimensional coefficient space.

Why promising: robust if direct algebraic manipulation is awkward; useful for a first formal proof, then sharpen later.

**Most promising:** Strategy B, because the same derivative/Hessian estimates can later feed directly into entropy contraction and chain analysis.

## Theorem 2: Robust Preservation of a Quantitative Negative Dependence / Rayleigh-Type Inequality

This theorem should be your bridge from algebraic geometry to probabilistic combinatorics.

### Mathematical statement
For coordinates `i ≠ j`, prove that small coefficient perturbations preserve a quantitative pairwise negative dependence inequality.

A representative statement:

> **Theorem (robust Rayleigh inequality).**  
> Under the assumptions of Theorem 1, there exists `κ = κ(μ, ε, δ) > 0` such that for all distinct coordinates `i,j`,
> \[
> \partial_i g_\nu(x)\,\partial_j g_\nu(x) - g_\nu(x)\,\partial_{ij} g_\nu(x) \ge \kappa
> \]
> for all `x` in the positive orthant normalized to a compact simplex region. In particular, the perturbed distribution `ν` remains quantitatively negatively dependent.

Lean-style sketch:

```lean
theorem robust_rayleigh_of_noisy_lorentzian
    {σ : Type*} [DecidableEq σ] [Fintype σ]
    (R : RobustLorentzianData σ)
    (ν : Finset σ → ℝ)
    (hν : IsNoisyPerturbation R ν C)
    (hi : i ∈ Finset.univ) (hj : j ∈ Finset.univ) (hij : i ≠ j) :
    ∃ κ > 0, ∀ x : σ → ℝ, x ∈ positiveSimplexRegion →
      κ ≤ rayleighExpression ν i j x
```

You may need to restrict to a compact positive simplex such as `∑ x_i = 1`, `0 < x_i`, to avoid scaling issues.

### Why this matters
The robust Rayleigh inequality is the **probabilistic shadow** of Lorentzianity. It is the exact kind of statement that can be fed into coupling, spectral comparison, and entropy contraction arguments. It also makes the theory legible to probabilists and statistical physicists.

### Proof strategy options

**Strategy A: Differentiate the quantitative Lorentzian inequalities**
1. Start from the Hessian-signature or reversed Cauchy–Schwarz inequality available from the gap.
2. Differentiate/instantiate on coordinate directions.
3. Use perturbation bounds to preserve strict positivity margin.

**Strategy B: Induction on degree/rank**
1. Show first derivatives of a Lorentzian polynomial are Lorentzian.
2. Use the catalog gap theorem recursively on derivatives.
3. Deduce pairwise inequalities for all lower-order minors, then lift back.

This gives a deep proof using induction, satisfying the depth requirement.

**Strategy C: Compactness on the simplex**
1. Show the Rayleigh expression for `μ` is continuous and strictly positive on a compact normalized domain.
2. Hence it has a positive minimum.
3. Bound the perturbation of the expression under coefficient noise to preserve positivity.

Most promising for formalization: **Strategy C** if you can establish continuity cleanly in finite dimensions. Most mathematically powerful: **Strategy A/B**.

## Theorem 3: Explicit Mixing-Time Bound for a Natural Markov Chain on the Perturbed Distribution

This is the theorem that makes the project paradigm-shifting.

### Mathematical statement
Define a natural Markov chain on the support of `ν` — for example, a single-site flip chain or exchange walk preserving the relevant support class. Then prove an explicit total variation mixing bound.

A representative statement:

> **Theorem (robust mixing certificate).**  
> Let `ν` satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem 1, and let `P_ν` be the reversible single-site Glauber chain (or exchange chain) with stationary law `ν`. Then there exist explicit constants `a,b > 0` such that the spectral gap and mixing time satisfy
> \[
> \operatorname{gap}(P_\nu) \ge a(\varepsilon - b\delta),
> \qquad
> t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\eta) \le \frac{1}{a(\varepsilon-b\delta)}
> \log\!\left(\frac{1}{\eta\,\nu_{\min}}\right),
> \]
> whenever `δ < ε/b`.

Lean-style theorem sketch:

```lean
theorem mixing_time_bound_of_noisy_strongly_log_concave
    {σ : Type*} [DecidableEq σ] [Fintype σ]
    (R : RobustLorentzianData σ)
    (ν : Finset σ → ℝ)
    (P : MarkovKernel (Finset σ) (Finset σ))
    (hstat : IsStationary ν P)
    (hreversible : IsReversible ν P)
    (hlocal : IsSingleSiteGlauber ν P ∨ IsExchangeWalk ν P)
    (hnoise : coeffDist R.coeff ν < explicitMixingRadius R) :
    ∃ γ > 0, spectralGap P ≥ γ ∧
      ∀ η : ℝ, 0 < η → mixingTimeTV P η ≤
        (Real.log ((νmin ν)⁻¹ / η)) / γ
```

If a full general theorem about Markov kernels is too heavy for the present library, prove a specialized finite-state statement for a concrete chain you define from scratch. A fully verified computational bound for a concrete chain is better than an abstract theorem that never closes.

### Why this is revolutionary
This theorem would be one of the first formalized results certifying that **approximate strong log-concavity is enough for efficient sampling**. That is exactly the regime practitioners face in machine learning and statistical physics: models are estimated, noisy, truncated, or numerically perturbed. A theorem of this form says that algorithmic tractability is not a knife-edge phenomenon.

### Proof strategy options

**Strategy A: Spectral comparison with the unperturbed chain**
1. Define corresponding chains `P_μ` and `P_ν`.
2. Show transition probabilities differ by at most `O(δ)` in operator norm or Dirichlet form.
3. Transfer the spectral gap lower bound from `P_μ` to `P_ν` using perturbation inequalities.

Why promising: likely the cleanest if you can define the chain explicitly enough.

**Strategy B: Modified log-Sobolev route**
1. Use robust negative dependence / Lorentzianity to derive a lower bound on an entropy contraction constant.
2. Deduce a modified log-Sobolev inequality.
3. Convert this into total variation mixing bounds.

Why promising: conceptually closest to the Anari–Oveis Gharan–Vinzant worldview; strongest long-term significance.

**Strategy C: Canonical paths/conductance from quantitative support geometry**
1. Use strong log-concavity to control bottlenecks in the support graph.
2. Show these bottleneck bounds persist under perturbation.
3. Apply Cheeger/canonical path bounds to obtain mixing estimates.

Why promising: may be easier if functional inequalities are not yet formalized in the current library.

**Most promising:** Strategy A for a first verified theorem; Strategy B for the deepest mathematical story.
