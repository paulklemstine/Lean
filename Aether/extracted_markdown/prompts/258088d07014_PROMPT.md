## The Real Breakthrough Theorem

### Theorem B: Berggren words admit a faithful symplectic shadow

The Clifford group on `n` qubits has a well-known quotient to the finite symplectic group over `𝔽₂`. For `n = 1` or `n = 2`, this quotient is finite and highly computable in Lean.

You should define a **shadow map**
from Berggren generators to a finite transition monoid or symplectic action, and prove that word evaluation is respected.

The key idea:
- Berggren generators are integer matrices preserving the quadratic form `x^2 + y^2 - z^2`.
- Clifford/symplectic transformations preserve a bilinear or quadratic mod-2 invariant on Pauli data.
- The bridge is not equality of categories, but a **functorial shadow** from arithmetic dynamics to stabilizer dynamics.

### Precise theorem statement
Define:
- a finite type `TeleGen` of teleportation/stabilizer primitives,
- an interpretation `ψ : BerggrenGen → TeleGen`,
- evaluation maps `evalWord` and `evalTele`,
- and a finite invariant `shadow : PrimTriple → SympState`.

Then prove:

```lean
theorem berggren_shadow_functorial
    (w : List BerggrenGen) :
    shadow (evalWord w rootPrimTriple) =
      evalTele (w.map ψ) (shadow rootPrimTriple) := by
```

A more concrete and probably easier variant uses parity data:

```lean
def parityState (t : ℕ × ℕ × ℕ) : Fin 2 × Fin 2 × Fin 2 := ...

theorem berggren_parity_transition
    (g : BerggrenGen) (t : ℕ × ℕ × ℕ)
    (ht : isPrimTriple t.1 t.2.1 t.2.2) :
    parityState (applyGen g t) = parityAction g (parityState t) := by
```

and then extend to words:

```lean
theorem berggren_word_parity_transition
    (w : List BerggrenGen) (t : ℕ × ℕ × ℕ)
    (ht : isPrimTriple t.1 t.2.1 t.2.2) :
    parityState (evalWord w t) = parityEval w (parityState t) := by
```

### Why this is a breakthrough
This would create a formally verified **arithmetic-to-quantum compiler invariant**. Even if it only lands at parity/symplectic shadow level in this cycle, it opens a path toward:
- certified synthesis of stabilizer circuits from arithmetic data,
- new normal forms for teleportation fragments,

This is exactly the kind of “I never thought of that connection” theorem that can launch a program.

## Proof Strategy Architecture

## Strategy 1: Direct matrix-invariant proof
**Best first path.**

1. Represent Berggren generators as explicit integer matrices preserving the quadratic form
   `Q(a,b,c) = a^2 + b^2 - c^2`.
2. Prove `Q (M • v) = Q v` by direct matrix calculation.
3. Transfer positivity/primitiveness by determinant-unimodularity or gcd-preservation lemmas.
4. Lift from generators to words by induction on `List BerggrenGen`.

Why promising:
- It aligns directly with existing catalog theorems such as `berggren_map_pythagorean`.
- It is highly Lean-friendly.
- It gives reusable infrastructure for every later theorem.

## Strategy 2: Euclid-parameter route
Use the classical parametrization
`a = m^2 - n^2`, `b = 2mn`, `c = m^2 + n^2`
for coprime `m > n`, opposite parity.

1. Define a parameter-space action induced by Berggren generators.
2. Prove that action preserves coprimality/parity conditions.
3. Push forward to primitive triples.
4. Extract parity/symplectic shadow from the parity of `(m,n)` rather than `(a,b,c)`.

Why promising:
- Primitive conditions become cleaner.
- Teleportation-like parity behavior may become more transparent.
- It may simplify optimality/depth arguments.

Risk:
- More setup than direct matrix proofs.
- Harder if the exact Berggren action on parameters is not already in the library.

## Strategy 3: Rewriting-system / normal-form route
Treat Berggren words as a free monoid with semantics in triples and in teleportation skeletons.

1. Define a syntax of teleportation skeletons generated by 3 primitives.
2. Define semantic interpretation into triples and into finite-state shadows.
3. Prove confluence or at least a canonical reduced form on a restricted fragment.
4. Use this to derive cost optimality.

Why promising:
- This is the right route for “protocol optimality”.
- It upgrades the arithmetic story into a compiler story.

Risk:
- It depends on discovering the correct syntax and reduction rules.
- Better as a second-phase theorem after Strategy 1 succeeds.

**Recommendation:**  
Start with **Strategy 1**, then extract a parity/symplectic shadow. If the shadow behaves cleanly, pursue Strategy 3 for optimality.
