## Precise theorem target

Define a category-like structure of **finite triple-tree Gram semimodules** and a structure of **finite lattice presentations with Pythagorean Gram profile**. Then prove a reconstruction duality with minimality and certified shortest-basis output.

### Core mathematical statement

You should aim for a theorem of the following form.

Let `B₁, B₂, B₃ : Matrix (Fin 3) (Fin 3) ℤ` be the standard Berggren matrices acting on primitive Pythagorean triples. Let a **Gram profile** be a finite family of integral symmetric positive semidefinite `2 × 2` matrices
\[
G = \begin{pmatrix} a^2 & ab \\ ab & b^2 \end{pmatrix}
\quad\text{or more generally}\quad
G = M^\top M
\]
whose diagonal/off-diagonal data is generated from primitive Pythagorean triples `(a,b,c)` with `a^2+b^2=c^2`, `gcd(a,b)=1`, parity constraint, and Berggren closure.

Construct:

1. a notion `TripleTreeGramSemimodule` of finitely generated semimodule-like objects over a semiring/monoid generated by Berggren transitions;
2. a notion `PythagoreanGramLatticePresentation` of finite integer lattice presentations equipped with certified Pythagorean Gram factorization;
3. a reduction/minimality relation preserving the **Gram length spectrum**;
4. a reconstruction map producing a shortest basis witness or bounded approximation witness.

Then prove:

### Theorem A: Realization
Every admissible finite Pythagorean Gram lattice presentation is realized by a finite Berggren Gram semimodule.

### Theorem B: Minimality/uniqueness
Each realization admits a canonical reduced representative, unique up to certified isomorphism, obtained by quotienting by the indistinguishability congruence induced by Gram length spectra / future Berggren behavior.

### Theorem C: Certified reconstruction
From the reduced representative one can algorithmically reconstruct a shortest basis witness for the original lattice presentation, or at minimum a basis together with a formally verified bound certifying optimality within a stated approximation factor.

## Proof architecture: three viable strategies

### Strategy A: Direct Berggren-to-Gram realization and quotient minimization
This is the most concrete and probably the best first route.

1. **Encode Berggren action on triples and induced Gram data.**
   Define the three Berggren matrices and prove that they preserve primitiveness and the Pythagorean equation. Then define `Gram2.ofTriple` and show Berggren action transports Gram profiles functorially.

2. **Build the finite realization from reachable Gram states.**
   For a finite admissible lattice presentation, define the finite set of Gram atoms appearing in a factorization and close it under the relevant Berggren transitions used by the presentation. This gives a finite semimodule candidate.

3. **Quotient by Gram-behavior equivalence.**
   Define two states equivalent if every future Berggren word yields the same Gram length spectrum / reconstruction behavior. Show the quotient is finite, reduced, realizes the same presentation, and is unique by extensionality.

Why promising: it mirrors classical automaton minimization, is structurally close to existing “minimal witness” patterns, and keeps the arithmetic content explicit.

---

### Strategy B: Hankel/Gram rank criterion and finite generation via recognizability
This is more ambitious and more paradigm-shifting.

1. **Define a Berggren-Hankel matrix.**
   Index rows/columns by Berggren words and fill entries with Gram invariants, length spectra, or bilinear pairing values extracted from the presentation.

2. **Prove finite rank iff finite semimodule realization.**
   This would be a semiring-linear analog of recognizable series / weighted automata theory. The finite-rank condition becomes the criterion for existence of a finite triple-tree Gram semimodule.

3. **Use rank-minimal factorization to obtain the canonical reduced object.**
   The minimal realization dimension/cardinality is controlled by the rank invariant, yielding uniqueness up to certified isomorphism.

Why revolutionary: it would connect weighted automata/Hankel theory to arithmetic lattice reduction. If this works, it is the deepest version. But it is technically heavier and may require more infrastructure than is practical in one cycle.

---

### Strategy C: Reduction monotonicity via a discriminant/height Lyapunov functional
This is the best route if exact reconstruction feels too hard initially.

1. **Define a Pythagorean discriminant/height functional.**
   For example, a measure on Gram states derived from trace, determinant, shortest-vector candidate norm, or a Pythagorean complexity score.

2. **Show Berggren transitions are monotone enough to support reduction.**
   Prove that reduced representatives are exactly the local minima/canonical representatives under congruences preserving the Gram spectrum.

3. **Extract certified basis witnesses from monotone descent.**
   Reconstruction then follows from reaching normal forms with explicit basis certificates and comparison inequalities.

Why useful: even a bounded shortest-basis theorem here would already be strong and cryptographically meaningful. This strategy may combine well with Strategy A.

## Cross-domain connections you should make explicit in the file/module docs

### 1. Weighted automata / Myhill–Nerode / semiring recognizability
The reduced realization theorem is a semimodule analogue of automaton minimization:
- states correspond to residual Gram behaviors,
- Berggren words are inputs,
- Gram spectra are outputs,
- reduced objects are Nerode quotients.

This is not metaphorical; it should guide the definitions.

### 2. Lattice reduction and proof-carrying cryptography
A shortest-basis witness is a proof object. This suggests a new cryptographic primitive:
- compress lattice instances into minimal Berggren certificates,
- reduce/combine certificates in the semimodule domain,
- reconstruct a basis with a machine-checkable proof of optimality or approximation.

This matters for verifiable preprocessing, proof-carrying key generation, and trusted reduction pipelines.

### 3. Arithmetic dynamics and discrete geometry
Berggren transitions are an arithmetic dynamical system on primitive triples. Showing that this dynamics controls a class of Gram-factorized lattice presentations would create a new bridge between:
- Diophantine generation,
- quadratic forms,
- finite-state algebra,
- geometric optimization.

### 4. Quantum / gravity style analogy
The “cryptographic-gravity” phrasing becomes mathematically meaningful if the reduced semimodule is viewed as a boundary/compressed encoding and the lattice basis as a bulk reconstruction. If formalized cleanly, this suggests a rigorous toy model of **holographic reconstruction in arithmetic discrete geometry**.
