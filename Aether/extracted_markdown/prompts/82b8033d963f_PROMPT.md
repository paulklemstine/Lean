## Core Vision

For a closed well-typed term `t : A`, the bounded transition system `toFTS d t` is a finite approximation to evaluation behavior. The breakthrough claim is that after quotienting by bisimulation, this finite transition system ceases to depend on accidental syntactic choices inside the β-equivalence class of `t`. In the strongest form, the quotient should stabilize to a canonical finite coalgebra determined by semantics; in the bounded form, its size should be uniformly controlled by the type alone.

If true, this opens a field: **higher-order automata minimization for typed programs**. It would give a canonical-state semantics for STLC terms analogous to minimal DFAs for regular languages, but now in a setting where states are not words but computational observations of higher-order syntax.

## Theorem 1: β-equivalence invariance of bisimulation-minimized bounded semantics

This theorem should be stated with explicit quantifiers and should use the catalog’s behavioral equivalence machinery, not brute-force computation.

### Mathematical statement
For closed, well-typed terms `t u : A`, if `t` and `u` are β-equivalent, then for every sufficiently large depth `d`, the bisimulation quotients of `toFTS d t` and `toFTS d u` are isomorphic; in particular, they have the same number of quotient states.

### Lean 4 target signature
A representative target is:

```lean
theorem betaEq_preserves_canonicalQuotientSize
  {Γ : Ctx} {A : Ty} {t u : Term} :
  WellTyped Γ t A →
  WellTyped Γ u A →
  BetaEq t u →
  ∃ d₀ : Nat, ∀ d ≥ d₀,
    canonicalQuotientSize d t = canonicalQuotientSize d u
```

If your framework has a notion of closedness, strengthen to `Γ = ∅` or the appropriate empty context hypothesis. If quotient isomorphism is already formalized, prove the stronger statement:

```lean
theorem betaEq_preserves_bisimQuotient
  {A : Ty} {t u : Term} :
  ClosedWellTyped t A →
  ClosedWellTyped u A →
  BetaEq t u →
  ∃ d₀ : Nat, ∀ d ≥ d₀,
    Nonempty (FTSIso (bisimQuotient (toFTS d t)) (bisimQuotient (toFTS d u)))
```

### Why this is a breakthrough
This upgrades β-equivalence from a syntactic congruence to a **canonical finite-state semantic invariant**. It says minimization destroys inessential syntax and retains exactly the observable higher-order behavior visible at bounded depth.

## Theorem 2: Type-uniform bound on canonical quotient size

This is the theorem that turns semantic invariance into automata theory: not only is the quotient canonical, it is **uniformly bounded by type**.

### Mathematical statement
For each simple type `A`, there exists a computable bound `B(A)` such that every closed well-typed term `t : A` has, beyond sufficient depth, a bisimulation quotient of size at most `B(A)`.

### Lean 4 target signature
```lean
theorem canonicalQuotientSize_bounded_by_type
  {A : Ty} :
  ∃ B : Nat, ∀ t : Term,
    ClosedWellTyped t A →
    ∃ d₀ : Nat, ∀ d ≥ d₀,
      canonicalQuotientSize d t ≤ B
```

Or using your new definition:

```lean
theorem canonicalQuotientSize_le_typeStateBound
  {A : Ty} {t : Term} :
  ClosedWellTyped t A →
  ∃ d₀ : Nat, ∀ d ≥ d₀,
    canonicalQuotientSize d t ≤ typeStateBound A
```

### Why this is a breakthrough
This is the coalgebraic analogue of “the number of Nerode classes is language-intrinsic and finite.” Here the “language” is the behavior of a typed higher-order term, and the theorem says **type itself constrains semantic state complexity**. That is a new kind of complexity theory for typed programming languages.

## Theorem 3: Eventual stabilization of the bisimulation quotient

The bounded systems `toFTS d t` vary with `d`; a true canonical form requires stabilization. Prove eventual constancy, at least under strong normalization hypotheses already present in the catalog.

### Mathematical statement
Every closed well-typed term has a depth threshold after which increasing the truncation depth does not change the bisimulation quotient size; ideally, the quotients themselves become isomorphic.

### Lean 4 target signature
```lean
theorem quotient_stabilizes_eventually
  {A : Ty} {t : Term} :
  ClosedWellTyped t A →
  ∃ d₀ : Nat, QuotientStableFrom t d₀
```

Stronger version:
```lean
theorem bisimQuotient_eventually_constant
  {A : Ty} {t : Term} :
  ClosedWellTyped t A →
  ∃ d₀ : Nat, ∀ d₁ d₂ ≥ d₀,
    Nonempty (FTSIso (bisimQuotient (toFTS d₁ t)) (bisimQuotient (toFTS d₂ t)))
```

### Why this matters
Without stabilization, bounded minimization is merely approximate. With stabilization, it becomes a **true semantic normal form**.

## Strong Optional Theorem 4: Canonical quotient of β-normal forms is terminal among bounded observations

This is the conceptual theorem that would elevate the project from solid to field-opening.

### Mathematical statement
If `nf` is the β-normal form of a closed well-typed term `t`, then for sufficiently large `d`, the bisimulation quotient of `toFTS d t` is isomorphic to the bisimulation quotient of `toFTS d nf`. If your existing theorems show all bounded behavior collapses onto normal-form observations, make that precise.

### Lean 4 target signature
```lean
theorem quotient_matches_normal_form
  {A : Ty} {t nf : Term} :
  ClosedWellTyped t A →
  BetaNormal nf →
  BetaRedStar t nf →
  ∃ d₀ : Nat, ∀ d ≥ d₀,
    canonicalQuotientSize d t = canonicalQuotientSize d nf
```

Or stronger via quotient isomorphism.

This theorem is where the “single sink state” intuition from the sketch must either be made rigorous or refined. Do not force the sink-state conclusion unless it is mathematically true in your FTS semantics. If normal forms retain nontrivial observable branching, the quotient may not collapse to one state. The right theorem is invariance under passage to normal form, not necessarily triviality.

## Proof Architecture: Multiple Strategies

You must pursue at least 2–3 viable proof routes and explicitly document which one succeeded and why.

### Strategy A: Normalize first, then compare quotients
1. Use strong normalization / bounded beta results from `Pythagorean/BoundedBetaTheorems.lean` to obtain a β-normal form `nf` for each closed well-typed `t : A`.
2. Use `betaEq_typed_behavioral_eq` from `Pythagorean/StrongNormBisimProof.lean` to transfer behavioral equivalence from `t` to `nf`.
3. Show that for depths beyond the normalization depth (or normalization depth + observational slack), the quotient of `toFTS d t` agrees with the quotient of `toFTS d nf`.
4. Deduce β-invariance by passing both `t` and `u` to their shared normal form or to β-equivalent normal forms.

**Why promising:** This is the closest route to the existing catalog and likely the most robust. It leverages normalization as a semantic compression theorem.

### Strategy B: Coalgebraic minimization via bisimulation universal property
1. Define the bisimulation quotient as the coarsest sound identification on states of `toFTS d t`.
2. Prove that β-equivalent terms induce behaviorally equivalent pointed coalgebras at sufficient depth.
3. Use the universal property of quotient/minimization to derive isomorphism of minimized systems.
4. For the type bound, prove that the set of reachable typed residual configurations is bounded by a type-indexed finite universe, then quotienting can only reduce size.

**Why promising:** This is conceptually stronger and aligns directly with Myhill–Nerode and final coalgebra ideas. It may require more infrastructure, but if successful it produces the cleanest theorem statement.

### Strategy C: Induction on type complexity
1. Define `typeStateBound : Ty → Nat` recursively, perhaps using product/sum/exponential structure if encoded, or arrow nesting depth and subterm complexity if in plain STLC.
2. Prove by induction on `A` that every closed β-normal term of type `A` has only finitely many distinct observable residual forms up to bisimulation.
3. Lift from normal forms to arbitrary terms via normalization and behavioral equivalence.
4. Strengthen to eventual stabilization by showing once all possible residual normal forms have appeared, deeper exploration adds no new bisimulation classes.

**Why promising:** This gives the type-only bound in a structurally transparent way. It is likely the right route for the complexity theorem even if Strategy A proves β-invariance first.

**Recommended synthesis:** Use **Strategy A** for β-invariance and eventual stabilization, then **Strategy C** for the type-level bound. Strategy B should guide the conceptual framing in the paper even if not all of its universal-property machinery is formalized.

## Warning on the “single sink state” heuristic

The initial sketch says the quotient may collapse to a single sink state corresponding to the normal form equivalence class. Treat this as a **heuristic, not a theorem**. It may fail if the bounded FTS records observable structure before normalization or retains branching among residual terms. Your job is to discover the correct theorem:
- either true collapse in a suitably pointed/reachable quotient,
- or canonical quotient agreement with the normal form’s quotient,
- or eventual stabilization to a finite nontrivial minimal coalgebra.

If the single-sink claim is false, produce a counterexample theorem or computational witness. That would still be scientifically valuable.
