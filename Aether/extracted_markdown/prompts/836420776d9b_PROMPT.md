## Assignment: Algebra–Pythagorean–Computation Berggren Automaton Realization via Triple-Tree Hankel Semimodules and Certified Minimal Register Reconstruction

**Mode:** prove

Prove a genuinely new Schützenberger–Myhill–Nerode realization theorem in the arithmetic setting of the Berggren tree of primitive Pythagorean triples. The breakthrough is to show that a Diophantine expansion system generated by Berggren moves admits a finite-state weighted realization theory governed by a Hankel object, exactly as in classical rational series theory, but now specialized to arithmetic orbit-geometry. This would open a new field: **Diophantine automata on arithmetic trees**, with immediate formal bridges to symbolic dynamics, weighted automata, semiring linear systems, and certified algorithm extraction.

The conceptual leap is this: primitive Pythagorean triples are usually treated as number-theoretic objects; you should force them to become **computational states in a realizability category**. The Berggren monoid is not just an enumeration mechanism — it is an input alphabet for arithmetic computation. If you succeed, then arithmetic propagation rules in projects like `quantum_pythagoras` and `gravitational_factoring` become finite realizable machines whenever their associated residual semimodule is finitely generated.

### Precise Target

Let `BerggrenLetter` be the finite generator alphabet, let words `List BerggrenLetter` represent elements of the Berggren expansion monoid, and let `K` be a semiring. A weighted Berggren stream is a function
`S : List BerggrenLetter → K`.

Define:
- left residuals `u⁻¹S` by `v ↦ S (u ++ v)`,
- the residual family of `S`,
- the Berggren-Hankel kernel `H_S(u,v) = S (u ++ v)`,
- finite Berggren rank as finite generation of the semimodule spanned by residuals, or equivalently finite factorization through a finite free semimodule,
- a finite Berggren weighted register automaton as a finite state space `Q` with transition action of `BerggrenLetter`, an initial weight vector, and an output map computing the stream.

You should formalize and prove an equivalence theorem of the following shape.

### Primary Theorem Statement

For an appropriate semiring class `K` admitting the needed semimodule API, and for any stream `S : List BerggrenLetter → K`, the following are equivalent:

1. The left residual semimodule generated by `S` is finitely generated.
2. The Berggren-Hankel kernel of `S` has finite semimodule/factor rank.
3. `S` is recognized by a finite weighted Berggren automaton.
4. `S` is recognized by the canonical residual automaton whose states are a finite basis of residual observables.

Moreover, the canonical residual automaton is minimal up to isomorphism among all such realizations.

## Why This Is a Breakthrough

Classical automata realization theory lives on free monoids over finite alphabets. Berggren dynamics are arithmetic, geometric, and highly structured: every word encodes a primitive Pythagorean triple via a nontrivial matrix action. By proving a finite realization theorem here, you show that **Diophantine generation mechanisms admit a canonical finite computational semantics**. That is a new paradigm.

This would create:
- a formal theory of **arithmetic weighted automata**,
- a certified bridge between **number theory and automata realization**,
- a reusable extraction mechanism turning arithmetic observables on the Berggren tree into finite-state machines,
- a pathway to symbolic compression of triple propagation in quantum and cryptographic arithmetic models.

This is not “weighted automata on another monoid.” The Berggren tree carries arithmetic meaning, geometric growth, and certified enumeration bounds. If finite realization can be pinned to residual arithmetic observables, then one can begin classifying number-theoretic processes by computational rank.

## Proof Strategy Options

### Strategy A: Residual Semimodule First, Then Build the Automaton
Most promising.

1. Define the subsemimodule `R(S)` generated by all left residuals `leftResidual S u`.
2. Assume finite generation; choose a finite generating family of residuals.
3. Show that left concatenation by each `BerggrenLetter` induces an endomorphism of `R(S)`.
4. Encode these finitely many endomorphisms as transition/update maps on coordinates relative to the chosen generators.
5. Define output as evaluation at the empty word.
6. Prove by induction on words that the automaton computes `S`.
7. For minimality, show every state in any recognizing automaton determines a residual, so reachable states span `R(S)`; hence any realization must dominate the residual one.

Why this is best: it matches Mathlib’s finite generation/submodule APIs and avoids premature matrix-factor-rank machinery.

### Strategy B: Hankel Factorization First
Potentially more elegant, especially for weighted generalization.

1. Define the Berggren-Hankel kernel as a bi-infinite matrix indexed by words.
2. Formalize finite rank as factorization
   `S (u ++ v) = ∑ i, α u i * β i v`
   through a finite type.
3. Use the factorization to define a linear representation:
   row vector for prefixes, transition matrices for letters, column vector for suffix evaluation.
4. Convert the linear representation into a finite weighted automaton.
5. Conversely, show any finite automaton gives such a factorization.

Why it matters: this is the true Schützenberger perspective and will be the strongest conceptual theorem. But in Lean it may be harder unless you first stabilize finite-support/matrix APIs.

### Strategy C: Boolean Skeleton Then Semiring Upgrade
Most robust if the semiring-general version gets stuck.

1. Prove the theorem first for `K = Bool`, where residuals are just residual languages and minimality is Myhill–Nerode.
2. Extract the canonical DFA on Berggren residual languages.
3. Upgrade to `ℕ`-weighted or semiring-weighted streams by replacing language equivalence with semimodule span equivalence.
4. Reuse the same residual automaton architecture.

Why useful: gives a clean fallback path and a theorem of independent significance: **Berggren-regular languages**.
