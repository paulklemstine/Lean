## Central Conjecture

Let `p : MvPolynomial (Fin n) ℝ` be a homogeneous multilinear polynomial with nonnegative coefficients and `p(1) > 0`. Assume `p` is Lorentzian in the Brändén–Huh sense. Then the logarithmic Hessian of `p` at the all-ones point is conditionally negative semidefinite on the zero-sum subspace:

\[
\forall v \in \mathbb{R}^n,\quad \Big(\sum_i v_i = 0\Big)\ \Longrightarrow\ 
v^\top \big(\nabla^2 \log p\big)(\mathbf 1)\, v \le 0.
\]

Equivalently, the symmetric matrix
\[
L_p := \big(\nabla^2 \log p\big)(\mathbf 1)
\]
is `CondNegSemidef`.

This is the **Lorentzian CondNSD Conjecture**.

If true, this would identify a **spectral shadow** of Lorentzianity visible directly at the generating-function level. That would mean:

## Core mathematical insight to exploit

For a positive polynomial `p`,
\[
\nabla^2 \log p = \frac{1}{p}\nabla^2 p - \frac{1}{p^2}(\nabla p)(\nabla p)^\top.
\]

At the all-ones point,
\[
L_p = \frac{1}{p(\mathbf 1)} H_p(\mathbf 1) - \frac{1}{p(\mathbf 1)^2} g_p g_p^\top,
\]
where `g_p = ∇p(1)` and `H_p(1) = ∇²p(1)`.

The second term is rank-one positive semidefinite, so it contributes a **negative outer-product correction** to the log-Hessian. This is exactly the kind of structure already visible in `negSemidefForm_neg_outer_sum`. Your task is to turn this identity into a robust CondNSD mechanism.

For multilinear homogeneous `p`, the entries of `H_p(1)` and `g_p` have direct combinatorial interpretations:
- `g_i` is the total weight of monomials containing variable `i`;
- `H_{ij}` is the total weight of monomials containing both `i` and `j`.

Thus `L_p` is a covariance-like interaction matrix. This is why the conjecture is plausible: Lorentzianity should force these pairwise interactions to behave like a generalized attractive geometry whose centered fluctuations are spectrally nonpositive.

## Proof architecture: 3 possible strategies

You must pursue at least 2 of these seriously, and explain in comments or markdown which seems most promising.

### Strategy A: Degree-2 spectral reduction
This is the cleanest entry point and likely the best first theorem.

1. For homogeneous multilinear quadratics,
   \[
   p(x)=\sum_{i<j} a_{ij}x_ix_j,
   \]
   identify `H_p(1)` explicitly with the coefficient matrix up to diagonal normalization.

2. Use the degree-2 Lorentzian criterion: the associated quadratic form has at most one positive eigenvalue.

3. Show that subtracting the rank-one outer product of marginals forces the resulting log-Hessian to be nonpositive on the zero-sum subspace.

Why promising:
- it is concrete,
- the matrix computations are formalizable in Lean,
- and it gives the first nontrivial evidence for the conjecture.

Deep tactics likely needed:
- `rcases` on degree-2 monomials,
- `field_simp` for the normalization by `p(1)`,
- multi-step `calc` chains for quadratic-form identities,
- `by_contra` for spectral contradiction on the zero-sum subspace.

---

### Strategy B: Induction on degree via derivatives
This is the visionary route and could lead to the full theorem.

1. Use Euler homogeneity identities at `1`:
   \[
   \sum_i \partial_i p(1)=d\,p(1), \qquad
   \sum_j \partial_{ij}p(1)=(d-1)\partial_i p(1).
   \]

2. Express the quadratic form of `L_p` on zero-sum vectors in terms of derivative polynomials `∂_i p` and mixed derivatives.

3. Invoke Lorentzian stability under differentiation: if `p` is Lorentzian, then `∂_i p` is Lorentzian. Try to bootstrap CondNSD from the derivative family to `p`.

Why promising:
- it mirrors the recursive architecture of Lorentzian theory,
- it could expose a hidden “spectral Hodge recursion,”
- and it would scale beyond special examples.

Risk:
- the exact induction statement may require correction terms or extra hypotheses.
That is acceptable. Finding the correct inductive invariant is a major contribution.

### Strategy C: Hodge/Rayleigh inequality route
Turn known negative dependence inequalities into spectral negativity.

1. Derive pairwise Rayleigh inequalities from Lorentzianity or from known consequences in special classes (matroids, DPPs, volume polynomials).

2. Assemble these inequalities into matrix negativity on the hyperplane `∑ v_i = 0`.

3. Interpret `-L_p` as a generalized covariance/Laplacian matrix and prove its energy form is nonnegative on centered vectors.

Why promising:
- it links local pairwise inequalities to a global matrix signature,
- and it connects naturally to graph Laplacians, resistance metrics, and entropy dissipation.

This route is especially attractive for cross-domain theorems and computational experiments.
