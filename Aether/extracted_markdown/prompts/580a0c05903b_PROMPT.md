**Mode:** `prove`

\[

## New Definitions You Must Introduce

You must define at least one genuinely new concept not already in the catalog. The recommended package is:

1. **Step obstruction** for a short exact step \(A \hookrightarrow B \twoheadrightarrow Q\):
   a datum extracted from the corresponding `Ext` class, or from its torsion-annihilation behavior under the equivalence from `Ext1_ZMod_ZMod_equiv`.

2. **Three-step filtration obstruction**
   for \(A \subseteq B \subseteq C\), encoding the failure of the obstruction of \(A \subseteq C\) to be determined solely by the two adjacent step obstructions.

3. **Interaction correction term**
   measuring the discrepancy between:
   - the obstruction of the composite filtration \(A \subseteq C\), and
   - the formal composition of the obstructions of \(A \subseteq B\) and \(B \subseteq C\).

A possible Lean-facing design is to define a structure packaging the data:

```lean
structure ThreeStepFiltration where
  A B C : Type
  [addCommGroup A] [addCommGroup B] [addCommGroup C]
  iAB : A →+ B
  iBC : B →+ C
  monoAB : Function.Injective iAB
  monoBC : Function.Injective iBC
```

and then specialize to finite cyclic or finitely generated abelian groups where the obstruction becomes computable.

You may also define a more concrete structure for cyclic \(p\)-power towers, which is likely the most Lean-feasible first breakthrough.

## Precise Theorem Targets

You must prove **at least 3 nontrivial theorems**, each requiring real proof architecture: induction, `rcases`, `by_contra`, `field_simp`, multi-step `calc`, diagram chasing, or case decomposition by divisibility. Avoid vacuous or computationally trivial statements.

### Theorem 1: Composite obstruction is controlled by stepwise data plus correction

In a concrete cyclic \(p\)-power filtration
\[
0 \subseteq \mathbb Z/p^a\mathbb Z \subseteq \mathbb Z/p^b\mathbb Z \subseteq \mathbb Z/p^c\mathbb Z
\quad (1 \le a \le b \le c),
\]
define:
- the obstruction of the step \( \mathbb Z/p^a \subseteq \mathbb Z/p^b \),
- the obstruction of the step \( \mathbb Z/p^b \subseteq \mathbb Z/p^c \),
- the obstruction of the composite \( \mathbb Z/p^a \subseteq \mathbb Z/p^c \),

and prove that the composite obstruction is given by a composition formula with an explicit correction term depending on \((a,b,c)\).

A Lean 4 target signature could look like:

```lean
theorem composite_obstruction_formula
  (p a b c : ℕ)
  [Fact p.Prime]
  (ha : 1 ≤ a) (hab : a ≤ b) (hbc : b ≤ c) :
  totalObstruction p a c =
    composeObstruction (stepObstruction p a b) (stepObstruction p b c)
      + correctionTerm p a b c
```

If your obstruction lands in `ZMod (p^a)` or `ZMod (p^m)`, make the codomain precise and use the catalog equivalence to identify the relevant `Ext` group with an explicit cyclic module.

**Why this matters:** this is the first theorem that makes “higher derived persistence” computational rather than merely existential.

### Theorem 2: Vanishing criterion for the correction term

Prove a sharp criterion under which the correction term vanishes. A strong candidate:

> If one step splits, or if the relevant exponent gap is trivial, then the interaction correction term is zero.

Possible Lean statement:

```lean
theorem correctionTerm_eq_zero_of_split_or_gap_zero
  (p a b c : ℕ)
  [Fact p.Prime]
  (ha : 1 ≤ a) (hab : a ≤ b) (hbc : b ≤ c) :
  (SplitsStep p a b ∨ b = c ∨ a = b) →
  correctionTerm p a b c = 0
```

If you do not formalize splitting in full category-theoretic generality, define a concrete arithmetic surrogate for cyclic \(p\)-groups and prove the theorem there.

**Why this matters:** it identifies exactly when filtration data are strictly local and when genuinely higher-order interactions appear.

## Proof Strategy Architectures

You must pursue at least 2–3 viable proof routes and choose the most promising one explicitly.

### Strategy A: Arithmetic realization via `Ext1_ZMod_ZMod_equiv` (most promising)

1. Use `Ext1_ZMod_ZMod_equiv` to identify the relevant `Ext¹` class for cyclic \(p\)-groups with an explicit cyclic group.
2. Define each step obstruction as the image of the extension class under this equivalence.
3. Compute the composite extension class for \(A \subseteq C\) and compare it to the formal combination of the two adjacent classes.
4. Extract the correction term as the difference.
5. Prove vanishing/nonvanishing by divisibility and exponent arguments in `ℕ` and `ZMod`.

**Why this is most promising:** it replaces difficult abstract diagram chasing with explicit arithmetic in cyclic groups, which Lean handles robustly once the right equivalence is in place.

### Strategy B: Long exact sequence / connecting morphism approach

1. Model each short exact sequence in the filtration.
2. Define the obstruction as the connecting morphism in the induced long exact Ext sequence.
3. Show that for the three-step filtration, the total connecting map differs from the composition of the stepwise connecting maps by a boundary defect.
4. Identify this defect with your correction term.

**Why it is deep:** this mirrors the true homological origin of the phenomenon.
**Why it is riskier:** Mathlib support for long exact sequences and derived functors may make this heavier than necessary.

### Strategy C: Yoneda extension composition / Baer sum viewpoint

1. Represent each filtration step by a short exact sequence and hence an `Ext¹` class.
2. Treat the three-step filtration as a composable pair of extensions.
3. Analyze how the composite filtration determines a two-step Yoneda-type object.
4. Show that the failure of naive additivity is encoded by Baer sum or a secondary operation.

**Why it is conceptually powerful:** this is closest to the language of higher extensions, spectral sequences, and Massey-product intuition.
**Why it may be intermediate in feasibility:** the algebra is elegant, but the Lean API may require custom scaffolding.

**Recommendation:** start with Strategy A, then explain in `RESEARCH_PAPER.md` how Strategies B and C reveal the conceptual meaning.
