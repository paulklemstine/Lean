# Core Theorem Target 1: Universal second-extremality of the C-ray

Let `A B C : Matrix (Fin 3) (Fin 3) ℤ` be the standard Berggren matrices, let `base : Fin 3 → ℤ` be the column vector `(3,4,5)`, and let `evalWord : List Gen → (Fin 3 → ℤ)` send a word in generators to the resulting triple. Let `hyp : (Fin 3 → ℤ) → ℤ := fun v => v 2`. Let `len : List Gen → ℕ` be word length.

You should prove the exact theorem:

> **Theorem (C-ray universal second-extremality).**  
> For every `n ≥ 1`, among all Berggren words of length `n`, the word `C^n` uniquely minimizes the hypotenuse after `A^n`. Equivalently:
> 1. `hyp (evalWord (replicate n C))` is minimal among all words `w` of length `n` with `w ≠ replicate n A`;
> 2. equality occurs iff `w = replicate n C`.

A precise Lean-style target:

```lean
theorem cRay_second_extremal
  (n : ℕ) (hn : 1 ≤ n) :
  let aWord := List.replicate n Gen.A
  let cWord := List.replicate n Gen.C
  ∀ w : List Gen,
    w.length = n →
    w ≠ aWord →
    hyp (evalWord cWord) ≤ hyp (evalWord w) ∧
    (hyp (evalWord w) = hyp (evalWord cWord) ↔ w = cWord)
```

If your library already packages words as `FreeMonoid Gen`, restate accordingly:

```lean
theorem cRay_second_extremal_free
  (n : ℕ) (hn : 1 ≤ n) :
  ∀ w : FreeMonoid Gen,
    wordLength w = n →
    w ≠ Gen.A ^ n →
    hyp (evalWord w) ≥ hyp (evalWord (Gen.C ^ n)) ∧
    (hyp (evalWord w) = hyp (evalWord (Gen.C ^ n)) ↔ w = Gen.C ^ n)
```

# Core Theorem Target 2: Third-extremal classification

Once Target 1 is done, attack the next eigenvalue-like layer.

> **Conjectural Theorem.** For every `n ≥ 2`, among all words of length `n` except `A^n` and `C^n`, the unique minimizer of the hypotenuse is `A^(n-1) C`, with
> \[
> c(A^{n-1}C)=6n^2+6n-3
> \]
> under the coordinate convention in the prompt.

You must first verify the formula against the actual Berggren matrices in Mathlib/your files; it may differ by convention. The real theorem to prove is:

```lean
theorem third_extremal_A_pow_C
  (n : ℕ) (hn : 2 ≤ n) :
  let aWord := List.replicate n Gen.A
  let cWord := List.replicate n Gen.C
  let acWord := List.replicate (n-1) Gen.A ++ [Gen.C]
  ∀ w : List Gen,
    w.length = n →
    w ≠ aWord →
    w ≠ cWord →
    hyp (evalWord acWord) ≤ hyp (evalWord w) ∧
    (hyp (evalWord w) = hyp (evalWord acWord)) ↔ w = acWord
```

This theorem would amount to the first nontrivial **extremal hierarchy theorem** for Berggren shells.

# Core Theorem Target 3: Strong connectivity of modular Berggren orbits

Now the cross-domain leap: Berggren dynamics mod `p` should behave like a finite-state thin-orbit machine.

Fix an odd prime `p`. Reduce `A,B,C` mod `p`, and consider the directed graph on the orbit of `(3,4,5)` under the generated semigroup. The conjecture is:

> **Theorem/Conjecture.** For every odd prime `p ≥ 7`, the directed multigraph `G_p` on the orbit of `(3,4,5) mod p` under generators `{A,B,C}` is strongly connected.

Lean target, abstractly:

```lean
theorem berggren_mod_p_strongly_connected
  (p : ℕ) [Fact p.Prime] (hp : 7 ≤ p) :
  StronglyConnected (berggrenOrbitDigraph p baseModP)
```

If strong connectivity for all primes is too ambitious in one cycle, formalize and prove a structurally weaker but meaningful theorem first:

```lean
theorem berggren_mod_p_reaches_base_of_orbit_point
  (p : ℕ) [Fact p.Prime] (hp : 7 ≤ p) :
  ∀ v ∈ berggrenOrbit p baseModP, Reachable (berggrenOrbitDigraph p baseModP) v baseModP
```

This is equivalent to strong connectivity once forward orbit-generation is built into the graph.

This target matters because it reframes Berggren dynamics as a finite quotient of a thin semigroup action, bringing in ideas from strong approximation, expander heuristics, and automata on algebraic varieties.

# Proof architecture: three possible strategies

## Strategy A: Lexicographic monotonicity + first-deviation analysis
This is the most promising for Target 1.

### Step 1
Define a ranking on positive primitive triples, ideally via hypotenuse or a lexicographic pair like `(c, b)` or `(c, a+b)`. Prove exact action formulas for how `A`, `B`, `C` transform `(a,b,c)`.

### Step 2
Prove local comparison inequalities valid on the positive cone of primitive triples:
- `A` gives the smallest child hypotenuse;
- `C` gives the second-smallest child hypotenuse;
- `B` gives the largest child hypotenuse.

More precisely, for every positive primitive triple `v`,
```lean
hyp (A ⬝ v) < hyp (C ⬝ v) < hyp (B ⬝ v)
```
or the convention-adjusted inequality that actually holds.

### Step 3
Use first deviation from the all-`A` word:
- If a length-`n` word differs from `A^n`, let `k` be the first position where it is not `A`.
- Show replacing that letter by `C` and all later letters by `A` minimizes hypotenuse among all such deviations.
- Then prove among these candidates the minimum occurs at `k = 1`, yielding `C^n`, or at the convention-correct pure `C` ray after a comparison of tail recurrences.

This is a discrete variational principle on the symbolic tree.

**Why most promising:** it converts the global theorem into local order-preservation plus a first-deviation argument, which is ideal for Lean because it breaks the proof into reusable inequality lemmas and induction on word length.

---

## Strategy B: Closed-form semigroup comparison via matrix entries
This is algebraically cleaner if the Berggren matrices have tractable powers/products.

### Step 1
Compute or bound the third row of products of Berggren matrices. Since the hypotenuse is the third coordinate, you only need row-3 growth.

### Step 2
Prove that among length-`n` products excluding `A^n`, the product `C^n` minimizes the third-row action on the base vector. This can be done by comparing matrix products in a monotone cone order:
```lean
M ≤cone N  :=  ∀ v in positiveCone, hyp (M ⬝ v) ≤ hyp (N ⬝ v)
```

### Step 3
Establish strictness and uniqueness by showing any occurrence of `B`, or any `A/C` mixing pattern other than `C^n`, creates a positive gap in the third coordinate polynomial.

**Why useful:** if successful, this yields a general-purpose “cone order” theory for matrix semigroups, potentially reusable for other thin-tree dynamics.

---

## Strategy C: Hyperbolic/spectral reinterpretation
This is the visionary route, perhaps less immediate in Lean but scientifically powerful.

### Step 1
Interpret Berggren generation as a semigroup action on the light cone of the Lorentz form `x^2 + y^2 - z^2 = 0`. Primitive triples are integer lightlike vectors.

### Step 2
Define a depth-shell energy function by hypotenuse and identify `A^n`, `C^n`, `A^(n-1)C`, ... as low-energy states in a symbolic transfer system.

### Step 3
Prove extremality through a transfer-operator or geodesic coding argument: `A` is the greedy minimizer, `C` the first excited ray, and `B` uniformly repulsive.

**Why important:** even if only partially formalized, this reframes the Berggren tree as a toy model of spectral ordering on thin orbits, with potential links to symbolic dynamics and automorphic counting.

# Cross-domain connections you should exploit explicitly

## 1. Thin semigroups and strong approximation
The semigroup generated by `A,B,C` is thin inside `SO(2,1)(ℤ)`-type ambient structure. Strong connectivity mod `p` is a finite-shadow analogue of strong approximation and expansion. Even proving connectivity, before any spectral gap, is a serious structural step.

## 2. Symbolic dynamics and transfer operators
Words in `{A,B,C}` are a subshift coding of integer points on the light cone. Extremal hypotenuse classification is a low-temperature/ground-state problem for a symbolic energy functional.

## 3. Lorentzian geometry and discrete geodesics
Primitive Pythagorean triples are null vectors for `x^2 + y^2 - z^2`. The Berggren semigroup acts by integral Lorentz transformations. Extremal rays correspond to preferred geodesic directions in this discrete Lorentzian space.

## 4. Automata over finite fields
The graph `G_p` is an automaton generated by three explicit linear transformations. Strong connectivity suggests synchronizing and mixing phenomena analogous to random walks on Cayley/Schreier graphs.

## 5. Arithmetic statistics
A complete low-lying extremal hierarchy at fixed depth is a “density of states” theorem for Berggren shells. This could eventually feed into counting triples by hypotenuse with symbolic constraints.

Because it changes the question from “what triples are generated?” to “what is the internal spectral geometry of the Berggren semigroup?” A proof of second- and third-extremality gives the first rigorous low-energy classification in depth shells. A proof of modular strong connectivity would then show that the same semigroup is simultaneously rigid in the archimedean direction and mixing in finite quotients. That archimedean/non-archimedean duality is exactly the sort of unexpected structure that opens a field.
