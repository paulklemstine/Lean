## Core Vision

Let `J` be a finite site and let `F` be a presheaf equipped with a finite filtration
\[
0 = F_0 \subseteq F_1 \subseteq \cdots \subseteq F_n = F.
\]
The central theorem should show that the compression number of `F` is bounded by the sum of the compression numbers of the graded pieces:
\[
\kappa_{\mathrm{sh}}(J,F)\ \le\ \sum_{i=1}^n \kappa_{\mathrm{sh}}(J, F_i/F_{i-1}).
\]

This is the sheaf-theoretic analogue of:
- subadditivity of entropy under decomposition,
- additivity bounds in algebraic K-theory,
- filtration control in spectral sequence arguments,
- Jordan–Hölder style complexity bounds in representation theory.

If you can make this precise in Lean 4 for the finite-site compression formalism already present in the catalog, then the next generation of results becomes possible: compression under exact couples, derived compression invariants, and algorithmic decomposition of complex sensing systems into atomic information carriers.

### Theorem 3: Semisimple / Jordan–Hölder style corollary

If every graded piece is a finite coproduct of simple presheaves, then compression is bounded by the sum over simple constituents. This theorem is where the catalog’s coproduct subadditivity becomes a key input.

#### Mathematical statement
If
\[
\operatorname{gr}_i F \cong \coprod_{j=1}^{m_i} S_{i,j},
\]
with each `S_{i,j}` simple, then
\[
\kappa_{\mathrm{sh}}(J,F)\le \sum_{i,j}\kappa_{\mathrm{sh}}(J,S_{i,j}).
\]

## Proof architecture: 3 viable strategies

You asked for 2–3 proof strategy steps. Here are three distinct routes.

### Strategy A — Induction on filtration length via quotient-extension inequality
**Most promising.**

1. Prove the one-step theorem:
   \[
   \kappa(B)\le \kappa(A)+\kappa(B/A).
   \]
   This is the essential new argument. It should package any separator/compressor for `A` and `B/A` into one for `B`.

2. Perform induction on the filtration length `n`.
   Peel off the top layer:
   \[
   0\to F_{n-1}\to F_n=F\to F_n/F_{n-1}\to 0.
   \]
   Then combine the extension inequality with the induction hypothesis for `F_{n-1}`.

3. Rewrite the resulting bound into the finite sum over graded pieces using `Fin.sum_univ_succ` or analogous sum lemmas.

**Why this is strongest:** it mirrors classical homological arguments and scales naturally to future exact-couple or spectral-sequence style statements.

---

### Strategy B — Compression witness gluing via local separators
1. Unpack the definition of `compressionNumber J F` into the existence of a covering family or probe set satisfying separation.

2. Given witnesses for `A` and for `Q = B/A`, explicitly construct a witness for `B` by combining:
   - probes detecting the quotient image,
   - probes resolving ambiguity inside fibers using the subobject `A`.

3. Show the combined witness size is additive. This yields the extension inequality directly, and filtration results follow by iteration.

**Why this matters:** this strategy gives not only a theorem but an **algorithm** for constructing compressors from graded data. That is scientifically superior because it produces a verified computational method.

---

### Strategy C — Order-theoretic / lattice-theoretic control of compression
1. View subpresheaves as a finite lattice and compression as a monotone complexity functional on that lattice.

2. Prove a modular-type inequality:
   \[
   \kappa(X\vee Y)\le \kappa(X)+\kappa(Y/(X\wedge Y))
   \]
   or a suitable finite-site analogue.

3. Derive the filtration theorem as a telescoping inequality along a maximal chain.

**Why this is exciting:** it connects sheaf compression to matroid rank, submodularity, and information inequalities. If it works, this opens combinatorial optimization methods for compression.
