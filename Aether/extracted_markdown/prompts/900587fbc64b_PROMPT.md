## Assignment: Direction 1: Uniform Spectral Gap Bound for GL₂(𝔽_q)

Prove a genuinely new theorem package around a **uniformly certified expander mechanism** for Cayley graphs of `GL(2, 𝔽_q)` generated by algebraically checkable pairs. The goal is not to numerically observe expansion, but to **derive representation-theoretic lower bounds from structural certificates** and turn them into a verified algorithm that outputs explicit expanding generators with proof objects.

This direction is promising because it sits at a rare junction: finite group representation theory, spectral graph theory, arithmetic matrix generation, and explicit computation. If successful, it opens a program of **certificate-driven expander synthesis**: instead of discovering expanders by eigenvalue search, one manufactures them from finite-field algebra and proves expansion from first principles.

### Core Breakthrough Target

Let `q` be an odd prime, let `G = GL(2, 𝔽_q)`, and let `S = {g, g⁻¹, h, h⁻¹}` where:

- `g` is **Singer-like**: its characteristic polynomial is irreducible over `𝔽_q`, equivalently it acts semisimply with eigenvalues in `𝔽_{q²} \ 𝔽_q`;
- `det(h)` is **primitive** in `𝔽_qˣ`;
- `g, h` generate `G`.

Define the normalized adjacency operator
\[
A_S := \frac{1}{4}\sum_{s\in S}\lambda(s)
\]
on functions `G → ℂ`, where `λ` is the left regular representation, and define the spectral gap
\[
\gamma(S) := 1 - \max\{ |\lambda| : \lambda \text{ is a nontrivial eigenvalue of } A_S\}.
\]

The conjectural breakthrough is:

> **Uniform Certified Gap Conjecture.**  
> There exists an absolute constant `C > 0` such that for every prime `q ≥ 5` and every certified pair `(g,h)` as above,
> \[
> \gamma(S) \ge \frac{C}{q}.
> \]

A proof even with an explicit but small constant would be a field-opening result: it would give the first broad family of **4-regular explicit expanders for `GL₂(𝔽_q)` with algebraic certificates**, bypassing brute-force spectral search.

## Proof architecture: multiple viable strategies

You should explicitly pursue **2–3 proof routes**, not just one.

### Strategy A: Irreducible representation decomposition of `GL₂(𝔽_q)`  
**Most promising.**

1. Decompose the regular representation into irreducibles: characters of `GL₂(𝔽_q)` split into one-dimensional determinant twists, principal series, Steinberg twists, and cuspidal representations.
2. For each family, bound
   \[
   \left\|\frac{\rho(g)+\rho(g^{-1})+\rho(h)+\rho(h^{-1})}{4}\right\|
   \]
   using:
   - Singer-likeness of `g` to eliminate line-stabilizing behavior and force nontrivial oscillation in principal series;
   - primitive determinant of `h` to rule out concentration on determinant characters;
   - generation to exclude accidental common invariant vectors.
3. Convert family-wise operator norm bounds into a bound on the second eigenvalue of the Cayley operator on `ℓ²(G)`.

Why this is most promising: the conjecture itself is representation-theoretic, and the hypotheses are tailor-made to obstruct exactly the low-complexity irreducibles where poor expansion could hide.

### Strategy B: Projective action + quasirandomness transfer
1. Study the induced action of `g` and `h` on `ℙ¹(𝔽_q)`.
2. Prove that a Singer-like element has no fixed point on the projective line, while primitive determinant behavior prevents collapse into scalar/determinant quotients.
3. Use the permutation representation on `ℙ¹(𝔽_q)` as a “small model” detecting obstructions; then lift mixing from the projective action to the full Cayley graph via a quasirandomness or product-mixing inequality.

Why it is exciting: this creates a conceptual bridge from **finite geometry** to **spectral expansion**, and may reveal the geometric mechanism behind the gap.

### Strategy C: Harmonic certificate → Poincaré inequality → gap
1. Start from the catalog’s harmonic triviality theorems:
   - `harmonic_meanzero_eq_zero`
   - `certified_pair_harmonic_trivial`
2. Show that the absence of nonzero harmonic mean-zero functions implies positivity of the Dirichlet form.
3. Quantify this implication by proving a lower bound on the Rayleigh quotient over the orthogonal complement of constants, leading to a spectral gap estimate.

Why it matters: even if the full representation classification is heavy in Lean, this route leverages existing certified infrastructure and may be the fastest path to a theorem that is both novel and mechanizable.
