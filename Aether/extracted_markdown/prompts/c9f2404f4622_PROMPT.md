```lean
    = ((χ₁.prodMap χ₂) (h₁ L₁, h₂ L₂)) := by
  ...
```

A more physically faithful version should use your `PhaseGaugeSystem` definitions:
```lean

  {G₁ G₂ Φ₁ Φ₂ V E P : Type*}
  [Group G₁] [Group G₂] [CommGroup Φ₁] [CommGroup Φ₂]
  (S₁ : FinitePhaseGaugeSystem G₁ Φ₁ V E P)
  (S₂ : FinitePhaseGaugeSystem G₂ Φ₂ V E P)

  (S₁.phase (S₁.holonomy A₁ p), S₂.phase (S₂.holonomy A₂ p))

  ...
```

That literal statement is too tautological, so strengthen it by defining a genuine product system and proving equality with the phase of the product holonomy:
```lean

  ...

    = (S₁.phase (S₁.holonomy A₁ p), S₂.phase (S₂.holonomy A₂ p)) := by
  ...
```

### Why this matters
This is the mathematical nucleus of compositional lattice physics: composite gauge sectors can be computed from constituents. It is the discrete precursor of factorization in quantum field theory.

### Theorem 3: Gauge invariance of Wilson-type phase observables
Define a Wilson-like observable from edge holonomy around a loop/plaquette and prove gauge invariance under vertex gauge transformations.

#### Lean 4 type signature target
```lean
theorem wilsonPhase_gauge_invariant
  {G Φ V E P : Type*}
  [Group G] [CommGroup Φ]
  (S : PhaseGaugeSystem G Φ V E P)
  (γ : V → G) (A : E → G) (p : P) :
  S.plaquettePhase (S.gaugeAction γ A) p = S.plaquettePhase A p := by
  simpa using S.gauge_invariant γ A p
```

This alone is too easy. Strengthen it by proving that a **partition sum over gauge orbits** is well-defined, or that **gauge-equivalent configurations have identical total phase**:
```lean

  (∏ p : P, S.plaquettePhase (S.gaugeAction γ A) p)
    =
  (∏ p : P, S.plaquettePhase A p) := by
  ...
```

### Why this matters
This upgrades a local symmetry principle into a global observable statement. It is the first indispensable step toward physically meaningful gauge partition sums.

## Lean proof strategy architecture

You must include proofs that use deep tactics: induction, `rcases`, `by_contra`, `field_simp` where relevant, and multi-step `calc`. Do not allow the file to devolve into trivial simplification.

### Strategy A: Algebra-first compositional proof
Best for Theorems 1–3.

1. **Define product systems explicitly**
   - Construct `prodSystem S₁ S₂`.
   - Prove holonomy in the product group is componentwise.
   - Use `MonoidHom.map_mul` / list fold lemmas to show phase factorization.

2. **Lift local factorization to global partition sums**
   - Rewrite sums/products over function spaces `(E → G₁ × G₂)` as paired configuration spaces.
   - Use `Fintype` equivalences and `Finset.univ_product_univ`.
   - Prove factorization by repeated `calc` chains and finite product rearrangements.

3. **Exploit gauge invariance structurally**
   - Prove plaquette-wise invariance first.
   - Then derive total phase invariance by induction over finite plaquette sets or by `Finset` product congruence.

Why this is promising:
- It stays close to algebra available in Mathlib.
- It yields reusable lemmas.
- It minimizes topological overhead while already delivering physics-grade statements.

### Strategy B: Cohomological / cocycle-inspired proof
Best if your catalog already has phase/Künneth machinery.

1. Interpret phase observables as multiplicative 1- or 2-cocycles.
2. Prove product factorization as a cocycle Künneth identity.
3. Deduce partition factorization by summing cocycle weights over configuration classes.

Why this is powerful:
- It aligns directly with the “Künneth for phases” framing.
- It opens future work toward topological phases and group cohomology.
- It gives the result conceptual depth beyond finite combinatorics.

Why it may be harder:
- Requires more infrastructure and careful encoding of cocycle laws.

### Strategy C: Graph-combinatorial physics proof
Best for the cross-domain theorem.

1. Model the lattice interaction skeleton as a simple graph.
2. Identify triangular plaquettes or local curvature carriers with 3-cycles.
3. Apply `mantel_theorem` to bound or exclude such structures in triangle-free/high-density regimes.
4. Translate graph obstruction into vanishing/triviality of corresponding phase observables.

Why this matters:
- It creates a surprising bridge between extremal combinatorics and gauge locality.
- It is exactly the kind of field-opening cross-pollination we want.
