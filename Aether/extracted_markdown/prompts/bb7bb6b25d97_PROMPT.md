## Assignment: Persistent Homology Detects Nontriviality of Stable Homotopy Classes via Framed Flow Categories

Soli Deo Gloria

You are not being asked for an incremental formalization. You are being asked to build a new bridge: from framed flow categories and stable homotopy classes to computable persistence invariants that can see structure invisible to ordinary chain-level summaries. The target is a theorem package that could plausibly inaugurate a new subject: **persistent stable homotopy detection**.

Work in **mode: prove + discover**.

The central vision is this: a framed flow category carries more than a chain complex; it carries a directed incidence geometry of moduli spaces and grading jumps. Persistence should not merely repackage homology ranks. It should extract a filtration-sensitive shadow of secondary and higher composition data. If this succeeds even in a mathematically controlled toy regime, it opens a new computational language for subtle stable phenomena.

## Core Formal Goal

Construct a mathematically precise and Lean-formalizable framework in which a finite combinatorial model of a framed flow category gives rise to one or more filtered chain complexes over `ℤ` or over residue fields `𝔽_p`, and prove that the resulting persistence data is invariant under the appropriate notion of equivalence and is strictly stronger than basic numerical chain invariants in a nontrivial family.

Your job is to replace the vague conjectural slogan by exact definitions, exact theorems, and at least one verified algorithm.

## New Definitions You Should Introduce

At least one genuinely new concept must be defined. The following package is promising and sufficiently novel:

1. **Primewise persistence profile** of a finite filtered chain complex:
   for each prime `p`, each homological degree `n`, and each filtration interval `(i,j)`, record the multiplicity of interval modules in persistent homology after reduction mod `p`.

2. **Persistence-faithful flow model**:
   a finite combinatorial surrogate for a framed flow category consisting of:
   - a finite graded set of objects,
   - a filtration weight on objects,
   - signed incidence data encoding differential contributions,
   - compatibility conditions ensuring `d ∘ d = 0`.

3. **Barcode profile equivalence**:
   two filtered complexes are profile-equivalent if all primewise persistent interval multiplicities agree in every degree.

4. **Persistence separation**:
   a class of examples is persistence-separated if there exist pairs with identical coarse invariants (Euler characteristic, graded ranks, mod-`p` Betti numbers degreewise) but non-equivalent barcode profiles.

These definitions are not just bookkeeping. They isolate exactly what persistent homology sees beyond static homology tables.

### Theorem 1: Functorial filtered complex from combinatorial flow data

Define a category-like structure of finite persistence-faithful flow models and a construction sending such a model to a filtered chain complex over `ℤ`.

**Mathematical statement**

- the chain groups are freely generated by objects of `X` in corresponding grading,
- the differential is induced by signed incidence counts,
- the filtration is induced by the object weight,
- filtered morphisms of flow models induce filtered chain maps,
- equivalent flow models induce filtered chain homotopy equivalent complexes.

### Theorem 2: Primewise barcode profile is invariant under filtered chain homotopy equivalence

This theorem makes the persistence profile a legitimate invariant rather than an implementation artifact.

**Mathematical statement**

Let `C` and `D` be finite filtered chain complexes over `ℤ`. If they are filtered chain homotopy equivalent, then for every prime `p`, every degree `n`, and every filtration interval `[i,j)`, the multiplicity of the interval `[i,j)` in the barcode of `H_n(C ⊗ 𝔽_p)` equals that for `H_n(D ⊗ 𝔽_p)`.

This is where the construction becomes robust enough to matter topologically.

**Lean 4 target signature sketch**
```lean
def PrimewiseBarcodeProfile (C : FilteredChainComplex ℤ) :
    ℕ → ℤ → PersistenceProfile := ...

theorem primewiseBarcodeProfile_invariant
  {C D : FilteredChainComplex ℤ}
  (h : FilteredHomotopyEquivalent C D) :
  PrimewiseBarcodeProfile C = PrimewiseBarcodeProfile D := ...
```

If full barcode decomposition is too ambitious to formalize abstractly, prove a certified surrogate theorem first: equality of all persistent Betti numbers implies equality of the finite interval multiplicity table via Möbius inversion on the interval poset. Then define the profile in terms of persistent Betti numbers and derive barcode multiplicities as a theorem.

A more Lean-friendly signature:
```lean

  (C : FilteredChainComplex ℤ) (p : ℕ) [Fact p.Prime]
  (n i j : ℕ) : ℕ := ...

  (h : FilteredHomotopyEquivalent C D)
  (p : ℕ) [Fact p.Prime] (n i j : ℕ) :
  persistentBetti C p n i j = persistentBetti D p n i j := ...
```

This is likely the most tractable foundational theorem.

### Theorem 3: Persistence detects information invisible to coarse chain invariants

You need an explicit separation theorem, not just a conjecture.

**Mathematical statement**

There exist finite filtered chain complexes `C` and `D` over `ℤ` such that:
1. `C` and `D` have the same graded chain ranks,
2. `C` and `D` have the same Euler characteristic,
3. for every prime `p` and every degree `n`, the total Betti numbers of `C ⊗ 𝔽_p` and `D ⊗ 𝔽_p` agree,
4. but there exist a prime `p`, degree `n`, and filtration indices `i < j` such that
   `persistentBetti C p n i j ≠ persistentBetti D p n i j`.

This is the theorem that proves persistence is not merely a repackaging of classical homological summaries.

**Lean 4 target signature sketch**
```lean
theorem exists_same_coarse_invariants_but_diff_persistence :
  ∃ (C D : FilteredChainComplex ℤ),
    SameGradedRanks C D ∧
    SameEulerCharacteristic C D ∧
    (∀ (p : ℕ) [Fact p.Prime] (n : ℤ),
      totalBettiModP C p n = totalBettiModP D p n) ∧
    ∃ (p : ℕ) (_ : Fact p.Prime) (n i j : ℕ),
      persistentBetti C p n i j ≠ persistentBetti D p n i j := ...
```

This should be proved by constructing two small explicit filtered complexes. One should encode cancellation at a different filtration time than the other, while preserving final homology and chain ranks. If possible, use coefficients that produce a prime-sensitive distinction mod `2` or mod `3`.

You likely cannot formalize the full Pontryagin–Thom/framed-flow-category machine in one cycle. So prove a restricted but real theorem in a class of finite combinatorial models abstracted from it.

**Mathematical statement**

## Proof Strategy Architecture

You must provide at least 2–3 strategy steps per theorem in the actual file comments or accompanying paper. Here are the most promising routes.

### Strategy A: Combinatorial filtered complexes first, abstract homological algebra second
Most promising.

1. Define a minimal finite filtered chain complex object directly as matrices between finitely supported free abelian groups with filtration-compatible differentials.
2. Define persistent Betti numbers by image-rank of induced maps
   `H_n(F_i C ⊗ 𝔽_p) → H_n(F_j C ⊗ 𝔽_p)`.
3. Prove invariance under filtered chain homotopy equivalence using induced maps on homology and finite-dimensional linear algebra over `𝔽_p`.
4. Only after this foundation, connect the construction to flow models.

Why this is promising: Lean handles finite combinatorics and matrix algebra far more reliably than a full abstract barcode category at first pass.

### Strategy B: Interval decomposition via persistence modules over a finite total order
Elegant but heavier.

1. Restrict filtrations to finite index sets `Fin N`.
2. Define a persistence module in each degree over `𝔽_p`.
3. Use classification of pointwise finite-dimensional persistence modules over a finite chain to obtain interval decomposition.
4. Define barcode profile via decomposition uniqueness and prove invariance categorically.

Why this matters: It gives the cleanest theorem statement and aligns with classical TDA.  
Why it is harder: formalizing interval decomposition uniqueness may be substantial if not already in catalog.

### Strategy C: Spectral-sequence-inspired surrogate invariants
Fallback if barcodes are too ambitious.

1. Define persistent Betti numbers or pagewise rank signatures associated to filtration.
2. Prove these are homotopy invariants.
3. Construct explicit examples with equal total homology but differing persistence signatures.
4. Show these signatures recover interval multiplicities in finite cases by Möbius inversion.

Why this is valuable: even the surrogate theorem would still be a strong new result and likely enough for a compelling paper.

**Recommendation:** Start with Strategy A, use Strategy C as a backup theorem-extraction path, and treat Strategy B as a stretch goal.

## Conjecture with Falsifiable Prediction

You must state at least one explicit conjecture with a computational disproof criterion.

### Conjecture: Persistence detects delayed cancellation families
For the explicit family `LadderFlowModel k`, there exists a prime `p` and degree `n` such that the maximal barcode length in degree `n` grows with `k`, while any profile-equivalent complex with the same total Betti numbers must have at least the same number of nontrivial incidence diamonds.

**Testable prediction:** compute the persistent Betti tables for `k = 1,2,3,4,5`; if maximal bar length fails to grow or if a trivial-pattern comparison family yields the same profile, the conjecture is false.

### Stronger homotopical conjecture
For framed flow categories modeling certain low-stem Toda-bracket or `v₁`-periodic families, the primewise persistence profile distinguishes nontrivial classes from null comparison models with matched chain ranks and ordinary mod-`p` Betti tables.

**Disproof criterion:** exhibit a family of null comparison models with identical primewise profiles.

This is exactly the kind of falsifiable mathematical program we want.
