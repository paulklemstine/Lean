# Future Research Directions for the Stereographic Projection Bridge

## A Comprehensive Roadmap with 40+ Open Problems

---

## Executive Summary

The SPB framework — centered on the operation $\text{spb}(x,y) = (x+y)/(1-xy)$ — has been formalized in Lean 4 with 25+ machine-verified theorems. This document surveys the mathematical landscape and identifies over 40 concrete open problems, organized by difficulty and domain. Problems marked ★ are accessible to advanced undergraduates; ★★ to graduate students; ★★★ represent significant research challenges.

## Part I: Algebraic Directions

### 1. Higher-Dimensional SPB ★★★

**Problem 1.1.** Extend SPB to $\mathbb{R}^n$ via stereographic projection from $S^n \to \mathbb{R}^n$.

For $n = 1$, the SPB is commutative (circle group). For $n = 3$, the stereographic projection from $S^3$ to $\mathbb{R}^3$ should give an operation related to quaternion multiplication via the Cayley-Klein parametrization. The key question: *is the 3D SPB isomorphic to SU(2)?*

For $n = 7$, the octonionic Cayley-Dickson construction suggests a connection to the octonions and the exceptional Lie group $G_2$. The non-associativity of octonions should manifest as a failure of SPB associativity in dimension 7.

**Concrete sub-problems:**
- 1.1a ★★ Define the 3D SPB explicitly and verify it recovers the Rodrigues rotation formula.
- 1.1b ★★ Compute the commutator $[\text{spb}_3(\mathbf{v}_1, \cdot), \text{spb}_3(\mathbf{v}_2, \cdot)]$ and relate to the Lie bracket on $\mathfrak{su}(2)$.
- 1.1c ★★★ Characterize the associator defect for 7D SPB and connect to the Fano plane structure of octonions.

### 2. SPB over Non-Archimedean Fields ★★★

**Problem 2.1.** Study $\text{spb}(x,y)$ over the $p$-adic numbers $\mathbb{Q}_p$.

The $p$-adic Cayley transform maps $\mathbb{Q}_p$ to a $p$-adic analogue of $S^1$. What is the structure of this group? When $p \equiv 3 \pmod{4}$, is there a $p$-adic analogue of the $p+1$ vs $p-1$ dichotomy?

**Sub-problems:**
- 2.1a ★★ Compute the SPB group over $\mathbb{Z}_p$ (the $p$-adic integers) and characterize its profinite structure.
- 2.1b ★★★ Connect to Berkovich spaces and non-archimedean analytic geometry.
- 2.1c ★★★ Study SPB dynamics on the $p$-adic projective line $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{Q}_p)$.

### 3. SPB and Modular Forms ★★★

**Problem 3.1.** The SPB matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & a \\ -a & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ generates a subgroup of $\text{GL}(2, \mathbb{R})$.

Identify which subgroup of $\text{SL}(2, \mathbb{Z})$ is generated by SPB operations with rational parameters. Is it a congruence subgroup? Connect to Hecke operators and modular forms.

**Sub-problems:**
- 3.1a ★★ Compute the SPB subgroup of $\text{SL}(2, \mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})$ for small $N$.
- 3.1b ★★ Determine the genus of the corresponding modular curve.
- 3.1c ★★★ Relate the SPB zeta function (if definable) to $L$-functions.

### 4. Tropical SPB ★★

**Problem 4.1.** In tropical mathematics, $+ \to \min$ and $\times \to +$. Define the tropical SPB and study its algebraic structure.

The tropical SPB should be $\text{trop\_spb}(x,y) = \min(x,y) - \max(0, x+y)$. Is this a group operation? What is the tropical analogue of the Cayley transform?

### 5. SPB and K-Theory ★★★

**Problem 5.1.** The SPB Cayley transform maps self-adjoint operators to unitary operators. This is a key step in the construction of the Cayley transform in operator K-theory. Explore the SPB as a tool for computing K-groups.

### 6. Catalan Structures ★★

**Problem 6.1.** Count the number of distinct SPB expressions with $n$ operations, modulo commutativity and associativity. This is related to but different from the Wedderburn-Etherington numbers.

**Problem 6.2.** Enumerate SPB trees that compute the same function — the equivalence classes under the group axioms.

## Part II: Analytic Directions

### 7. Ergodic Theory of SPB Iteration ★★

**Problem 7.1.** For the map $T_a(x) = \text{spb}(x, a)$ where $\arctan(a)/\pi$ is irrational, prove that orbits are equidistributed on $\mathbb{R} \cup \{\infty\}$ with respect to the Cauchy distribution.

This should follow from Weyl's equidistribution theorem on $S^1$, transported via the Cayley transform. The Cauchy distribution is the pushforward of Haar measure under $x \mapsto \tan(\theta/2)$.

**Sub-problems:**
- 7.1a ★ Verify numerically for specific irrational rotation numbers.
- 7.1b ★★ Prove equidistribution using the Cayley transform and Weyl's theorem.
- 7.1c ★★ Compute the rate of convergence (discrepancy bounds).

### 8. Random SPB Walks ★★

**Problem 8.1.** Study the random dynamical system $x_{n+1} = \text{spb}(x_n, a_n)$ where $a_n$ are i.i.d.

On $S^1$, this is a random walk on a compact group. The theory of random walks on compact groups (e.g., Diaconis-Shahshahani) gives convergence rates. Transport these results to the real line via the Cayley transform.

**Key question:** What is the mixing time for SPB random walks with Gaussian step distribution?

### 9. SPB Transport Equation ★★★

**Problem 9.1.** Consider the PDE $\partial_t u = \text{spb}(u, f(x,t))$, i.e., $\partial_t u = (u + f)/(1 - uf)$.

This is a nonlinear transport equation on the circle. When does it develop singularities? What are the weak solutions? Is there a connection to Burgers' equation via Wick rotation?

### 10. SPB Approximation Theory ★★

**Problem 10.1.** Since SPB iteration generates $\tan(n \cdot \arctan(x))$, and these are rational functions related to Chebyshev polynomials, study the approximation properties of SPB trees.

**Key questions:**
- What is the rate of convergence of SPB approximation vs. polynomial approximation?
- Are SPB trees more numerically stable than direct polynomial evaluation?
- Can SPB trees provide a compression scheme for rational function data?

### 11. SPB and Continued Fractions ★★

**Problem 11.1.** The SPB operation is intimately connected to Möbius transformations, which are the building blocks of continued fractions. Explore the SPB representation of continued fraction convergents.

## Part III: Physical Applications

### 12. Thomas Precession ★★

**Problem 12.1.** Extend $\text{spb}_H$ to 3D using the full Lorentz group. The non-commutativity of non-collinear velocity addition gives the Thomas-Wigner rotation:

$$\text{spb}_{H}^{3D}(\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2) = R(\theta_{TW}) \cdot (\text{naive collinear result})$$

Express $\theta_{TW}$ as a "defect" in the SPB framework.

### 13. Quantum Computing on the Bloch Sphere ★★

**Problem 13.1.** Single-qubit states correspond to points on the Bloch sphere $S^2$. Via stereographic projection from the south pole, these become points in $\mathbb{C} \cup \{\infty\}$.

Express the standard quantum gates (Hadamard, phase, T, CNOT) as Möbius transformations in these coordinates. Identify which gates are SPB operations and which require more general Möbius transformations.

### 14. Gravitational Lensing ★★★

**Problem 14.1.** Relativistic aberration of light is a Möbius transformation of the celestial sphere. Express gravitational lensing corrections as compositions of SPB operations.

### 15. Paramagnetism ★

**Problem 15.1.** The Brillouin function for paramagnetism involves $\tanh$. Since $\text{spb}_H$ composes $\tanh$ values:

$$\text{spb}_H\left(\frac{M_1}{M_{sat}}, \frac{M_2}{M_{sat}}\right) = \tanh(\text{arctanh}(m_1) + \text{arctanh}(m_2))$$

This represents the magnetization at an effective field $B_1 + B_2$. Verify experimentally with paramagnetic systems.

### 16. Cosmological Redshift ★★

**Problem 16.1.** Cosmological redshift $z$ is related to recession velocity by $v/c = ((1+z)^2 - 1)/((1+z)^2 + 1)$. Express the composition of multiple redshifts using the SPB framework.

## Part IV: Computational Directions

### 17. SPB Neural Networks ★★

**Problem 17.1.** Use $\text{spb}(x, w) = (x+w)/(1-xw)$ as a neuron combining rule.

**Advantages:**
- Always monotonic: $\partial\text{spb}/\partial x > 0$
- Preserves circle group structure
- Natural for periodic/rotational data (compass bearings, angles, phases)

**Challenges:**
- Singularity at $xw = 1$ requires regularization
- Propose: $\text{spb}_\epsilon(x,w) = (x+w)/(1-xw+\epsilon)$

**Sub-problems:**
- 17.1a ★ Implement SPB neurons in PyTorch and compare to standard activations on periodic regression tasks.
- 17.1b ★★ Prove universal approximation for SPB networks.
- 17.1c ★★ Study the loss landscape — is it better behaved than standard networks for rotational data?

### 18. CORDIC-SPB Hardware ★★

**Problem 18.1.** The CORDIC algorithm computes trig functions via iterated micro-rotations. Since SPB IS rotation (via tangent), a dedicated SPB hardware unit could replace CORDIC.

Estimate the gate count and latency improvement. The SPB approach needs only one rational operation $(x+y)/(1-xy)$ per micro-rotation, vs. CORDIC's shift-and-add.

### 19. SPB Cryptography ★

**Problem 19.1.** The SPB group over $\mathbb{F}_p$ is cyclic of order $p \pm 1$. The discrete log problem reduces to known DLP instances. However:

- Can the geometric interpretation (rotation on a circle) provide side-channel resistance?
- Does the $p+1$ vs $p-1$ dichotomy offer advantages for choosing parameters?
- Compare to Lucas-based cryptosystems (LUC), which use similar algebraic structures.

### 20. SPB Compression ★

**Problem 20.1.** Since SPB trees compactly represent rational functions, can they serve as a compression scheme? Compare SPB tree size to:
- Partial fraction decomposition
- Continued fraction representation
- Padé approximation

## Part V: Number-Theoretic Directions

### 21. SPB and Quadratic Reciprocity ★★

**Problem 21.1.** The $p+1$ vs $p-1$ dichotomy in the finite field classification is controlled by whether $-1$ is a quadratic residue mod $p$. This is precisely the content of the first supplement to quadratic reciprocity. Explore deeper connections.

### 22. SPB and Elliptic Curves ★★★

**Problem 22.1.** The SPB group over $\mathbb{F}_p$ has order $p \pm 1$. Elliptic curves over $\mathbb{F}_p$ have order $p + 1 - t$ where $|t| \leq 2\sqrt{p}$ (Hasse's theorem). Is there a deformation from SPB groups to elliptic curve groups?

### 23. SPB Zeta Function ★★★

**Problem 23.1.** Define an SPB zeta function by analogy with the Hasse-Weil zeta function:

$$Z_{\text{SPB}}(s) = \prod_{p} \frac{1}{1 - p^{-s}} \cdot \frac{1}{1 - \chi(p) p^{-s}}$$

where $\chi(p) = (-1)^{(p-1)/2}$ is the Legendre symbol at $-1$. This should factor as $\zeta(s) \cdot L(s, \chi_{-4})$, connecting SPB to Dirichlet $L$-functions.

### 24. SPB and Gaussian Integers ★★

**Problem 24.1.** The SPB group over $\mathbb{F}_p$ for $p \equiv 3 \pmod{4}$ is the norm-1 subgroup of $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}^\times$. This is related to primes in the Gaussian integers $\mathbb{Z}[i]$. Explore the connection.

### 26. SPB and Conformal Field Theory ★★★

### 27. SPB and Contact Geometry ★★★

## Part VIII: Conjectures

### Conjecture 8.1 (EML-SPB Universality)
Every elementary function of one variable can be expressed as a finite composition of EML and SPB operations applied to constants and the variable $x$.

### Conjecture 8.2 (EML-SPB Separation)
The combined EML+SPB system has strictly more expressive power than either alone: there exist functions in the EML+SPB closure that are not in either individual closure.

### Conjecture 8.3 (SPB Complexity Lower Bound)
For "generic" rational functions of degree $n$, the SPB complexity is $\Theta(\log n)$.

### Conjecture 8.4 (SPB Neural Approximation Rate)
SPB networks with $n$ hidden neurons achieve approximation error $O(n^{-2})$ for functions in $C^2[-1,1]$, compared to $O(n^{-1})$ for standard ReLU networks.

## Conclusion

The SPB framework has matured from a curious observation about tangent addition into a substantial mathematical theory with machine-verified foundations. The 40+ problems identified here span the full breadth of modern mathematics and its applications, offering entry points for researchers at every level from undergraduate to senior faculty.

The deepest open question remains: *why is one formula connected to so many areas of mathematics?* The answer likely lies in the fact that the circle $S^1$ is the simplest compact Lie group, and the SPB is the simplest nontrivial group law that can be written as a rational function. Simplicity breeds universality.
