# Future Directions — Tropical Closure Semimodules as Algorithmic Fixed-Point Systems

The file `Catalog/Bridges/TropicalClosureReconstruction.lean` establishes the core
bridge: any family of *probes* (predicates) on an ambient type induces a genuine
`SetClosureOperator` via `probeClosure`, this closure is exactly the intersection of
the satisfied probe-halfspaces (`probeClosure_eq_iInter`), it is reconstructible from
its evaluation profile alone (`probeClosure_eq_of_profile_eq`), it carries a monotone
and closure-invariant integer potential (`potential_anti`, `potential_closure_eq`),
and it specializes to tropical affine-piece dominance systems with the fixed-point ⇔
inequality characterization `tropicalClosure_mem_iff`. The empty-family boundary
result `probeClosure_empty_family` pins down where the construction degenerates. The
directions below extend this frontier.

## 1. Finite separation ⇒ closure equals the genuine tropical convex hull

The current `tropicalClosure` is the intersection of dominance halfspaces; it is the
*probe hull*, which a priori can be strictly larger than the honest tropical convex
hull generated by `S`. The conjecture is that under a **finite separation
hypothesis** — informally, that the affine-piece family `P` distinguishes any two
points lying in different tropical cells, i.e. for `x ≠ x'` there exist `a, b ∈ P`
with `a.eval x ≤ b.eval x` but `a.eval x' > b.eval x'` — the probe hull and the
tropical convex hull coincide exactly: `tropicalClosure P S = tconv P S`.
The key insight is that tropical cells are precisely the equivalence classes of the
dominance sign-vector, so a family that separates sign-vectors forces the probe hull
down to the cell-union that the tropical hull already is. Why now: `tropicalClosure_mem_iff`
already exposes the closure as a pure sign-vector condition, so the remaining work is
to formalize `tconv` and a `Separating P` predicate and prove the two-way inclusion —
the hard inclusion reduces to a finite case analysis over sign-vectors that the
proven `probeClosure_eq_iInter` makes tractable. This is *falsifiable*: a family that
fails to separate two cells will produce a point in `tropicalClosure` outside the
tropical hull, exhibiting the predicted failure mode.

## 2. Strict monotonicity of the potential as a termination certificate

`potential_anti` and `potential_closure_eq` show the closure potential is antitone and
fixed on the closure, but they do not yet yield a *termination bound* for an iterative
reconstruction algorithm. The conjecture is that any reconstruction step that strictly
enlarges the current candidate set without reaching a fixed point strictly *decreases*
a dual potential `coPotential P S = P.card - potential P S`, so the algorithm
terminates in at most `P.card` steps. The key insight is that each genuine refinement
step must drop at least one satisfied probe, making `coPotential` a strictly
decreasing ℕ-valued well-founded measure. Why now: the invariance lemma
`potential_closure_eq` already certifies that fixed points are exactly potential
plateaus, so wiring it into `Nat`-valued well-founded recursion is a short, self-contained
step that converts the present static certificate into a dynamic complexity bound.

## 3. Closure-preserving morphisms of probe families (a category of tropical closures)

The catalog's `ClosurePreservingEnd` monoid suggests organizing probe families into a
category whose morphisms are maps `f : α → β` that pull tropical dominance probes back
to dominance probes, hence satisfy `f ⁻¹' (probeClosure Q T) ⊇ probeClosure P (f ⁻¹' T)`
or its image-side dual. The conjecture is that affine reparametrizations of the domain
(`x ↦ c·x + d`, `c > 0`) are exactly the invertible such morphisms for tropical
affine-piece families, giving a clean automorphism group of the tropical closure
system. The key insight is that an orientation-preserving affine change of variable
permutes affine pieces while preserving every dominance inequality, so it acts on
profiles by relabeling and on closures by transport. Why now: with `probeClosureOperator`
and `tropicalClosureOperator` already bundled as first-class objects, defining a
functor and checking the closure-preservation square is purely formal, and it directly
generalizes the catalog's endomorphism-monoid reconstruction theorems to the tropical
setting.

## 4. Profile rank and a tropical analogue of matroid closure

Define the **profile rank** of `S` as the number of *independent* dominance
constraints in `profile P S`, i.e. the minimal sub-family whose probe hull already
equals `probeClosure P S`. The conjecture is that this rank function satisfies the
matroid closure exchange axiom on the tropical affine-piece arrangement, making
`probeClosure` a genuine matroid closure operator whenever the pieces are in "tropical
general position." The key insight is that dominance halfspaces in general position
behave like hyperplanes of an oriented matroid, and the closure of a set is its
flat. Why now: `probeClosure_eq_iInter` already presents the closure as an explicit
intersection of constraints, which is exactly the data needed to define independence
and test the exchange axiom; connecting it to the catalog's matroid certificate files
(`ClosureMatroidSecretSharing.lean`) would create a second cross-domain bridge.

## 5. Quantitative reconstruction stability under perturbed evaluations

In applications the affine-piece evaluations are known only approximately. The
conjecture is a **robustness theorem**: if every piece's slope and intercept are known
to additive error `ε`, then the reconstructed closure changes only within an `O(ε)`
margin in the sense that `probeClosure P_ε S` and `probeClosure P S` agree away from
an `O(ε)`-neighbourhood of the cell boundaries. The key insight is that a dominance
inequality `a.eval x ≤ b.eval x` flips under an `ε`-perturbation only when `x` is
within `O(ε)/|slope gap|` of the crossing point, so the symmetric difference of the
two closures is controlled by the total boundary measure. Why now: the closure is now
a transparent intersection of explicit affine inequalities (`tropicalClosure_mem_iff`),
so a Lipschitz-style perturbation estimate can be stated and proved directly, linking
this development to the catalog's certified-robustness theorems
(`lipschitz_certified_robustness_identity`) and giving the algorithmic pipeline a
numerical-stability guarantee.
