### Key Mathematical Discovery

The depth of a primitive Pythagorean triple in the Berggren tree is NOT uniformly Θ(log c). Instead, it exhibits a **spectrum**:

- **B-branch (spectral radius 3+2√2 ≈ 5.83):** depth = Θ(log c) — exponential hypotenuse growth
- **A/C-branches (spectral radius 1, unipotent):** depth = Θ(√c) — polynomial hypotenuse growth
- **Factoring implication:** The trivial triple gives depth Θ(N), no better than trial division. Quasi-polynomial factoring requires efficiently finding "short" triples—an open problem connected to lattice reduction.
