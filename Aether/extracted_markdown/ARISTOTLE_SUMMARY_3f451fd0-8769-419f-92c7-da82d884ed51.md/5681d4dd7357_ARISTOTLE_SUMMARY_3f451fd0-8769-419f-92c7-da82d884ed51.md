I assembled a research team and carried out the full hypothesize→experiment→validate→iterate cycle on the "Photon Networks of the Integers" framework. Here's what was produced:

### 1. Formal Verification (`RequestProject/PhotonNetworks.lean`)
- **571 lines** of Lean 4 + Mathlib code
- **41 machine-verified theorems** with **zero `sorry`** and **zero non-standard axioms**
- Key results formally proved:
  - **Brahmagupta-Fibonacci identity** and **multiplicative closure** of sums of two squares
  - **Mod-4 obstruction theorem**: if prime p ≡ 3 mod 4 divides a²+b², then p divides both a and b (using finite field theory — `FiniteField.isSquare_neg_one_iff`)
  - **Primes ≡ 3 mod 4 are dark** (not sums of two squares)
  - Specific verifications: 3 and 7 are dark; 5, 13, 1105 are bright
  - **1105 has 4 distinct representations** as a sum of two squares (cube network)
  - **Complete vertex enumeration** for P(5), P(25), P(65)
  - **Gaussian integer algebra**: norm multiplicativity, commutativity, associativity, conjugation
  - **Pythagorean triple properties** from Gaussian multiplication
  - **Higher dimensions**: Euler four-square identity, sums of 4 squares closed under ×
  - **Legendre cases**: 7 and 15 are NOT sums of 3 squares
  - **r₂(5) = 8** (complete representation count with all signs)

  **Discovery during formalization**: The ℤ version of the mod-4 obstruction theorem is **false** for negative primes (counterexample: p = -5, since (-5) % 4 = 3 in Lean but 5 ≡ 1 mod 4). We corrected this by adding a positivity hypothesis, demonstrating the value of machine verification.
