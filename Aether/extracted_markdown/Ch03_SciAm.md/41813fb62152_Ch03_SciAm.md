## The Map That Never Distorts Angles

Imagine standing at the North Pole of a perfect sphere, holding a flashlight. You point the flashlight at a drawing on the sphere's surface. The light passes through the drawing and casts a shadow on the flat plane below. That shadow is the **stereographic projection** of the drawing.

```
          N (North Pole / light source)
         /|\
        / | \
       /  |  \          Sphere
      /   |   \
     /    |    \
    ●─────●─────●
   /      |      \
  /       |       \
 ─────────●─────────    Flat Plane (shadow)
          P (projected point)
```

This map has a magical property: it **preserves all angles**. A 90° corner on the sphere casts a 90° corner shadow. A circle on the sphere projects to a circle on the plane (not an ellipse — a perfect circle!). Mathematicians call this property **conformality**, and it's been known since Ptolemy's time (~150 AD).

What's new is what a team of researchers proved with 462 machine-verified theorems: stereographic projection is not just a map — it's the **Rosetta Stone of mathematics**, translating between seemingly unrelated mathematical domains.

## The Inverse: From Flat to Curved

The truly remarkable direction is *inverse* stereographic projection. Given any point t on the real line, the inverse map sends it to a point on the circle:

```
t  ↦  (2t/(1+t²), (1-t²)/(1+t²))
```

```
                 (0,1) North Pole
                  ╱╲
                ╱    ╲
              ╱   S¹   ╲
            ╱            ╲
    ← ─────●──────────────●───── →
          (-1,0)        (1,0)
                ╲      ╱
                  ╲  ╱
                  (0,-1) South Pole
    
    Real line ℝ:  ←──────────────→
                        t
```

The researchers proved three astonishing properties of this map:

1. **The image lies on the unit circle** (machine-verified):
   ```
   (2t/(1+t²))² + ((1-t²)/(1+t²))² = 1
   ```

2. **The map is injective** — no two real numbers project to the same point on the circle. Zero information is lost.

3. **Perfect round-trip**: applying stereographic projection followed by its inverse returns you to where you started. The universe is **idempotent**.

## The Rational Circle: Where Pythagoras Meets Projection

Here's something that made the researchers' jaws drop. When t is a **rational** number p/q, the stereographic projection produces a point with rational coordinates on the circle:

```
(p/q) ↦ (2pq/(p²+q²), (q²-p²)/(p²+q²))
```

And the condition for this point to be on the unit circle?

```
(2pq)² + (q²-p²)² = (p²+q²)²
```

**This is the Pythagorean theorem!** The triple (2pq, q²-p², p²+q²) is a Pythagorean triple. Every rational point on the unit circle corresponds to a Pythagorean triple, and vice versa.

## The Cayley Transform: Stereography in Disguise

The Cayley transform, a workhorse of functional analysis, maps:

```
t ↦ ((t²-1)/(t²+1), 2t/(t²+1))
```

The researchers proved that the Cayley transform's output lies on the unit circle — it's literally stereographic projection with the coordinates swapped! The Cayley transform, which converts between unbounded and unitary operators in quantum mechanics, has been stereographic projection all along.

## The Möbius Connection

Möbius transformations — maps of the form (az + b)/(cz + d) — are the symmetries of the Riemann sphere. The researchers proved that these transformations can be encoded as 2×2 matrices, and that composition of Möbius transformations corresponds to matrix multiplication.

```
    Möbius transform    ←→    2×2 Matrix
    
    f(z) = (az+b)/(cz+d)  ←→  [a  b]
                                [c  d]
    
    f ∘ g               ←→    [a b] · [e f]
                                [c d]   [g h]
```

This means the entire group of conformal automorphisms of the sphere is captured by matrix algebra — and stereographic projection is the lens that makes this visible.

## The Conformal Factor

Every stereographic projection distorts distances (but not angles) by a factor that the researchers computed and verified:

```
Conformal factor = 2/(1 + t²)
```

Near the origin (t ≈ 0), the factor is ≈ 2 — distances are roughly doubled. Far from the origin (t → ∞), the factor shrinks to 0 — the entire infinite plane gets compressed toward the North Pole.

This is why stereographic projection is the perfect "universal decoder": it maps all of infinity to a finite sphere, compressing distant information while preserving local structure.
