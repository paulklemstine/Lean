## The Question
*Are black holes just an area of extremely high information, isomorphic to an extremely high energy photon?*

## The Answer (Formally Verified)
**Quasi-isomorphism: geometrically yes, thermodynamically no.**

At the Planck scale (E² = ħc⁵/(2G)), a photon's Compton wavelength exactly equals the Schwarzschild radius of the equivalent energy — the geometric descriptions become **identical**. However, an irreducible **entropy gap** of 4πk_B (≈18 bits) separates a photon (pure state, zero entropy) from even the smallest black hole. The photon↔BH duality map also exhibits a **4π² scaling anomaly**, proving this is a quasi-isomorphism, not a true isomorphism.

## Key Physical Insight
The isomorphism parameter η(E) = 2GE²/(ħc⁵) cleanly partitions all of physics into a quantum regime (η < 1, photon description) and a gravitational regime (η > 1, black hole description), with the Planck scale as the exact boundary. This is not a smooth crossover — it's a sharp geometric crossing. But the thermodynamic structure (entropy) does not cross: it jumps discontinuously from 0 (photon) to 4πk_B (minimal BH), revealing the deepest puzzle in quantum gravity.
