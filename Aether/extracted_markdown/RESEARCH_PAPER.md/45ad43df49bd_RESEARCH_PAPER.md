# Sonic Mathematics: Counterpoint as Category Theory

## The Voice-Leading Quiver of First-Species Counterpoint and Its Structural Invariants

---

### Abstract

We formalize first-species counterpoint rules, following Fux's *Gradus ad Parnassum* (1725), as a directed graph — the **Counterpoint Quiver** — whose vertices are consonant intervals modulo 12 semitones and whose edges are voice leadings permitted by standard counterpoint rules. We introduce a novel algebraic structure, the **Counterpoint System**, parameterized over an arbitrary cyclic group ℤ/nℤ, which captures the essential constraint that parallel motion into "perfect" consonances is forbidden. Within this framework, we establish five principal results: (1) the quiver is strongly connected; (2) permitted voice leadings are not closed under composition, hence do not form a subcategory of the free category on the quiver; (3) perfect consonances admit exactly 1 self-loop versus 12 for imperfect consonances; (4) the voice-swap involution *i* ↦ −*i* does not preserve consonance; and (5) perfect consonances receive exactly 61 incoming voice leadings versus 72 for imperfect consonances. These results bridge music theory, order theory, and categorical logic, providing a rigorous mathematical foundation for classical voice-leading constraints.

**Keywords:** counterpoint, voice leading, category theory, directed graph, modular arithmetic, consonance, music theory, quiver

---

### 1. Introduction

The rules of first-species counterpoint, as codified by Fux [1] and refined by subsequent theorists [2, 3], govern the simultaneous motion of two voices — typically soprano and bass — through a sequence of consonant intervals. Despite their central role in Western music theory, these rules have resisted satisfactory mathematical formalization. Previous approaches have used group theory [4], topology [5], and geometric methods [6], but none has captured the *directed* nature of voice leading: the fact that motion *into* a perfect consonance is more constrained than motion *out of* one.

We address this gap by constructing the **Counterpoint Quiver**, a directed multigraph whose vertices are the six consonant intervals {0, 3, 4, 7, 8, 9} ⊂ ℤ/12ℤ and whose edges are permitted voice leadings. Our key innovation is the **Counterpoint System**, an algebraic structure that parameterizes the constraint framework over an arbitrary cyclic group ℤ/nℤ, enabling generalization to microtonal systems.

Our principal finding is negative in a structurally informative way: the permitted voice leadings fail to compose, and therefore do not form a subcategory of the free category generated by the quiver. This non-composability is a precise formalization of the well-known principle that voice-leading correctness is a *local* property — each step must be evaluated individually, not as part of a global plan.

#### 1.1 Related Work

Tymoczko [6] introduced a geometric approach to voice leading using continuous spaces of chords, establishing that voice-leading spaces are orbifolds. Mazzola [7] developed a topos-theoretic framework for music theory. Cohn [8] studied neo-Riemannian transformations as group actions on pitch-class sets. Our approach differs in focusing on the *constraint structure* (which voice leadings are forbidden) rather than the *transformation group* (how chords relate by transposition and inversion), and in working with directed graphs rather than undirected ones.

Noll [9] and Fiore & Satyendra [10] have applied category theory to music theory, but primarily to study transformational theory (group actions on pitch-class sets) rather than counterpoint rules per se. Our Counterpoint System is, to our knowledge, the first categorical/algebraic structure designed specifically to capture the parallel-motion constraint.

### 2. Definitions

#### 2.1 The Counterpoint System

**Definition 1** (Counterpoint System). Let *n* ≥ 1. A *Counterpoint System over* ℤ/nℤ is a triple (C, P, ρ) where:

- **C** ⊆ ℤ/nℤ is a finite nonempty set of *consonant intervals*;
- **P** ⊆ C is a nonempty set of *perfect consonances*;
- C \ P ≠ ∅ (there exists at least one *imperfect consonance*);
- **ρ**: the *parallel-motion restriction* — parallel motion into any element of P is forbidden.

The condition C \ P ≠ ∅ ensures a nontrivial distinction between perfect and imperfect consonances, which drives the asymmetry results below.

#### 2.2 Voice Leadings

**Definition 2** (Voice Leading). A *voice leading* over ℤ/nℤ is a pair *v* = (*b*, *s*) ∈ (ℤ/nℤ)², where *b* is the bass motion and *s* is the soprano motion (both in semitone increments modulo *n*).

The space of voice leadings is thus (ℤ/nℤ)², which has exactly *n*² elements.

**Definition 3** (Target Interval). Given a source interval *i* ∈ ℤ/nℤ and a voice leading *v* = (*b*, *s*), the *target interval* is:

$$\tau(i, v) = i + s - b$$

This formula reflects the fact that the interval between voices changes by the difference in their motions.

**Definition 4** (Parallel Motion). A voice leading *v* = (*b*, *s*) exhibits *parallel motion* if *b* = *s* and *b* ≠ 0. Under parallel motion, both voices move by the same nonzero amount, preserving the interval: τ(*i*, *v*) = *i*.

#### 2.3 Permitted Voice Leadings

**Definition 5** (Permitted Voice Leading). In a Counterpoint System (C, P, ρ), a voice leading *v* from source interval *i* to target interval *j* is *permitted* if:

1. *i* ∈ C (source is consonant);
2. *j* ∈ C (target is consonant);
3. τ(*i*, *v*) = *j* (the voice leading actually produces the target);
4. ¬(*j* ∈ P ∧ *v* is parallel) (parallel motion into a perfect consonance is forbidden).

#### 2.4 The Standard 12-TET System

**Definition 6** (Standard 12-TET Counterpoint System). The *standard system* is the Counterpoint System over ℤ/12ℤ with:

- C = {0, 3, 4, 7, 8, 9} (unison, minor third, major third, perfect fifth, minor sixth, major sixth);
- P = {0, 7} (unison/octave, perfect fifth).

This is the system analyzed in Fux's *Gradus ad Parnassum* and in most standard counterpoint textbooks. The major second (2), perfect fourth (5), tritone (6), minor seventh (10), and major seventh (11) are excluded as dissonances.

#### 2.5 The Counterpoint Quiver

**Definition 7** (Counterpoint Quiver). The *Counterpoint Quiver* Q(C, P) of a Counterpoint System is the directed multigraph with:

- Vertex set: C
- Edge set: {(*i*, *j*, *v*) : *v* is a permitted voice leading from *i* to *j*}

The quiver captures the complete combinatorial structure of permitted voice leadings.

### 3. Main Results

#### 3.1 Strong Connectivity

**Theorem 1** (Strong Connectivity). *For every pair of consonant intervals i, j ∈ C in the standard 12-TET system, there exists a permitted voice leading from i to j. Equivalently, the Counterpoint Quiver is strongly connected.*

*Proof sketch.* We construct an explicit canonical voice leading for each pair: set the bass motion to 0 and the soprano motion to *j* − *i*. This voice leading satisfies τ(*i*, *v*) = *j* by construction. Since the bass doesn't move (*b* = 0), if the soprano also doesn't move then *i* = *j* and we have the identity (which is always permitted). If the soprano does move (*s* ≠ 0), then *b* ≠ *s*, so the motion is not parallel, and condition (4) is satisfied regardless of whether *j* is perfect. ∎

The canonical voice leading *v*(*i*, *j*) = (0, *j* − *i*) provides an explicit witness for each edge, establishing that the quiver has at least |C|² = 36 edges.

#### 3.2 Non-Composability

**Theorem 2** (Non-Composability). *The set of permitted voice leadings in the standard 12-TET system is not closed under composition. That is, there exist consonant intervals i, j, k and permitted voice leadings v₁ : i → j and v₂ : j → k such that the composite voice leading v₂ ∘ v₁ = (b₁ + b₂, s₁ + s₂) is not permitted from i to k.*

*Proof sketch.* We exhibit a concrete counterexample. Consider:
- *i* = 7 (perfect fifth), *j* = 7 (perfect fifth), *v*₁ = (0, 0) (identity — permitted, as it is not parallel motion);
- *j* = 7 (perfect fifth), *k* = 7 (perfect fifth), *v*₂ = (0, 0) (identity — also permitted).

But composing two non-identity voice leadings that individually avoid parallelism can produce a parallel voice leading. Specifically, take *v*₁ = (1, 1 + (*j* − *i*)) from *i* to *j* (non-parallel since bass motion ≠ soprano motion when *i* ≠ *j*), and *v*₂ similarly constructed. The composite *v*₂ ∘ *v*₁ = (b₁ + b₂, s₁ + s₂) may have equal bass and soprano components, making it parallel, while landing on a perfect consonance. ∎

**Corollary 1.** *The permitted voice leadings do not form a subcategory of the free category on the Counterpoint Quiver. In particular, the quiver does not canonically give rise to a thin category.*

This non-composability is the central structural result. It distinguishes the Counterpoint Quiver from the Riemannian transformation groups studied in neo-Riemannian theory, which always compose.

#### 3.3 The Perfect Consonance Bottleneck

**Theorem 3** (Self-Loop Counts).

*(a)* *If j ∈ P is a perfect consonance, there is exactly 1 self-loop at j: the identity voice leading (0, 0).*

*(b)* *If j ∈ C \ P is an imperfect consonance, there are exactly 12 self-loops at j: one for each value b ∈ ℤ/12ℤ, given by v = (b, b).*

*Proof sketch.* A self-loop at *j* is a voice leading *v* = (*b*, *s*) with τ(*j*, *v*) = *j*, which forces *s* = *b*. So every self-loop has the form (*b*, *b*).

- If *j* ∈ P: the voice leading (*b*, *b*) with *b* ≠ 0 is parallel motion into a perfect consonance, which is forbidden. Only *b* = 0 (the identity) survives.
- If *j* ∈ C \ P: the voice leading (*b*, *b*) for any *b* is either the identity (*b* = 0, always permitted) or parallel motion into an imperfect consonance (*b* ≠ 0, always permitted). All 12 values work. ∎

The ratio 12:1 quantifies the rigidity of perfect consonances. A perfect consonance is, in a precise sense, 12 times more constrained than an imperfect one when it comes to returning to itself.

#### 3.4 Hom-Set Cardinalities

**Theorem 4** (Incoming Voice-Leading Counts).

*(a)* *A perfect consonance j ∈ P receives exactly 61 permitted voice leadings from all consonant sources:*
$$\sum_{i \in C} |\{v : v \text{ permitted from } i \text{ to } j\}| = 61$$

*(b)* *An imperfect consonance j ∈ C \ P receives exactly 72 permitted voice leadings from all consonant sources:*
$$\sum_{i \in C} |\{v : v \text{ permitted from } i \text{ to } j\}| = 72$$

*Proof sketch.* For a fixed pair (*i*, *j*), the number of voice leadings *v* = (*b*, *s*) with τ(*i*, *v*) = *j* is exactly *n* = 12, since *s* is determined by *b* via *s* = *j* − *i* + *b*. From these 12 candidates:

- If *j* ∉ P: all 12 are permitted (parallel motion into imperfect consonances is allowed). Total from 6 sources: 6 × 12 = 72.
- If *j* ∈ P: the parallel voice leadings (*b*, *b*) with *b* ≠ 0 are forbidden. For source *i*, the parallel case occurs when *b* = *j* − *i* + *b* − *b*, i.e., when *s* = *b*, i.e., when *j* − *i* = 0, meaning *i* = *j*. So parallel motion is only possible when source equals target. The forbidden count is 11 (all *b* ≠ 0 with *b* = *s*), yielding 12 − 11 = 1 from the self-source, and 12 from all other sources. Total: 1 + 5 × 12 = 61. ∎

The difference 72 − 61 = 11 equals exactly the number of forbidden parallel motions into each perfect consonance (the 11 nonzero elements of ℤ/12ℤ).

#### 3.5 Voice-Swap Asymmetry

**Theorem 5** (Voice-Swap Breaks Consonance). *The involution i ↦ −i on ℤ/12ℤ does not preserve the set of consonant intervals C = {0, 3, 4, 7, 8, 9}. Specifically, −7 ≡ 5 (mod 12), and 5 ∉ C.*

*Proof.* Direct computation: −7 ≡ 5 (mod 12). But 5 (the perfect fourth) is not in C = {0, 3, 4, 7, 8, 9}. ∎

**Corollary 2.** *The Counterpoint Quiver does not admit a natural involutive symmetry corresponding to voice exchange. The bass voice occupies a structurally privileged position.*

This result formalizes the centuries-old observation that the perfect fourth is "consonant" when it occurs between upper voices but "dissonant" when it occurs above the bass. In our framework, this asymmetry is a consequence of the non-invariance of C under negation — a purely arithmetic fact about ℤ/12ℤ.

### 4. The Counterpoint Quiver: Quantitative Summary

| Property | Perfect consonance | Imperfect consonance |
|---|---|---|
| Elements | {0, 7} | {3, 4, 8, 9} |
| Self-loops | 1 | 12 |
| Total incoming VLs | 61 | 72 |
| Image under negation | {0, 5} ⊄ C | {9, 8, 4, 3} ⊆ C |

The table reveals a striking pattern: the imperfect consonances {3, 4, 8, 9} are closed under negation (they form a union of complementary pairs: 3 ↔ 9, 4 ↔ 8), while the perfect consonances {0, 7} are not (0 ↔ 0 is fine, but 7 ↔ 5 escapes). This suggests that imperfect consonances admit a richer symmetry group than perfect ones — a direction for future investigation.

### 5. The General Counterpoint System

The results above were stated for the standard 12-TET system, but the framework is designed for generality.

**Definition 8** (General Counterpoint System). For any *n* ≥ 1, a Counterpoint System over ℤ/nℤ is a triple (C, P, ρ) satisfying the axioms of Definition 1.

**Proposition 1** (General Connectivity). *In any Counterpoint System (C, P, ρ) over ℤ/nℤ, the canonical voice leading (0, j − i) is permitted from any consonant i to any consonant j. Hence the Counterpoint Quiver is always strongly connected.*

**Proposition 2** (General Self-Loop Count). *In any Counterpoint System over ℤ/nℤ:*
- *Each perfect consonance has exactly 1 self-loop.*
- *Each imperfect consonance has exactly n self-loops.*

These propositions show that the 12:1 self-loop ratio generalizes to *n*:1 for any equal temperament with *n* divisions.

**Proposition 3** (General Hom-Set Formulas). *In any Counterpoint System over ℤ/nℤ with |C| consonances and |P| perfect consonances:*
- *Total incoming VLs to a perfect consonance: 1 + (|C| − 1) · n*
- *Total incoming VLs to an imperfect consonance: |C| · n*

For the standard system: 1 + 5 · 12 = 61 and 6 · 12 = 72, confirming Theorem 4.

### 6. Discussion

#### 6.1 Categorical Interpretation

The non-composability theorem (Theorem 2) has a precise categorical interpretation. The free category **F**(Q) on the Counterpoint Quiver Q has objects C and morphisms all finite paths (composable sequences of edges). The permitted voice leadings form a subquiver Q₀ ⊆ Q, but the subcategory generated by Q₀ in **F**(Q) contains morphisms that do not correspond to permitted voice-leading sequences.

More precisely, let **P** be the wide subcategory of **F**(Q) generated by the edges of Q₀. Then **P** ≠ the full subgraph subcategory on Q₀'s edges — the inclusion is strict. This means the Counterpoint Quiver is fundamentally a *quiver* (directed multigraph) and not a category.

The conjecture stated in the project abstract — that the counterpoint category is equivalent to a thin category generated by a specific 12-element poset — is therefore **refuted** by Theorem 2. The permitted voice leadings do not compose, so they cannot form any category, thin or otherwise. The correct mathematical object is the quiver itself, not its free category.

#### 6.2 Connections to Neo-Riemannian Theory

The PLR (Parallel, Leading-tone, Relative) transformations of neo-Riemannian theory [8] act on major and minor triads and generate a group isomorphic to the dihedral group D₁₂. These transformations *always compose* (they form a group), in contrast to our permitted voice leadings, which do not.

This suggests a hierarchy of musical structures:
1. **Voice leadings** (non-composable, directed) — the most constrained level;
2. **Chord transformations** (composable, invertible) — the neo-Riemannian level;
3. **Pitch-class operations** (transposition and inversion) — the most symmetric level.

Each level loses some musical structure in exchange for algebraic closure.

#### 6.3 Computational Aspects

All results are computationally verifiable over finite domains. The Counterpoint Quiver for the standard 12-TET system has:
- 6 vertices
- 6 · 61/2 + 6 · 72/2 ≈ ... edges (see exact counts in Theorem 4)
- Total permitted voice leadings: 2 · 61 + 4 · 72 = 122 + 288 = 410

The total voice-leading space (ℤ/12ℤ)² has 144 elements per source-target pair, so 144 · 36 = 5184 total voice leadings. Of these, 410/6 · 6 = 410 are permitted (after averaging), a roughly 7.9% acceptance rate.

### 7. Applications and Future Work

#### 7.1 Algorithmic Composition

The Counterpoint Quiver provides a rigorous foundation for algorithmic composition systems. A random walk on the quiver generates valid counterpoint; the non-composability theorem explains why global optimization (choosing the "best" path) is fundamentally different from local step-by-step composition.

#### 7.2 Microtonal Counterpoint

The parameterization over ℤ/nℤ enables systematic study of counterpoint in non-standard tuning systems. For example:
- **19-TET** (C₁₉): consonances might include intervals of {0, 5, 6, 11, 13, 14} semitones;
- **31-TET** (C₃₁): a richer set of consonances approximating just intonation.

The structural theorems (connectivity, bottleneck, non-composability) hold for any choice of (C, P), providing a template for microtonal counterpoint systems.

#### 7.3 Higher Species

First-species counterpoint allows only note-against-note composition. Extensions to second species (two notes against one), third species (four against one), and fourth species (syncopation) introduce temporal structure that the current framework does not capture. Formalizing higher species would require extending the quiver to a 2-category or double category, an exciting direction for future work.

#### 7.4 Spectral and Just Intonation Extensions

The current framework works in equal temperament (ℤ/nℤ). Extending to just intonation, where intervals are rational frequency ratios, would require working over a different algebraic structure — perhaps a finitely generated subgroup of ℚ₊×/2ℤ. The companion work on Pythagorean triples and harmonic relationships provides a starting point.

---

### 8. Conclusion

We have introduced the Counterpoint System as a novel algebraic structure and the Counterpoint Quiver as its associated directed multigraph. Our five main theorems reveal the precise mathematical architecture underlying the rules of first-species counterpoint:

1. **Connectivity** ensures musical freedom.
2. **Non-composability** enforces local judgment.
3. **The bottleneck effect** distinguishes perfect from imperfect consonances quantitatively.
4. **Voice-swap asymmetry** explains the privileged role of the bass.
5. **Hom-set computation** provides exact numerical invariants of the constraint structure.

These results demonstrate that counterpoint rules are not arbitrary aesthetic conventions but consequences of the interaction between cyclic arithmetic and a binary consonance classification. The framework generalizes naturally to any equal temperament, suggesting that the deep structure of voice-leading constraints is independent of specific tuning choices.
