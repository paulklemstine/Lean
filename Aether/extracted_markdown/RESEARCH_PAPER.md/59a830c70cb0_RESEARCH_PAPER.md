# The Counterpoint Quiver: Categorical Structure of First-Species Voice Leading

## Abstract

We formalize first-species counterpoint (Fux) as a directed graph (quiver) where objects are consonant interval classes modulo 12 semitones and morphisms are permitted voice-leading transitions labeled by motion type. We prove that the counterpoint quiver is the complete directed graph K₆ on the six consonant intervals {0, 3, 4, 7, 8, 9}, that the associated weight matrix W(i,j) = |{permitted motions from i to j}| has rank 1 with W² = 20·W, and that voice exchange defines an involutive automorphism. We disprove the conjecture that the counterpoint category is equivalent to a thin category generated by a poset, showing instead that the transition relation is the total equivalence relation. The interesting structure resides not in the thin categorical skeleton but in the enriched counterpoint category, where the 2:4 asymmetry ratio between perfect and imperfect consonances determines all quantitative properties. All results are machine-verified in Lean 4 with Mathlib.

**Keywords**: counterpoint, category theory, quiver, voice leading, weight matrix, spectral gap, Fux

## 1. Introduction

The rules of first-species counterpoint, codified by Fux (1725) and still central to music theory pedagogy, govern the simultaneous movement of two melodic voices. At each beat, the voices must form a *consonant interval*, and the transition from one beat to the next must satisfy motion constraints — most famously, the prohibition of parallel perfect consonances.

Previous formalizations of counterpoint have treated it as a constraint satisfaction problem (Agmon 1997, Tymoczko 2011), a geometric space (Callender, Quinn, and Tymoczko 2008), or a group-theoretic structure (Mazzola 2002). We take a categorical approach: we construct a directed graph (quiver) whose vertices are consonant interval classes and whose edges are permitted voice-leading transitions, weighted by the number of available motion types.

### 1.1 Main Results

1. **Transition completeness** (Theorem 4.1): The counterpoint quiver is K₆ — every consonant interval can transition to every other.

2. **Rank-one weight matrix** (Theorem 6.1): The weight matrix W satisfies W² = 20·W, implying rank 1 and maximal spectral gap.

3. **Poset conjecture disproof** (Theorem 8.1): The transition relation is not a partial order; the counterpoint category is codiscrete.

4. **Voice exchange symmetry** (Theorem 5.1): The complement involution is an automorphism of the counterpoint quiver.

5. **Border asymmetry** (Theorem 9.1): Cross-border morphism counts satisfy a 2:1 ratio.

### 1.2 Novel Structure

We introduce the **Counterpoint Quiver** as a formal mathematical object: a Fintype-valued quiver on consonant interval classes with hom-sets classified by a binary perfectness predicate. This structure captures the essential algebra of counterpoint in a way that is both computationally tractable and categorically meaningful.

## 2. Definitions

### 2.1 Consonant Interval Classes

**Definition 2.1** (CInterval). A *consonant interval class* is an element of the set
$$\mathcal{C} = \{0, 3, 4, 7, 8, 9\} \subset \mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$$
representing unison, minor third, major third, perfect fifth, minor sixth, and major sixth respectively.

**Definition 2.2** (Perfectness). An interval class c ∈ C is *perfect* if c ∈ {0, 7} and *imperfect* if c ∈ {3, 4, 8, 9}.

**Definition 2.3** (Complement). The *complement* function σ : C → C is defined by σ(c) = (12 − c) mod 12, restricted to C:
- σ(0) = 0, σ(3) = 9, σ(4) = 8, σ(7) = 7, σ(8) = 4, σ(9) = 3

### 2.2 Motion Types

**Definition 2.4** (MotionKind). A *motion kind* is an element of {contrary, oblique, similar, parallel}, classifying the relative direction and magnitude of two simultaneous melodic movements.

**Definition 2.5** (Permission predicate). A motion kind m is *permitted* for target interval t if and only if:
- t is imperfect (all motions permitted), OR
- t is perfect and m ∈ {contrary, oblique}

Equivalently, the forbidden configurations are exactly {(similar, perfect), (parallel, perfect)}.

### 2.3 The Counterpoint Quiver

**Definition 2.6** (Counterpoint Quiver). The *counterpoint quiver* is the directed graph Q = (C, E) where:
- Objects: C = {unison, min3, maj3, perf5, min6, maj6}
- Morphisms: Hom(s, t) = {m ∈ MotionKind | m is permitted for t}

Note that Hom(s, t) depends only on t, not on s.

**Definition 2.7** (Weight matrix). The *weight matrix* W : C × C → ℕ is defined by W(s, t) = |Hom(s, t)|.

## 3. The Perfectness Partition

**Proposition 3.1**. |{c ∈ C : c is perfect}| = 2 and |{c ∈ C : c is imperfect}| = 4.

**Proposition 3.2**. For all c ∈ C, exactly one of isPerfect(c) and isImperfect(c) holds.

**Proposition 3.3**. The complement σ preserves perfectness: isPerfect(σ(c)) = isPerfect(c) for all c ∈ C.

## 4. Transition Completeness

**Theorem 4.1** (K₆ structure). For all s, t ∈ C, Hom(s, t) is nonempty. That is, the counterpoint quiver is the complete directed graph on 6 vertices.

*Proof*. Contrary motion is always permitted, regardless of target. Thus contrary ∈ Hom(s, t) for all s, t. □

**Corollary 4.2**. The counterpoint relation cpReachable(s, t) ≡ ∃ m, m ∈ Hom(s, t) is total.

**Theorem 4.3** (Hom-set cardinalities).
- If t is perfect: |Hom(s, t)| = 2 for all s
- If t is imperfect: |Hom(s, t)| = 4 for all s

**Corollary 4.4**. The total number of morphisms is Σ_{s,t} |Hom(s,t)| = 6 × (2·2 + 4·4) = 120.

## 5. Voice Exchange Symmetry

**Theorem 5.1** (Complement automorphism). The complement σ : C → C together with the identity on motion kinds defines a quiver automorphism:
$$\text{Hom}(s, t) \xrightarrow{\sim} \text{Hom}(\sigma(s), \sigma(t))$$

*Proof*. Since σ preserves perfectness, the permission predicate is invariant under σ. □

**Theorem 5.2** (Complement involution). σ² = id, i.e., σ(σ(c)) = c for all c ∈ C.

**Theorem 5.3** (Complement orbits). σ has exactly 2 fixed points (unison, perf5) and 2 orbits of size 2 ({min3, maj6} and {maj3, min6}).

## 6. The Rank-One Weight Matrix

**Theorem 6.1** (Spectral gap). W² = 20 · W. Equivalently, the weight matrix has rank 1 with unique nonzero eigenvalue 20.

*Proof*. Since W(s, t) depends only on t, we can write W = 1 · v^T where 1 is the all-ones column vector and v(t) = permittedMotionCount(t). Then:
$$W^2 = (1 \cdot v^T)(1 \cdot v^T) = 1 \cdot (v^T \cdot 1) \cdot v^T = (\sum_t v(t)) \cdot W = \text{tr}(W) \cdot W$$
The trace is 2 + 4 + 4 + 2 + 4 + 4 = 20. □

**Corollary 6.2**. The 2-step transition count from a to c equals 20 · W(a, c):
- To a perfect target: 40 for any source
- To an imperfect target: 80 for any source

**Theorem 6.3** (Row/column sums).
- Row sums: constant at 20 for all rows
- Column sums: 12 for perfect columns, 24 for imperfect columns
- The ratio of column sums is 1:2

## 7. The Enriched Counterpoint Category

**Definition 7.1**. The *enriched counterpoint category* is the category enriched over (ℕ, ×) where:
- Objects: C
- Enriched hom: enrichedHom(s, t) = W(s, t)

**Theorem 7.1** (Source independence). enrichedHom(s₁, t) = enrichedHom(s₂, t) for all s₁, s₂, t.

**Theorem 7.2** (Positivity). enrichedHom(s, t) > 0 for all s, t.

## 8. Disproof of the Poset Conjecture

**Conjecture** (Research Direction). The category of first-species counterpoint is equivalent to the thin category generated by a specific poset of 12 elements.

**Theorem 8.1** (Disproof). The counterpoint transition relation is not a partial order.

*Proof*. Antisymmetry fails: cpReachable(min3, maj3) and cpReachable(maj3, min3) hold, but min3 ≠ maj3. □

**Theorem 8.2** (Actual structure). The counterpoint transition relation is the total equivalence relation on C. The associated thin category is the codiscrete category on 6 objects.

**Remark 8.3**. The conjecture fails for a fundamental reason: the transition relation depends only on whether a valid motion *exists* (which is always true, via contrary motion), not on *how many* valid motions exist. The categorically interesting structure is in the enriched category, not the thin one.

## 9. Border Analysis

**Definition 9.1**. The *border* of the counterpoint quiver is the set of morphisms between the perfect subquiver {unison, perf5} and the imperfect subquiver {min3, maj3, min6, maj6}.

**Theorem 9.1** (Border asymmetry).
- Perfect → Imperfect: 2 × 4 × 4 = 32 morphisms
- Imperfect → Perfect: 4 × 2 × 2 = 16 morphisms
- Ratio: 2:1

**Theorem 9.2** (Partition). The morphism partition is:
- Perfect ↔ Perfect: 8
- Perfect → Imperfect: 32
- Imperfect → Perfect: 16
- Imperfect ↔ Imperfect: 64
- Total: 120 ✓

## 10. Parameterized Strictness

**Definition 10.1**. The *strictness-s counterpoint system* (s ∈ {0,1,2,3}) modifies the permission predicate:

| Strictness | Forbidden to perfect | Perfect accessibility | Imperfect accessibility |
|-----------|---------------------|----------------------|------------------------|
| 0 | nothing | 4 | 4 |
| 1 | parallel | 3 | 4 |
| 2 | parallel, similar | 2 | 4 |
| 3 | parallel, similar, oblique | 1 | 4 |

**Theorem 10.1** (Monotonicity). Perfect accessibility is monotonically non-increasing in strictness.

**Theorem 10.2** (Uniformity at strictness 0). At strictness 0, all transitions have accessibility 4.

**Corollary 10.3**. The "interesting" structure of counterpoint — the perfect/imperfect asymmetry — arises entirely from the prohibition of specific motion types to perfect consonances.

## 11. The Asymmetry Index

**Definition 11.1**. The *asymmetry index* of a counterpoint system is (maxAccessibility − minAccessibility) / maxAccessibility.

**Theorem 11.1**. For the standard (strictness 2) system:
- maxAccessibility = 4, minAccessibility = 2
- Asymmetry index = 1/2
- The relation 2 · minAccessibility = maxAccessibility holds

This means perfect consonances have exactly half the compositional freedom of imperfect ones.

## 12. Falsifiable Conjecture

**Conjecture 12.1** (Enriched Chromatic Number). The "enriched chromatic number" — the minimum number of colors to color consonant intervals so that no two same-colored intervals are connected by all 4 motion types — is exactly 2.

**Evidence**: The complement involution provides a 2-coloring: {min3, maj6} and {maj3, min6}. These pairs are the complement orbits on imperfect consonances. Perfect consonances (which are not connected by all 4 motion types to anything) are unconstrained.

**Computational test**: Verify that no two intervals in the same complement orbit are connected by all 4 motion types to each other. Since the accessibility to any imperfect target is always 4 regardless of source, ANY two imperfect consonances are fully connected. Therefore the chromatic number of the 4-vertex complete graph K₄ on imperfect consonances is actually... well, the chromatic number of K₄ is 4, not 2.

This means the conjecture as naively stated is **false**: you need 4 colors, not 2. The complement pairing, while algebraically natural, does not solve the chromatic coloring problem. This refutation itself is informative: it shows that the complement involution, despite being a structural automorphism, does not decompose the full-accessibility subgraph.

## 13. Discussion

### 13.1 Why the Poset Conjecture Fails

The poset conjecture embodies an appealing intuition: that consonant intervals might be naturally ordered by some measure of "stability" or "consonance," and that voice-leading rules respect this ordering. Our disproof shows this intuition is misleading in the context of transition accessibility.

The failure is structural, not contingent. As long as contrary motion is always available (which it is in all historical counterpoint systems), every pair of consonant intervals is mutually reachable. No non-trivial partial order can arise from a total symmetric relation.

### 13.2 The Rank-One Property

The rank-one structure of the weight matrix is perhaps the most surprising result. It implies that the entire quantitative structure of counterpoint reduces to a single vector — the perfectness classification — with no higher-order interactions. This is a formal expression of the pedagogical observation that counterpoint rules "really only have one rule" (the prohibition on approaching perfect consonances by similar/parallel motion).

### 13.3 Musical Implications

The 2:1 border asymmetry (32 outgoing vs 16 incoming perfect consonances) provides a mathematical explanation for the musical observation that perfect consonances are "hard to reach." The instant-mixing property of the Markov chain explains why counterpoint lines do not feel "trapped" — compositional freedom is restored after every single beat.

## 15. PEGB Analysis of Major Theorems

### 15.1 Theorem: Transition Completeness (K₆ Structure)

**Proof**: The proof is constructive: for any pair (src, tgt) of consonant intervals, the contrary motion kind is always permitted (since the permission predicate for contrary motion returns true regardless of the target's perfectness). Therefore ⟨contrary, proof⟩ witnesses the existence of a morphism.

**Example**: Consider the transition from perfect fifth (7 semitones) to unison (0 semitones). Despite both being perfect consonances, the transition is valid via contrary motion — one voice moves up while the other moves down to arrive at a unison. In practice, this is the classic "resolution" of a fifth to a unison at a cadence.

**Generalization**: The completeness theorem generalizes to ANY counterpoint system where at least one motion type is unconditionally permitted. If we define a "counterpoint-like system" as one with a finite set of objects, a finite set of motion types, and a permission predicate isPermitted(motion, target), then the transition graph is K_n whenever there exists a motion type m₀ such that isPermitted(m₀, t) = true for all targets t.

**Boundary**: If we remove contrary motion from the permitted set (hypothetically), the transition graph is no longer complete. Specifically, with only {oblique, similar, parallel} as motion types, the transition to perfect consonances via similar or parallel motion is still forbidden, but oblique motion remains available. The graph only becomes non-complete if we remove BOTH contrary and oblique — requiring motion to perfect consonances via similar or parallel only, which are both forbidden. At this extreme, perfect consonances become isolated vertices.

### 15.2 Theorem: Rank-One Weight Matrix (W² = 20·W)

**Proof**: The proof exploits source-independence: W(s,t) = v(t) for a vector v encoding the permitted motion count. Thus W = 𝟏·v^T, and W² = (𝟏·v^T)(𝟏·v^T) = 𝟏·(v^T·𝟏)·v^T = (Σᵢ v(i))·W = tr(W)·W. The machine verification confirms the computation for all 36 entries.

**Example**: The two-step count from min3 to perf5 is 40: summing over all intermediates, we get 2·2 + 4·2 + 4·2 + 2·2 + 4·2 + 4·2 = 4 + 8 + 8 + 4 + 8 + 8 = 40 = 20 × 2 = 20 × W(min3, perf5). Similarly, the two-step count from unison to maj3 is 80 = 20 × 4.

**Generalization**: Any source-independent weight matrix over n objects has rank 1 and satisfies W² = tr(W)·W. This is a general theorem about "column-constant" matrices. The counterpoint case is an instance with n = 6 and column values in {2, 4}.

**Boundary**: If the permission predicate depends on the source (e.g., "no unison may follow a unison"), the weight matrix loses source-independence and generally has rank > 1. In second-species counterpoint, where the source interval DOES affect which motions are legal (e.g., dissonances must resolve in specific ways), the weight matrix is no longer rank-1, and the spectral gap is < 1.

### 15.3 Theorem: Poset Conjecture Disproof

**Proof**: We exhibit a concrete counterexample to antisymmetry: cpReachable(min3, maj3) and cpReachable(maj3, min3) both hold (via contrary motion), but min3 ≠ maj3 (they have different semitone values: 3 ≠ 4).

**Example**: Starting from a minor third (e.g., C-E♭), the voices can move by contrary motion to reach a major third (e.g., B-D♯ or C-E). And starting from a major third, they can move by contrary motion to reach a minor third. This bidirectional accessibility with distinct intervals violates the antisymmetry requirement of a partial order.

**Generalization**: The disproof generalizes to show that NO counterpoint system with at least 2 consonant intervals and at least one universally permitted motion type can have a poset transition structure. The proof is identical: the universal motion type makes the relation total and symmetric.

**Boundary**: If we restrict to a single consonant interval class (say, only unisons are consonant), the relation becomes trivially a one-element poset. At the other extreme, if we have consonant intervals but NO universally permitted motion type, the relation may fail to be total and could potentially be a non-trivial partial order. However, in all historically known counterpoint systems, contrary motion is always permitted, making this case vacuous.

### 15.4 Theorem: Border Asymmetry (2:1 Ratio)

**Proof**: Perfect-to-imperfect cross-border: 2 perfect sources × 4 imperfect targets × 4 motions each = 32. Imperfect-to-perfect: 4 imperfect sources × 2 perfect targets × 2 motions each = 16. Ratio: 32/16 = 2.

**Example**: From a perfect fifth, a composer can reach any imperfect consonance (min3, maj3, min6, maj6) via any of 4 motion types — that's 16 options from this single source. But from a minor third, reaching a perfect consonance allows only 2 motion types per target — only 4 options. The composer has 4× more freedom moving AWAY from perfection than toward it.

**Generalization**: In a counterpoint system with p perfect and q imperfect consonant intervals, where perfect targets allow k motions and imperfect targets allow m motions, the border ratio is (p·q·m)/(q·p·k) = m/k. For our system, m/k = 4/2 = 2.

**Boundary**: At strictness 0 (no restrictions), k = m = 4 and the ratio is 1:1 — no asymmetry. At strictness 3, k = 1 and the ratio is 4:1 — extreme asymmetry. The ratio m/k increases monotonically with strictness, reaching its maximum when only contrary motion reaches perfect consonances.

### 15.5 Theorem: Complement Involution Automorphism

**Proof**: The complement σ preserves isPerfect: σ(unison) = unison (perfect), σ(perf5) = perf5 (perfect), σ(min3) = maj6 (imperfect), etc. Since the permission predicate depends only on isPerfect(target), and σ preserves perfectness, σ preserves the permission predicate on each morphism.

**Example**: The voice exchange that turns C-E (major third, 4 semitones) into E-C (minor sixth, 8 semitones) preserves all voice-leading properties. Any motion permitted to C-E is also permitted to E-C, because both are imperfect consonances.

**Generalization**: Any permutation of consonant intervals that preserves the perfectness partition is an automorphism of the counterpoint quiver. The automorphism group contains at minimum: the identity, the complement involution, and any permutation of the perfect intervals among themselves composed with any permutation of the imperfect intervals among themselves.

**Boundary**: The complement involution is NOT the only automorphism. Since the weight matrix depends only on the perfectness of the target, any permutation that preserves {perfect, imperfect} is an automorphism. The full automorphism group of the counterpoint quiver is S₂ × S₄ (all permutations of the 2 perfect and 4 imperfect consonances independently), which has order 2! × 4! = 48.
