# Sonic Mathematics: Counterpoint as Category Theory

## The Voice-Leading Quiver of First-Species Counterpoint

---

**Abstract.** We formalize the rules of first-species counterpoint (Fux, 1725) as a directed graph—the *Counterpoint Quiver*—whose vertices are consonant interval classes modulo 12 semitones and whose edges are voice leadings permitted by classical counterpoint rules. We introduce a novel algebraic structure, the *Counterpoint System*, parameterized over an arbitrary equal temperament ℤ/nℤ, which abstracts the essential components of any counterpoint-like constraint system: a set of consonant intervals, a distinguished subset of perfect consonances, and the prohibition of parallel motion into perfect consonances.

Within this framework, we establish five main results for the standard 12-TET system: (1) **Strong Connectivity**—between any two consonant intervals, at least one permitted voice leading exists; (2) **Non-Composability**—permitted one-step voice leadings are not closed under composition, hence do not form a subcategory; (3) **Perfect Consonance Bottleneck**—perfect consonances admit exactly 1 self-loop versus 12 for imperfect consonances; (4) **Voice-Swap Asymmetry**—the involution i ↦ −i on ℤ/12ℤ does not preserve consonance, formalizing the privileged role of the bass; (5) **Hom-Set Computation**—perfect consonances admit exactly 61 incoming voice leadings versus 72 for imperfect consonances, a 15% reduction.

These results bridge music theory, combinatorics, order theory, and categorical logic, and demonstrate that the classical rules of counterpoint encode non-trivial mathematical structure.

**Keywords:** counterpoint, category theory, voice leading, directed graph, quiver, modular arithmetic, music theory, combinatorics

---

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Motivation

First-species counterpoint, as codified by Johann Joseph Fux in *Gradus ad Parnassum* (1725), is the simplest and most fundamental form of polyphonic composition. Two voices move simultaneously, note against note, constrained by rules governing which intervals are consonant and how voices may move between them. These rules have been the foundation of Western compositional training for three centuries.

Despite the centrality of counterpoint to music theory, rigorous mathematical formalization of its constraint structure has been limited. Previous work has approached voice leading through geometric methods (Tymoczko, 2006, 2011), group-theoretic analysis of pitch-class sets (Forte, 1973; Morris, 1987), and neo-Riemannian theory (Cohn, 1998). However, the *directed graph structure* generated by the specific combination of consonance constraints and the parallel-motion prohibition has not been systematically analyzed.

We propose a novel approach: treat the counterpoint rules as defining a *quiver* (directed multigraph) and study its combinatorial and categorical properties. This perspective reveals that the classical rules generate a rich mathematical structure with precise, provable properties.

### 1.2 Overview of Results

Our main contributions are:

1. **The Counterpoint System** (Definition 2.1): An abstract algebraic structure `CounterpointSystem n` over ℤ/nℤ that captures counterpoint-like constraints in any equal temperament.

2. **The Counterpoint Quiver** (Definition 2.4): A directed multigraph whose vertices are consonant intervals and whose edges are permitted voice leadings.

3. **Five structural theorems** (Section 4) about the standard 12-TET quiver, including connectivity, non-composability, asymmetric self-loop counts, voice-swap failure, and precise hom-set cardinalities.

4. **Generalization** (Section 5): Discussion of how these results extend to microtonal systems (19-TET, 31-TET, etc.).

### 1.3 Relation to Prior Work

Tymoczko's geometric theory of voice leading (2006, 2011) represents voice leadings as paths in a continuous orbifold. Our approach is complementary: we work in the discrete setting of ℤ/nℤ and focus on the combinatorial constraints imposed by counterpoint rules rather than the geometry of the underlying pitch space. The key novelty is treating the *prohibition* structure—what is forbidden, not just what is possible—as the primary object of study.

Mazzola's *topos of music* (2002) uses category theory extensively in music theory, but at a higher level of abstraction (functors between musical categories). Our work is more concrete: we ask whether a specific set of musical rules generates a category, and prove that it does not.

## 2. Definitions

### 2.1 The Counterpoint System

**Definition 2.1** (Counterpoint System). A *Counterpoint System of order n* is a tuple (C, P, n) where:
- n ≥ 1 is the number of pitch classes (the *temperament order*)
- C ⊆ ℤ/nℤ is a nonempty finite set of *consonant intervals*
- P ⊆ C is a nonempty set of *perfect consonances*
- There exists at least one *imperfect consonance*: some i ∈ C with i ∉ P

The requirement that imperfect consonances exist is essential—without it, the parallel-motion prohibition would apply universally, and the asymmetric structure we study would collapse.

### 2.2 Voice Leadings

**Definition 2.2** (Voice Leading). A *voice leading* over ℤ/nℤ is a pair (b, s) ∈ (ℤ/nℤ)² where b is the bass motion and s is the soprano motion.

The total number of voice leadings is n².

**Definition 2.3** (Target Interval). Given a source interval i ∈ ℤ/nℤ and a voice leading (b, s), the *target interval* is:

$$\text{target}(i, b, s) = i + s - b$$

This follows from the fact that if the interval is i = (soprano pitch) − (bass pitch), and each voice moves by s and b respectively, the new interval is (soprano + s) − (bass + b) = i + s − b.

**Definition 2.4** (Parallel Motion). A voice leading (b, s) is *parallel* if b = s and b ≠ 0.

**Definition 2.5** (Permitted Voice Leading). A voice leading (b, s) from source interval i to target interval j is *permitted* in a Counterpoint System (C, P, n) if:
1. i ∈ C (source is consonant)
2. j ∈ C (target is consonant)
3. j = i + s − b (the arithmetic is consistent)
4. ¬(j ∈ P ∧ b = s ∧ b ≠ 0) (no parallel motion into a perfect consonance)

### 2.3 The Counterpoint Quiver

**Definition 2.6** (Counterpoint Quiver). The *Counterpoint Quiver* Q(C, P, n) is the directed multigraph with:
- Vertex set: C
- Edge set: {(i, j, b, s) | the voice leading (b, s) is permitted from i to j}

Note that this is genuinely a multigraph: there may be multiple permitted voice leadings between the same pair of consonant intervals.

### 2.4 The Standard 12-TET System

**Definition 2.7** (Standard System). The *standard 12-TET Counterpoint System* is:
- n = 12
- C = {0, 3, 4, 7, 8, 9} (unison, minor third, major third, perfect fifth, minor sixth, major sixth)
- P = {0, 7} (unison/octave, perfect fifth)

This is denoted `standard12` in our formalization.

## 3. The Canonical Voice Leading

A key construction is the *canonical voice leading* between any two intervals.

**Definition 3.1** (Canonical Voice Leading). For intervals i, j ∈ ℤ/nℤ, the *canonical voice leading* is (0, j − i): the bass holds while the soprano moves by j − i.

**Lemma 3.2** (Target Correctness). The canonical voice leading from i to j has target interval j:
$$\text{target}(i, 0, j - i) = i + (j - i) - 0 = j$$

**Lemma 3.3** (Non-Parallelism). If i ≠ j, the canonical voice leading from i to j is not parallel, since b = 0 and s = j − i ≠ 0.

These lemmas are the workhorses of the strong connectivity proof: for any two *distinct* consonant intervals, the canonical voice leading is permitted (it cannot violate the Fux constraint because it is not parallel). The case i = j requires separate treatment.

## 4. Main Results

### 4.1 Theorem 1: Strong Connectivity

**Theorem 4.1** (Strong Connectivity / `exists_permitted_voice_leading`). For any consonant intervals i, j ∈ C in the standard 12-TET system, there exists a permitted voice leading from i to j.

*Proof sketch.* Two cases:

**Case i ≠ j:** The canonical voice leading (0, j − i) has bass motion 0, so it is not parallel (parallel requires both voices to move by the same nonzero amount). Since both i and j are consonant by hypothesis, and the target is correct by Lemma 3.2, and the motion is not parallel, the voice leading is permitted.

**Case i = j:** If i is imperfect, any voice leading (k, k) for k ∈ ℤ/12ℤ preserves the interval (target = i + k − k = i) and is permitted (the target is imperfect, so the Fux constraint does not apply). If i is perfect (i ∈ {0, 7}), the identity voice leading (0, 0) is trivially permitted. ∎

**Corollary.** The Counterpoint Quiver Q(C, P, 12) is strongly connected as a directed graph.

### 4.2 Theorem 2: Non-Composability

**Theorem 4.2** (Non-Composability / `non_composability`). There exist consonant intervals i, j, k and permitted voice leadings v₁ : i → j, v₂ : j → k such that the composed voice leading (v₁.bass + v₂.bass, v₁.soprano + v₂.soprano) from i to k is NOT permitted.

*Proof sketch.* Consider the following specific instance:
- i = 0 (unison), j = 3 (minor third), k = 0 (unison)
- v₁ = (0, 3): bass holds, soprano rises by a minor third. This moves from unison to minor third. The target (minor third) is imperfect, so no Fux constraint applies. ✓
- v₂ = (3, 0): bass rises by a minor third, soprano holds. This moves from minor third back to unison (target = 3 + 0 − 3 = 0). The target (unison) is perfect, but the motion is not parallel (bass moves by 3, soprano by 0). ✓
- Composition: (0 + 3, 3 + 0) = (3, 3). This voice leading from i = 0 has target 0 + 3 − 3 = 0 (unison, perfect). The motion is parallel (both voices move by 3 ≠ 0). This violates the Fux constraint. ✗

Therefore the permitted voice leadings are not closed under composition and do not form a category. ∎

**Remark.** This result has a musical interpretation: a composer cannot plan a two-step voice leading by considering only the endpoints. The intermediate step matters. Counterpoint is inherently *path-dependent*.

### 4.3 Theorem 3: The Perfect Consonance Bottleneck

**Theorem 4.3a** (Perfect Self-Loop Uniqueness / `perfect_self_loop_unique`). For any perfect consonance p ∈ P, the only permitted self-loop at p is the identity voice leading (0, 0).

*Proof sketch.* A self-loop at p is a voice leading (b, s) with target p, i.e., p + s − b = p, hence s = b. If b ≠ 0, then the motion is parallel into a perfect consonance—forbidden. Therefore b = s = 0. ∎

**Theorem 4.3b** (Imperfect Self-Loops / `imperfect_self_loops_all`). For any imperfect consonance q ∈ C \ P, there are exactly 12 permitted self-loops at q, one for each value k ∈ ℤ/12ℤ: the voice leading (k, k).

*Proof sketch.* A self-loop at q requires s = b. Since q is imperfect, the Fux constraint does not apply regardless of whether the motion is parallel. All 12 choices of b = s ∈ ℤ/12ℤ yield permitted self-loops. ∎

**Corollary.** The ratio of self-loops at imperfect vs. perfect consonances is exactly 12:1.

### 4.4 Theorem 4: Voice-Swap Asymmetry

**Theorem 4.4** (Voice-Swap Breaks Consonance / `voice_swap_breaks_consonance`). The involution ι : ℤ/12ℤ → ℤ/12ℤ defined by ι(i) = −i does not preserve the consonance set C = {0, 3, 4, 7, 8, 9}.

*Proof.* We have ι(7) = −7 ≡ 5 (mod 12). But 5 ∉ C. ∎

**Musical interpretation.** The interval from bass to soprano is measured upward. Swapping voices replaces interval i with −i (or equivalently 12 − i). The perfect fifth (7 semitones up) becomes a perfect fourth (5 semitones up), which is classified as *dissonant* against the bass in first-species counterpoint. This asymmetry—the fourth is consonant between upper voices but dissonant against the bass—is one of the most debated points in music theory, and our result shows it is an inescapable consequence of the structure of the consonance set in ℤ/12ℤ.

### 4.5 Theorem 5: Hom-Set Cardinalities

**Theorem 4.5** (Hom-Set Computation / `total_permitted_to_perfect`, `total_permitted_to_imperfect`).

Let H(j) = |{(i, b, s) : i ∈ C, (b,s) permitted from i to j}| be the total number of incoming permitted voice leadings to interval j from all consonant sources.

- If j ∈ P (perfect): H(j) = 61
- If j ∈ C \ P (imperfect): H(j) = 72

The total number of edges in the Counterpoint Quiver is:
$$|E| = 2 \times 61 + 4 \times 72 = 122 + 288 = 410$$

out of a theoretical maximum of 6 × 6 × 12 = 432 (if no Fux constraint applied and we allowed one edge per voice leading per source-target pair, with 12 voice leadings preserving each interval).

The **constraint ratio** is 410/432 ≈ 94.9%, meaning the Fux rule eliminates about 5.1% of all possible consonant-to-consonant voice leadings—but this elimination is concentrated at perfect consonances, where the reduction is (72 − 61)/72 ≈ 15.3%.

## 5. Generalization: Microtonal Counterpoint Systems

The `CounterpointSystem n` structure is defined for any n ≥ 1, enabling the study of counterpoint-like constraints in arbitrary equal temperaments.

### 5.1 19-TET

In 19-tone equal temperament, the consonant intervals differ. The "perfect fifth" is 11 steps (rather than 7), and the consonance set must be redefined according to the approximations of just intonation ratios. The Counterpoint System framework accommodates this by simply instantiating with different values of C, P, and n.

### 5.2 Structural Questions

For a general Counterpoint System (C, P, n):
- **Is strong connectivity always guaranteed?** Not necessarily—it depends on the arithmetic relationship between elements of C.
- **Is non-composability generic?** Likely yes, whenever |P| ≥ 1 and n ≥ 3, but this remains open.
- **How do the hom-set cardinalities scale?** For a system with |C| = c consonances and |P| = p perfect consonances, the self-loop count is n·(c − p) + p at identity self-loops alone, suggesting a bottleneck ratio of approximately n : 1.

## 6. The Categorical Perspective

### 6.1 Why Not a Category?

The Counterpoint Quiver has objects (consonant intervals) and morphisms (permitted voice leadings), identity morphisms (the identity voice leading at each consonance), and a natural notion of composition (componentwise addition of bass and soprano motions). It satisfies all category axioms *except* closure under composition (Theorem 4.2).

This makes the Counterpoint Quiver a *quiver* in the technical sense of category theory—a directed graph that may serve as the underlying graph of a category but is not itself one.

### 6.2 The Free Category

One can take the *free category* generated by the quiver: its morphisms are finite paths of permitted voice leadings. This category is well-defined and captures the notion of "reachable by a sequence of legal moves." The strong connectivity theorem (Theorem 4.1) ensures that this free category has morphisms between all pairs of objects—it is a *connected* category.

### 6.3 Thin Category Comparison

A natural question is whether the free category is equivalent to a *thin category*—a category where each hom-set has at most one morphism, equivalently a preorder. Since the free category has infinitely many morphisms between each pair of objects (one for each path), it is far from thin. However, the *quotient* by the equivalence relation "same source and target" yields a thin category on 6 objects, which is simply the complete preorder on C—reflecting the strong connectivity.

## 7. Discussion

### 7.1 Musical Implications

The bottleneck theorem provides a quantitative explanation for the experiential observation that approaching perfect consonances "feels" more constrained. The 15% reduction in available voice leadings is not merely a count—it affects the *probability* of a random voice leading landing on a permitted one, which may relate to perceptual expectations.

The non-composability result formalizes the pedagogical wisdom that counterpoint must be checked "beat by beat." No global shortcut exists.

### 7.2 Computational Music Theory

The Counterpoint System framework is inherently computational. Given any Counterpoint System (C, P, n), one can:
1. Enumerate all permitted voice leadings by brute force in O(|C|² · n²) time
2. Compute connectivity by standard graph algorithms
3. Test composability by checking all triples
4. Count hom-sets by direct summation

This opens the door to systematic computational exploration of counterpoint-like constraints across temperaments.

### 7.3 Limitations

Our model considers only the interval between two voices, not absolute pitch. Real counterpoint involves additional constraints: voice ranges, prohibition of certain melodic intervals (augmented seconds, tritones), preference for stepwise motion, and the rule against "direct" (similar) motion into perfect consonances (not just parallel motion). A richer model would incorporate these constraints, likely yielding a sparser quiver with more interesting structure.

## 8. Future Work

1. **Higher species counterpoint.** In second species (two notes against one) and beyond, the constraint structure involves sequences of intervals, yielding a higher-dimensional combinatorial object.

2. **Four-voice counterpoint.** With four voices, the state space expands to triples of intervals (or more precisely, elements of (ℤ/12ℤ)³), and the constraint structure becomes dramatically more complex.

3. **Topological analysis.** The simplicial complex generated by consonant chords (sets of mutually consonant intervals) may have interesting homological properties.

4. **Algorithmic composition.** Random walks on the Counterpoint Quiver, weighted by musical preferences, could serve as a generative model for counterpoint.

5. **Microtonal exploration.** Systematic computation of Counterpoint System invariants across temperament orders n = 12, 15, 17, 19, 22, 24, 31, 41, 53 would reveal how voice-leading geometry varies with tuning system.
