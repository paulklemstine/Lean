# Schanuel's Conjecture: Formalization of Conditional Transcendence Results

## Abstract

We present a machine-verified formalization of Schanuel's Conjecture and several of its conditional consequences in transcendental number theory. Working within a rigorous formal framework, we establish:

1. A precise formulation of Schanuel's Conjecture as a lower bound on transcendence degree for ℚ-adjunctions generated by complex numbers and their exponentials.
2. A structural lemma showing that ℚ-subalgebras generated entirely by algebraic elements have transcendence degree zero.
3. A proof that Schanuel's Conjecture implies the Hermite-Lindemann theorem (single element case): for any nonzero algebraic α, exp(α) is transcendental.
4. A corollary establishing conditional transcendence of *e*.
5. A sharpness result demonstrating the bound in Schanuel's Conjecture cannot be improved.
6. A two-point Lindemann consequence and a computational certification method for ℚ-linear independence.

All results are formalized at the level of machine-checkable proofs, providing certified logical dependencies between Schanuel's Conjecture and classical transcendence theorems.

**Keywords**: Schanuel's Conjecture, transcendence degree, Hermite-Lindemann theorem, algebraic independence, formal verification, transcendental number theory

## 1. Introduction

Schanuel's Conjecture, formulated by Stephen Schanuel in the early 1960s during Serge Lang's lectures at Columbia University, is among the most far-reaching open conjectures in transcendental number theory [1, 2]. It provides a single, unified prediction about the interaction between the exponential function and algebraic relations:

> **Schanuel's Conjecture.** If z₁, ..., zₙ are complex numbers that are linearly independent over ℚ, then the transcendence degree of ℚ(z₁, ..., zₙ, e^{z₁}, ..., e^{zₙ}) over ℚ is at least n.

The conjecture, if true, would subsume essentially all known results in transcendental number theory as special cases—including the Hermite-Lindemann theorem, the Gelfond-Schneider theorem, Baker's theorem on linear forms in logarithms, and the algebraic independence of e and π [3].

Despite its extraordinary power, Schanuel's Conjecture remains unproven in full generality. The best partial results include the Lindemann-Weierstrass theorem (the case where all zᵢ are algebraic), the six exponentials theorem, and various results in the Pfaffian setting due to Wilkie [4].

This paper takes a different approach: rather than attempting to prove the conjecture, we rigorously verify its *logical consequences*. We formalize Schanuel's Conjecture and prove, with machine-checked certainty, that it implies classical transcendence results. This work serves both as a certified "consequence map" for the conjecture and as a demonstration of formal methods in transcendental number theory.

### 1.1 Related Work

Formal verification of transcendence results has been explored in several contexts. Prior work has formalized the irrationality of √2, the transcendence of specific constants, and parts of algebraic number theory. Our contribution is distinguished by its focus on *conditional* results—theorems that take Schanuel's Conjecture as a hypothesis—and by the use of transcendence degree as the primary algebraic invariant.

The algebraic infrastructure we rely on—including `Algebra.trdeg`, `AlgebraicIndependent`, and `Algebra.adjoin`—is drawn from Mathlib's extensive library of commutative algebra.

## 2. Definitions and Framework

### 2.1 Schanuel's Conjecture: Formal Statement

We formalize the conjecture in two complementary forms.

**Algebraic Independence Form.** Given z : Fin n → ℂ that is ℚ-linearly independent, the *Schanuel Lower Bound Predicate* asserts the existence of n algebraically independent elements among the 2n elements {z₁, ..., zₙ, e^{z₁}, ..., e^{zₙ}} (see `SchanuelLowerBoundPredicate` in @Catalog/Algebra/Schanuel/Theorems.lean):

```
def SchanuelLowerBoundPredicate {n : ℕ} (z : Fin n → ℂ) : Prop :=
  LinearIndependent ℚ z →
    ∃ (e : Fin n ↪ Fin n ⊕ Fin n),
      AlgebraicIndependent ℚ (fun i => combinedTuple z (e i))
```

The global conjecture asserts this for all n and all tuples:

```
def SchanuelConjecture : Prop :=
  ∀ (n : ℕ) (z : Fin n → ℂ), SchanuelLowerBoundPredicate z
```

**Transcendence Degree Form.** An equivalent formulation uses transcendence degree directly (see `SchanuelProperty` in the supplementary formalization):

```
def SchanuelProperty : Prop :=
  ∀ (n : ℕ) (z : Fin n → ℂ),
    LinearIndependent ℚ z →
    (n : Cardinal) ≤ Algebra.trdeg ℚ
      (Algebra.adjoin ℚ (Set.range z ∪ Set.range (fun i => exp (z i))))
```

### 2.2 Supporting Structures

We introduce several auxiliary structures:

- **`ExpAlgConfig n`**: A structure packaging a tuple z : Fin n → ℂ together with its component-wise exponential `expTuple z`.
- **`combinedTuple z`**: The 2n-tuple (z₁, ..., zₙ, e^{z₁}, ..., e^{zₙ}), indexed by Fin n ⊕ Fin n.
- **`SchanuelDeficient z`**: A tuple that is ℚ-linearly independent yet fails the Schanuel lower bound—the conjecture asserts no such tuple exists.
- **`LindemannWeierstrassConfig n`**: A structure bundling a tuple of algebraic, ℚ-linearly independent complex numbers with a Schanuel hypothesis.
- **`IndependenceCertificate n m`**: A rational matrix of full column rank, certifying ℚ-linear independence.

### 2.3 Transcendence Degree

We use Mathlib's `Algebra.trdeg`, defined as the supremum of cardinalities of algebraically independent subsets. For a subalgebra A of ℂ over ℚ, `Algebra.trdeg ℚ A = 0` if and only if every element of A is algebraic over ℚ.

## 3. Main Results

### 3.1 Algebraic Generators Yield Transcendence Degree Zero

**Theorem** (`algebraic_set_adjoin_trdeg_zero`). *If S ⊆ ℂ is a set of elements all algebraic over ℚ, then*

$$\operatorname{trdeg}_ℚ\, ℚ[S] = 0.$$

*Proof sketch.* By adjoin induction: rational elements are algebraic, and the algebraic elements are closed under addition and multiplication. Hence every element of the adjoin is algebraic, giving trdeg = 0 by the characterization `trdeg_eq_zero_iff`. □

This result, proved in the supplementary formalization, serves as the key structural ingredient for deriving Hermite-Lindemann from Schanuel.

### 3.2 Rational Dependence Destroys Linear Independence

**Theorem** (`not_linearIndependent_of_rational_relation`, @Catalog/Algebra/Schanuel/Theorems.lean). *If there exist rational coefficients q₁, ..., qₙ, not all zero, with Σ qᵢ zᵢ = 0, then z₁, ..., zₙ are not ℚ-linearly independent.*

*Proof sketch.* Contrapositive of `Fintype.linearIndependent_iff`: linear independence forces all coefficients to vanish. □

**Corollary** (`schanuel_vacuous_on_dependent_tuples`). *No ℚ-linearly dependent tuple can be Schanuel-deficient.* The Schanuel lower bound is vacuously satisfied when the hypothesis of linear independence fails.

### 3.3 Algebraic Components Force Embedding to Exponential Side

**Lemma** (`embedding_maps_to_inr_of_algebraic`, @Catalog/Algebra/Schanuel/Theorems.lean). *If all zᵢ are algebraic over ℚ, and e : Fin n ↪ Fin n ⊕ Fin n selects n algebraically independent elements from the combined tuple, then e maps entirely into the exponential side (Sum.inr).*

*Proof sketch.* If e(i) = Sum.inl j for some i, then the selected element is z_j, which is algebraic. But an algebraically independent family cannot contain an algebraic element (since algebraic independence implies transcendence of each component). Contradiction. □

This lemma is the bridge between the Schanuel lower bound and concrete transcendence statements about exponentials.

### 3.4 Schanuel Implies Transcendence of Exponentials

**Theorem** (`schanuel_implies_exists_transcendental_exp`, @Catalog/Algebra/Schanuel/Theorems.lean). *Let z₁, ..., zₙ (n ≥ 1) be ℚ-linearly independent algebraic complex numbers. If the Schanuel lower bound holds for z, then at least one e^{zᵢ} is transcendental over ℚ.*

*Proof sketch.* By the Schanuel lower bound, there exist n algebraically independent elements among {z₁, ..., zₙ, e^{z₁}, ..., e^{zₙ}}. By Lemma 3.3, all selected elements lie on the exponential side. In particular, e^{z_{j₀}} is transcendental for some j₀. □

### 3.5 Schanuel Implies Hermite-Lindemann (Single Case)

**Theorem** (`schanuel_implies_hermite_lindemann_single`, supplementary formalization). *Assume Schanuel's Conjecture. Let α ∈ ℂ be nonzero and algebraic over ℚ. Then exp(α) is transcendental over ℚ.*

*Proof sketch.* Since α ≠ 0, the singleton {α} is ℚ-linearly independent. Apply Schanuel with n = 1: the transcendence degree of ℚ(α, exp(α)) is at least 1. If exp(α) were algebraic, then since α is also algebraic, every generator of ℚ(α, exp(α)) would be algebraic, giving trdeg = 0 by Theorem 3.1—contradicting trdeg ≥ 1. □

### 3.6 Schanuel Implies Transcendence of *e*

**Corollary** (`schanuel_implies_e_transcendental`, supplementary formalization). *Schanuel's Conjecture implies e = exp(1) is transcendental over ℚ.*

*Proof.* Take α = 1 in Theorem 3.5. The number 1 is nonzero and algebraic (it is rational). Therefore exp(1) = e is transcendental. □

### 3.7 Two-Point Lindemann Consequence

**Theorem** (`schanuel_pair_forces_transcendence`, @Catalog/Algebra/Schanuel/Theorems.lean). *Let a, b ∈ ℂ be algebraic over ℚ and ℚ-linearly independent. If the Schanuel lower bound holds for (a, b), then at least one of exp(a), exp(b) is transcendental.*

This is the n = 2 case of Theorem 3.4 and a stepping stone toward the full Lindemann-Weierstrass theorem.

### 3.8 Sharpness of the Schanuel Bound

**Theorem** (`schanuel_bound_tight_example`, supplementary formalization). *Assuming e is transcendental, the transcendence degree of ℚ(1, e) over ℚ is at most 1.*

*Proof sketch.* Since 1 is rational, ℚ(1, e) = ℚ(e). The transcendence of e yields an algebra isomorphism ℚ(e) ≅ ℚ[x] (the polynomial ring in one indeterminate), which has transcendence degree 1. This matches the Schanuel lower bound for z = (1), showing the bound n is optimal. □

### 3.9 Computational Certification of Linear Independence

**Theorem** (`coordinate_matrix_full_rank_implies_q_linearIndependent`, @Catalog/Algebra/Schanuel/Theorems.lean). *Let M be an m × n rational matrix of rank n, and let {b₁, ..., bₘ} be a ℚ-linearly independent basis. If zⱼ = Σᵢ Mᵢⱼ bᵢ for each j, then z₁, ..., zₙ are ℚ-linearly independent.*

*Proof sketch.* A linear relation Σ gⱼ zⱼ = 0 expands to Σᵢ (Σⱼ gⱼ Mᵢⱼ) bᵢ = 0. By linear independence of the basis, Σⱼ gⱼ Mᵢⱼ = 0 for all i—that is, M^T g = 0. Since M has full column rank, g = 0. □

This result provides a practical method for verifying the hypotheses of Schanuel's Conjecture for specific numerical examples.

## 4. The Logical Architecture

The dependency structure of our results is:

```
SchanuelConjecture / SchanuelProperty
        │
        ├── algebraic_set_adjoin_trdeg_zero
        │       │
        │       ├── schanuel_implies_hermite_lindemann_single
        │       │       │
        │       │       └── schanuel_implies_e_transcendental
        │       │
        │       └── schanuel_bound_tight_example (uses e transcendence)
        │
        ├── embedding_maps_to_inr_of_algebraic
        │       │
        │       └── schanuel_implies_exists_transcendental_exp
        │               │
        │               └── schanuel_pair_forces_transcendence
        │
        └── not_linearIndependent_of_rational_relation
                │
                └── schanuel_vacuous_on_dependent_tuples
```

The two formulation branches (algebraic independence via embeddings, and transcendence degree) capture complementary aspects of the conjecture and lead to different proof strategies for the consequences.

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 Methodological Contributions

Our formalization demonstrates several methodological points:

1. **Conditional reasoning at scale.** By taking Schanuel's Conjecture as a hypothesis, we can rigorously explore its consequences without waiting for a proof of the conjecture itself. The formal framework ensures that the logical dependencies are correct—no circular reasoning, no hidden assumptions.

2. **Algebraic infrastructure.** The structural lemma `algebraic_set_adjoin_trdeg_zero` is a general-purpose tool in formal algebra: it characterizes when subalgebras have trivial transcendence degree. This lemma is independent of Schanuel's Conjecture and has potential applications in other formalization projects.

3. **Computational-algebraic bridge.** The rank certification theorem connects numerical computation (matrix rank) with algebraic certainty (linear independence), enabling formal verification of specific instances of Schanuel's Conjecture.

### 5.2 Comparison with Classical Proofs

The classical proof of the Hermite-Lindemann theorem (due to Lindemann, 1882, and Weierstrass, 1885) uses analytic methods: careful integral estimates on polynomial-exponential functions, combined with divisibility arguments in the ring of algebraic integers. Our conditional proof from Schanuel is dramatically simpler—it requires only basic algebra (adjoin, trdeg, algebraic closure under addition and multiplication) and no analysis whatsoever.

This simplicity is, in a sense, the point of Schanuel's Conjecture: it isolates the *algebraic essence* of transcendence phenomena, stripping away the analytic apparatus that makes classical proofs technically demanding.

### 5.3 Limitations

Our formalization includes one result stated but not yet fully proved:

- **Full Lindemann-Weierstrass** (`schanuel_implies_lindemann_weierstrass_full`): For n algebraic, ℚ-linearly independent numbers, Schanuel implies all n exponentials are algebraically independent. The key missing ingredient is a "trdeg tower" lemma relating trdeg of an adjunction to the trdeg of its sub-adjunction modulo algebraic generators.

## 6. Future Directions

### 6.1 Full Lindemann-Weierstrass from Schanuel

The natural next step is proving the full Lindemann-Weierstrass theorem from Schanuel's Conjecture: if α₁, ..., αₙ are ℚ-linearly independent algebraic numbers, then exp(α₁), ..., exp(αₙ) are algebraically independent over ℚ. The key insight is that this requires a "trdeg tower" lemma: if S ⊆ T generates an algebra where every element of S is algebraic, then trdeg(adjoin T) = trdeg(adjoin(T \ S)).

### 6.2 Algebraic Independence of e and π

Under Schanuel's Conjecture, the pair (e, π) should be algebraically independent over ℚ. The proof would use SC with z = (1, iπ), noting that these are ℚ-linearly independent and their exponentials are e and −1 respectively.

### 6.3 Iterated Exponentials

Under Schanuel's Conjecture, the tower e, e^e, e^{e^e}, ... should consist of algebraically independent elements. The Hermite-Lindemann consequence provides the base case.

### 6.4 Unconditional Results

A direct formalization of Hermite's 1873 proof of the transcendence of *e*, using integral estimates rather than Schanuel's Conjecture, would provide an unconditional anchor point.
