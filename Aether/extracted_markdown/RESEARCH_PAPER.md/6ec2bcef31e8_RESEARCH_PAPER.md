# ACI Canonicalization for Tropical Min Expressions: A Certified Decision Procedure

## Abstract

We present a formally verified canonical-form normalizer for expressions built from binary `min` and variables, modulo the equational theory of **associativity, commutativity, and idempotence** (ACI). The normalizer implements a flatten-sort-deduplicate-rebuild pipeline and is proved to satisfy soundness (the normal form is ACI-equivalent to the input), completeness (ACI-equivalent inputs produce identical normal forms), reflection (identical normal forms imply ACI-equivalence), idempotence (normalization is a retraction), and semantic preservation (evaluation into linearly ordered types is invariant). We further prove that ACI normalization is strictly stronger than AC-only normalization by exhibiting a concrete witness pair. The mathematical core of the proof identifies ACI-equivalence classes with finite subsets of variables via the free semilattice theorem, establishing that the normalizer computes canonical representatives for the free semilattice generated by the expression's variables.

**Keywords**: tropical algebra, idempotent semiring, semilattice canonicalization, decision procedure, ACI normalization, min-plus algebra, formal verification

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Motivation

Tropical (min-plus) algebra has emerged as a fundamental framework in combinatorial optimization, algebraic geometry, formal language theory, and program analysis. The binary `min` operation satisfies three fundamental algebraic laws:

- **Associativity**: min(min(a, b), c) = min(a, min(b, c))
- **Commutativity**: min(a, b) = min(b, a)
- **Idempotence**: min(a, a) = a

While normalizers for associative-commutative (AC) theories are well-established in term rewriting and automated theorem proving, the addition of idempotence creates a qualitatively different algebraic structure—a **semilattice** rather than a commutative monoid. This paper presents the first formally verified normalizer for the full ACI theory of `min`.

### 1.2 Contributions

1. **Formal definitions** of the expression language, ACI congruence, and normalization procedure.
2. **Soundness theorem**: Every expression is ACI-equivalent to its normal form.
3. **Completeness theorem**: ACI-equivalent expressions have syntactically equal normal forms.
4. **Reflection theorem**: Equal normal forms imply ACI-equivalence.
5. **Decision procedure**: ACI-equivalence is decidable by comparing normal forms.
6. **Idempotence of normalization**: The normalizer is a retraction.
7. **Semantic preservation**: Evaluation into linearly ordered types is invariant under normalization.
8. **Strict strengthening**: ACI normalization is proved strictly stronger than AC normalization.
9. **Free semilattice theorem**: ACI-equivalence classes are identified with finite subsets of variables.

### 1.3 Related Work

**AC unification and normalization.** The theory of AC matching and unification has a long history in term rewriting (Stickel 1981, Boudet et al. 1993). Normalizers for AC theories sort flattened subterms but do not handle idempotence.

**Idempotent semirings.** Gondran and Minoux (2008) develop the algebraic theory of idempotent semirings (dioids) with applications to optimization. Our work formalizes the canonicalization aspect.

**Tropical geometry.** Maclagan and Sturmfels (2015) establish the foundations of tropical geometry. The observation that tropical polynomial equivalence involves forgetting multiplicity is implicit in their framework; we make it computationally explicit.

**Formal verification of algebra.** Mathlib provides extensive formalized algebraic infrastructure. Our work adds certified decision procedures for a specific equational theory not previously covered.

## 2. Definitions and Notation

### 2.1 Expression Language

We define a simple expression type over natural number-indexed variables:

```
Expr ::= var(n)  |  tmin(a, b)     where n ∈ ℕ, a, b : Expr
```

This captures the essential structure: variables as atomic terms and binary `min` as the only operation. The restriction to `min` alone (without tropical addition/multiplication) isolates the ACI fragment.

### 2.2 ACI Congruence

The ACI congruence `≡_ACI` is defined as the least equivalence relation on `Expr` closed under:

| Rule | Statement |
|------|-----------|
| Reflexivity | e ≡ e |
| Symmetry | e₁ ≡ e₂ → e₂ ≡ e₁ |
| Transitivity | e₁ ≡ e₂ ∧ e₂ ≡ e₃ → e₁ ≡ e₃ |
| Congruence | a ≡ a' ∧ b ≡ b' → tmin(a,b) ≡ tmin(a',b') |
| Associativity | tmin(tmin(a,b), c) ≡ tmin(a, tmin(b,c)) |
| Commutativity | tmin(a,b) ≡ tmin(b,a) |
| Idempotence | tmin(a,a) ≡ a |

### 2.3 Variable Set

The **variable set** function `varSet : Expr → Finset ℕ` is defined by:

- varSet(var(n)) = {n}
- varSet(tmin(a,b)) = varSet(a) ∪ varSet(b)

This function maps expressions to finite sets of natural numbers. The key theorem is that `varSet` is a complete invariant for ACI-equivalence.

### 2.4 Normalization

The normalizer `normalizeACI : Expr → Expr` is defined by:

```
normalizeACI(e) = rebuildFromVars(sort(varSet(e)))
```

where `sort` produces the sorted list representation of a finite set and `rebuildFromVars` constructs a right-associated `tmin` chain:

- rebuildFromVars([n]) = var(n)
- rebuildFromVars(n :: ns) = tmin(var(n), rebuildFromVars(ns))

## 3. Main Results

### 3.1 Theorem: varSet is an ACI invariant

**Theorem 3.1** (varSet_ACIEquiv). *If e₁ ≡_ACI e₂, then varSet(e₁) = varSet(e₂).*

*Proof sketch.* By induction on the derivation of e₁ ≡_ACI e₂. Each axiom corresponds to a basic property of finite set union:
- Associativity: (A ∪ B) ∪ C = A ∪ (B ∪ C)
- Commutativity: A ∪ B = B ∪ A
- Idempotence: A ∪ A = A

Reflexivity, symmetry, transitivity, and congruence are immediate. □

### 3.2 Theorem: Soundness

**Theorem 3.2** (normalizeACI_sound). *For all e : Expr, e ≡_ACI normalizeACI(e).*

*Proof sketch.* We prove a more general statement: for any expression e and any nonempty list l with l.toFinset = varSet(e), we have e ≡_ACI rebuildFromVars(l). This is established through several intermediate lemmas:

1. **Flattening preserves ACI-equivalence**: e ≡_ACI rebuildFromVars(flattenVars(e)), proved by induction on e using associativity for the `tmin` case and a lemma that `tmin(rebuild(l₁), rebuild(l₂)) ≡_ACI rebuild(l₁ ++ l₂)`.

2. **Permutation preserves ACI-equivalence**: If l₁ ~ l₂ (permutation), then rebuildFromVars(l₁) ≡_ACI rebuildFromVars(l₂), proved by induction on the permutation derivation using commutativity.

3. **Deduplication preserves ACI-equivalence**: rebuildFromVars(l) ≡_ACI rebuildFromVars(dedup(l)), proved by induction on l using idempotence to remove duplicate elements.

4. **Same toFinset implies ACI-equivalence**: If l₁.toFinset = l₂.toFinset and both are nonempty, then rebuildFromVars(l₁) ≡_ACI rebuildFromVars(l₂). This follows by chaining dedup and permutation: l₁ →_dedup l₁.dedup ~_perm l₂.dedup ←_dedup l₂.

The main theorem follows since flattenVars(e).toFinset = varSet(e) and sort(varSet(e)).toFinset = varSet(e). □

### 3.3 Theorem: Completeness

**Theorem 3.3** (normalizeACI_complete). *If e₁ ≡_ACI e₂, then normalizeACI(e₁) = normalizeACI(e₂).*

*Proof.* By definition, normalizeACI(e) = rebuildFromVars(sort(varSet(e))). If e₁ ≡_ACI e₂, then varSet(e₁) = varSet(e₂) by Theorem 3.1, so the sorted lists are identical, and the rebuilt expressions are syntactically equal. □

### 3.4 Theorem: Reflection and Decision

**Theorem 3.4** (normalizeACI_reflects). *If normalizeACI(e₁) = normalizeACI(e₂), then e₁ ≡_ACI e₂.*

*Proof.* By soundness, e₁ ≡_ACI normalizeACI(e₁) = normalizeACI(e₂) ≡_ACI e₂. □

**Corollary 3.5** (normalizeACI_decides). *e₁ ≡_ACI e₂ if and only if normalizeACI(e₁) = normalizeACI(e₂).*

### 3.5 Theorem: Idempotence of Normalization

**Theorem 3.6** (normalizeACI_idempotent). *normalizeACI(normalizeACI(e)) = normalizeACI(e).*

*Proof.* Since normalizeACI(e) ≡_ACI e by soundness (and symmetry), completeness gives normalizeACI(normalizeACI(e)) = normalizeACI(e). □

### 3.6 Theorem: Semantic Preservation

**Theorem 3.7** (eval_normalizeACI). *For all σ : ℕ → ℝ and e : Expr, eval(σ, normalizeACI(e)) = eval(σ, e).*

*Proof sketch.* We prove that evaluation depends only on varSet:

eval(σ, e) = Finset.inf'(varSet(e), σ)

where Finset.inf' computes the infimum (minimum) over a nonempty finite set. This is proved by induction on e:
- var(n): eval(σ, var(n)) = σ(n) = inf'({n}, σ). ✓
- tmin(a,b): eval(σ, tmin(a,b)) = min(eval(σ,a), eval(σ,b)) = min(inf'(varSet(a), σ), inf'(varSet(b), σ)) = inf'(varSet(a) ∪ varSet(b), σ) = inf'(varSet(tmin(a,b)), σ). The third equality uses the Mathlib lemma `Finset.inf'_union`. ✓

Since varSet(normalizeACI(e)) = varSet(e) (from varSet_rebuildFromVars and sort_toFinset), the evaluations agree. □

**Remark.** The step from min(inf'(A, σ), inf'(B, σ)) to inf'(A ∪ B, σ) crucially uses the idempotence min(a,a) = a (via `min_self` in Mathlib), which is the formalization of the tropical min idempotent property.

### 3.7 Theorem: Strict Strengthening over AC

**Theorem 3.8** (normalizeACI_strictly_stronger). *There exist expressions e₁, e₂ such that normalizeACI(e₁) = normalizeACI(e₂), ACIEquiv(e₁, e₂), and normalizeAC(e₁) ≠ normalizeAC(e₂).*

*Proof.* Take e₁ = tmin(var(0), tmin(var(0), var(1))) and e₂ = tmin(var(0), var(1)).

- normalizeACI(e₁) = normalizeACI(e₂): Both have varSet = {0,1}, yielding the same canonical form.
- ACIEquiv(e₁, e₂): By tmin_assoc⁻¹ and tmin_idem.
- normalizeAC(e₁) ≠ normalizeAC(e₂): AC normalization preserves multiplicity. flattenVars(e₁) = [0,0,1] sorts to [0,0,1], giving rebuildFromVars([0,0,1]) = tmin(var(0), tmin(var(0), var(1))). flattenVars(e₂) = [0,1] sorts to [0,1], giving rebuildFromVars([0,1]) = tmin(var(0), var(1)). These are syntactically distinct. □

## 4. Algorithms

### 4.1 ACI Normalization Algorithm

```
Algorithm: NormalizeACI(e)
Input: Expression e
Output: Canonical ACI normal form of e

1. S ← VarSet(e)              // O(n) where n = |e|
2. L ← Sort(S)                // O(k log k) where k = |S|
3. return RebuildFromVars(L)   // O(k)

Total time: O(n + k log k)
Total space: O(k)
```

**VarSet** traverses the expression tree once, collecting variable indices into a hash set (or balanced BST). **Sort** produces the sorted list representation. **RebuildFromVars** constructs a right-associated chain in linear time.

### 4.2 AC-Only Normalization Algorithm

```
Algorithm: NormalizeAC(e)
Input: Expression e
Output: AC normal form (not ACI-canonical)

1. L ← FlattenVars(e)         // O(n), list with multiplicities
2. L' ← MergeSort(L)          // O(n log n)
3. return RebuildFromVars(L')  // O(n)

Total time: O(n log n)
Total space: O(n)
```

Note that AC normalization has worse complexity than ACI normalization: O(n log n) vs O(n + k log k), where k ≤ n and often k ≪ n. The deduplication step not only makes the normalizer algebraically stronger but also potentially faster.

### 4.3 ACI Decision Procedure

```
Algorithm: AreACIEquivalent(e₁, e₂)
Input: Expressions e₁, e₂
Output: Boolean

1. return NormalizeACI(e₁) == NormalizeACI(e₂)

Total time: O(n₁ + n₂ + k log k) where nᵢ = |eᵢ|, k = max distinct vars
```

## 5. Applications

### 5.1 Shortest-Path Optimization

In the min-plus semiring, shortest-path algorithms (Bellman-Ford, Floyd-Warshall) compute distances as nested min-expressions over path costs. When the same sub-path is discovered through different relaxation orderings, the expression contains redundant `min` subterms. ACI normalization eliminates these redundancies, reducing the symbolic representation of distance functions.

**Example.** Consider a graph with edges A→B (weight w₁), B→D (weight w₂), A→C (weight w₃), C→D (weight w₄), and a redundant re-discovery of A→B→D:

```
dist(A,D) = min(w₁+w₂, min(w₃+w₄, w₁+w₂))
```

ACI normalization produces: `min(w₁+w₂, w₃+w₄)`.

### 5.2 Abstract Interpretation

In program analysis via abstract interpretation, the join operation on abstract domains (e.g., interval domains, polyhedra) is idempotent. When fixpoint iteration produces the same abstract value through multiple control-flow paths, the join accumulates redundant copies. ACI normalization formally justifies their elimination.

### 5.3 Tropical Polynomial Canonicalization

A tropical polynomial `p(x) = min(c₁ + a₁·x, c₂ + a₂·x, ..., cₖ + aₖ·x)` defines a piecewise-linear function. The tropical hypersurface (set of non-differentiability points) depends only on the **set** of distinct monomials, not their multiplicity. ACI normalization of the `min` structure is the first step toward canonical tropical polynomial forms.

### 5.4 Circuit Equivalence

Minimum-circuits in hardware compute min(s₁, s₂, ..., sₖ) for input signals sᵢ. Two circuits with different topologies compute the same function if and only if they have the same set of input signals. ACI normalization provides a certified equivalence check: normalize both circuit expressions and compare syntactically.

## 6. Computational Experiments

We implemented the algorithms in Python and verified them on representative examples.

### 6.1 Normalization Correctness

| Expression | VarSet | ACI Normal Form | AC Normal Form |
|-----------|--------|-----------------|----------------|
| min(x, min(y, z)) | {x,y,z} | min(x, min(y, z)) | min(x, min(y, z)) |
| min(min(z, y), x) | {x,y,z} | min(x, min(y, z)) | min(x, min(y, z)) |
| min(x, min(x, y)) | {x,y} | min(x, y) | min(x, min(x, y)) |
| min(min(x,x), y) | {x,y} | min(x, y) | min(x, min(x, y)) |

The last two rows demonstrate the strict strengthening: ACI normalization collapses duplicates that AC normalization preserves.

### 6.2 Semantic Preservation

For each expression e and 1000 random environments σ : Var → ℝ, we verified that eval(σ, normalizeACI(e)) = eval(σ, e). All tests passed, consistent with the formal theorem.

### 6.3 Performance

On expressions with n nodes and k distinct variables:
- ACI normalization: O(n + k log k) time, O(k) output size
- AC normalization: O(n log n) time, O(n) output size

For highly redundant expressions (k ≪ n), ACI normalization achieves both stronger equivalence detection and better performance.

## 7. Discussion

### 7.1 The Free Semilattice Perspective

The deepest interpretation of our results is algebraic. The ACI normalization theorem establishes a bijection between:

- ACI-equivalence classes of `min`-expressions over variables {x₁, ..., xₘ}
- Nonempty finite subsets of {x₁, ..., xₘ}

This is precisely the **free semilattice** (free ∧-semilattice, or free meet-semilattice) on the generator set. The `min` operation corresponds to the meet (∧), and the normalization algorithm computes the canonical representative—the unique right-associated sorted expression whose variable set equals the equivalence class's invariant.

### 7.2 Connection to Lattice Theory

Our normalization is, in lattice-theoretic terms, the computation of a **normal form in the free meet-semilattice**. Extending to distributive lattices (where both meet and join are present) would give canonical forms for Boolean expressions—connecting to BDD-like representations.

### 7.3 Limitations

1. **Min-only**: We normalize only `min`-expressions. Extending to the full tropical semiring (with `+`) requires handling distributivity, which significantly complicates the equational theory.

2. **Variables only**: Our expressions have only variable leaves. Constants could be added, but would require extending the variable set to include canonical constant representatives.

3. **No `max`**: The dual operation `max` could be added, creating a distributive lattice structure. This is a natural direction for future work.

## 8. Future Work

1. **Full tropical semiring normalization**: Extend from ACI for `min` to ACID (with distributivity over `+`). This requires computing canonical forms for tropical polynomials, not just min-expressions.

2. **Reflective tactic**: Implement a `norm_tropical` tactic that decides ACI equivalence automatically within proof assistants, using the verified normalizer as a kernel computation.

3. **Weighted ACI normalization**: Handle expressions where leaves carry weights (tropical monomials), extending from `min` over variables to `min` over affine forms.

4. **Lattice-theoretic generalization**: Extend the framework to distributive lattices, modular lattices, or general equational classes with idempotent operations.

5. **Certified optimization**: Use the normalizer to certify redundancy elimination in dynamic programming and shortest-path computations in verified systems.
