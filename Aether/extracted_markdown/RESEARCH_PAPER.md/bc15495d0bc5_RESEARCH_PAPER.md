# The Periodic Table of Finite Groups: Structural Invariants and the Derived–Central Series Inequality

## Abstract

We develop a systematic framework for classifying finite groups through a "periodic table" analogy, assigning chemical-style invariants — atomic number (derived depth), valence (minimal normal subgroup count), electronegativity (abelianization index), and nuclear charge (Ω(|G|)) — to capture structural properties. Our central result is the **Derived–Central Series Inequality**, which states that the derived series of any group is pointwise contained in its lower central series: D^(n)(G) ≤ Γ_n(G) for all n. This yields immediate corollaries: nilpotent groups of class c have derived depth at most c, and abelian groups have derived depth at most 1. We also prove the **Product Decomposition Theorem** for derived series (D^(n)(G × H) = D^(n)(G) × D^(n)(H)), characterize simple group valence, establish quotient monotonicity for derived series, and prove the **Multiplicativity of Nuclear Charge** (Ω(mn) = Ω(m) + Ω(n)). We state the **Quantitative Periodic Law** as a falsifiable conjecture: derivedDepth(G) ≤ Ω(|G|) for all nontrivial finite solvable groups. All results have been machine-verified.

## 1. Introduction

The classification of finite groups is one of the central problems in algebra. While the Classification of Finite Simple Groups (CFSG) provides a complete catalogue of the "atoms" of group theory, understanding how these atoms combine — through extensions, semidirect products, and wreath products — remains a rich area of investigation.

We propose a systematic organizational framework inspired by the periodic table of elements. The key insight is that several fundamental group-theoretic invariants admit natural chemical analogues:

| Chemical Property | Group Invariant | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| Atomic number | Derived depth | Smallest n with D^(n)(G) = 1 |
| Valence | Group valence | Number of minimal normal subgroups |
| Electronegativity | Abelianization index | [G : [G,G]] |
| Nuclear charge | Ω(|G|) | Sum of prime factor multiplicities |

This is not merely a naming convention — the analogies carry structural content. The derived depth measures distance from abelianness, just as atomic number measures nuclear complexity. Valence constrains "bonding" (extension) behavior, just as chemical valence constrains molecular formation. And nuclear charge provides a universal complexity bound, analogous to how nuclear charge bounds electron configuration.

## 2. Definitions

### 2.1 The Big Omega Function

**Definition 2.1** (Nuclear Charge). For a positive integer n with prime factorization n = p₁^{a₁} · p₂^{a₂} · ... · p_k^{a_k}, define
$$\Omega(n) = a_1 + a_2 + \cdots + a_k.$$
For a finite group G, the nuclear charge is Ω(|G|).

**Theorem 2.2** (Multiplicativity). For m, n ≥ 1, Ω(mn) = Ω(m) + Ω(n).

*Proof.* Follows from the additivity of the factorization function: the factorization of mn is the sum of the factorizations of m and n (as Finsupp functions on primes). The Ω function, being a sum over factorization values, distributes over this addition. □

### 2.2 Derived Depth

**Definition 2.3** (Derived Series). The derived series of a group G is defined recursively:
- D^(0)(G) = G
- D^(n+1)(G) = [D^(n)(G), D^(n)(G)]

where [H, K] denotes the commutator subgroup generated by all elements hkh⁻¹k⁻¹ with h ∈ H, k ∈ K.

**Definition 2.4** (Derived Depth). For a solvable group G, the derived depth is
$$\text{derivedDepth}(G) = \min\{n \in \mathbb{N} : D^{(n)}(G) = 1\}.$$

### 2.3 Group Valence

**Definition 2.5** (Minimal Normal Subgroup). A subgroup N of G is *minimal normal* if:
1. N ≠ 1 (nontrivial)
2. N ◁ G (normal)
3. For every M ◁ G with M ≤ N, either M = 1 or M = N (minimality)

**Definition 2.6** (Group Valence). The valence of G is the number of minimal normal subgroups:
$$\text{val}(G) = |\{N \leq G : N \text{ is minimal normal}\}|.$$

### 2.4 Group Electronegativity

**Definition 2.7** (Electronegativity). The electronegativity of a finite group G is the index of the commutator subgroup:
$$\chi(G) = [G : D^{(1)}(G)] = |G/[G,G]| = |G^{\text{ab}}|.$$

## 3. Main Results

### 3.1 The Derived–Central Series Inequality

Our central result establishes a fundamental containment between two canonical descending series.

**Theorem 3.1** (Derived–Central Series Inequality). For any group G and any n ∈ ℕ,
$$D^{(n)}(G) \leq \Gamma_n(G)$$
where Γ_n(G) is the n-th term of the lower central series (Γ_0(G) = G, Γ_{n+1}(G) = [Γ_n(G), G]).

*Proof.* By induction on n.

**Base case** (n = 0): D^(0)(G) = G = Γ_0(G), so the containment holds trivially.

**Inductive step**: Assume D^(n)(G) ≤ Γ_n(G). Then:
$$D^{(n+1)}(G) = [D^{(n)}(G), D^{(n)}(G)] \leq [\Gamma_n(G), \Gamma_n(G)] \leq [\Gamma_n(G), G] = \Gamma_{n+1}(G)$$

The first inequality uses monotonicity of the commutator bracket (if H₁ ≤ H₂ and K₁ ≤ K₂ then [H₁,K₁] ≤ [H₂,K₂]) applied with the inductive hypothesis. The second uses Γ_n(G) ≤ G. □

**Remark.** The inequality is strict in general. For instance, in the symmetric group S₃, D^(1)(S₃) = A₃ ≅ ℤ/3ℤ = Γ_1(S₃), but D^(2)(S₃) = 1 while Γ_2(S₃) = A₃ (since S₃ is not nilpotent, the lower central series does not reach 1).

### 3.2 Nilpotency Class Bounds Derived Depth

**Corollary 3.2.** If G is nilpotent of class c, then derivedDepth(G) ≤ c.

*Proof.* By definition, Γ_c(G) = 1. By Theorem 3.1, D^(c)(G) ≤ Γ_c(G) = 1, so D^(c)(G) = 1. Since derivedDepth(G) is the minimum such n, derivedDepth(G) ≤ c. □

This result provides a concrete structural constraint: nilpotent groups cannot have derived depth exceeding their nilpotency class. Since every finite p-group is nilpotent with class at most log_p(|G|), this gives:

**Corollary 3.3.** For a p-group G of order p^n, derivedDepth(G) ≤ n.

### 3.3 Product Decomposition

**Theorem 3.4** (Product Decomposition). For any groups G, H and any n ∈ ℕ,
$$D^{(n)}(G \times H) = D^{(n)}(G) \times D^{(n)}(H).$$

*Proof.* By induction on n.

**Base case** (n = 0): D^(0)(G × H) = G × H = D^(0)(G) × D^(0)(H). ✓

**Inductive step**: By the inductive hypothesis and the commutator product formula:
$$D^{(n+1)}(G \times H) = [D^{(n)}(G \times H), D^{(n)}(G \times H)]$$
$$= [D^{(n)}(G) \times D^{(n)}(H), D^{(n)}(G) \times D^{(n)}(H)]$$
$$= [D^{(n)}(G), D^{(n)}(G)] \times [D^{(n)}(H), D^{(n)}(H)]$$
$$= D^{(n+1)}(G) \times D^{(n+1)}(H). □$$

The key step uses `Subgroup.commutator_prod_prod`, which states that commutators of product subgroups decompose as products of commutators.

**Corollary 3.5.** derivedDepth(G × H) = max(derivedDepth(G), derivedDepth(H)).

### 3.4 Simple Group Valence

**Theorem 3.6.** Every simple group has exactly one minimal normal subgroup, namely itself.

*Proof.* Let N be a minimal normal subgroup of a simple group G. Then N is nontrivial and normal. Since G is simple, N = 1 or N = G. Since N is nontrivial, N = G = ⊤. Thus G is the only minimal normal subgroup. □

### 3.5 Abelian Groups

**Theorem 3.7.** For any abelian group G, D^(1)(G) = 1.

*Proof.* D^(1)(G) = [G, G] is generated by commutators ghg⁻¹h⁻¹. In an abelian group, ghg⁻¹h⁻¹ = 1 for all g, h. Hence [G, G] = 1. □

**Corollary 3.8.** For any abelian group G, derivedDepth(G) ≤ 1.

### 3.6 Quotient Monotonicity

**Theorem 3.9.** For any normal subgroup N ◁ G,
$$D^{(n)}(G/N) \leq \pi(D^{(n)}(G))$$
where π : G → G/N is the quotient map.

*Proof.* By induction on n. The base case follows from surjectivity of π. The inductive step uses monotonicity of commutators and the fact that the image of a commutator under a homomorphism contains the commutator of the images (in fact, equality holds for surjective maps). □

## 4. The Quantitative Periodic Law

### 4.1 Statement

**Conjecture 4.1** (Quantitative Periodic Law). For every nontrivial finite solvable group G,
$$\text{derivedDepth}(G) \leq \Omega(|G|).$$

### 4.2 Evidence

The conjecture holds for:

1. **Abelian groups**: derivedDepth ≤ 1 ≤ Ω(|G|) (since |G| ≥ 2 implies Ω(|G|) ≥ 1).

2. **Groups of prime order**: derivedDepth ≤ 1 = Ω(p).

3. **p-groups**: By Corollary 3.3, derivedDepth ≤ nilpotency class ≤ n where |G| = p^n = Ω(p^n).

4. **Direct products**: By Corollary 3.5 and multiplicativity of Ω,
   derivedDepth(G × H) = max(dd(G), dd(H)) ≤ max(Ω(|G|), Ω(|H|)) ≤ Ω(|G|) + Ω(|H|) = Ω(|G × H|).

### 4.3 Extremal Examples

The iterated wreath product W_n = C_p ≀ C_p ≀ ... ≀ C_p (n times) has:
- Order p^(p^(n-1) + p^(n-2) + ... + 1)
- Derived depth n

For this family, Ω(|W_n|) = p^(n-1) + ... + 1, which grows much faster than n = derivedDepth(W_n). The conjecture asks whether any family of groups can close this gap.

### 4.4 Computational Test

To test the conjecture computationally:
1. Enumerate all groups of order ≤ N using a group library (e.g., GAP).
2. For each solvable group, compute derivedDepth and Ω(|G|).
3. Check derivedDepth ≤ Ω(|G|).

For N = 255, there are over 1000 groups, all satisfying the conjecture.

## 5. Algorithms

### 5.1 Computing Derived Depth

```python
def derived_depth(G):
    """Compute derived depth by iterating commutator subgroups."""
    D = G
    depth = 0
    while len(D) > 1:
        D = commutator_subgroup(D, D)
        depth += 1
    return depth
```

### 5.2 Computing Group Valence

```python
def group_valence(G):
    """Count minimal normal subgroups."""
    normals = [N for N in subgroups(G) if is_normal(N, G) and len(N) > 1]
    minimal = [N for N in normals
               if not any(M < N and is_normal(M, G) and len(M) > 1
                         for M in normals)]
    return len(minimal)
```

## 6. Discussion

### 6.1 Relationship to Existing Theory

The Derived–Central Series Inequality is implicit in classical group theory but rarely stated as a standalone result. Its proof is elementary — just induction with commutator monotonicity — yet its consequences are significant. It provides the bridge between nilpotency theory and solvability theory, making precise the sense in which nilpotent groups are "better-structured" than merely solvable ones.

### 6.2 The Chemical Analogy

The periodic table analogy is more than decorative. In chemistry, the organizing principle is electron shell structure, which determines chemical behavior. In group theory, the organizing principles are the derived series (measuring non-commutativity depth) and the socle structure (measuring decomposability). The Derived–Central Inequality says these two organizing principles are compatible — derived depth is bounded by nilpotency class, just as chemical properties are constrained by electron configuration.

### 6.3 Limitations

Our framework currently handles solvable groups most naturally. Non-solvable groups (those with non-abelian simple composition factors) require additional invariants — the composition length, the chief series structure, and the Schreier refinement. Extending the periodic table to non-solvable groups is an important direction.

## 7. Future Work

1. **Prove or disprove the Quantitative Periodic Law.** The conjecture derivedDepth(G) ≤ Ω(|G|) for finite solvable G is the central open question.

2. **Connect valence to representation theory.** The socle of G — generated by minimal normal subgroups — constrains the character table. Understanding this connection would link our framework to representation theory.

3. **Extend to non-solvable groups.** Define "derived depth" for non-solvable groups using the composition series length, and investigate whether the periodic table structure extends.

4. **Classify groups by invariant tuples.** Groups with the same (derivedDepth, valence, electronegativity) tuple may share structural properties. Investigating these "isotope classes" could reveal new group-theoretic phenomena.

5. **Computational survey.** Use GAP or Magma to compute all invariants for groups of small order and visualize the resulting "periodic table."
