# Future Directions: Tropical Matroid Theory

This research cycle formalized, over a finite ground type, the combinatorial core of
the Ardila–Klivans identification of the **Bergman fan** of a matroid `M` with the
**tropical linear space cut out by the circuit ideal** (`bergmanFan_eq_tropicalLinearSpace`),
together with its first structural features: all-ones translation invariance
(`bergmanFan_translation_invariant`), cone structure (`bergmanFan_smul_nonneg`), the
coloop fragment of the lineality space (`bergmanFan_coloop_shift`), the loop boundary
case (`bergmanFan_loop_empty`), and membership of constant weights in the loopless case
(`const_mem_bergmanFan_of_loopless`). The following directions extend this foundation.

## 1. Lineality dimension equals the number of connected components

The lineality space of `bergmanFan M` (the largest linear subspace it contains) should
have dimension exactly the number of connected components of `M`. We proved the two
extreme fragments — the all-ones line is always in the lineality space, and each coloop
adds an independent free coordinate. The general statement is the next milestone: define
`lineality M := { v | ∀ w ∈ bergmanFan M, w + v ∈ bergmanFan M }` and prove
`Module.rank ℝ (lineality M) = (M.connectedComponents).card` for loopless `M`.

The key insight is that a translation direction `v` preserves every `MinAttainedTwice`
condition **iff** `v` is constant on each connected component, because two ground
elements lie in a common circuit exactly when they are in the same component, and a
circuit's tie can only be maintained under translation when the moved coordinates shift
together. This reduces a topological dimension count to the purely combinatorial
"same-component ⇔ co-circuit" relation already visible in `bergmanFan_coloop_shift`.

Why now? Mathlib's `Matroid` API now exposes connectivity and the circuit/component
relationship, and our `minAttainedTwice_congr` already isolates the "depends only on
values on `C`" principle that the constant-on-components argument iterates over circuits.

## 2. Direct sums of matroids give products of Bergman fans

If `M = M₁ ⊕ M₂` is a direct sum on disjoint ground sets, then
`bergmanFan M = bergmanFan M₁ × bergmanFan M₂` under the canonical coordinate splitting.
This is the multiplicative counterpart of Direction 1 and the precise sense in which the
Bergman fan "sees" the block structure of a disconnected matroid.

The key insight is that the circuits of a direct sum are exactly the circuits of the two
summands (no circuit crosses the partition), so the defining family of `MinAttainedTwice`
constraints splits as a disjoint union, and each constraint involves only one block's
coordinates. The set-level product equality then follows constraint-by-constraint with
no new geometry.

Why now? The circuit-set characterization of direct sums is elementary and our Bergman
fan is *defined* by quantifying over circuits, so the proof is a clean refactor of
`bergmanFan_eq_tropicalLinearSpace`'s circuit-by-circuit reasoning rather than new theory.

## 3. Nested (shifted) matroids yield tropical linear *subspaces* with explicit cones

Nested matroids — those whose cyclic flats form a chain — are the matroids whose Bergman
fans are simplicial in a strong sense. The conjecture: for a nested matroid the Bergman
fan equals an explicit union of cones indexed by chains of cyclic flats, and in
particular it is a *coarsening-free* tropical linear space whose maximal cones are in
bijection with maximal flags of the nesting chain.

The key insight is that for nested matroids the circuit system is generated by a chain of
"interval" circuits, so the `MinAttainedTwice` conditions become *nested* tie conditions
that can be solved greedily from the smallest flat outward, producing one cone per
admissible ordering of the chain. This converts the global fan description into a
recursive, certifiably finite cone enumeration.

Why now? The greedy/recursive structure matches the descent-certificate machinery already
present in this catalog (`Pythagorean/ValuatedMatroidExchange.lean`: `TropicalDepthCertificate`,
`tropical_descent_chain_bound`), so the cone enumeration can reuse its no-infinite-descent
termination guarantee instead of building new well-foundedness infrastructure.

## 4. Valuated matroids deform the Bergman fan into the Dressian

Replacing the constant circuit coefficients by a valuation `val : circuits → ℝ` turns the
`MinAttainedTwice` condition into "the minimum of `w + val` over each circuit is attained
twice", whose solution set is the tropical linear space of a *valuated* matroid. The
conjecture: this deformed locus is nonempty and pure-dimensional exactly when `val`
satisfies the tropical Plücker (three-term) relations, placing the deformed Bergman fan
inside the Dressian.

The key insight is that the unweighted theorems in this file are the `val ≡ 0` slice of a
one-parameter family, and the exchange inequality already formalized in
`ValuatedMatroidExchange.lean` (`exists_exchange_nondecrease`, `TropicalExchangeFamily.exchange`)
is precisely the local certificate that the deformed tie conditions remain simultaneously
satisfiable. The bridge is to show that family's two-basis valuation inequality implies
the per-circuit "min attained twice" of the deformed `bergmanFan`.

Why now? Both halves already live in this catalog — the geometric `bergmanFan`
(this file) and the algebraic `TropicalExchangeFamily` (Pythagorean) — so this is a
genuine cross-domain bridge connecting tropical geometry to M-convex exchange, not new
foundations.

## 5. The Bergman fan is closed and conic-convex along circuit walls

Beyond being a cone (`bergmanFan_smul_nonneg`) and translation-invariant
(`bergmanFan_translation_invariant`), the Bergman fan is topologically closed in `ℝ^E`
and is a finite union of polyhedral cones. The conjecture: `bergmanFan M` is closed
(as a subset of `α → ℝ` with the product topology) and equals the support of a pure
polyhedral fan of dimension `M.rank`.

The key insight is that each single-circuit condition `MinAttainedTwice w C` is a finite
union of closed halfspace-intersections (one per choice of the tied pair `i, j ∈ C`),
so the whole fan is a finite intersection of finite unions of closed cones — closedness
and the fan structure are then finitary consequences with no limiting arguments.

Why now? Our `MinAttainedTwice` predicate is already written as an existential over a
finite index set of linear (in)equalities, so the closedness proof is a direct
`Set.Finite`/`IsClosed.iInter`/`IsClosed.union` assembly over circuits and tied pairs,
well within reach of current Mathlib topology API.
