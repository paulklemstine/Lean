# Future Directions: Algebraic Closure Operators as Idempotent Semimodule Nuclei

The new file `Catalog/Bridges/AlgebraEMLNucleusSemilattice.lean` establishes the
order-theoretic backbone of the "closure operators as nuclei" program: a
`JoinNucleus` on a complete lattice `L`, its fixed points as a `Moore set`, the
meet/nucleus law (`sInf_fixed_mem`), the least-fixed-point description
(`apply_eq_sInf_fixed`), the join law and its supremum generalization
(`apply_sup`, `apply_sSup`), the **reconstruction equivalence**
`nucleus_moore_equiv : JoinNucleus L ≃ {M // IsMooreSet M}`, and the bridge to the
catalog's `SetClosureOperator` (`setClosureToNucleus`,
`setClosure_closed_eq_fixedPoints`, `setClosure_closed_isMooreSet`).

These directions push the bridge from the *order-theoretic* (semilattice) layer into
the *algebraic* (semimodule) and *computational* (finite saturation) layers that the
catalog already gestures at in `Bridges/AlgebraEMLClosureComputation.lean`
(`ClosureSemimoduleSystem`, `ProbeFamily`, `ClosureStableProbe`) and
`Algebra/EMLClosureUnification/Core.lean` (`IsEMLClosureOn`, `EMLClosureFixed`).

## 1. The fixed-point family is a complete lattice and the equivalence is an order-isomorphism

Conjecture: for any `N : JoinNucleus L`, the type `N.fixedPoints` carries a canonical
`CompleteLattice` structure with `sInf` inherited from `L` and `sSup' T := N (sSup T)`,
and `nucleus_moore_equiv` upgrades to an isomorphism of the poset of nuclei (ordered
pointwise) with the poset of Moore sets (ordered by reverse inclusion).

The key insight is that `apply_sSup` already proves `N (sSup T)` is the least fixed
point above the family `T`, so the suprema needed for completeness are *definable from
the nucleus itself* — no extra data is required, only repackaging
`sInf_fixed_mem` + `apply_sSup` into the `CompleteLattice` fields.

Why now? The two halves of the round trip (`mooreNucleus_fixedPoints`,
`mooreNucleus_apply_of_isJoinNucleus`) are already proven, so the bijection exists; the
only missing piece is monotonicity of both directions, which is a short order argument
on top of the lemmas already in the file.

## 2. Genuine idempotent-semimodule packaging on the powerset

Conjecture: on `L = Set α` viewed as a module over the Boolean idempotent semiring
`(Prop, ∨, ∧)` (equivalently the two-element tropical semiring), every `JoinNucleus`
is exactly an idempotent-semilinear, extensive, idempotent endomap, and `apply_sSup`
is its `sSup`-(semi)linearity law. Formally: `N` commutes with the semimodule action up
to closure, `N (c • x ⊔ y) = N (c • N x ⊔ N y)`.

The key insight is that the supremum law `apply_sSup` proved here is *precisely* the
join-semimodule linearity condition once `⊔` is read as idempotent addition and `sSup`
as the infinite sum, so the semimodule statement is a re-typing of an already-proven
theorem rather than new analysis.

Why now? The catalog's `ClosureSemimoduleSystem` provides the target algebraic
interface but states closure only as a raw `Set σ → Set σ`; connecting it to the
proven `apply_sSup` immediately turns those systems into semimodule nuclei and is
falsifiable — if the scalar-shift law fails, the chosen semiring action is wrong.

## 3. Finite computable saturation with canonical minimal output

Conjecture: for `Fintype α` and `DecidableEq α`, the `mooreClosure` of a finite Moore
family is computed by a terminating fixed-point iteration `s ↦ s ∪ ⋃ {generated steps}`
that stabilizes in at most `Fintype.card α` rounds, and its output equals the
order-minimal closed superset, matching `mooreNucleus`.

The key insight is that on a finite lattice the descending/ascending chain condition
makes `apply_eq_sInf_fixed` *executable*: the abstract `sInf` over closed supersets
coincides with the least fixed point reached by bounded iteration, so minimality
(already abstractly proven via `apply_le_of_le_fixed`) certifies the algorithm's output.

Why now? `Bridges/AlgebraEMLClosureComputation.lean` and the catalog's
`FiniteClosureSystem` (on `Finset α`) supply the finite scaffolding, and the
abstract minimality theorem is already in hand; the remaining work is an executable
`Finset` recursion plus a termination/length bound, which is self-contained.

## 4. Probe families correspond to finitely generated fixed-point objects

Conjecture: a `ClosureStableProbe` (catalog) `p : σ → K` is constant on every fixed
point of the induced nucleus, and the lattice of probe-invariant fixed points is the
sublattice generated by the fibers of `p`; hence "closure-stable probes" are in
bijection with finitely generated subobjects of the fixed-point lattice.

The key insight is that `ClosureStableProbe` says exactly that `p` factors through the
closure, i.e. `p` is `N`-invariant, so the probe-closed elements form a *sub-Moore
set* and inherit the reconstruction equivalence of this file at the level of
sublattices.

Why now? The probe machinery exists in the catalog but is not yet tied to fixed-point
structure; `setClosure_closed_eq_fixedPoints` provides the missing identification of
closed sets with fixed points, making the probe-to-subobject correspondence a direct
corollary to formalize and test.

## 5. Frame nuclei vs. join nuclei: the meet-preservation boundary

Conjecture: a `JoinNucleus` on a frame `L` is a *frame nucleus* (in the sense of
pointless topology, preserving finite meets `N (x ⊓ y) = N x ⊓ N y`) **iff** its
Moore set of fixed points is itself a sub-frame; and there exist closure operators
(e.g. convex-hull or affine-span style operators on a finite Boolean lattice) that are
join nuclei but not frame nuclei, pinning down where the analogy with Mathlib's
`Order.Frame` `Nucleus` breaks.

The key insight is that the present development deliberately drops meet-preservation
(only `sInf`-closure of fixed points is forced), so frame nuclei are exactly the
meet-preserving refinement — making the gap between the two notions a precise,
checkable extra hypothesis rather than a vague analogy.

Why now? Mathlib ships `Order.Nucleus`/frame theory and this file ships the general
join-nucleus theory; juxtaposing them isolates the exact meet-law hypothesis, and the
conjecture is falsifiable by a single explicit finite counterexample (a convex-type
closure where `N (x ⊓ y) ≠ N x ⊓ N y`).
