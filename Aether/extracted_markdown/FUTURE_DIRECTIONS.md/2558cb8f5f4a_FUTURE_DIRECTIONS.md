# Future Directions: Convergent Rewrite Systems as Quotient Optimizers

## Synthesis

The theorems established in this work—most centrally, the Master Theorem (`nf_preserves_eval`) and the quotient factorization (`nf_factors_through_quotient`)—reveal that convergent rewriting is not merely a technique for simplifying expressions but a universal optimization principle grounded in quotient semantics. Every convergent presentation of an equational theory automatically generates a certified optimizer for all models of that theory. This synthesis opens five interconnected research directions: (1) scaling to many-sorted and higher-order theories, (2) extracting efficient computable normalizers, (3) bridging to Gröbner basis computation, (4) connecting to e-graph equality saturation, and (5) characterizing the compression power of canonical forms. Each direction builds on the certified normalizer infrastructure and extends the quotient optimizer paradigm into new mathematical and computational territory.

---

## Direction 1: Many-Sorted and Higher-Order Convergent Optimization

**Conjecture:** The Master Theorem (`nf_preserves_eval`) extends to many-sorted and simply-typed equational theories: given a convergent rewrite system R on many-sorted terms that is sound for a many-sorted equational theory E, the normal-form map preserves semantics in every many-sorted Σ-algebra satisfying E.

**Test:** Formalize a two-sorted equational theory (e.g., a ring acting on a module) with a convergent rewrite system. Instantiate the theory with at least 5 concrete models (ℤ acting on ℤ², ℚ acting on ℚ³, etc.) and verify computationally that normal forms preserve evaluation in each model across 10,000 random terms per model.

**Impact:** This would extend the quotient optimizer paradigm from single-sorted to multi-sorted algebra, covering the majority of algebraic structures encountered in practice (rings with modules, groups with actions, categories with functors).

**Catalog References:** `Pythagorean/ConvergentRewriteOptimizer.lean` — `nf_preserves_eval`, `ConvergentOptimizer`, `CertifiedNormalizer`

**Proof Strategy:** Define `ManySortedTerm` indexed by a sort type, extend `CertifiedNormalizer` to sorted terms, prove multi-step soundness sort-by-sort, then invoke the same quotient factorization argument.

**Domain Bridges:** Universal algebra → type theory → module theory → representation theory

**Lineage:** Direct generalization of the single-sorted Master Theorem

**Ambition:** ★★★★ — Covers the vast majority of practical algebraic theories

## Direction 2: Certified Extraction of Efficient Normalizers

**Conjecture:** For any finite convergent rewrite system R on terms of bounded arity, there exists a normalizer computable in O(n · |R| · d) time, where n is term size, |R| is the number of rules, and d is the maximum reduction chain length. Moreover, the extracted normalizer's correctness is a direct corollary of `rewriteNormalize_correct`.

**Test:** Implement the extraction pipeline for 20 random convergent rewrite systems of varying sizes (5–50 rules, arity ≤ 4). Benchmark the extracted normalizers against naive repeated scanning. Measure: (a) wallclock time ratio, (b) number of rule applications, (c) whether the O(n · |R| · d) bound holds empirically.

**Impact:** Transforms the abstract theorem into a practical compiler optimization tool. Any equational theory with a known convergent presentation immediately yields a verified peephole optimizer.

**Catalog References:** `Pythagorean/ConvergentRewriteOptimizer.lean` — `rewriteNormalize`, `rewriteNormalize_correct`, `compiler_pass_correct`

**Proof Strategy:** Show that each call to `applyFirstRule` takes O(|R|) time, each successful application strictly decreases a well-founded measure, and the total number of applications is bounded by d. Use `rewriteNormalize_reduces` to certify each step.

**Domain Bridges:** Term rewriting → compiler construction → verified optimization → program extraction

**Lineage:** Builds on `rewriteNormalize_correct` and `compiler_pass_correct`

**Ambition:** ★★★ — Engineering-heavy but mathematically straightforward

## Direction 3: Gröbner Bases as Convergent Quotient Optimizers

**Conjecture:** A Gröbner basis G for a polynomial ideal I, equipped with the S-polynomial reduction relation, instantiates `ConvergentOptimizer` with `Eqv` being congruence modulo I. The normal-form map is polynomial remainder, and `polynomial_rewrite_semantics` specializes to: for every commutative ring A and every ring homomorphism φ: k[x₁,...,xₙ] → A with I ⊆ ker(φ), remainder(f, G) and f have the same image under φ.

**Test:** For 10 randomly generated polynomial ideals in ℚ[x,y,z] (generated by 3–5 polynomials of degree ≤ 4), compute Gröbner bases, verify confluence computationally (all S-polynomials reduce to zero), and check that remainder preserves evaluation in 100 random ℚ-algebra homomorphisms.

**Impact:** This would unify Buchberger's algorithm with the abstract quotient optimizer framework, showing that Gröbner basis computation is an instance of the general theory. It bridges symbolic computation and formal verification.

**Catalog References:** `Pythagorean/ConvergentRewriteOptimizer.lean` — `polynomial_rewrite_semantics`, `ConvergentOptimizer.ofCertifiedNormalizer`, `confluence_of_cps_joinable`

**Proof Strategy:** (1) Define `PolyRewrite` as the S-polynomial reduction relation. (2) Show soundness: each reduction step preserves congruence modulo I. (3) Buchberger's criterion gives joinability of all critical pairs (S-polynomials). (4) Degree-bounded termination gives well-foundedness. (5) Apply `confluence_of_cps_joinable` and `ConvergentOptimizer.ofCertifiedNormalizer`.

**Domain Bridges:** Commutative algebra → algebraic geometry → symbolic computation → automated theorem proving

**Lineage:** Extends `polynomial_rewrite_semantics` from abstract ring expressions to concrete polynomial rings

**Ambition:** ★★★★★ — Grand challenge: would be the first machine-verified proof that Gröbner bases are quotient optimizers

## Direction 4: E-Graph Equality Saturation as Convergent Optimization

**Conjecture:** The extraction phase of e-graph equality saturation can be formalized as constructing a `ConvergentOptimizer` where `Eqv` is the congruence closure of discovered equalities, `nf` is the extraction function (choosing minimal-cost representatives), and the canonical property holds because the e-graph maintains the invariant that equivalent terms share an e-class.

**Test:** Implement a simplified e-graph with 5 rewrite rules (e.g., commutativity, associativity, identity, distribution, and one domain-specific rule). Run equality saturation on 1,000 random terms. Verify: (a) extracted terms evaluate identically to originals in 10 random models, (b) the `ground_decide_by_nf` characterization holds (equivalent terms yield equal extractions).

**Impact:** Would provide the first formal bridge between e-graphs (the dominant technique in modern compiler optimization) and convergent rewriting theory, enabling transfer of verified correctness results between the two paradigms.

**Catalog References:** `Pythagorean/ConvergentRewriteOptimizer.lean` — `ground_decide_by_nf`, `eqv_iff_same_nf`, `nf_constant_on_eqvGen`

**Proof Strategy:** Model e-classes as equivalence classes under `EqvGen R`. Show that extraction is a section of the quotient map (using `quotientNf`). The key challenge is showing that cost-optimal extraction is confluent—this may require restricting to monotone cost functions.

**Domain Bridges:** E-graphs → SMT solvers → compiler optimization → program synthesis

**Lineage:** Extends `ground_decide_by_nf` from abstract theory to concrete e-graph implementations

**Ambition:** ★★★★ — Would connect two major research communities

## Direction 5: Size-Optimality and Compression Power of Canonical Forms

**Conjecture:** For randomly generated finite convergent systems over signatures with arity ≤ 3 and at most 20 rules, the canonical normal form minimizes term size within its equivalence class with probability > 0.8. Moreover, the average compression ratio (nf size / original size) is positively correlated (r > 0.5) with the average reduction chain length.

**Test:** Generate 50 random convergent rewrite systems. For each, enumerate all equivalence classes of terms up to depth 5. For each class, compare `nf` size to the minimum size of any class representative. Compute: (a) fraction of classes where `nf` achieves minimum size, (b) Pearson correlation between average chain length and compression ratio, (c) distribution of compression ratios.

**Impact:** If confirmed, this would justify the use of canonical normal forms as optimization targets—not just for semantic preservation, but for actual size reduction. If refuted, it would motivate the search for size-optimal normal form strategies beyond standard convergent rewriting.

**Catalog References:** `Pythagorean/ConvergentRewriteOptimizer.lean` — `simplifying_nf_bounded`, `rtc_size_mono`, `nf_idempotent`

**Proof Strategy:** For the theoretical direction: show that for simplifying systems (where every rule strictly decreases term size), `nf` always achieves minimum size. For non-simplifying systems, construct explicit counterexamples (e.g., distribution expanding `a*(b+c)` to `ab+ac`) and characterize when size-optimality fails.

**Domain Bridges:** Combinatorics → information theory → data compression → program optimization

**Lineage:** Extends `simplifying_nf_bounded` from upper bounds to optimality characterization

**Ambition:** ★★★ — Computationally testable, mathematically interesting
