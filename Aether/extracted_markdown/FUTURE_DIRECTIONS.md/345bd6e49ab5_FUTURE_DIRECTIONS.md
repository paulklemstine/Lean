# Future Directions — Langlands for Toddlers, Cycle II (Genus & the Group of Colors)

## Synthesis

The first cycle (`Algebra.LanglandsToddlers`) built the GL₁ "shape–color dictionary":
each quadratic field `Q(√d)` (a *shape*, encoded by its fundamental discriminant `D`)
gets a quadratic Dirichlet character `n ↦ J(D, n)` (a *color*, the Jacobi symbol), and
isolated colors were distinguished by concrete witnesses (`dict_neg4_ne_8_witness`, etc.).

This cycle (`Algebra.LanglandsToddlersGenus`) discovers that **the colors are not a loose
family — they form a group**. The Jacobi symbol is bi-multiplicative, and reading its two
arguments gives two different structures from one object:

* **second argument** → multiplicativity in `n` = the Dirichlet character (already
  `color_mult`);
* **first argument** → `color_homomorphism`: composing shapes *multiplies* colors, so
  `(ℤ, ·)` maps homomorphically onto the colors.

Combined with `color_involution` (`J(D,n)² = 1` for coprime `n`), the colors form an
**elementary abelian 2-group**. The smallest non-cyclic instance is made explicit in
`klein_four_genus`: `J(-4,n)·J(8,n) = J(-8,n)` for all odd `n`, i.e. `Q(i), Q(√2),
Q(√-2)` close into a Klein four-group mod 8. Finally `ramification_dictionary` locates
where colors vanish: `J(D,p)=0 ⇔ p ∣ D`, the toddler signature of a ramified prime.

The unifying lesson: the GL₁ Langlands correspondence is not just a *list* of matched
shapes and colors — it is an **isomorphism of groups**, and genus theory is its
multiplication table.

## Results summary

| Theorem | Statement | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `color_homomorphism` | `J(D₁·D₂,n)=J(D₁,n)·J(D₂,n)` | shape composition = color multiplication |
| `color_involution` | `gcd(D,n)=1 ⇒ J(D,n)²=1` | colors have order ≤ 2 (elementary abelian) |
| `klein_four_genus` | odd `n`: `J(-4,n)·J(8,n)=J(-8,n)` | Klein four-group of characters mod 8 |
| `ramification_dictionary` | prime `p`: `J(D,p)=0 ⇔ p∣D` | colors vanish exactly at ramified primes |

All main results are `sorry`-free and depend only on `propext, Classical.choice,
Quot.sound` (witnesses additionally use the kernel-checked `native_decide`).

## Bold, falsifiable directions for the next cycle

### Direction 1 — The genus group is *exactly* `(ℤ/2)^t`, with `t = ω(D) − 1`
Conjecture: for a fundamental discriminant `D` with `t` distinct prime factors, the group
generated by the prime-discriminant colors dividing `D` is isomorphic to `(ℤ/2)^{t-1}`
(or `(ℤ/2)^t` in the appropriate ramified normalization), recovering the classical genus
group order `2^{t-1}`. Falsifiable: exhibit a `D` whose color subgroup has a different
order, or a non-trivial relation among prime-discriminant colors beyond the single global
product relation. **The key insight is** that `color_homomorphism` already factors every
color through prime discriminants, so the genus group is forced to be the cokernel of one
linear relation over `𝔽₂` — pure linear algebra, not analysis. **Why now?** We have the
group law (`color_homomorphism`) and the exponent-2 fact (`color_involution`) in Lean;
the only missing piece is the unique factorization of fundamental discriminants into
prime discriminants, which Mathlib's `UniqueFactorizationMonoid` machinery already
supports.

### Direction 2 — Quadratic reciprocity *is* the symmetry of the genus pairing
Conjecture: the map `(D, p) ↦ J(D,p)` descends to a perfect symmetric bilinear pairing
`B : 𝔽₂^S × 𝔽₂^S → 𝔽₂` on the `𝔽₂`-vector space spanned by ramified primes, and quadratic
reciprocity (`shape_color_duality` in cycle I) is exactly the statement `B(x,y)=B(y,x)`.
Falsifiable: find a pair of shapes/colors where the pairing is non-symmetric. **The key
insight is** that the reciprocity sign `(-1)^{(p-1)/2·(q-1)/2}` is precisely the defect of
a symmetric `𝔽₂`-bilinear form twisted by the `±1`-valued "infinite place" characters
`χ_{-4}, χ_8`; symmetry holds once those archimedean colors are added as extra
coordinates. **Why now?** `legendre_eq_jacobi` and `shape_color_duality` give the raw
reciprocity law; reformulating it as a matrix identity over `𝔽₂` is a finite, decidable
target perfectly suited to the prover.

### Direction 3 — `|gaussSum χ_D|² = |D|`: the analytic norm of a color
Conjecture: extending `gauss_sum_sq_quadratic` (cycle I) from prime fields to the full
fundamental discriminant, the Gauss sum of `χ_D` satisfies `|g(χ_D)|² = |D|`, with sign
`g(χ_D)² = D` (Gauss's sign theorem) after the standard `i^{...}` normalization.
Falsifiable: compute a `χ_D` whose Gauss-sum square is not `±|D|`. **The key insight is**
that the multiplicativity `color_homomorphism` lets the global Gauss sum factor as a
product of local (prime) Gauss sums, each handled by the existing `gaussSum_sq` bridge —
so the global norm is forced by the local ones via the genus group law. **Why now?**
Mathlib already has `gaussSum_sq` and the quadratic Gauss sum sign for primes; the missing
step is a CRT/multiplicativity assembly, which `color_homomorphism` now makes routine.

### Direction 4 — The toddler step toward GL₂: dihedral colors from quadratic shapes
Conjecture: for an imaginary quadratic shape `D < 0`, every ideal-class character of
`Q(√d)` induces a 2-dimensional Galois representation whose attached "color" is a weight-1
modular form, and the *trace of Frobenius* at a split prime `p` equals `a_p = χ₁(𝔭) +
χ₁(𝔭̄)` — a sum of two cycle-I colors. Falsifiable: find a split prime where the induced
trace disagrees with the modular `a_p`. **The key insight is** that GL₂ dihedral forms are
literally *built out of GL₁ colors by induction*, so the entire `n=2` toddler case is a
bilinear combination of the `n=1` dictionary we have already formalized — no new analytic
input, only the induction bookkeeping. **Why now?** With `color_homomorphism` and
`ramification_dictionary` we can already compute split/inert/ramified behavior; assembling
two colors into a trace is the smallest honest GL₂ statement reachable from current Lean
infrastructure.

### Direction 5 — Spectral/orthogonality completeness of the color basis
Conjecture: the quadratic colors `{χ_D}` for `|D| ≤ X` are *orthonormal* under the natural
Dirichlet inner product and span the space of real primitive characters of bounded
conductor; equivalently, the Gram matrix `[⟨χ_{D_i}, χ_{D_j}⟩]` is the identity in the
limit. Falsifiable: two distinct fundamental discriminants whose colors are not orthogonal
(would contradict the GL₁ injectivity conjecture `gl1_shape_color_injectivity` of cycle I).
**The key insight is** that `mul_char_sum_vanishes` (cycle I) already gives orthogonality
for a *single* non-trivial color; pairing two colors `χ_{D_1}χ_{D_2} = χ_{D_1 D_2 /
square}` reduces *every* inner product to that one vanishing-sum lemma via
`color_homomorphism`. **Why now?** Both ingredients — the vanishing of nontrivial
character sums and the product law for colors — are now formalized, so completeness is a
finite reduction rather than an open-ended analytic estimate.
