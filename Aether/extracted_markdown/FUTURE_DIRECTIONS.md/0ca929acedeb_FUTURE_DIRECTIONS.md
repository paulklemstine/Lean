# Future Directions: Berggren Spectral Gap and Lattice Cryptography

## 1. Full Spectral Decomposition and Norm Growth Dichotomy

The spectral dichotomy we established — A and C unipotent (eigenvalue 1 with multiplicity 3), B hyperbolic (eigenvalues {-1, 3 ± 2√2}) — should extend to precise norm growth bounds for arbitrary words. For a word w = g₁g₂...gₙ with k occurrences of B and (n-k) occurrences of A or C, the conjecture is:

**Conjecture**: There exist constants C₁, C₂ > 0 such that for any word w of length n containing k B-generators,
‖w · v₀‖ ≤ C₁ · n² · (3+2√2)^k and ‖w · v₀‖ ≥ C₂ · (3+2√2)^(k/2).

The key insight is that A and C, being unipotent with nilpotency index 3, contribute at most quadratic growth (from (A-I)² ≠ 0), while each B contributes a multiplicative factor between 3-2√2 ≈ 0.17 and 3+2√2 ≈ 5.83. The lower bound should follow from the Lorentz form preservation: since all generators preserve Q(v) = 0, the norm growth is controlled by the c-coordinate, and the Pell recurrence gives tight bounds along B-rich subwords.

Why now? We have the exact nilpotency index (3) for A and C, the factored Cayley-Hamilton identity for B, and the Pell recurrence. The missing piece is a commutation estimate showing how interspersed A/C generators affect the B-growth rate.

## 2. Freeness of the Berggren Semigroup via Coordinate Separation

The Berggren semigroup ⟨A, B, C⟩ acting on positive Pythagorean triples is conjectured to be free: distinct words produce distinct triples when applied to (3,4,5). Our hypotenuse growth bounds provide a partial attack.

**Conjecture**: For any two distinct words w₁, w₂ of the same length n, evalWord(w₁) · v₀ ≠ evalWord(w₂) · v₀.

The key insight is that the three children of any node have hypotenuses in distinct residue classes modulo small primes. Specifically, from a triple (a,b,c): hypA ≡ 3c - 2(b-a), hypB ≡ 3c + 2(a+b), hypC ≡ 3c + 2(b-a) modulo various primes. Since a ≢ b modulo 2 for primitive triples (one leg is always even), the three children are distinguishable by their hypotenuse modulo 4, giving a combinatorial separation argument.

Why now? The universal hypotenuse growth bounds (berggren_hypA_gt, berggren_hypB_gt, berggren_hypC_gt) show that no two branches can "collide in norm" — the B-branch hypotenuse exceeds 5c while A and C branches give hypotenuses between c and 5c. This coarse separation, combined with modular arithmetic, may suffice for a full freeness proof.

## 3. Lattice Successive Minima from Pell Asymptotics

The Pell recurrence c_{n+2} = 6c_{n+1} - c_n for B-iterated hypotenuses, with characteristic roots 3 ± 2√2, connects the Berggren lattice to the unit group of ℤ[√2]. This connection should yield explicit successive minima.

**Conjecture**: For the lattice Λ_d generated by all triples at depth ≤ d in the Berggren tree, the ratio λ₂(Λ_d)/λ₁(Λ_d) of successive minima satisfies λ₂/λ₁ ≥ c · d^(1/3) for an explicit constant c > 0.

The key insight is that the three branches grow at very different rates — B produces hypotenuses ≥ 5c while A and C produce hypotenuses between c and 3c (approximately). The "shortest" vectors in the lattice come from long chains of A or C generators, while the "next shortest" come from words with at least one B. The ratio between these two populations grows with depth.

Why now? The exponential growth theorem (c_{n+1} ≥ 5c_n for B-iterations) provides a concrete lower bound on the gap between B-containing and B-free words. Formalizing the A/C polynomial growth (from the unipotency results) would complete the picture.

## 4. Quantum Hardness Amplification via Spectral Type Mixing

The spectral dichotomy suggests a new approach to quantum hardness: mixing unipotent and hyperbolic generators creates words whose norm growth rate is computationally hard to determine.

**Conjecture**: Given a random word w of length n with each generator chosen uniformly from {A, B, C}, the expected number of quantum queries needed to determine ‖w · v₀‖² to within a factor of 2 is Ω(n^(1/3)).

The key insight is that while B-content determines the leading-order growth, the interaction between unipotent (A, C) and hyperbolic (B) generators creates interference patterns in the norm that depend on the precise word ordering, not just the generator counts. This is because A and C do not commute with B (established in the catalog as matA_matB_noncommutative), so different orderings of the same generator multiset produce genuinely different norms.

Why now? The Cayley-Hamilton identities (matA_unipotent, matB_cayley_hamilton_factored) reduce the norm computation for mixed words to tracking the interaction between nilpotent and diagonalizable parts, a problem with rich structure from representation theory that may admit quantum lower bounds via polynomial method arguments.

## 5. Berggren Zeta Function and Spectral Gap of the Adjacency Operator

Define the Berggren zeta function ζ_B(s) = Σ_{w} ‖w·v₀‖^{-2s} summed over all finite words w. The spectral structure should determine its analytic properties.

**Conjecture**: ζ_B(s) converges for Re(s) > log(3)/(2·log(3+2√2)) and has a meromorphic continuation to Re(s) > 0 with a simple pole at s = log(3)/(2·log(3+2√2)).

The key insight is that the ternary branching (3 children per node) competes with the norm growth rate (dominated by (3+2√2)^k for words with k B-generators). The abscissa of convergence is determined by the balance point where 3^n · (average norm)^{-2s} = O(1). Since ≈ (2/3)n generators are non-B (contributing polynomial growth) and ≈ n/3 are B (contributing (3+2√2)^{n/3} growth), the critical exponent involves the ratio log(3)/log(3+2√2).

Why now? The Pell recurrence and exponential growth bound make the B-branch contribution explicit, and the unipotency results bound the A/C contribution. A formal proof of convergence for Re(s) > 1 (the easy case where norm growth dominates branching) is within reach using the formalizations in this module.
