# Future Directions: Tropical Satake Polytope Duality

## Status of Current Work

We have established the first formally verified bridge between tropical convex geometry and crystal representation theory. The core results prove that:

1. Every tropical weight profile admits a crystal realization (existence)
2. Multiplicity-free operator-free crystals are uniquely determined by their support profile (reconstruction/uniqueness up to isomorphism)
3. Extremal vertices correspond to support atoms in the operator-free regime
4. The support profile functor is invariant under crystal isomorphism

These results are fully machine-verified with no axioms beyond the standard foundational ones (propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound).

---

## Direction 1: Multiplicity-Free Crystals with Non-Trivial Operators

**Goal**: Extend the reconstruction theorem from operator-free crystals to crystals with non-trivial Kashiwara operators, under the multiplicity-free assumption.

**Key insight**: In a multiplicity-free crystal, the Kashiwara operators f_i and e_i are completely determined by the weight map. If f_i(b) = b', then wt(b') is forced, and since the crystal is multiplicity-free, b' is the unique vertex with that weight. This means the operator structure is encoded in the support profile together with the root datum.

**Concrete next theorem**:
```
theorem reconstruction_mult_free
    (K₁ K₂ : FiniteCrystal R)
    (hK₁ : MultFree K₁) (hK₂ : MultFree K₂)
    (h_supp : crystalSupportProfile R K₁ = crystalSupportProfile R K₂)
    (h_wt_compat : ∀ i b, (K₁.f i b).isSome ↔ 
      (K₂.f i (weightBijection K₁ K₂ hK₁ hK₂ _ b)).isSome) :
    Nonempty (CrystalIso K₁ K₂)
```

**Difficulty**: Medium. The weight bijection automatically commutes with operators when it exists, but proving operator compatibility from weight data requires formalizing how weights change under Kashiwara operators (wt(f_i(b)) = wt(b) - α_i).

**Impact**: This would establish the full tropical-crystal duality in the multiplicity-free regime, covering minuscule representations and many important cases in type A.

## Direction 2: Tropical Demazure Crystal Reconstruction

**Goal**: Formalize Demazure crystals (truncated crystals corresponding to Demazure modules) and prove that their tropical support profiles satisfy a strengthened convexity condition that determines them uniquely.

**Key definitions needed**:
- Demazure crystal: a subcrystal generated by applying a subset of Kashiwara operators to the highest weight
- Demazure support profile: support with a distinguished "Demazure direction" recording which operators were used
- Demazure convexity: the support is convex along each simple root direction used in the generation

**Concrete next theorem**:
```
theorem demazure_crystal_from_profile
    (R : FiniteRootDatum) (w : List R.ι)
    (χ : DemazureProfile R w) :
    ∃! K : DemazureCrystal R w, DemazureRealizesProfile K χ
```

**Difficulty**: Hard. Requires formalizing the combinatorics of reduced words, Demazure operators, and string parametrizations.

**Impact**: Would connect tropical geometry to Schubert calculus and flag variety geometry, opening a formal path to tropical Schubert polynomials.

## Direction 3: Polytope-Normal-Fan Crystal Reconstruction

**Goal**: Formalize the Newton polytope / string polytope associated to a crystal and prove that the normal fan structure encodes the crystal graph.

**Key insight**: The weight support of a crystal, viewed as a polytope in the weight lattice, has edges that correspond to root directions. The 1-skeleton of this polytope, oriented by the dominance order, recovers the crystal graph.

**Concrete definitions**:
- Crystal polytope: convex hull of weight support in the real weight space
- Edge coloring: assignment of simple root labels to polytope edges
- Normal fan: the fan dual to the crystal polytope

**Concrete next theorem**:
```
theorem crystal_graph_from_polytope_skeleton
    (R : FiniteRootDatum) (K : FiniteCrystal R) (hK : MultFree K) :
    CrystalIsomorphicToOneSkeleton K (crystalPolytope R K)
```

**Difficulty**: Very hard. Requires formalizing polyhedral geometry (convex hulls, faces, normal fans) which is largely absent from Mathlib. However, the finite case could be developed from scratch.

**Impact**: Revolutionary. Would establish a formal dictionary between polyhedral combinatorics and representation theory, enabling certified algorithms for computing representation-theoretic data from polytope computations.

## Direction 4: Tropical Littlewood-Richardson Multiplicity Bounds

**Goal**: Formalize the relationship between tropical Minkowski sums and tensor product multiplicities. Prove that the tropical convolution of two crystal support profiles gives bounds on the support of their tensor product.

**Key insight**: The support of a tensor product crystal K₁ ⊗ K₂ is contained in the Minkowski sum of the individual supports. Under multiplicity-free conditions, equality holds. In general, the tropical profile of the tensor product encodes Littlewood-Richardson coefficients.

**Concrete next theorem**:
```
theorem tensor_support_minkowski_bound
    (R : FiniteRootDatum) [AddCommGroup R.P]
    (K₁ K₂ : FiniteCrystal R) :
    (crystalSupportProfile R (tensorCrystal K₁ K₂)).support ⊆
      minkowskiSum (crystalSupportProfile R K₁).support 
                   (crystalSupportProfile R K₂).support

theorem tensor_support_minkowski_exact_mult_free
    (R : FiniteRootDatum) [AddCommGroup R.P]
    (K₁ K₂ : FiniteCrystal R) 
    (hK : MultFree (tensorCrystal K₁ K₂)) :
    (crystalSupportProfile R (tensorCrystal K₁ K₂)).support =
      minkowskiSum (crystalSupportProfile R K₁).support 
                   (crystalSupportProfile R K₂).support
```

**Difficulty**: Hard. Requires formalizing tensor product crystals with correct Kashiwara operator rules (signature rule or tensor product rule) and Minkowski sums of finite sets.

**Impact**: Would give the first formally verified connection between tropical algebra and the Littlewood-Richardson rule, with potential applications to verified computation in algebraic combinatorics and symmetric function theory.

## Direction 5: Geometric Satake Shadows via Idempotent Perverse Sheaf Invariants

**Goal**: Formalize a simplified, finite version of the geometric Satake correspondence where:
- The representation side is replaced by crystal data
- The geometric side is replaced by tropical / combinatorial objects
- The Tannakian reconstruction is replaced by tropical profile reconstruction

**Key insight**: The geometric Satake correspondence identifies representations of a reductive group G with perverse sheaves on the affine Grassmannian. The weight functors correspond to hyperbolic localization. In the tropical limit, these become support profile functors, and the Tannakian reconstruction becomes our crystal reconstruction theorem.

**Concrete program**:
1. Define a tropical affine Grassmannian as a poset of lattice cosets
2. Define tropical perverse sheaves as graded support data on this poset
3. Prove that the support functor from crystals to tropical sheaf data is faithful
4. Prove reconstruction: tropical sheaf data determines crystal data

**Difficulty**: Extremely hard. Even the simplified version requires substantial new infrastructure. However, the finite combinatorial version is within reach.

**Impact**: Would be the first formal theorem in the geometric Langlands program, even in a simplified combinatorial form. This would demonstrate that formal verification can reach deep structural results in modern representation theory.

## Cross-Cutting Technical Improvements

### A. Weight Lattice Infrastructure
Current limitation: weights are just a type with DecidableEq. Future work should add:
- AddCommGroup structure for weight arithmetic
- Partial order (dominance order) on weights
- Root system axioms (Cartan matrix, Weyl group)
- Root string parametrization

### B. Crystal Category
Formalize crystals as a category with:
- Crystal morphisms as functors preserving Kashiwara operators
- Crystal isomorphisms as natural isomorphisms  
- Tensor product as monoidal structure
- Duality (contragredient crystal)
- Direct sum decomposition

### C. Computational Verification
Implement executable crystal algorithms:
- Crystal graph enumeration from highest weight
- Support profile computation
- Tensor product decomposition
- Character computation
- Comparison with Weyl character formula

### D. Connection to Existing Mathlib
Bridge to existing Mathlib infrastructure:
- Connect weight lattices to `Module` theory
- Connect root systems to `RootSystem` (if available)
- Connect crystal categories to `CategoryTheory`
- Connect polytopes to `Convex` and `Polytope` (when available)
