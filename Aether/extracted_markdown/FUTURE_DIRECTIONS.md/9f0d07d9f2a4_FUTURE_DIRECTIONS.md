# Future Directions: Idempotent Probability and Large Deviations

## 1. Fenchel-Moreau Biconjugation Theorem

We proved the inequality direction `f**(x) ≤ f(x)` for arbitrary functions. The natural next step is the **Fenchel-Moreau theorem**: equality `f** = f` holds if and only if `f` is convex and lower semicontinuous. The key insight is that the biconjugate is the largest convex lsc minorant of `f`, so for convex lsc functions the gap closes. This would require formalizing lower semicontinuity in Lean 4 and proving that the pointwise supremum of continuous affine functions is lsc. **Why now?** Mathlib already has `LowerSemicontinuous` and `ConvexOn`; the remaining gap is showing that the Legendre-Fenchel transform preserves these properties. A proof would immediately yield the convex envelope characterization used throughout optimization and economics.

## 2. Cramér's Theorem via Idempotent Deformation

The classical Cramér theorem states that for i.i.d. random variables with finite MGF, the empirical mean satisfies an LDP with rate function `I(x) = sup_θ {θx - Λ(θ)}`. We proved that the max-plus rate function is non-negative for normalized densities, but the deeper result is that **classical Cramér's theorem is the deformation-theoretic limit of the idempotent version**: as the Planck parameter h → 0 in Maslov's dequantization, the classical LDP emerges from the idempotent identity. The key insight is that the log-sum-exp operation `(1/c) log(Σ exp(c·xᵢ))` interpolates between the classical sum (c=0) and the tropical max (c→∞), and our `SoftMaxConvergence` results already formalize one direction. **Why now?** We have both the SoftMax convergence (`Catalog.Tropical.SoftMaxConvergence`) and the general Fenchel-Young framework; connecting them via a parametric family `Λ_c(θ) = (1/c) log E[exp(cθX)]` would yield a unified proof of Cramér's theorem as a tropical limit.

## 3. Inf-Convolution Conjugate Additivity (Full Proof)

We defined the inf-convolution `(f □ g)(x) = inf_y {f(y) + g(x-y)}` and stated the conjugate additivity theorem `(f □ g)* = f* + g*`. A full proof requires careful handling of the exchange of sup and inf, which in the general case needs the minimax theorem or specific coercivity assumptions. The key insight is that in the max-plus world, this identity is *exact* (not just asymptotic) and corresponds to the additivity of rate functions for independent random variables—the max-plus analog of the convolution theorem for moment generating functions. **Why now?** Our `legendreFenchel_antitone` and `fenchel_young_general` provide the upper bound direction; the lower bound requires either a compactness argument (which Mathlib's topology library supports) or a supercoercivity hypothesis that we already defined as `IsSupercoercive`.

## 4. Tropical Central Limit Theorem

In classical probability, the CLT says normalized sums converge to a Gaussian. In idempotent probability, the analog is: **max-plus normalized sums converge to the quadratic rate function** `I(x) = x²/(2σ²)`. This is precisely because the quadratic is a fixed point of the Legendre-Fenchel transform (as proven in `Catalog.Tropical.LegendreDuality`). The key insight is that our `legendre_half_sq` (the self-duality of x²/2) is the tropical analog of the Gaussian being a fixed point of the Fourier transform—both are manifestations of the same algebraic self-duality, connected by Maslov dequantization. **Why now?** We now have both the general convexity theorem for f* and the specific quadratic fixed-point result; proving the tropical CLT would bridge them by showing that the quadratic emerges as the universal attractor under iterated inf-convolution.

## 5. Bridge to Optimal Transport: Kantorovich Duality as Tropical Duality

The Kantorovich dual formulation of optimal transport states that the transport cost equals `sup{∫φdμ + ∫ψdν : φ(x) + ψ(y) ≤ c(x,y)}`. When the cost is `c(x,y) = -x·y`, this reduces to `sup{∫φdμ - ∫φ*dν}`, directly involving the Legendre-Fenchel transform. The key insight is that Kantorovich duality IS tropical Fenchel duality in the measure-theoretic setting, and our `fenchel_young_general` provides the pointwise building block. **Why now?** Mathlib has growing optimal transport infrastructure (Wasserstein distances, couplings), and our general Fenchel-Young inequality provides the missing analytic foundation. A formalization would connect `Catalog.Tropical.Wasserstein` to the Legendre-Fenchel framework, unifying two major threads in the tropical catalog.
