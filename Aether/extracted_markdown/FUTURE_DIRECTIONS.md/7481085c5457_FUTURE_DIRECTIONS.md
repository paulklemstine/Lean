# Future Directions — Stochastic Galois Theory over Finite Fields

## Synthesis

The original concept ("random polynomials have *generic* Galois group `S_n`")
is a true and beautiful statement over the rationals (probabilistic Hilbert
irreducibility), but it **does not transfer verbatim to finite fields**. Over a
finite field the Galois group of any separable irreducible polynomial is *cyclic*
— it is generated by the Frobenius automorphism — so it is `ZMod n`, never the
full `S_n` for `n ≥ 3`. The right "genericity" statement over `F_q` is about the
**splitting profile** (the degree partition of the irreducible factors), which
mirrors the **cycle type** of a uniformly random permutation. This is the deep
structural bridge: *factorization statistics of random polynomials over `F_q` ≈
cycle-type statistics of random elements of `S_n`* (the Frobenius is the cycle
structure).

This cycle records, in Lean, both the *correction* and several exact counting
laws that make the corrected picture quantitative.

## Results Summary (file `Algebra/StochasticGaloisExtension.lean`, building on `Algebra/StochasticGalois.lean`)

1. **`quadratic_discriminant_zero_count`** — Over any finite field `F` of odd
   characteristic, the discriminant-zero (non-squarefree) quadratic locus has size
   exactly `|F|`. Hence its density among the `|F|²` monic quadratics is `1/|F|`,
   confirming and *generalizing* the concept's `n = 2` prediction `1/p` from
   `ZMod p` to all finite fields of odd characteristic.

2. **`frobenius_not_full_symmetric`** — For `n ≥ 3`, the cyclic group `ZMod n`
   (the genuine Galois group of an irreducible degree-`n` polynomial over a finite
   field) is **never** isomorphic to `S_n`, by the cardinality obstruction
   `n ≠ n!`. This is the Critic's correction to the headline conjecture, made
   formal.

3. **`monic_root_incidence_count`** — The number of (monic degree `n+1`
   polynomial, root) incidence pairs over a finite field `F` equals `|F|^{n+1}`.

4. **`expected_roots_eq_one`** — Consequently the incidence count equals the number
   of monic polynomials, so a uniformly random monic polynomial over a finite field
   has, on average, *exactly one* root — independent of degree and field size.

All four results are `sorry`-free and depend only on the standard axioms
(`propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`).

## Research Directions

### 1. The squarefree-density law `|{squarefree monic deg n}| = q^n − q^{n−1}`.

**Conjecture.** For `n ≥ 2`, the number of monic squarefree (= separable)
polynomials of degree `n` over `F_q` is exactly `q^n − q^{n−1}`, i.e. density
`1 − 1/q`. Theorem 1 here is precisely the `n = 2` complement (`q² − q` separable
quadratics). **The key insight is** that the non-squarefree locus is the image of
the "repeated-factor" map `(g, h) ↦ g² h`, whose fibers are controlled by a
Möbius/inclusion–exclusion over factor multiplicities, collapsing telescopically
to a single power gap `q^{n−1}`. **Why now?** Theorem 1 already gives the base case
constructively (graph of `c = b²/4`), and the incidence machinery
(`rootPairs`, `rootFiber`) in the companion file provides exactly the fiber-counting
vocabulary needed to push from `n = 2` to general `n` by induction on degree.

### 2. Gauss's necklace formula for irreducible counts.

**Conjecture.** The number of monic irreducible degree-`n` polynomials over `F_q`
is `N(n,q) = (1/n) Σ_{d|n} μ(d) q^{n/d}`; in particular `N(n,q)/q^n → 1/n`.
**The key insight is** that `x^{q^n} − x` factors as the product of *all* monic
irreducibles of degree dividing `n`, turning the count into a Möbius inversion of
the degree-sum identity `Σ_{d|n} d·(#irred deg d) = q^n`. **Why now?** Mathlib has
`X^(q^n) - X` splitting theory for finite fields and full Möbius-inversion support;
the missing glue is the degree-bookkeeping lemma, which is a finite combinatorial
identity well within reach of the incidence-counting style used here.

### 3. Splitting profile = Frobenius cycle type, distributionally.

**Conjecture.** As `q → ∞` with `n` fixed, the distribution of the splitting
profile of a uniform random monic degree-`n` polynomial over `F_q` converges to the
distribution of the cycle type of a uniform random permutation in `S_n` (e.g.
`P(irreducible) → 1/n`, matching `P(n`-cycle`) = 1/n`). **The key insight is** that
Frobenius acts on the roots as a permutation whose cycle lengths are exactly the
degrees of the irreducible factors, so polynomial factorization statistics *are*
permutation cycle statistics. **Why now?** The `SplittingProfile` structure already
formalizes the partition side, and direction 2 supplies the `1/n` irreducible mass;
together they let one state and test the `n = 3` profile table
(`[3] : 1/3`, `[1,1,1] : 1/6`, `[1,2] : 1/2` in the limit) by exact enumeration.

### 4. Average number of roots is a robust invariant — second moment and beyond.

**Conjecture.** Building on `expected_roots_eq_one`, the *variance* of the number of
`F_q`-roots of a uniform random monic degree-`n` polynomial tends to `1` as
`q → ∞` (Poisson(1) limit for the full root-count distribution). **The key insight
is** that the `k`-th factorial moment counts ordered `k`-tuples of distinct roots,
each contributing an independent linear constraint, so the `k`-th factorial moment
is `≈ 1` and the limiting law is Poisson with mean `1`. **Why now?** The mean is
already pinned exactly to `1` by the double-counting identity proved here; the same
`rootFiber`/`rootPairs` apparatus, applied to *pairs* of distinct roots, yields the
second factorial moment with no new conceptual machinery.

### 5. Discriminant-square density and the `A_n` vs `S_n` analogue over `F_q`.

**Conjecture.** Over `F_q` (`q` odd), the density of monic degree-`n` polynomials
whose discriminant is a nonzero square tends to `1/2` (the "`A_n` side"), matching
the parity-of-Frobenius split. For `n = 2` this is the irreducible-quadratic density
`(q−1)/2q → 1/2` already recorded in the companion file. **The key insight is** that
the discriminant being a square is detected by the Legendre/quadratic-character
value, which is equidistributed `±1` over nonzero discriminants, so the square-locus
is asymptotically half. **Why now?** Theorem 1 isolates the *degenerate* (disc `= 0`)
slice exactly; subtracting it leaves the `±1`-character sum, and Mathlib's quadratic
character and Gauss-sum API make the equidistribution `1/2` estimate concretely
attackable for small `n`.
