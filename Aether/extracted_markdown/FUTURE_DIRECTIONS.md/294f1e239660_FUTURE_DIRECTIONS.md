# Future Directions

## Synthesis

The compositional verification framework established here—decomposing the Regev reduction into certified module-theoretic morphisms—opens five interconnected research directions. At the foundation, we have proved that TVD contracts under pushforward, that hybrid telescope bounds compose, and that certified reduction steps chain. These results create a verified algebraic skeleton for the Regev pipeline. The directions below extend this skeleton in complementary ways: Direction 1 fills the quantum gap with a certified sampler, Direction 2 completes the lattice geometry foundation, Direction 3 instantiates the abstract framework to concrete cryptographic parameters, Direction 4 pushes toward the first end-to-end verified reduction, and Direction 5 explores whether this compositional methodology generalizes beyond LWE to other cryptographic hardness transfers.

Each direction builds directly on the catalog theorems proved in this work. Together, they form a roadmap toward the first machine-verified security proof for a post-quantum cryptographic standard.

## Direction 1: Certified Discrete Gaussian Sampling Interface

**Conjecture:** There exists a classical polynomial-time algorithm that, for any lattice Λ ⊂ ℤⁿ and parameter s > η_ε(Λ) (the smoothing parameter), produces samples from a distribution D within TVD ε of the discrete Gaussian D_{Λ,s}, where ε is exponentially small in n. Moreover, this sampler satisfies the `ApproxDiscreteGaussian` interface defined in our framework.

**Test:** Implement Peikert's generic sampler [Peikert 2010] for small lattices (n ≤ 8, q ≤ 23). Compute exact TVD between the sampler's output distribution and the ideal discrete Gaussian (enumerable for these parameters). Check whether TVD ≤ 2^{-n} for all tested instances. A single instance where TVD exceeds the claimed bound falsifies the conjecture for those parameters.

**Impact:** Filling this interface would complete the quantum step of the Regev reduction without requiring full quantum circuit semantics. Combined with our `approx_gaussian_pushforward_error` theorem, it would give a machine-verified bound on the total error introduced by the sampling step.

**Catalog References:**
- `approx_gaussian_pushforward_error` (Theorems.lean): Proves that certified approximation errors propagate through pushforward.
- `ApproxDiscreteGaussian` (Defs.lean): The formal interface this direction would instantiate.

**Proof Strategy:** Formalize the smoothing lemma (Micciancio-Regev Lemma 4.1) as a Lean theorem about lattice sums converging to continuous Gaussians. Then construct a `PMF` over the lattice by normalizing Gaussian weights and prove the TVD bound using Poisson summation machinery.

**Domain Bridges:** Probability theory ↔ lattice geometry (the smoothing parameter connects distributional guarantees to geometric properties of the lattice). Harmonic analysis ↔ cryptography (Poisson summation underpins the smoothing lemma).

**Lineage:** Extends `ApproxDiscreteGaussian` from this work. Builds toward filling the quantum gap identified in Section 7.2 of the research paper.

**Ambition:** Grand challenge — completing this direction would eliminate the single largest obstacle to end-to-end verified Regev reduction.

## Direction 2: Formalized GapSVP → BDD Reduction via Lattice Geometry

**Conjecture:** The Goldreich-Goldwasser-Halevi (GGH) reduction from GapSVP_{γ} to BDD_{1/(2γ)} can be expressed as a `ModuleReductionStep` over finitely generated free ℤ-modules, with the TVD contraction bound holding for the induced distributional transformation.

**Test:** For lattices generated by random bases in dimensions n ≤ 6, enumerate all lattice points within radius γ·λ₁ (where λ₁ is the shortest vector length). Verify that the BDD decoder using the GGH transformation produces unique outputs when γ > 2, and non-unique outputs for some instances when γ < 2. The conjecture is falsified if the reduction cannot be instantiated as a `ModuleReductionStep` for any tested lattice.

**Impact:** This would complete the worst-case end of the Regev pipeline. Combined with our `bdd_solution_unique` theorem and `ModuleReductionStep.comp_tvd_bound`, it would give a machine-verified chain from worst-case lattice hardness to the BDD interface.

**Catalog References:**
- `bdd_solution_unique` (Theorems.lean): Guarantees decoder output uniqueness under well-separation.
- `ModuleReductionStep` (Defs.lean / Theorems.lean): The compositional structure this direction would instantiate.
- `intDist_triangle` (Theorems.lean): Used in the uniqueness proof.

**Proof Strategy:** Formalize Minkowski's successive minima for free ℤ-modules using Mathlib's `Submodule` infrastructure. Prove the gap-to-bounded-distance conversion as a `have` step producing a `BDDInstance` from a `GapSVP` witness. The TVD contraction follows from the deterministic nature of the reduction map.

**Domain Bridges:** Lattice geometry ↔ module theory (lattices as submodules of free ℤ-modules). Convex geometry ↔ cryptographic hardness (Minkowski's theorem connects volume bounds to vector lengths).

**Lineage:** Extends `BDDInstance` and `bdd_solution_unique` from this work.

**Ambition:** Solid extension — the mathematical ingredients exist in Mathlib (partial Minkowski coverage). The main work is formalization and assembly.

## Direction 3: Concrete Instantiation for ML-KEM Parameters

**Conjecture:** The abstract `ModuleReductionStep` framework, when instantiated over `ZMod 3329` modules of rank 2-4 with concrete discrete Gaussian noise of standard deviation σ ≈ √(q/2π), produces TVD bounds that match or improve upon the known security estimates for ML-KEM-512/768/1024.

**Test:** For ML-KEM-512 parameters (n=256, k=2, q=3329), compute exact TVD for small sub-instances (projected to dimension ≤ 4) and compare with the bounds predicted by the abstract framework. Verify that the composed `ModuleReductionStep` bound agrees with the known concrete security estimate of 2^{118} operations. The conjecture is falsified if the formal bounds are more than a factor 2^{10} looser than concrete estimates.

**Impact:** Would connect the formal verification framework directly to NIST standardization. A machine-verified security bound for ML-KEM would be a historic first for any deployed cryptographic standard.

**Catalog References:**
- `tvd_contracts_under_pushforward` (Theorems.lean): Core contraction used in modulus switching.
- `affine_hybrid_telescope_bound` (Theorems.lean): Bounds the search→decision advantage.
- `ModuleReductionStep.comp_tvd_bound` (Theorems.lean): Chains reduction steps.

**Proof Strategy:** Instantiate `ModuleReductionStep` with `R = ZMod 3329`, `M = (ZMod 3329)^k`, using Lean's `ZMod` infrastructure and `Fintype` instances for finite modules. The modulus switching step (3329 → 2^d) becomes a concrete quotient map. Use `Decidable` instances for explicit computation of small-parameter bounds.

**Domain Bridges:** Abstract algebra ↔ applied cryptography (finite module theory instantiated to NIST parameters). Number theory ↔ computer science (properties of the prime 3329 and its algebraic consequences).

**Lineage:** Builds on all theorems from this work. Connects to the existing catalog in `Cryptography/LWE/Security.lean`.

**Ambition:** Solid extension with high practical impact.

## Direction 4: End-to-End Verified Regev Reduction

**Conjecture (Grand Challenge):** The complete Regev reduction—from GapSVP_{γ(n)} to decision-LWE_{n,q,χ}—can be expressed as a composition of ≤ 5 `ModuleReductionStep`s and one `ApproxDiscreteGaussian` instantiation, with total advantage bound:

$$\text{Adv}_{\text{decision-LWE}} \leq n \cdot q \cdot (\text{Adv}_{\text{GapSVP}} + \delta)$$

where δ is the certified Gaussian approximation error.

**Test:** Formalize each step as a separate Lean lemma and verify that the composed bound matches Regev's original Theorem 1.1 (up to polynomial factors in n, q). The conjecture is falsified if any step cannot be expressed in the `ModuleReductionStep` framework, or if the composed bound is super-polynomially looser than Regev's.

**Impact:** This would be the first machine-verified worst-case-to-average-case reduction for any post-quantum cryptographic primitive. It would establish a new paradigm for certified cryptographic hardness transfer.

**Catalog References:**
- All theorems from this work (compositional infrastructure).
- `search_from_decision_coordinate` (Cryptography/LWE/Security.lean): Coordinate-based S2D reduction.
- `abstract_hybrid_telescope` (Cryptography/ModuleLWE/SearchDecision.lean): Abstract hybrid framework.

**Proof Strategy:** Layer the results:
1. GapSVP → BDD (Direction 2)
2. BDD → Gaussian sampling (Direction 1)
3. Gaussian → search-LWE (`ModuleReductionStep` with noise injection)
4. Search-LWE → decision-LWE (`affine_hybrid_telescope_bound` + coordinate replacement)
5. Decision-LWE with parameter management (`tvd_contracts_under_pushforward`)

Compose using `ModuleReductionStep.comp_tvd_bound`.

**Domain Bridges:** All domains simultaneously: lattice geometry, quantum mechanics, probability theory, module algebra, and formal methods.

**Lineage:** This is the ultimate target. All other directions feed into it.

**Ambition:** Grand challenge — paradigm-shifting if achieved. Estimated timeline: 2-3 years of focused effort.

## Direction 5: Categorical Framework for Hardness Transfer Beyond LWE

**Conjecture:** The `ModuleReductionStep` framework generalizes to a category `HardnessTransfer` whose objects are pairs (module, distribution family) and whose morphisms are certified TVD-contracting transformations. This category admits:
- A monoidal product (for parallel composition of independent reductions)
- A functor to `Set` (forgetting the distributional structure)
- Natural transformations encoding hybrid arguments

Moreover, at least two other major cryptographic reductions (Ring-LWE from LWE, SIS from LWE) can be expressed as compositions of morphisms in this category.

**Test:** Formalize the category in Lean using Mathlib's category theory library. Attempt to express the Ring-LWE reduction (Lyubashevsky-Peikert-Regev) and the SIS↔LWE equivalence as compositions of morphisms. The conjecture is falsified if either reduction requires primitives outside the category (e.g., non-TVD-contracting steps, or steps that are not functorial).

**Impact:** Would establish a general mathematical framework for compositional verification of cryptographic reductions, applicable far beyond LWE. This is the "meta-theorem" direction: proving theorems about classes of reductions rather than individual ones.

**Catalog References:**
- `ModuleReductionStep` (Defs.lean): The morphism type to be categorified.
- `ModuleReductionStep.comp_tvd_bound` (Theorems.lean): Composition law.
- `ModuleReductionStep.id` (Theorems.lean): Identity morphism.
- `ringLWE_advantage_transport` (Cryptography/LWE/Security.lean): An existing result that should be a morphism in this category.

**Proof Strategy:** Use Mathlib's `CategoryTheory.Category` typeclass. Define objects as `Σ (M : Type*) [Fintype M], Set (PMF M)` and morphisms as `ModuleReductionStep`-like structures. Prove associativity and identity from the existing theorems. Define the monoidal product using `PMF.bind` or product distributions.

**Domain Bridges:** Category theory ↔ cryptography (a genuinely new connection). Abstract algebra ↔ formal methods (using algebraic structures to organize verification). Universal algebra ↔ security proofs (compositionality as a universal property).

**Lineage:** Directly generalizes the compositional structure established in this work.

**Ambition:** Grand challenge — would open a new subfield of formal cryptography.
