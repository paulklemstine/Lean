# Future Directions — Galois Theory of Cellular Automata Reversibility

Derived from this cycle's findings (see `Computation/CellularAutomataReversibility.lean`
and `Computation/ElementaryCAReversibility.lean`). This cycle established:

* The reversibility group is correctly **defined** as the centralizer of the shift
  inside `Equiv.Perm (configurations)`.
* On the **infinite** binary lattice `ℤ` it is **infinite** (the shift has infinite
  order): `reversibilityGroup_infinite`.
* On the cyclic lattice `ℤ/3` it has order **36**, decisively refuting the
  conjectured `8!/4 = 10080`: `reversibilityGroup3_card`,
  `reversibilityGroup3_card_ne_10080`.
* The CA-canonical generators (shift `σ`, complement `c`) commute and generate an
  abelian `ℤ/3 × ℤ/2` subgroup of order `6`, a proper part of the order-`36`
  centralizer.

## Conjecture 1 — The centralizer order is `∏ k^{m_k} m_k!` over shift orbits
For the cyclic lattice `ℤ/n` with `2^n` binary configurations, the reversibility
group (centralizer of the shift) has order `∏_{k∣n} k^{m_k} · m_k!`, where `m_k`
is the number of length-`k` orbits of the shift (binary necklaces of period `k`).
For `n=3` this gives `1^2·2! · 3^2·2! = 36`, matching the proved value.

**The key insight is** that the reversibility group is the *centralizer of a
permutation*, whose order depends only on its cycle type — here the cycle type is
the multiset of shift-orbit (necklace) lengths, computable by Möbius inversion.

**Why now?** We have a fully formal `Equiv.Perm`-based model and a verified base
case (`n=3 → 36`); the general formula is a finite, enumerable target that the
necklace-counting machinery in Mathlib (`Nat.totient`, Möbius) can now reach.

## Conjecture 2 — CA-generated reversibility is exponentially thin
The subgroup *generated by genuine reversible CA local rules* (shift and
complement and their products) on `ℤ/n` has order `2n`, whereas the full
centralizer grows super-exponentially in `n`. Hence the fraction of the
reversibility group realized by actual CAs tends to `0` as `n → ∞`.

**The key insight is** that CA reversibility is *local* (a permutation of finitely
many neighbourhoods) while centralizer membership is merely *global commutation
with the shift*; locality is a far stronger constraint, so almost no
shift-commuting permutation is a CA.

**Why now?** This cycle proved the `n=3` instance (CA group order `6 = 2·3` inside
the order-`36` centralizer); the gap `6 vs 36` is the first data point of a
provable asymptotic separation.

## Conjecture 3 — On the infinite lattice the CA reversibility group is the
infinite dihedral-like product `ℤ × ℤ/2`
On `ℤ` the group generated by the shift `σ` and complement `c` is isomorphic to
`ℤ × ℤ/2` (abelian), **not** the infinite dihedral group `ℤ ⋊ ℤ/2`. The dihedral
structure appears only when the *spatial mirror* reflection is added as a third
generator.

**The key insight is** that complement acts cellwise and therefore commutes with
the shift, killing the semidirect twist; only an index-reversing symmetry
(mirror) anticommutes with `σ` and produces a dihedral relation.

**Why now?** We proved `σ` has infinite order and `c·σ = σ·c` with `c² = 1`; the
remaining step is to show these relations are *complete*, i.e. no further
relations hold — a presentation-theoretic statement within reach.

## Conjecture 4 — Reversible CAs are exactly the zero-history computations
A CA global map is reversible **iff** it admits a Bennett `RevWitness` with a
trivial (one-point) auxiliary type. Irreversible CAs require auxiliary history of
size equal to their maximum fiber, which is `> 1`.

**The key insight is** that reversibility is the bijectivity boundary of the
data-processing inequality: bijections preserve information exactly, so no history
is needed, while every non-injective rule erases information measured by its
largest fiber.

**Why now?** We already built the easy direction (`reversible_revWitness`: a
bijection needs only `PUnit` history) on top of the catalog's
`Computation.ReversibleSortingBennett`; the converse follows from the fiber
lower bound `rev_witness_aux_lower_bound` in the same file.

## Conjecture 5 — Radius-`r` binary reversibility groups are never the full `S_{2^{2r+1}}`
For every radius `r ≥ 1`, the group generated by genuine reversible CA local rules
is a **proper** subgroup of `S_{2^{2r+1}}`; in particular the conjectured
`G(r,{0,1}) = S_{2^{2r+1}}` for `r ≥ 2` is false, because every CA-induced
permutation commutes with the shift while a transposition of two configurations
generally does not.

**The key insight is** that shift-commutation is a nontrivial linear constraint
inherited by *every* reversible CA, so the CA group is confined to the
centralizer, which is a proper subgroup whenever the shift is not central in
`S_{2^{2r+1}}` (i.e. always, for `2^{2r+1} ≥ 3`).

**Why now?** The `ℤ/3` computation shows the centralizer itself (order `36`) is
already a proper subgroup of `S_8` (order `40320`); the same shift-commutation
obstruction generalizes verbatim to all radii.
