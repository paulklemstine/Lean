# Future Directions: Tropical Kernel Rigidity Theory

## Synthesis

The tropical kernel uniqueness theorem opens a new research frontier at the intersection of tropical algebra, graph theory, matroid theory, and discrete potential theory. The central insight is that combinatorial separation hypotheses (edge-disjoint cycles, distinct visibility profiles) force algebraic rigidity (unique generators up to projective equivalence). This pattern — combinatorial conditions implying algebraic canonicality — is likely a special case of a much broader phenomenon. The directions below explore this phenomenon along five axes: deeper algebraic structure (Direction 1), computational boundaries (Direction 2), physical interpretation (Direction 3), higher-dimensional generalization (Direction 4), and cross-domain application (Direction 5). Together, they outline a multi-year research program that could establish tropical kernel theory as a foundational tool in algebraic combinatorics and network science.

## Direction 1: Tropical Extremal Ray Classification

**Conjecture:** The canonical generators of tropKer(L_S) are precisely the extremal rays of the tropical cone generated by the kernel, under the edge-disjoint cycle basis hypothesis.

**Test:** For each connected graph G on n ≤ 8 vertices, compute the tropical cone structure of tropKer(L_S) using the tropical analogue of vertex enumeration (e.g., tropical double description). Verify that extremal rays correspond bijectively to canonical cycle/component indicators.

**Impact:** Would establish a deep connection between tropical kernel rigidity and tropical convex geometry, unifying our uniqueness theorem with the theory of tropical polytopes and idempotent convex sets.

**Catalog References:**
- `Pythagorean/TropicalBridge/KernelDefs.lean`: `tropicalKernel`, `componentIndicator`
- `Pythagorean/TropicalBridge/KernelRigidity.lean`: `support_separated_irredundant`, `tropProjEquiv_equivalence`

**Proof Strategy:** Define a tropical cone structure on the kernel. Show that component indicators cannot be written as tropical convex combinations of other kernel elements. Use the support separation property (proved in KernelRigidity.lean) as the key technical tool. **The key insight is** that support separation is equivalent to extremality in the tropical cone, providing a geometric reinterpretation of the algebraic uniqueness theorem.

**Domain Bridges:** Tropical convex geometry ↔ graph Laplacian theory ↔ optimization theory.

**Lineage:** Builds directly on the support rigidity theorems (Theorem 5.1 and 5.2 in KernelRigidity.lean).

**Ambition:** Grand challenge. Would open a new chapter in tropical convex geometry with combinatorial applications.

**Why now?** The formal infrastructure for tropical kernels and support separation is now in place. Recent advances in tropical polytope algorithms (Allamigeon–Gaubert 2018) provide the computational tools needed for exhaustive verification.

## Direction 2: Weighted Laplacian Universality

**Conjecture:** The tropical kernel uniqueness theorem extends to positively-weighted graph Laplacians: for any edge weighting w : E → ℤ₊, the canonical generators of tropKer(L_S^w) are unique up to tropical projective equivalence under the same combinatorial hypotheses.

**Test:** Implement weighted tropical kernel computation. For each connected graph on n ≤ 6 vertices and each edge weighting with weights in {1, 2, 3}, verify uniqueness. Count the fraction of weighted instances satisfying the hypotheses.

**Impact:** Would establish that tropical kernel rigidity is a *metric-free* phenomenon — depending only on combinatorial structure, not on edge weights. This mirrors the universality of the structural defect formula proved in `WeightedDefect.lean`.

**Catalog References:**
- `Pythagorean/TropicalBridge/WeightedDefect.lean`: weighted Laplacian definitions, metric-free defect
- `Pythagorean/TropicalBridge/KernelRigidity.lean`: unweighted uniqueness theorem

**Proof Strategy:** Modify the restricted Laplacian definition to use weighted entries. Show that the support structure of canonical generators is weight-independent (it depends only on the graph topology). The propagation argument extends because the tropical kernel condition is homogeneous in the matrix entries. **The key insight is** that the "double minimum" condition in the tropical kernel is topological (which pairs of indices achieve it) rather than metric (what the actual values are).

**Domain Bridges:** Weighted graph theory ↔ tropical algebra ↔ network flow optimization.

**Lineage:** Extends the weighted defect universality (WeightedDefect.lean) to the full kernel structure.

**Ambition:** Solid extension. A natural next step that would significantly strengthen the theory.

**Why now?** The weighted Laplacian infrastructure already exists in the catalog. The main obstacle is extending the support propagation argument, which the formal framework is well-prepared for.

## Direction 3: Chip-Firing Mode Decomposition

**Conjecture:** In the chip-firing (sandpile) dynamics on a graph G with sink q, the canonical tropical kernel generators correspond to the independent modes of avalanche propagation: each generator defines a partition of S into "active" and "passive" regions such that firing the active region produces a balanced configuration.

**Test:** Simulate chip-firing dynamics on all connected graphs with n ≤ 7, starting from each canonical generator configuration. Track avalanche patterns and verify that generators produce independent (non-interacting) avalanche cascades.

**Impact:** Would provide the first rigorous connection between tropical kernel theory and the physics of self-organized criticality. This bridges pure mathematics to statistical mechanics and complex systems theory.

**Catalog References:**
- `Pythagorean/TropicalBridge/KernelDefs.lean`: `componentIndicator`, `tropicalKernel`
- `Pythagorean/TropicalBridge/KernelRigidity.lean`: `visible_component_generator_potential_mode`

**Proof Strategy:** Define the chip-firing operator formally. Show that component indicators are eigenvectors (in a tropical sense) of the firing operator. Use the potential mode theorem (KernelRigidity.lean) to establish that the indicator's constant-on-component property implies avalanche independence. **The key insight is** that component indicators partition the vertex set into firing zones that do not interact, because no edges cross between components of G[S].

**Domain Bridges:** Tropical linear algebra ↔ chip-firing dynamics ↔ statistical mechanics ↔ self-organized criticality.

**Lineage:** Builds on the potential mode theorem and component indicator structure.

**Ambition:** Grand challenge (paradigm-shifting). Would establish tropical kernel theory as a tool in mathematical physics.

**Why now?** The formal component indicator theory is now complete (8 theorems in KernelRigidity.lean). Chip-firing on graphs has a mature computational infrastructure (SageMath, etc.) ready for large-scale testing.

## Direction 4: Simplicial Tropical Kernels

**Conjecture:** For the higher Laplacian L_k of a simplicial complex K, the tropical kernel tropKer(L_k) admits canonical generators corresponding to the k-dimensional "holes" and boundary components, with uniqueness under a higher-dimensional analogue of edge-disjoint cycle basis.

**Test:** Compute tropical kernels of the 1-Laplacian for triangulated surfaces (sphere, torus, Klein bottle) with ≤ 20 triangles. Identify generators and test uniqueness.

**Impact:** Would generalize tropical kernel rigidity from graphs (1-dimensional) to simplicial complexes (arbitrary dimension), opening connections to computational topology, persistent homology, and topological data analysis.

**Catalog References:**
- `Pythagorean/TropicalBridge/KernelDefs.lean`: `tropicalKernel` (generalize from graphs to simplicial complexes)
- `Pythagorean/TropicalBridge/DefectTheory.lean`: cycle rank, component count (generalize to Betti numbers)

**Proof Strategy:** Define the higher Laplacian L_k as the combinatorial Hodge Laplacian of K at dimension k. The tropical kernel generalizes directly. Canonical generators should arise from (k+1)-dimensional cochains supported on boundary components. The separation hypothesis generalizes to requiring that k-cycles in the complex have non-overlapping support. **The key insight is** that the support separation argument (Theorem 5.1) is dimension-independent — it works for any Fintype-indexed matrix, not just graph Laplacians.

**Domain Bridges:** Tropical algebra ↔ algebraic topology ↔ topological data analysis ↔ computational topology.

**Lineage:** Generalizes all results in KernelDefs.lean and KernelRigidity.lean from graphs to simplicial complexes.

**Ambition:** Grand challenge. Would create a new field of "tropical computational topology."

**Why now?** The abstract formulation of tropical kernels in KernelDefs.lean already works for arbitrary Fintype-indexed matrices, providing the formal scaffolding. Recent work on tropical homology (Itenberg–Katzarkov–Mikhalkin–Zharkov 2019) provides the mathematical framework.

## Direction 5: Network Fingerprinting Applications

**Conjecture:** Tropical kernel signatures — the multiset of canonical generator support patterns across all choices of basepoint q — form a polynomial-time computable graph invariant that distinguishes more graph pairs than the Weisfeiler-Leman color refinement algorithm.

**Test:** Compute tropical kernel signatures for all non-isomorphic graphs on n ≤ 10 vertices. Compare distinguishing power to 1-WL, 2-WL, and spectral methods. Identify the smallest pair of graphs distinguished by tropical signatures but not by 1-WL.

**Impact:** Would establish tropical kernel theory as a practical tool for graph isomorphism testing and network classification, with applications to cheminformatics, social network analysis, and cybersecurity (network anomaly detection).

**Catalog References:**
- `Pythagorean/TropicalBridge/KernelRigidity.lean`: `tropProjEquiv_preserves_constant_count`, `tropProjEquiv_preserves_membership`
- `Pythagorean/TropicalBridge/KernelDefs.lean`: `CycleMatroidEquivOn`, `SameQVisibilityData`

**Proof Strategy:** Define the tropical kernel signature as a tuple of invariants (kernel dimension, support pattern multiset, component count) computed for each basepoint. Prove that isomorphic graphs have equal signatures (routine). The hard direction — finding graphs distinguished by tropical signatures but not by WL — requires computational search. **The key insight is** that tropical kernel signatures capture matroid-level information (via Theorem 6.2), which is strictly finer than what WL can detect in general.

**Domain Bridges:** Graph theory ↔ machine learning ↔ computational chemistry ↔ network science.

**Lineage:** Applies the matroidal corollary (Theorem 6.2) to practical graph comparison.

**Ambition:** Solid extension with high practical impact. Could lead to a new family of graph kernels for machine learning.

**Why now?** Graph neural networks and graph kernels are a rapidly growing area of machine learning. A mathematically grounded invariant with provable properties would be a valuable addition to the toolkit. The computational infrastructure (algorithms.py, demo.py) is already in place for large-scale testing.
