# Future Directions: EML Single Operator Universality

## Conjecture 1: EML Elementary Completeness with Polynomial Size

**Conjecture.** Every unary elementary real function on its natural domain admits an EML-normal-form representation (using only `eml(x,y) = exp(x) - log(y)`, constants, and field operations) of size at most polynomial in the original expression-tree size.

**Test.** Implement a compiler on a finite grammar of unary expressions up to depth `d = 10`. For each expression:
1. Compile to EML-only form using the verified algorithm.
2. Measure the size ratio `|compiled| / |original|`.
3. Check whether the ratio stays bounded by `O(n^k)` for fixed `k` as depth increases.

Our current proof establishes a *linear* bound (≤ 5×) for the syntactic compilation. The conjecture asks whether semantic simplification can maintain polynomial size even for more aggressive normalizations.

**Impact.** If true, this establishes that EML is not just universal but *efficiently* universal — the compiled form is never more than polynomially larger. This would be analogous to the polynomial simulation theorem in Boolean circuit complexity.

**How it could be falsified.** Find a family of expressions (e.g., iterated compositions `exp(exp(...exp(x)...))`) where every semantically correct EML compilation has superpolynomial growth after simplification.

## Conjecture 2: Single-Neuron Elementary Universality

**Conjecture.** Every unary elementary function can be expressed as a finite composition of affine maps `x ↦ ax + b` and the binary primitive `(a, b) ↦ eml(a, b) = exp(a) - log(b)`, on a suitable positive-domain encoding.

**Test.** Brute-force search over a bounded grammar of compositions for exact or approximate recovery of target functions:
- `x²`, `x³`, `1/x`
- `sinh(x)`, `cosh(x)`, `tanh(x)`
- `exp(exp(x))`, `log(1+x)`
- Polynomial `p(x)` of degree ≤ 5

For each target, search compositions up to depth 8 and measure:
- Whether a composition achieving `< 10⁻⁶` error on `[0.1, 10]` exists
- The minimal composition depth required

**Impact.** This would connect EML universality to *neural network expressivity*: a single "eml neuron" with affine pre/post-processing suffices for all elementary computation, giving a minimal-architecture universality theorem.

**How it could be falsified.** Demonstrate that polynomial functions of degree ≥ 2 cannot be approximated to within `ε = 0.01` by any depth-`d` composition for `d < 100`, suggesting that affine + eml compositions have fundamental approximation barriers for algebraic functions.

## Conjecture 3: Differential Closure is Tight

**Conjecture.** The differential field generated by `{exp, log, +, -, ×, ÷, constants}` over `ℝ` is closed under differentiation, and this closure is the *smallest* such field containing `exp` and `log`.

**Test.** Formalize in Lean that:
1. For every EML expression `e`, the function `d/dx[eval(e)]` is again EML-representable (we have proved the base cases; the conjecture is about full inductive closure).
2. No proper sub-algebra of EML expressions is closed under differentiation (requires showing that removing any generator breaks closure).

**Impact.** This would establish the EML algebra as the *canonical* differential algebra for elementary real analysis, connecting to Liouville's theorem on integration in finite terms and Risch's algorithm for symbolic integration.

**How it could be falsified.** Find an EML-representable function whose derivative requires transcendental operations not in the EML algebra (unlikely for the full algebra, but possible for restricted fragments).

## Conjecture 4: Trigonometric Obstruction

**Conjecture.** No nonconstant periodic function `f : ℝ → ℝ` is EML-representable (i.e., expressible via `exp`, `log`, constants, and field operations over the reals).

**Test.**
1. Attempt to prove in Lean that if `f` is EML-representable and differentiable, then `f` satisfies a polynomial-exponential ODE, and such ODEs cannot have periodic solutions with irrational period ratios.
2. Alternatively, use the Ax–Schanuel theorem to show that `sin(x)` cannot satisfy any algebraic relation over the field `ℝ(x, exp(x), log(x))`.

**Impact.** This would precisely delimit the boundary of EML universality: the elementary exp-log closure generates all non-periodic elementary functions, but trigonometric functions require either complexification (`exp(ix)`) or an additional primitive. This is scientifically important because it identifies the *exact* obstruction to full elementary universality.

**How it could be falsified.** Construct an explicit representation of `sin(x)` or any periodic function using only real `exp`, `log`, and field operations (this would be a major surprise in real analysis).

## Conjecture 5: EML Circuit Depth Separation

**Conjecture.** There exist elementary functions that require depth `Ω(log n)` in EML-only circuits (expression trees using `eml`, constants, and field operations) even though they have size-`n` representations in the full EML language (with separate `exp` and `log`).

**Test.**
1. Define a formal notion of EML circuit depth and size.
2. For the function family `f_n(x) = exp(exp(...exp(x)...))` (n-fold iterated exponential), measure the minimum depth of an EML-only representation.
3. Each `exp` becomes `eml(·, 1)`, so depth should grow linearly. But can clever sharing reduce depth?

**Impact.** A depth separation would show that while EML is *expressively* equivalent to {exp, log}, it may have a *complexity* overhead. This connects EML universality to algebraic circuit complexity and could inform analog circuit design (where circuit depth corresponds to signal delay).

**How it could be falsified.** Show that every EML expression can be rewritten to an EML-only expression of the same depth (up to a constant), implying no depth penalty for the single-operator restriction.
