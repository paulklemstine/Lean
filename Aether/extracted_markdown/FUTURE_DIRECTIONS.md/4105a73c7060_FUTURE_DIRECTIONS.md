# FUTURE DIRECTIONS — Algebraic Statistics: Markov Bases for Contingency Tables

## Synthesis

This cycle built, from scratch, a small but complete formal theory of **Markov bases for
log-linear models on contingency tables** — a corner of algebraic statistics that is absent
from Mathlib. We modelled a contingency table as a free `ℤ`-module element
(`Fin 2 → Fin 2 → ℤ` and `Fin 2 → Fin 2 → Fin 2 → ℤ`), defined the *margins* (sufficient
statistics) as explicit linear functionals (`rowSum`/`colSum`, and the three two-way margins
`marginIJ`/`marginIK`/`marginJK`), and defined fiber connectivity via the reflexive–transitive
closure of single non-negative `±M` moves. Inside this language we proved the two pillars of
the theory for the two smallest models: (1) the **move lattice is the kernel of the margin
map** (`twoWay_kernel`, `noThreeWay_kernel`), and (2) for the no-three-way interaction model a
single explicit move **connects every fiber** (`noThreeWay_fiber_connected`) — a concrete
instance of the Diaconis–Sturmfels *Fundamental Theorem of Markov Bases*. The analogous
connectivity theorem for the `2×2` independence model (`twoWay_fiber_connected`) was proved by
the same machinery.

The structural insight that emerged is that **both showcase models have a rank-one move
lattice**, generated by a single alternating sign pattern (`M2 i j = (-1)^{i+j}` for the `2×2`
independence model, `M3 i j k = (-1)^{i+j+k}` for the `2×2×2` no-three-way model). This
collapses the deep algebraic content (toric ideals, Gröbner bases) into a finite linear-algebra
fact provable by `fin_cases` + `omega`, and reduces connectivity to a single clean idea:
**discrete convexity**. The non-negative locus along the move line is an integer interval, so a
monotone walk of unit `±M` steps from one fiber element to another never leaves the
non-negative orthant. This convexity argument, packaged as `connected_add_smul` (built from the
two monotone helpers `connected_move_nat`/`connected_move_neg`), is the reusable engine and the
technical heart of the cycle; everything else is bookkeeping. A second, organizational insight
is that the engine is **model-agnostic**: it depends only on the move taking values `±1`, so the
*same* proof skeleton served both the `2×2` and the `2×2×2` model.

What did *not* generalise for free: rank-one is special. The moment the model has more than two
slices (`2×2×n`) the kernel acquires several generators and the single-line walk argument
breaks — connectivity then genuinely needs the multi-generator content of the Fundamental
Theorem. We deliberately recorded the boundary (the `TableN` type and the conjectures below)
rather than papering over it, because identifying exactly where rank-one stops working is the
most valuable signpost for the next team.

## Results Summary

- `MarkovBases.TwoWay.M2_apply_eq`: proved — the basic `2×2` swap takes only the values `±1` (support lemma for omega/convexity).
- `MarkovBases.TwoWay.twoWay_move_preserves_margins`: proved — every multiple of `M2` lies in the kernel of the margin map, so it is a legal model move.
- `MarkovBases.TwoWay.twoWay_kernel`: proved — the move lattice of the `2×2` independence model is rank one (`{M2}` is a Markov basis).
- `MarkovBases.TwoWay.connected_add_smul`: proved — discrete-convexity engine for the `2×2` model.
- `MarkovBases.TwoWay.twoWay_fiber_connected`: proved — Fundamental Theorem for the `2×2` independence model: `{M2}` connects every fiber.
- `MarkovBases.NoThreeWay.M3_apply_eq`: proved — the degree-4 alternating move takes only the values `±1`.
- `MarkovBases.NoThreeWay.noThreeWay_move_preserves_margins`: proved — `M3` lies in the kernel of the two-way margin map.
- `MarkovBases.NoThreeWay.noThreeWay_kernel`: proved — despite 8 cells and 12 margin equations the move lattice is rank one, generated by the single move `M3` (it is *the* Markov basis).
- `MarkovBases.NoThreeWay.connected_add_smul`: proved — discrete-convexity engine: equal-margin tables are joined by a monotone non-negative walk of `±M3` steps.
- `MarkovBases.NoThreeWay.noThreeWay_fiber_connected`: proved — **Fundamental Theorem of Markov Bases for the no-three-way model**: `{M3}` connects every fiber.
- `MarkovBases.NoThreeWay.TableN` (the `2×2×n` extension): recorded — a type marking the boundary where rank-one breaks; the connectivity conjecture is stated below, not yet formalized.

## Research Directions

### Direction 1: The `2×2×n` no-three-way model has an explicit slice-pair Markov basis
**Hypothesis**: For the `2 × 2 × n` no-three-way interaction model, the finite set of moves
`{ M_{a,b} : a < b in Fin n }`, where `M_{a,b}` is the `2×2×2` alternating move on the pair of
slices `a, b` (zero elsewhere), is a Markov basis: any two non-negative tables with equal
two-way margins are connected by a non-negative walk using `±` these moves.
**Test**: Formalise `TableN n` (already declared in `NoThreeWay.lean`), define the slice-pair
moves, and prove a multi-generator analogue of `noThreeWay_fiber_connected`. Disproof would be
a small explicit counterexample (two equal-margin tables with no non-negative slice-pair walk
between them), which `decide`/search can hunt for at `n = 3`.
The key insight is that the difference of two equal-margin tables, restricted to any pair of
slices, has zero two-way margins on that `2×2×2` sub-block, so `noThreeWay_kernel` applies
slice-pair-by-slice-pair — turning the global problem into a sequence of the rank-one moves we
already control.
**Why now**: We already proved the rank-one building block (`noThreeWay_kernel`) and the
convexity walk (`connected_add_smul`); this direction is exactly "iterate the block we have"
rather than inventing new machinery.
**If true**: It gives the first formally verified *non-trivial* (multi-generator) Markov basis,
the natural stepping stone to general no-three-way models.
**If false**: A counterexample would pin down the smallest model where slice-pair moves fail to
connect, sharpening the conjectured form of the basis.

### Direction 2: Markov basis ⇔ generators of the toric (lattice) ideal
**Hypothesis**: For these models, a finite move set `B ⊆ ker(margin map)` connects every fiber
**iff** the corresponding binomials generate the lattice ideal `I_L` of the margin map — the
Diaconis–Sturmfels equivalence in its purest, ideal-free `ℤ`-lattice form.
**Test**: State connectivity and "`B` generates the saturated sublattice `L = ker ∩ ℤ^N`" and
prove the equivalence for the rank-one cases (where it reduces to: `B` connects ⇔ `B` spans
`L`). Then attempt the general lattice version using `Submodule.span`.
The key insight is that, stripped of commutative-algebra packaging, the Fundamental Theorem is
a statement about *lattice walks vs. lattice generation*: connectivity of fibers is the
combinatorial shadow of `Submodule.span_eq`.
**Why now**: Our `*_kernel` theorems already exhibit the move lattice explicitly as a rank-one
`ℤ`-submodule (the span of a single generator), so the span side of the equivalence is in reach
without building any toric-ideal infrastructure.
**If true**: It yields a Mathlib-ready, ideal-free formulation of the Fundamental Theorem,
usable far beyond contingency tables.
**If false**: The gap (saturation / primitivity of the lattice) would reveal exactly which
algebraic hypothesis the purely combinatorial statement is missing.

### Direction 3: Fiber connectivity via a Graver bound on walk length
**Hypothesis**: In the no-three-way `2×2×2` model, every two equal-margin tables are connected
by a walk of length exactly `|v₀₀₀ − u₀₀₀|`, and this is the diameter of the fiber graph; more
generally a Graver-type argument controls a linear bound on walk length.
**Test**: Strengthen `connected_add_smul` to return the path and prove its length equals
`t.natAbs`; show no shorter walk exists (a lower bound from the linear functional `u ↦ u₀₀₀`,
which changes by exactly `±1` per move).
The key insight is that the single coordinate `u₀₀₀` is a *potential function* that changes by
exactly `±1` per move, so it simultaneously certifies reachability and optimality.
**Why now**: The induction in `connected_move_nat`/`connected_move_neg` already walks in
`t.natAbs` steps; reading off the length and proving optimality is a small refactor of an
existing proof.
**If true**: It converts our qualitative connectivity into a quantitative *mixing/diameter*
statement, the quantity statisticians actually care about for MCMC on tables.
**If false**: A shorter walk would mean the move lattice is not "primitive" in the metric
sense — an interesting and testable surprise.

### Direction 4: Negative result — `2×2` swaps alone do NOT connect the no-three-way model
**Hypothesis (expected true, i.e. a disproof of naive sufficiency)**: The set of simple `2×2`
swap moves (which suffice for every *two-way* table model) is **not** a Markov basis for the
`2×2×2` no-three-way model: there exist two equal-margin tables not connected by any
non-negative walk of `2×2` swaps, because every such swap changes some two-way margin.
**Test**: Exhibit an explicit pair of `0/1` tables with identical two-way margins whose
difference is `±M3`, and prove that every `2×2` swap necessarily alters one of the two-way
margins (so no swap is even a legal move) — a finite check building on
`noThreeWay_move_preserves_margins` and `noThreeWay_kernel`.
The key insight is that every simple `2×2` swap lives in the kernel of the *one-way* map, not
the *two-way* map, so it is *illegal* in this model — which is the whole reason the degree-4
move `M3` is forced.
**Why now**: `noThreeWay_move_preserves_margins` already characterises which moves preserve the
two-way margins; the negative result is its contrapositive made explicit, and `noThreeWay_kernel`
already shows `M3` is the unique (up to scaling) legal move.
**If true**: It rigorously demonstrates *why* higher-degree Markov moves are unavoidable — the
pedagogical punchline of the no-three-way example.
**If false**: It would mean we mis-specified the margins; a valuable consistency check on the
formalisation itself.

### Direction 5: Connectivity is an equivalence relation, and fibers are exactly its classes
**Hypothesis**: `Connected` is an equivalence relation on non-negative tables, and its classes
are *exactly* the fibers; equivalently `Connected u v ↔ Nonneg u ∧ Nonneg v ∧ SameMargins u v`
(for both the `TwoWay` and `NoThreeWay` models).
**Test**: Prove symmetry and transitivity of `Connected` (transitivity is
`Relation.ReflTransGen.trans`; symmetry needs that `Step` is symmetric on the `±M` choice),
then combine with `*_fiber_connected` for the `←` direction and `*_move_preserves_margins` for
the `→` direction.
The key insight is that `Step` is *self-inverse* (`v = move u 1 ↔ u = move v (-1)`), so the
reflexive-transitive closure is automatically symmetric — connectivity is a genuine equivalence,
and `*_fiber_connected` says it has the coarsest possible classes.
**Why now**: We already have one direction (`*_fiber_connected`) and margin invariance
(`*_move_preserves_margins`); only the easy symmetry/transitivity glue remains.
**If true**: It packages each model as a clean "fibers = connectivity classes" theorem, the
cleanest possible statement of the Fundamental Theorem for downstream use.
**If false**: It would expose a missing non-negativity or margin hypothesis, i.e. a flaw in the
fiber definition itself.
