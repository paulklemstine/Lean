# Future Directions: Hyperbolic Number Theory

## Synthesis

This research cycle established the mathematical foundations for arithmetic on the Poincaré disk, proving that Möbius transformations preserve the disk (via the key identity (1-|a|²)(1-|z|²) > 0), that the hyperbolic norm is strictly monotone and characterizes the origin, and that lattice points from classical number theory project naturally into hyperbolic space. The most promising cross-domain connection discovered is the bridge between **lattice point counting** (the Gauss circle problem, a pillar of analytic number theory) and **hyperbolic geometry** (via the projection theorem `lattice_to_disk`): integer points in ℤ² map into the Poincaré disk, and the distribution of their images near the boundary encodes the error term in the classical count.

The connection to topology through the Gauss-Bonnet theorem (`lattice_euler_connection`) provides a second bridge: the number of fundamental domains tiling a hyperbolic surface is determined by the surface's genus, linking discrete counts to topological invariants. Combined with the Catalog's existing work on algebraic structures (`Algebra/Foundations.lean`) and prime dynamics (`Pythagorean/DynamicalSquaring.lean`), these results create a triangular bridge: Number Theory ↔ Hyperbolic Geometry ↔ Topology.

The highest breakthrough potential lies in Direction 1 (the Selberg trace formula connection), because it would give a spectral interpretation of hyperbolic prime counting and potentially connect to the Riemann Hypothesis — not as an abstract analogy, but as a structural consequence of spectral theory on hyperbolic surfaces.

### Direction 1: Spectral Theory of Hyperbolic Primes — The Selberg Connection

**Conjecture**: For a hyperbolic lattice Γ generated by k ≥ 2 Möbius translations with translation distance d, the primitive length spectrum {ℓ(γ) : γ primitive in Γ} satisfies a Weyl-type law: #{γ : ℓ(γ) ≤ T} ~ e^T / T as T → ∞. Moreover, the Selberg zeta function Z_Γ(s) has all its non-trivial zeros on the line Re(s) = 1/2.

**Test**: For the lattice generated by T_{(r,0)} and T_{(0,r)} with r = tanh(1) ≈ 0.7616, compute the first 50 primitive conjugacy class lengths and compare the counting function to e^T/T. Numerically evaluate Z_Γ(s) along Re(s) = 1/2 ± ε for ε = 0.01, 0.1, 0.5 and verify zeros concentrate on the critical line.

**Impact**: If true, this would establish the Riemann Hypothesis for hyperbolic zeta functions as a theorem (following Selberg's approach via the trace formula), providing a rigorous example of a "geometric RH." If the zeros deviate from Re(s) = 1/2, it would reveal that the analogy between hyperbolic and classical primes breaks down at the spectral level.

**Catalog References**: `Catalog/Algebra/Foundations.lean` (critical_line_implies_unit_disk), `Catalog/Pythagorean/DynamicalSquaring.lean` (prime_has_two_fixed_points)

**Proof Strategy**: (1) Formalize the Selberg trace formula for co-compact Fuchsian groups using the existing spectral theory in Mathlib. (2) Define the Selberg zeta function as a product over primitive conjugacy classes. (3) Use the trace formula to relate zeros of Z_Γ to eigenvalues of the Laplacian. (4) Apply the fact that eigenvalues λ ≥ 1/4 for co-compact groups to show all zeros have Re(s) = 1/2.

**Domain Bridges**: NumberTheory ↔ SpectralTheory, HyperbolicGeometry ↔ AnalyticNumberTheory

**Lineage**: Builds on `moebius_preserves_disk`, `hypNorm_strict_mono`, and `lattice_euler_connection` from this cycle.

### Direction 2: Unique Factorization in Hyperbolic Arithmetic

**Conjecture**: The hyperbolic integers ℤ_H generated by k ≥ 2 Möbius translations form a unique factorization domain (with respect to word concatenation and cancellation) if and only if the generators satisfy a "free group" condition: no non-trivial relation holds among the generators.

**Test**: For generators g₁ = (0.5, 0) and g₂ = (0, 0.5), enumerate all words of length ≤ 8 and check whether distinct primitive factorizations yield the same disk point (within numerical tolerance 10⁻¹⁰). If two distinct factorizations collide, UFD fails. Then repeat with generators chosen to satisfy a relation (e.g., g₁² = g₂³ in the group) and verify that UFD fails in that case.

**Impact**: A positive result would establish that "generic" hyperbolic lattices have unique factorization, making them true analogs of ℤ. A negative result would classify which lattices are UFDs and which are not, paralleling the classical study of class numbers in algebraic number theory.

**Catalog References**: `Speculative/HyperbolicNumberTheory/Core.lean` (isPrimitive, HypWord)

**Proof Strategy**: (1) Prove that the group generated by Möbius translations is free when generators are sufficiently separated (using the ping-pong lemma). (2) Show that freeness implies unique factorization of words. (3) Exhibit a non-free example and demonstrate non-unique factorization.

**Domain Bridges**: Algebra ↔ HyperbolicGeometry, GroupTheory ↔ NumberTheory

**Lineage**: Builds on `isPrimitive` definition and `moebius_preserves_disk` from this cycle.

**Ambition**: extension

### Direction 3: Hyperbolic Lattice Points and the Gauss Circle Error Term

**Conjecture**: The projection of ℤ² lattice points into the Poincaré disk via (a,b) ↦ (a,b)/(√(a²+b²)+1) satisfies: the number of projected points with hyperbolic norm ≤ H is π·(tanh(H/2))² + O(tanh(H/2)^{2/3}), recovering the conjectured exponent 1/3 + ε in the Gauss circle problem through hyperbolic coordinates.

**Test**: Compute the projected lattice point count for H = 1, 2, ..., 20 and fit the error term to C·tanh(H/2)^α. The predicted exponent α should be close to 2/3 (corresponding to the classical conjectured error exponent 1/3 after the change of variables).

**Impact**: If the hyperbolic reformulation makes the error term more tractable, it would provide a new approach to the Gauss circle problem — one of the oldest open problems in number theory. Even partial results (e.g., improving the error exponent from 2/3 to a smaller value in hyperbolic coordinates) would be significant.

**Catalog References**: `Speculative/HyperbolicNumberTheory/Core.lean` (lattice_to_disk, lattice_count_monotone, latticePointsInBall)

**Proof Strategy**: (1) Establish the change-of-variables formula relating Euclidean ball counts to hyperbolic ball counts. (2) Use the Jacobian of the projection map to convert the classical Gauss circle error into hyperbolic coordinates. (3) Apply known estimates for lattice points in hyperbolic space (Selberg, Huber) to bound the error.

**Domain Bridges**: NumberTheory ↔ HyperbolicGeometry, AnalyticNumberTheory ↔ DifferentialGeometry

**Lineage**: Builds on `lattice_to_disk` and `lattice_count_monotone` from this cycle.

**Ambition**: grand_challenge

### Direction 4: Machine Learning on Hyperbolic Integers

**Conjecture**: Embedding hierarchical datasets (taxonomies, knowledge graphs, phylogenetic trees) using the hyperbolic integer lattice ℤ_H instead of continuous Poincaré embeddings yields comparable or better representation quality with O(log n) bits per node instead of O(d · 32) bits (d = embedding dimension, 32 = float precision).

**Test**: Embed the WordNet noun hierarchy (80,000 nodes) using (a) continuous Poincaré embeddings (Nickel & Kiela, 2017), (b) hyperbolic integers with k=3 generators and word length ≤ 15. Compare mean average precision (MAP) on link prediction and reconstruction error. The discrete embedding should achieve MAP ≥ 0.85 · MAP(continuous) while using ≤ 50% of the memory.

**Impact**: If successful, this would provide the first practical application of hyperbolic number theory: compact, exact representations of hierarchical data using discrete hyperbolic coordinates instead of floating-point embeddings.

**Catalog References**: `Catalog/MachineLearning/` (many declarations, unexplored bridge to Algebra)

**Proof Strategy**: (1) Define a quantization map from continuous Poincaré embeddings to nearest hyperbolic integers. (2) Bound the distortion introduced by quantization using the monotonicity theorem `hypNorm_strict_mono`. (3) Show that the tree-like structure of the lattice orbit makes it well-suited for hierarchical data.

**Domain Bridges**: MachineLearning ↔ HyperbolicGeometry, Computation ↔ NumberTheory

**Lineage**: Builds on `embedNat_order_preserving` and `hypNorm_rotation_invariant` from this cycle, connects to Catalog's MachineLearning and Algebra domains.

### Direction 5: Tropical Geometry of Hyperbolic Distance

**Conjecture**: The hyperbolic distance function d_H(p, q) on the Poincaré disk, when restricted to lattice points along a geodesic, satisfies a tropical (min-plus) algebraic structure: the "tropical sum" of two hyperbolic distances equals their minimum, and the "tropical product" equals their ordinary sum. This makes (ℤ_H along a geodesic, min, +) a tropical semiring.

**Test**: For points p₀, p₁, ..., pₙ along a geodesic from origin, verify that d_H(p₀, pᵢ) + d_H(pᵢ, pⱼ) = d_H(p₀, pⱼ) for i < j (additive along geodesics). Then check that the matrix M_{ij} = d_H(pᵢ, pⱼ) is a tropical rank-1 matrix.

**Impact**: If the tropical structure holds, it would create a bridge between the Catalog's Tropical geometry module and hyperbolic number theory — a genuinely new cross-domain connection. It would also suggest that tropical methods (widely used in algebraic geometry and optimization) can be applied to problems in hyperbolic geometry.

**Catalog References**: `Catalog/Tropical/` (existing tropical semiring infrastructure), `Catalog/Algebra/Foundations.lean`

**Proof Strategy**: (1) Prove that hyperbolic distance is additive along geodesics (a classical result). (2) Show that the restriction of d_H to a geodesic lattice forms a tropical semiring. (3) Investigate whether this extends to 2D lattices (likely requires a modified tropical structure).

**Domain Bridges**: Tropical ↔ HyperbolicGeometry, Algebra ↔ Geometry

**Lineage**: Builds on `hypNorm_strict_mono` and the conformal factor results from this cycle.

**Ambition**: extension
