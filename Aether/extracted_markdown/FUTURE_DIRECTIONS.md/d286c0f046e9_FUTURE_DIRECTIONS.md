# Future Directions: EML Depth Hierarchy with Inversions

## Synthesis

The EML Depth Hierarchy establishes that exponential nesting depth is a fundamental complexity measure for algebraic-exponential expressions, robust even under the addition of inversions. The current work proves the hierarchy for the inverse-free fragment and reduces the full hierarchy to a single analytic claim: the Full EML Majorant conjecture. This conjecture sits at the intersection of Hardy field theory, o-minimal model theory, and differential algebra. The five directions below systematically attack this conjecture from different angles while extending the hierarchy's reach into new mathematical domains.

The key insight linking all directions: the depth hierarchy is ultimately a statement about the **growth rate structure** of the Hardy field generated by EML expressions. Directions 1 and 2 attack the Hardy field gap directly. Direction 3 connects to computation. Directions 4 and 5 open new mathematical territory.

## Direction 1: Hardy Field Closure for EML Expressions

**Conjecture:** The set of germs of EML expressions at +∞ forms a Hardy field — i.e., every non-zero EML expression is eventually sign-definite. Moreover, every non-zero element of tower-level d has a tower-level-d lower bound.

**Test:** Take specific EML expressions with inversions, e.g., f(x) = exp(x) + 1/(exp(x) - x). Verify computationally that |f(x)| ≥ 1/tower(1, C·x^N) for large x and appropriate C, N. Test 100 random depth-1 expressions with inversions at x = 10, 100, 1000.

**Impact:** Would immediately prove the Full EML Majorant conjecture and complete the hierarchy theorem for expressions with inversions. This is the single most important open problem.

**Catalog References:** `Pythagorean/FullEMLHierarchy/Theorems.lean` — `fullEML_has_majorant`, `inv_majorant_of_lower_bound`

**Proof Strategy:** Use the Khovanskii theory of fewnomials and Pfaffian functions. EML expressions are Pfaffian functions, and Pfaffian functions in one variable are known to have finitely many zeros. This implies eventual sign-definiteness. The lower bound then follows from a quantitative version of the Łojasiewicz inequality for Pfaffian functions.

**Domain Bridges:** Model Theory (o-minimality) ↔ Analysis (Hardy fields) ↔ Algebraic Geometry (Pfaffian varieties)

**Lineage:** Extends `invFree_has_majorant` and `inv_majorant_of_lower_bound`

**Ambition:** ★★★★☆ — Requires substantial formalization of Pfaffian function theory but builds on well-established mathematics.

## Direction 2: Tropical Depth Hierarchy

**Conjecture:** The *tropicalization* of EML (replacing exp with tropical exponentiation max(·, 0), and multiplication with addition) has an analogous strict depth hierarchy. Tropical tower(n, x) requires tropical expDepth ≥ n.

**Test:** Define tropical EML where exp → max(·, 0), add → max, mul → +. Verify computationally that no tropical expression of depth 2 matches tropical tower(3) at 10 test points. Enumerate all tropical expressions with ≤ 6 nodes.

**Impact:** Would provide a combinatorial skeleton for the analytic hierarchy. Tropical arguments are often dramatically simpler and could serve as a template for the full proof.

**Catalog References:** `Pythagorean/FullEMLHierarchy/Defs.lean` — `FullEML`, `expDepth`

**Proof Strategy:** Tropicalize the majorant bound. In the tropical world, tower(d, C + N·x) = max^[d](C + N·x, 0), and the hierarchy becomes a statement about iterated max operations vs. linear functions. This should be provable by elementary combinatorics.

**Domain Bridges:** Tropical Geometry ↔ Algebraic Complexity ↔ Combinatorics

**Lineage:** New direction, inspired by tropical techniques in algebraic complexity

**Ambition:** ★★★☆☆ — Highly tractable; could yield quick wins.

## Direction 3: Neural Network Approximation Lower Bounds

**Conjecture:** A neural network with exp activation functions at depth d cannot uniformly approximate tower(n, x) on any interval [a, b] with a > 0 when d < n, regardless of the network's width. More precisely, the L∞ approximation error on [1, 2] is at least tower(n, 1) - tower(d, C) for any depth-d network of width ≤ W.

**Test:** Train depth-d networks (d = 1, 2) to approximate tower(3) on [0.5, 2.0] using gradient descent with various widths (10, 100, 1000). Measure L∞ error. Compare error scaling with width for d < 3 vs d = 3.

**Impact:** Would provide rigorous expressivity separation results for neural networks with transcendental activations, complementing existing results for ReLU networks.

**Catalog References:** `Pythagorean/FullEMLHierarchy/Theorems.lean` — `no_lowExpDepth_represents_tower`, `hierarchy_from_majorant`

**Proof Strategy:** A depth-d exp-network computes a function f(x) = σ(W_d · σ(... σ(W_1 · x + b_1) ...) + b_d) where σ = exp. This is an EML expression of expDepth ≤ d. The hierarchy theorem gives the impossibility; quantifying the approximation gap requires estimating the majorant constants.

**Domain Bridges:** Machine Learning ↔ Algebraic Complexity ↔ Approximation Theory

**Lineage:** Extends `hierarchy_from_majorant`

**Ambition:** ★★★★★ — Grand challenge. Would be the first rigorous depth separation for exp-networks.

## Direction 4: Differential Transcendence Degree

**Conjecture:** The *differential transcendence degree* of tower(n, x) over the field of rational functions is exactly n. That is, the minimum number of nested differential equations needed to define tower(n) from scratch is n.

**Test:** For n = 1: exp(x) satisfies y' = y (one equation). For n = 2: exp(exp(x)) satisfies y' = y·f₁ where f₁ = exp(x) satisfies f₁' = f₁ (two equations). Verify that no single differential equation of order ≤ n-1 has tower(n) as a solution, by checking the Wronskian determinant numerically.

**Impact:** Would provide a differential-algebraic characterization of the depth hierarchy, independent of the syntactic definition. This connects to the classical theory of differential fields and Kolchin's work.

**Catalog References:** `Pythagorean/FullEMLHierarchy/Theorems.lean` — `expDepth_formalDerivative_le`

**Proof Strategy:** Use the theorem that differentiation preserves expDepth (already proved). Show that the chain of differential equations satisfied by tower(n) cannot be shortened. This requires proving that tower(n) is not differentially algebraic over tower(n-1) — which follows from the growth rate separation.

**Domain Bridges:** Differential Algebra ↔ EML Complexity ↔ Model Theory

**Lineage:** Extends `expDepth_formalDerivative_le`

**Ambition:** ★★★★☆ — Requires substantial differential algebra but proof strategy is clear.

## Direction 5: Effective Hierarchy with Complexity Bounds

**Conjecture:** For any EML expression f of expDepth d < n with at most S nodes, the first x₀ at which tower(n, x₀) > |f(x₀)| satisfies x₀ ≤ tower(d, poly(S, |constants|)). That is, the hierarchy becomes effective at a computably bounded threshold.

**Test:** For randomly generated depth-1 expressions of size S ∈ {3, 5, 7, 10}, find the smallest x₀ such that tower(2, x₀) > |f(x₀)|. Plot x₀ vs S and fit to tower(1, ·) to estimate the growth rate of the threshold.

**Impact:** Would make the hierarchy theorem *constructive* and provide explicit bounds for applications in computational complexity and neural network theory.

**Catalog References:** `Pythagorean/FullEMLHierarchy/Theorems.lean` — `tower_succ_escapes_poly_tower`, `poly_dominated_by_exp`

**Proof Strategy:** Track the constants C, N, X₀ through the inductive proof of the majorant theorem. The key is bounding the absorption constant in the tower_poly_absorbs_sum lemma — specifically, how the threshold X₀ depends on the expression size and depth.

**Domain Bridges:** Computational Complexity ↔ Analytic Number Theory ↔ Algebraic Complexity

**Lineage:** Extends `tower_succ_escapes_poly_tower`, `invFree_has_majorant`

**Ambition:** ★★★☆☆ — Technically demanding but straightforward conceptually.
