# Future Directions: Tropical Differential Equations

## 1. Tropical Fundamental Theorem of Differential Algebra

The classical fundamental theorem of differential algebra (Ritt–Raudenbush) states that every radical differential ideal is an intersection of prime differential ideals. Its tropical analogue should assert that the tropicalization of a differential ideal equals the tropical differential ideal of the tropicalization — that is, the "shadow" under valuation of the solution set of a system of ODEs can be computed purely tropically. The key insight is that our `formalDeriv` and `order_formalDeriv_ge` provide the base case: the derivative operator shifts the Newton polygon by at most one unit, which is precisely the local version of this correspondence. Why now? We have the formal derivative infrastructure and order bounds that serve as the foundation; the next step is to define tropical differential ideals (as sub-semirings of `ℕ →₀ WithTop ℤ` closed under a tropical derivation operator) and prove the correspondence for principal ideals first.

## 2. Newton Polygon Slope–Root Correspondence

For a polynomial $f = \sum a_i x^i$ over a valued field $(K, v)$, the slopes of the lower convex hull of the points $(i, v(a_i))$ determine the valuations of the roots of $f$. This is the content of the classical Newton polygon theorem. The key insight is that our tropical polynomial evaluation `tropEval` already computes the Newton polygon function: the corners of $\min_i(a_i + ix)$ are exactly the negatives of the slopes. A formalization would define the Newton polygon as a `Finset (ℤ × ℤ)`, prove that the slopes biject with tropical roots (corners of `tropEval`), and establish the multiplicity formula. Why now? The `tropEval_attained` theorem proves that the minimum is attained, which is the starting point for identifying corners; what remains is characterizing when two distinct terms simultaneously achieve the minimum.

## 3. Tropical Wronskian and Linear Independence of Power Series

The Wronskian order bound `order_wronskian_ge` established here gives a necessary condition for two power series to be "tropically dependent." A natural conjecture: two power series $f, g$ over a valued field are linearly dependent over the constants if and only if their Wronskian vanishes, AND the order of the Wronskian is *exactly* $\operatorname{ord}(f) + \operatorname{ord}(g) - 1$ when they are independent. The key insight is that strict inequality in the Wronskian bound forces cancellation in the leading terms of $fg' - f'g$, which is a strong arithmetic constraint. Why now? The `order_wronskian_ge` bound is the "easy direction"; proving sharpness requires understanding when the leading coefficients of $fg'$ and $f'g$ fail to cancel, which connects to the non-vanishing of a tropical determinant.

## 4. Tropical Differential Galois Theory

Classical differential Galois theory associates a linear algebraic group to a linear ODE over a differential field. Tropicalization should reduce this group to a "tropical group" — a polyhedral object encoding the combinatorics of the solution space. Concretely, for a second-order linear ODE $y'' + p(x)y' + q(x)y = 0$ over $K((x))$, the tropical differential Galois group should be determined by the Newton polygons of $p$ and $q$. The key insight is that the Wronskian bound gives the order of the "tropical discriminant" of the ODE, and the slopes of the Newton polygon of $p$ and $q$ determine a filtration on the solution space that encodes the tropical Galois group. Why now? The formal derivative and Wronskian infrastructure provide the analytic backbone; connecting to Galois theory requires defining tropical matrix groups and their action on tropical solution spaces.

## 5. Effective Bounds on Power Series Solutions via Tropical Geometry

A concrete application: given a polynomial ODE $F(x, y, y', \ldots, y^{(n)}) = 0$ with coefficients in $\mathbb{Z}[[x]]$, the tropicalization of $F$ gives a piecewise-linear system whose solutions bound the possible orders and growth rates of classical power series solutions. This could yield computable certificates that an ODE has no formal power series solution of order below a given threshold. The key insight is that `order_formalDeriv_ge` iterates: $\operatorname{ord}(f^{(k)}) \geq \operatorname{ord}(f) - k$, so substituting into $F$ and tropicalizing gives a finite system of min-plus inequalities. Why now? The `order_formalDeriv_ge` theorem is the inductive step; iterating it $n$ times for an order-$n$ ODE and combining with the tropical evaluation framework from Part I gives the system of constraints, which can then be solved by linear programming over the tropical semiring.
