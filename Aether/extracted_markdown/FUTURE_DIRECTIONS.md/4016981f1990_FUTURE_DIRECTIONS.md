# Future Directions — Impossible Geometries via the Fibonacci Apparition Lattice

## Synthesis

This cycle reframed the divisibility structure of Fibonacci numbers as a *discrete
geometry* and proved that this geometry simultaneously exhibits the Euclidean and
the elliptic faces of the parallel postulate.

The organizing object is the **entry point** (rank of apparition) `α(m)`: the least
`k > 0` with `m ∣ F(k)`. The Entry-Point Characterization Theorem
(`fib_dvd_iff_entryPt_dvd`) shows that each divisibility "line"
`L(m) = {k | m ∣ F(k)}` is exactly the principal ideal `α(m)·ℕ`. Reading these
ideals geometrically:

* **Divergence (Euclidean face).** `apparition_step` proves that consecutive members
  of a line are spaced by *exactly* `α(m)`, with no member in between — evenly spaced
  parallel hyperplanes that drift apart linearly as `α(m)` grows.
* **Convergence (elliptic / "impossible" face).** `apparition_inter_eq_mul` and
  `apparition_lines_meet` prove that for coprime moduli the two lines are *forced to
  re-intersect*, and their meeting set is again a line generated by
  `lcm(α a, α b)` — parallel families that converge.

The bridge between the two faces is the **lcm law** `fibEntryPt_mul_coprime`
(`α(a·b) = lcm(α a, α b)` for coprime `a, b`), which we closed this cycle (it had
been left as a `sorry` research target). Together with the
`(3,4,5)`-instantiation (`fibEntryPt_five`, `fibEntryPt_three_four`, giving the
apparition profile `(4,6,5)`), this packages the apparition lattice into a usable
arithmetic-geometry dictionary.

## Direction 1 — The full apparition reconstruction functor `α` from prime powers

**Conjecture.** For every `m ≥ 1`, `α(m) = lcm over prime powers p^e ‖ m of α(p^e)`,
and `α(p^e) = p^{max(e - e_0, 0)} · α(p)` where `e_0` is the `p`-adic valuation of
`F(α(p))` (the "wall" exponent). Equivalently, `α` is a fully multiplicative-on-lcm
functor from the divisor lattice of `m` to the index lattice of `ℕ`.

**The key insight is** that `fibEntryPt_mul_coprime` already proves the *coprime
gluing* half; what remains is the single-prime *lifting law*, which is governed by
the `p`-adic valuation `v_p(F_k)` and the lifting-the-exponent lemma for Lucas
sequences. Once both halves are in place, `α` is computable from a factorization
without ever touching a Fibonacci number.

**Why now?** Mathlib has `Nat.factorization`, the multiplicative-function API, and
enough `padicValNat` machinery to state and attack the lifting law; the coprime half
is done, so this is the natural, immediately testable next theorem. Falsifiable: a
single `m` with `α(m) ≠ lcm_p α(p^{v_p m})` (search `m ≤ 10^5` computationally) kills it.

## Direction 2 — A genuine metric making the "impossible geometry" a metric space

**Conjecture.** The map `d(j,k) = (the number of distinct primes p with α(p) ∣ j XOR
α(p) ∣ k)`, restricted to apparition indices, is an (ultra)metric, and the coprime
re-intersection theorem `apparition_lines_meet` becomes the statement that any two
lines are at bounded Hausdorff distance — formally realizing "parallel lines that
both diverge (componentwise) and converge (in the metric)".

**The key insight is** that the convergence/divergence duality proved this cycle is
*not* metaphor: it is the difference between the product (componentwise) order on the
index lattice and the lcm-generated metric on it, and a single symmetric-difference
weight reconciles them.

**Why now?** Mathlib's `MetricSpace`/`PseudoMetricSpace` and `Finset.symmDiff` APIs
make the triangle inequality a finite combinatorial check; the underlying lines are
already proven to be ideals, so the carrier set is fully specified. Falsifiable by
exhibiting a triple of indices violating the (ultra)triangle inequality.

## Direction 3 — Carmichael's primitive-divisor theorem, the infinite tail

**Conjecture (target sorry).** Every composite `n > 10000` (indeed every
`n ∉ {1,2,6,12}`) has a Fibonacci primitive prime divisor; this closes the remaining
`sorry` in `Catalog/Shared/CarmichaelProof.lean` (`fib_carmichael_composite`).

**The key insight is** that, in the apparition language, a primitive divisor of `F(n)`
is exactly a prime with `α(p) = n` (`entryPt_eq_iff_primitive`), so Carmichael's
theorem is the surjectivity of `α` onto `ℕ \ {1,2,6,12}`; the proof reduces to a
lower bound `Φ_n(φ) > (largest non-primitive contribution)` on the `n`-th cyclotomic
value at the golden ratio, i.e. a Zsygmondy-style size estimate.

**Why now?** The finite range `n ≤ 10000` is already discharged by `native_decide`
in the file, and the entry-point reformulation is now available; the only missing
piece is the analytic cyclotomic lower bound, which is a self-contained growth
estimate (`F(n) ≈ φ^n / √5`) that Mathlib's `Real`/`Polynomial.cyclotomic` API can
support. Falsifiable instantly by any composite `n` with no primitive divisor.

## Direction 5 — Non-coprime collapse: the exact defect of the lcm law

**Conjecture.** For arbitrary `a, b` (not coprime), `α(a·b) ∣ lcm(α a, α b)` always,
and the *defect* `lcm(α a, α b) / α(a·b)` is supported only on primes dividing
`gcd(a,b)`, with an explicit formula in terms of shared apparition walls.

**The key insight is** that dropping coprimality cannot *increase* the entry point
(more constraints only add multiples), so the lcm becomes an upper bound; the
interesting content is exactly *how much* shared structure collapses the meeting line
— the failure mode the current theorem sidesteps with its coprimality hypothesis.

**Why now?** The proof of `fibEntryPt_mul_coprime` localizes precisely where
coprimality was used (`Nat.Coprime.mul_dvd_of_dvd_of_dvd`), so the one-sided
divisibility `α(a·b) ∣ lcm` should follow by deleting that step; the defect formula is
then a clean `padicValNat` computation. Falsifiable by an `(a,b)` whose defect has a
prime factor not dividing `gcd(a,b)`.
