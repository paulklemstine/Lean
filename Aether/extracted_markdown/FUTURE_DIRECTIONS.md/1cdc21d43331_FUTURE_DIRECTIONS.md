# Future Directions: The Periodic Table of Finite Groups

## Synthesis

This research cycle established a rigorous chemical-algebraic framework for organizing finite groups, proving eight core theorems that formalize the analogy between Mendeleev's periodic table and the structure theory of finite groups. The most productive insight was that the Derived-Central Series Inequality — the formal statement that commuting with yourself is easier than commuting with everyone — is the single structural principle underlying the entire classification hierarchy: abelian ⊂ nilpotent ⊂ solvable ⊂ general.

The strongest cross-domain connection emerged between group valence theory and lattice theory: the normal subgroup lattice encodes the group's "chemical bonding structure," with atoms corresponding to minimal normal subgroups. This bridge is the most promising avenue for future work because lattice-theoretic invariants (width, height, Möbius function) provide quantitative tools not native to pure group theory.

The highest breakthrough potential lies in Direction 1 (the Quantitative Periodic Law), because it would provide the first universal bound relating a group's "chemical complexity" to its "atomic mass," completing the analogy between derived depth and the period number in Mendeleev's table. The existing `quantitative_periodic_law_conjecture` in the Catalog (`EML/PeriodicTableGroups.lean`) provides a starting template, but the proof requires substantial infrastructure around subgroup indices and composition series.

### Direction 1: Quantitative Periodic Law — Universal Depth Bound for Solvable Groups

**Conjecture**: For every nontrivial finite solvable group G, the derived depth satisfies derivedDepth(G) ≤ Ω(|G|), where Ω(n) is the number of prime factors of n counted with multiplicity.

**Test**: Prove that each quotient D_i(G)/D_{i+1}(G) in the derived series of a solvable group is a nontrivial abelian group, hence has order ≥ 2. This gives |G| ≥ 2^{derivedDepth(G)}, so derivedDepth(G) ≤ log₂(|G|) ≤ Ω(|G|). The key lemma is: if D_i(G) ≠ D_{i+1}(G), then the quotient is a nontrivial abelian group. This requires showing that (a) D_{i+1} is normal in D_i (which follows from the recursive definition), and (b) D_i/D_{i+1} is abelian (which follows from the fact that D_{i+1} = [D_i, D_i]).

**Impact**: If true, this completes the "mass-energy" analogy: a group's chemical complexity cannot exceed its informational content. This would be the group-theoretic analogue of the fact that an atom's electron shell structure is constrained by its atomic number. If false, it would reveal exotic solvable groups with unexpectedly deep derived series.

**Catalog References**: `EML/PeriodicTableGroups.lean` (quantitative_periodic_law_conjecture), `Novelty/PeriodicTableDeep.lean` (groupInfoDimension, derivedDepth')

**Proof Strategy**: 
1. Prove D_{i+1} = [D_i, D_i] is normal in D_i.
2. Prove D_i/D_{i+1} is abelian.
3. Show that if D_i ≠ D_{i+1}, then |D_i/D_{i+1}| ≥ 2.
4. Use Lagrange's theorem: |G| = |D_0/D_1| · |D_1/D_2| · ... · |D_{d-1}/D_d| ≥ 2^d.
5. Conclude d ≤ log₂(|G|) ≤ Ω(|G|).

**Domain Bridges**: Group Theory <-> Number Theory (via prime factorization and Ω function), Group Theory <-> Information Theory (derived depth as information complexity)

**Lineage**: Builds on `noble_gas_depth_bound`, `derivedDepth_le_nilpotencyClass'`, and `groupInfoDimension_prod` from this cycle.

**Ambition**: grand_challenge

### Direction 2: Socle Decomposition and the Chief Factor Classification

**Conjecture**: The socle of a finite group G (the subgroup generated by all minimal normal subgroups) is a direct product of simple groups. Furthermore, for solvable groups, every minimal normal subgroup is an elementary abelian p-group for some prime p.

**Test**: 
1. Define the socle as Soc(G) = ⟨N : N is minimal normal in G⟩.
2. Prove that if N₁, N₂ are distinct minimal normal subgroups, then N₁ ∩ N₂ = 1 (because the intersection is normal and properly contained in both).
3. Prove that for solvable G, every minimal normal subgroup N is abelian (because [N,N] is normal in G and strictly contained in N).
4. Prove the elementary abelian structure: a minimal normal abelian subgroup of a finite group is an elementary abelian p-group.

**Impact**: This would provide the group-theoretic analogue of "electron configuration" — the socle tells you what the group's valence shell looks like. For solvable groups, the valence shell consists only of "abelian electrons" (elementary abelian p-groups), while non-solvable groups can have "non-abelian electrons" (simple groups in the socle).

**Catalog References**: `EML/PeriodicTableGroups.lean` (IsMinimalNormal, groupValence), `Novelty/PeriodicTableDeep.lean` (simple_top_minimal_normal', groupValence')

**Proof Strategy**:
1. Prove N₁ ∩ N₂ = 1 for distinct minimal normal subgroups (minimality + normality of intersection).
2. Prove [N,N] is normal in G when N is normal (commutator of normal subgroup is normal).
3. For solvable G: if [N,N] ≠ 1, then [N,N] = N by minimality, making N perfect; but perfect solvable groups are trivial.
4. For the elementary abelian structure: use that pN = {n^p : n ∈ N} is a characteristic subgroup of N, hence normal in G; if pN ≠ 1, then pN = N, contradicting finiteness.

**Domain Bridges**: Group Theory <-> Lattice Theory (socle = join of atoms in normal subgroup lattice), Group Theory <-> Module Theory (minimal normal subgroups of solvable groups are irreducible G-modules)

**Lineage**: Builds on `simple_valence_one'`, `simple_unique_minimal_normal'`, and `solvable_extension'` from this cycle.

**Ambition**: extension

### Direction 3: The Nilpotent Product Theorem and p-Group Periodic Table

**Conjecture**: The periodic table of p-groups (groups of prime-power order) has a refined structure: for p-groups of order p^n, the nilpotency class is at most n-1, and this bound is tight (achieved by iterated wreath products or lower-exponent groups). Furthermore, the number of groups of order p^n with nilpotency class exactly c satisfies asymptotic formulas related to partition theory.

**Test**:
1. Prove that every p-group of order p^n has nilpotency class ≤ n-1.
2. Construct explicit p-groups achieving class n-1 (e.g., certain iterated wreath products).
3. Compute the class distribution for small cases (p = 2, n ≤ 8) and look for partition-theoretic patterns.

**Impact**: p-groups are the "noble gases" of the periodic table, and understanding their fine structure is crucial because the vast majority of finite groups of a given order are p-groups (for orders that are prime powers). The asymptotic counting would connect to analytic number theory and partition theory.

**Catalog References**: `Novelty/PeriodicTableDeep.lean` (nilpotencyClass_one_iff_comm', noble_gas_depth_bound, nilpotencyClass_prod_eq_max')

**Proof Strategy**:
1. Use the class equation: |G| = |Z(G)| + Σ|G : C_G(x_i)| for representatives of non-central conjugacy classes.
2. Show Z(G) ≠ 1 for p-groups, giving |Z(G)| ≥ p.
3. Induct on n using G/Z(G): |G/Z(G)| = p^{n-k} where k = v_p(|Z(G)|) ≥ 1, so class(G/Z(G)) ≤ n-k-1 by IH, giving class(G) ≤ n-k ≤ n-1.

**Domain Bridges**: Group Theory <-> Combinatorics (partition-theoretic counting), Group Theory <-> Analytic Number Theory (asymptotic formulas for group counts)

**Lineage**: Builds on `nilpotencyClass_one_iff_comm'` and `nilpotencyClass_prod_eq_max'` from this cycle, connects to the Higman-Sims conjecture on p-group enumeration.

**Ambition**: grand_challenge

### Direction 4: Galois-Periodic Table Bridge — Solvability and Radical Extensions

**Conjecture**: The "chemical synthesis theorem" for groups (solvable extensions of solvable groups are solvable) is the algebraic reflection of the Galois-theoretic fact that radical extensions of radical extensions are radical. Formalize this bridge: given a polynomial f over ℚ with Galois group G, the polynomial is solvable by radicals if and only if G is solvable (Abel-Ruffini), and this correspondence respects the periodic table structure (the derived depth of G equals the minimum number of nested radical extensions needed to express the roots).

**Test**:
1. Formalize the statement that Gal(f/ℚ) solvable ⟹ f solvable by radicals.
2. Prove that the minimum tower height of radical extensions equals the derived depth of the Galois group.
3. Give an explicit example: the general quintic has Galois group S₅ (not solvable), so it's not solvable by radicals.

**Impact**: This would be the deepest cross-domain bridge in the periodic table framework, connecting abstract group classification to concrete polynomial solvability. It would show that the periodic table is not just an analogy but a genuine organizational principle for Galois theory.

**Catalog References**: `Novelty/PeriodicTableDeep.lean` (solvable_extension', halogen_unsolvable'), `Algebra/FutureExploration.lean` (symmetric_group_order)

**Proof Strategy**:
1. Use Mathlib's `IsSolvableByRad` and the Abel-Ruffini infrastructure.
2. Prove the tower height theorem by induction on the derived series: each quotient D_i/D_{i+1} is abelian, corresponding to one radical extension.
3. For the quintic example: use `Equiv.Perm.not_solvable` for S₅ and the Galois correspondence.

**Domain Bridges**: Group Theory <-> Galois Theory (the periodic table classifies polynomial solvability), Algebra <-> Number Theory (radical extensions and Kummer theory)

**Lineage**: Builds on `solvable_extension'` and `halogen_unsolvable'` from this cycle.

**Ambition**: extension

### Direction 5: Computational Periodic Table — Enumeration and Prediction for Orders ≤ 100

**Conjecture**: A computational classification of all groups of order ≤ 100 into the periodic table framework reveals that: (a) ≥ 90% of groups are nilpotent (noble gases dominate), (b) the average valence decreases with order for solvable groups, and (c) groups with the same composition factor multiset always share solvability but may differ in nilpotency class by arbitrarily large amounts.

**Test**:
1. Implement the group classification algorithm (computing derived series, lower central series, minimal normal subgroups) for GAP or SageMath.
2. Classify all groups of order ≤ 100 (there are ~1,000 such groups, not counting the ~50 billion groups of order 1024 = 2^10).
3. Verify predictions: do groups with the same composition factors share solvability? (They must, by Jordan-Hölder.) Do they share nilpotency class? (They need not — find counterexamples.)

**Impact**: A computational validation of the periodic table framework, producing a concrete artifact (the table itself) that can be used for teaching and research. Finding groups with the same composition factors but different nilpotency classes would demonstrate that the periodic table analogy has sharp limits — "isotopes" in group chemistry can have very different "nuclear properties."

**Catalog References**: `Novelty/PeriodicTableDeep.lean` (all main theorems), `EML/PeriodicTableGroups.lean` (GroupPeriodicEntry structure)

**Proof Strategy**: Primarily computational. For the theoretical parts:
1. Jordan-Hölder guarantees composition factor invariance.
2. Construct explicit counterexamples for nilpotency class non-invariance: Z/4Z × Z/4Z (class 1) vs. D₈ (class 2), both of order 16 with same composition factors {Z/2Z, Z/2Z, Z/2Z, Z/2Z}.

**Domain Bridges**: Group Theory <-> Computational Algebra (GAP/SageMath algorithms), Group Theory <-> Data Science (pattern detection in the periodic table)

**Lineage**: Builds on the full framework from this cycle, particularly `GroupPeriodicEntry` and the classification theorems.

**Ambition**: extension
