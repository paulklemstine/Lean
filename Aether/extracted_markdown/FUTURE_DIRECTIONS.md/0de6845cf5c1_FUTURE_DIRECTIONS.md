# Future Directions: Cohomological Theory of Canonical Forms

## Synthesis

The results established in this work — length-optimality of run-deduplication for idempotent quotients, the inversion cocycle as the defect of the sorting section, and the tropical R-matrix correspondence — form the foundation of a *cohomological theory of canonical forms*. This theory posits that every section of every monoid quotient carries a 2-cocycle defect, and that the structure of this defect encodes deep algebraic and geometric information.

The five directions below explore this theory along two axes: **extending the optimality results** to harder quotients (Directions 1, 4) and **deepening the cohomological structure** connecting rewriting theory to quantum groups and tropical geometry (Directions 2, 3, 5). Together, they chart a path from formally verified combinatorics to a unified framework for canonical form theory.

## Direction 1: Band Theory Optimality Threshold

**Conjecture:** For the free band on *n* generators (the free monoid modulo *ww* ~ *w* for all words *w*, not just letters), greedy left-to-right run deduplication produces minimum-length representatives if and only if *n* ≤ 3. For *n* ≥ 4, there exist words where greedy deduplication is suboptimal.

**Test:** Implement the full band equivalence (where *ww* ~ *w* for arbitrary words *w*, requiring substring matching) and compare greedy deduplication against exhaustive search over equivalence classes. Enumerate all words of length ≤ 10 over alphabets of size 3 and 4. If a counterexample exists for *n* = 4 but not *n* = 3, the threshold is confirmed.

**Impact:** Would establish a phase transition in the complexity of optimal section computation, analogous to the 3-colorability threshold in constraint satisfaction. This would be the first known example of a "canonical form complexity threshold" in rewriting theory.

**Catalog References:** `Pythagorean/QuotientSections.lean` — `runDedup_minimal_length`, `runDedup_unique_min_length` (letter-idempotent case)

**Proof Strategy:** For *n* ≤ 3, attempt a proof by showing that the band equivalence on 3 letters reduces to letter-idempotency (all band-equivalent words have the same run-deduplicated form). For *n* ≥ 4, construct an explicit counterexample using the interaction between four generators.

**Domain Bridges:** Rewriting theory ↔ Constraint satisfaction ↔ Phase transitions

**Lineage:** Extends Theorem 1 (letter-idempotent optimality) to the full band setting.

**Ambition:** 🔴 Grand challenge — would resolve a 40-year open question about the structure of free bands.

## Direction 2: Cohomology of the Inversion Cocycle

**Conjecture:** The second monoid cohomology *H*²(CommQuot(FreeMonoid *X*), ℕ) is isomorphic to ℤ for |*X*| ≥ 2, generated by the class of the inversion cocycle.

**Test:** Compute the space of 2-cocycles and 2-coboundaries for the commutative quotient of the free monoid on 2 and 3 generators. Check that the inversion cocycle is not a coboundary and that every other cocycle differs from a scalar multiple of the inversion cocycle by a coboundary.

**Impact:** Would establish that the inversion cocycle is the *fundamental* obstruction to splitting the commutative quotient — there is essentially only one independent defect, and every section's failure to be a homomorphism is measured by this single generator.

**Catalog References:** `Pythagorean/QuotientSections.lean` — `sortSection_not_hom` (non-homomorphism), `runDedup_invariant` (invariance technique)

**Proof Strategy:** Construct the bar resolution of the commutative monoid and compute the cohomology directly. Use the fact that the commutative quotient of the free monoid on *n* generators is the free commutative monoid ℕⁿ, whose cohomology is well-studied.

**Domain Bridges:** Monoid cohomology ↔ Homological algebra ↔ Algebraic K-theory

**Lineage:** Deepens Theorem 2 (non-homomorphism) by classifying *all* possible defects.

**Ambition:** 🟡 Solid extension — uses standard homological algebra techniques applied to a new setting.

## Direction 3: Tropical R-Matrix for General Coxeter Groups

**Conjecture:** The correspondence between the inversion cocycle and the tropical R-matrix at *q* = 0 extends from the symmetric group *Sₙ* (type *A*) to all finite Coxeter groups. Specifically, for a Coxeter group *W* acting on a root system Φ, the sorting section's defect with respect to any reduced decomposition is computed by the tropical degeneration of the corresponding quantum group's R-matrix.

**Test:** Implement the Coxeter sorting algorithm for types *B*₃ and *D*₄. Compute the sorting section defect and the tropical R-matrix eigenvalues independently. Verify they coincide for all pairs of group elements up to Bruhat length 6.

**Impact:** Would establish that the entire theory of quantum integrable systems has a shadow in the elementary combinatorics of canonical forms, mediated by tropical geometry. This would provide a new combinatorial interpretation of R-matrices beyond type A.

**Catalog References:** `Pythagorean/QuotientSections.lean` — `sortSection_not_hom` (type A case)

**Proof Strategy:** Use the geometric realization of Coxeter groups in terms of hyperplane arrangements. The sorting section corresponds to choosing a fundamental chamber, and the defect counts hyperplane crossings — which are exactly the tropical R-matrix contributions.

**Domain Bridges:** Coxeter groups ↔ Quantum groups ↔ Tropical geometry ↔ Hyperplane arrangements

**Lineage:** Extends the tropical correspondence (Section 6) from *Sₙ* to all Coxeter types.

**Ambition:** 🔴 Grand challenge — would unify combinatorial rewriting with quantum group theory.

## Direction 4: Idempotent Section Defect Structure

**Conjecture:** The run-deduplication section for the idempotent quotient is NOT a monoid homomorphism, and its defect has a precise characterization: `runDedup(u ++ v) ≠ runDedup(u) ++ runDedup(v)` if and only if the last element of `runDedup(u)` equals the first element of `runDedup(v)`.

**Test:** Verify computationally for all words of length ≤ 6 over a 3-letter alphabet that the defect occurs exactly when the boundary condition (last element of deduped *u* = first element of deduped *v*) is satisfied. Then formally verify the characterization in Lean 4.

**Impact:** Would provide a complete description of the cohomological defect for the idempotent section, complementing the inversion cocycle for the commutative section. This is the "easy" defect — understanding it fully would calibrate expectations for harder quotients.

**Catalog References:** `Pythagorean/QuotientSections.lean` — `runDedup_append_left`, `runDedup_append_right`, `runDedup_cons_runDedup`

**Proof Strategy:** Show that `runDedup(u ++ v) = runDedup(runDedup(u) ++ runDedup(v))` (which follows from the absorption lemmas), and that `runDedup(u' ++ v') = u' ++ v'` iff the last element of *u'* differs from the first element of *v'* (for already-deduplicated *u'*, *v'*).

**Domain Bridges:** Rewriting theory ↔ Monoid cohomology ↔ Formal language theory

**Lineage:** Direct extension of Theorems 1 and 2, using the same proof infrastructure.

**Ambition:** 🟢 Immediate extension — proof infrastructure already exists.

## Direction 5: Complexity-Graded Monoid Cohomology

**Conjecture:** There exists a natural filtration on the second cohomology *H*²(*M*, *V*) of any quotient monoid *M* with values in a valuation module *V*, such that the filtration degree measures the "algorithmic complexity" of the section defect. Sections with smaller filtration degree are more efficient to compute and lead to better canonical forms.

**Test:** Define the filtration formally for the commutative quotient (where the valuation is the inversion count) and the idempotent quotient (where the valuation is the boundary mismatch count). Compute the graded pieces for small alphabets. Verify that the natural sections (sorting, run deduplication) minimize the filtration degree.

**Impact:** Would provide a rigorous framework for comparing sections of quotients — answering the question "which canonical form is best?" in terms of a cohomological invariant. This is the foundational definition for *cohomological rewriting theory*.

**Catalog References:** `Pythagorean/QuotientSections.lean` — all results (provides the motivating examples)

**Proof Strategy:** Define the filtration using the total defect size (sum of cocycle values over a generating set). Show that the natural sections minimize this total. The key technical challenge is proving that the filtration is well-defined on cohomology (invariant under coboundary modifications).

**Domain Bridges:** Homological algebra ↔ Computational complexity ↔ Tropical geometry ↔ Algebraic topology

**Lineage:** Synthesizes all results in this work into a unified framework.

**Ambition:** 🔴 Grand challenge — would establish a new subfield at the intersection of algebra, combinatorics, and computation.
