# Future Directions: Proof-Theoretic Symbolic Dynamics

## Conjecture 1: Transfer Matrix Zeta Rationality for the Full Shift

**Conjecture:** For any one-dimensional nearest-neighbor CA over a finite alphabet $\alpha$ on the full bi-infinite shift $\alpha^{\mathbb{Z}}$, the Artin–Mazur zeta function is rational if and only if the CA is a factor of a shift of finite type.

**Test:** For CA known to be factors of SFTs (all additive CA, all left/right permutative CA), verify zeta rationality by computing the transfer matrix and checking that $\zeta_T(z) = 1/\det(I - zA)$ for the adjacency matrix $A$ of the de Bruijn graph. For candidate non-SFT-factor CA, compute periodic point counts for large rings and test whether the growth rate of the period of the sequence $n \mapsto |\text{Fix}(T^m)|$ (as a function of the ring size $n$) is polynomial or superpolynomial.

**Refutation condition:** Exhibit a CA that is not a factor of any SFT but whose zeta function is rational, or vice versa.

**Impact:** If true, this would provide a dynamical characterization of the SFT-factor property, connecting symbolic dynamics to algebraic geometry via the zeta function. It would also establish that zeta rationality is a strictly stronger invariant than we proved in the finite-ring case.

## Conjecture 2: Star-Free Spacetime Languages for Permutative CA

**Conjecture:** For every one-sided permutative CA over a finite alphabet, the fixed-height spacetime block language (columns viewed as letters over $\alpha^h$) is not merely regular but **star-free** (equivalently, its syntactic monoid is aperiodic / group-free).

**Test:** For small heights $h = 2, 3, 4$ and specific permutative rules (left-shift, Rule 90, etc.), compute the syntactic monoid of the column language and check aperiodicity. The syntactic monoid can be computed from the transfer matrix representation: it is generated by the column transition matrices and their products.

**Refutation condition:** Exhibit a permutative CA and a height $h$ such that the syntactic monoid of the spacetime column language contains a non-trivial group (i.e., the language requires a counting modality beyond first-order logic).

**Impact:** Star-freeness would place spacetime languages in the first-order definable class, connecting CA dynamics to descriptive complexity and the Krohn-Rhodes decomposition theorem. This would strengthen the bridge theorem: not only are certificates short, but the *logic* needed to express realizability is restricted.

## Conjecture 3: Polynomial Recurrence Order for Additive CA over $\mathbb{F}_p$

**Conjecture:** For additive CA over $\mathbb{F}_p$ with local rule $f(x,y,z) = ax + by + cz$, the eventual period of the sequence $m \mapsto |\text{Fix}(T^m)|$ on the ring of size $n$ is bounded by $p^{O(n)}$, and the preperiod is bounded by $O(n)$.

**Test:** Compute the minimal polynomial of the circulant matrix representing the CA action on $\mathbb{F}_p^n$. The period divides $\text{lcm}$ of the multiplicative orders of the eigenvalues (over $\overline{\mathbb{F}_p}$), and the preperiod is bounded by the nilpotency index. Tabulate these for $p = 2, 3, 5$ and $n = 1, \ldots, 50$ across multiple rules (Rule 90, Rule 150, general $a,b,c$).

**Refutation condition:** Exhibit a rule and a sequence of ring sizes $n_k \to \infty$ such that the period grows faster than $p^{Cn}$ for any constant $C$ (e.g., doubly exponential in $n$).

**Impact:** Polynomial bounds on the recurrence order would make the zeta function *effectively* computable from the rule and ring size, enabling algorithmic prediction of long-term dynamics. Superpolynomial growth would indicate that finite-ring zeta functions, while always rational, can be computationally intractable.

## Conjecture 4: Certificate Complexity Separation Between Reversible and Non-Reversible CA

**Conjecture:** There exists a reversible (bijective global map) nearest-neighbor CA over a finite alphabet with rational finite-approximant zeta function but **superlinear** certificate complexity — specifically, $\Omega(w \cdot h^{1/2})$ for certain $w \times h$ blocks — demonstrating that the naive linear bound $O(w + h)$ cannot be improved to $O(w)$ for general reversible rules.

**Test:** Consider reversible CA with complex local structure (e.g., Margolus partitioning rules, second-order CA). For fixed width $w$ and increasing height $h$, measure the minimum boundary data needed to uniquely determine a spacetime block. If boundary state space grows with $h$, the certificate must encode this growing state.

**Refutation condition:** Prove a uniform $O(w + h)$ certificate bound for ALL reversible CA, showing that reversibility alone (without permutativity) guarantees linear certificates.

**Impact:** A separation would show that the bridge theorem's certificate bound, while always linear, can hide fundamentally different constants for different rule classes. This would motivate a finer classification of CA by "certificate efficiency" as a new dynamical invariant.

## Conjecture 5: Rule 110 Finite Approximants Exhibit Superpolynomial Recurrence Growth

**Conjecture:** For Wolfram's Rule 110 (known to be Turing-universal on the full shift), the eventual period of $m \mapsto |\text{Fix}(T^m)|$ on rings of size $n$ grows faster than any fixed polynomial in $n$ — specifically, $\exp(\Omega(n^{1/3}))$.

**Test:** Compute periodic point counts for Rule 110 on rings of size $n = 1, \ldots, 40$ for periods $m = 1, \ldots, 1000$. Detect the eventual period $d(n)$ as a function of $n$. Plot $\log d(n)$ vs $n$ and test for polynomial vs exponential growth.

**Refutation condition:** Exhibit an explicit recurrence of fixed polynomial order (independent of $n$) governing the periodic point counts, or a uniform period bound $d(n) \leq n^C$ for some constant $C$.

**Impact:** Superpolynomial growth would be the first formal evidence that Turing-universal CA have qualitatively different zeta behavior from additive/permutative CA. It would suggest a complexity hierarchy:
- *Additive*: polynomial period, polynomial-time computable zeta
- *Permutative*: exponential period but polynomial-depth certificates
- *Universal*: superpolynomial period, no uniform short certificates

This hierarchy would be the first *dynamically-derived* computational complexity classification of CA, connecting Wolfram's empirical classification to rigorous complexity theory.
