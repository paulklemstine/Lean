# Future Directions — The Arithmetic of Games

## Synthesis

This cycle formalized the *ring*-theoretic core of Conway's "numbers born on a finite
day". Working from Mathlib's `Surreal.dyadicMap : ℤ[1/2] →+ Surreal` (supplied only as an
**additive** monoid hom, with injectivity and multiplicativity left as explicit `TODO`s),
we proved in `Catalog/Pythagorean/SurrealDyadicField.lean`:

* `powHalf_mul` — `2⁻ᵐ · 2⁻ⁿ = 2⁻⁽ᵐ⁺ⁿ⁾` in `Surreal`, by cancelling `2^(m+n)` and using
  that `Surreal` is an integral domain. This single exponent-additivity law is the engine
  behind everything else.
* `dyadicMap_injective` — the dyadic rationals embed **faithfully** into the surreals.
* `dyadicMap_pos_iff` — the embedding **reflects and preserves order**.
* `dyadicMap_mul`, `dyadicMap_one`, and the packaged `dyadicRingHom : ℤ[1/2] →+* Surreal`
  with `dyadicRingHom_injective` — `No_ω` is a **subring of `Surreal` isomorphic to `ℤ[1/2]`**.

**Critical correction to the source conjecture.** The brief asserted that `No_ω` is a
*subfield* equal to "ℚ extended with dyadic rationals". This is false: `ℤ[1/2]` is *not*
a field (`3` has no dyadic inverse, since `1/3` is not dyadic), and it already contains the
dyadics, so no extension by them occurs. The faithful theorem — proved here with `sorry = 0`
— is that `No_ω ≅ ℤ[1/2]` *as ordered rings*. The field generated by `No_ω` inside `Surreal`
is `ℚ`, whose non-dyadic elements first appear only in the limit *at* day `ω`.

## Research Directions

### 1. Promote `dyadicRingHom` to an ordered ring embedding and order-bound `No_ω`
We proved positivity reflection pointwise; the next step is a genuine `OrderRingHom`
(or `RelEmbedding`) witnessing that `ℤ[1/2]` sits inside `Surreal` as an *ordered* subring,
together with the density theorem that every surreal of finite birthday is one of these
dyadics. **The key insight is** that order reflection plus multiplicativity already pin down
the embedding up to a unique ordered-ring map, so the only missing ingredient is a birthday
bound `birthday x < ω ↔ x ∈ Set.range dyadicMap`. **Why now?** Mathlib's `SetTheory.Game.Birthday`
gives the birthday function and `dyadicMap` gives the candidate range; the two have never been
connected, and our `dyadicMap_injective`/`dyadicMap_pos_iff` provide exactly the faithfulness
needed to make the equivalence provable rather than merely plausible.

### 2. Construct the field embedding `ℚ ↪ Surreal` as the fraction field of `No_ω`
`Surreal` is a `Field` (real-closed), and `ℤ[1/2]` is a subring with fraction field `ℚ`. The
universal property of localization should yield a canonical `RingHom ℚ → Surreal` extending
`dyadicRingHom`, and it should be injective and order preserving. **The key insight is** that
once `dyadicRingHom` is a *ring* hom into a field, `IsFractionRing.lift` produces the `ℚ`-map
for free — division is supplied by `Surreal`, not by `No_ω`. **Why now?** This is the precise,
falsifiable repair of the brief's "subfield" claim: the field is `ℚ` at day `ω`, not `ℤ[1/2]`,
and the machinery (`IsFractionRing`, `Rat.cast`) is all present once multiplicativity (this
cycle) is in hand.

### 3. Identify `powHalf` with `(1/2)^n` and obtain a monoid hom `(ℕ, +) → (Surreal, ×)`
We proved `powHalf` is multiplicative on exponents; the conjecture is the definitional bridge
`powHalf n = (2 : Surreal)⁻¹ ^ n`, making `n ↦ powHalf n` literally a monoid homomorphism
`(ℕ,+) → (Surreal,×)` and `powHalf` a group hom on `ℤ` after inverting. **The key insight is**
that `nsmul_pow_two_powHalf` (`2ⁿ · powHalf n = 1`) already says `powHalf n` *is* the inverse
of `2ⁿ`, so the identity is forced in the field `Surreal`. **Why now?** The combinatorial
`PGame.powHalf` definition has always obstructed seeing `powHalf` as an honest power; the field
structure of `Surreal` lets us bypass game trees entirely and reason purely algebraically.

### 4. A no-2-torsion universal property witnessed inside `Surreal`
`ℤ[1/2]` is the initial object among rings with no 2-torsion. Conjecture: the corestriction of
`dyadicRingHom` onto its image is the *initial* 2-torsion-free subring of `Surreal`, i.e. any
2-torsion-free subring of `Surreal` contains `range dyadicRingHom`. **The key insight is** that
2-divisibility of `1` (via `powHalf`) is the only generator needed, and `Surreal` realizes it
canonically, so initiality transfers from the abstract localization to the concrete subring.
**Why now?** With `dyadicRingHom` a verified ring hom, the abstract initiality of `Localization.Away 2`
can finally be *instantiated* and tested against the concrete arithmetic of games.

### 5. Birthday-graded multiplicativity: `birthday (x * y) ≤ birthday x ⊗ birthday y`
Beyond `No_ω`, conjecture a Hessenberg-product (natural ordinal product `⊗`) bound on the
birthday of surreal products, with equality on dyadics giving back `powHalf_mul`'s additivity
of exponents (`birthday (powHalf m) = m+1` for `m ≥ 1`). **The key insight is** that exponent
addition in `powHalf_mul` is the `ω = finite` shadow of natural ordinal multiplication, so the
finite-birthday case we proved is the base of a transfinite grading. **Why now?** `birthday_half`
and our `powHalf_mul` give the first concrete data point relating multiplication to birthdays;
generalizing it is the natural falsifiable next probe into how the birthday filtration interacts
with the ring structure, extending the order/topology theory in
`Catalog/Bridges/SurrealTopologyDeep.lean` to the *multiplicative* setting.
