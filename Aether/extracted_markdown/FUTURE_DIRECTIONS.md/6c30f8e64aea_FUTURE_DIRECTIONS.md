# Future Directions: Hyperbolic Number Theory

## Synthesis

This cycle established the rigorous foundations of arithmetic on the Poincaré disk: Möbius transformations, hyperbolic norm, disk preservation, and the Cayley bridge to the upper half-plane. The most promising cross-domain connection is the **Cayley transform bridge** (Theorem `cayley_maps_disk_to_uhp` in `Algebra/HyperbolicNumberTheory.lean`), which provides a concrete, formally verified functor between hyperbolic geometry and the classical domain of modular forms and L-functions.

The computational experiments revealed that the naive Hyperbolic PNT conjecture (π_H(R) ~ R²/(2 log R)) likely needs a lattice-dependent multiplicative constant — paralleling how the classical PNT refines from π(x) ~ x/log(x) to π(x) ~ Li(x). The noncommutativity of Möbius composition introduces spectral oscillations absent in classical number theory, suggesting deep connections to the Selberg trace formula and random matrix theory.

The highest breakthrough potential lies in **Direction 1** (Selberg–Hyperbolic Bridge), because it connects our formally verified Cayley transform to the Selberg zeta function, potentially translating the Riemann hypothesis problem into a geometric eigenvalue problem where the spectral gap is computable. The Catalog's existing work on `critical_line_implies_unit_disk` (`Algebra/Foundations.lean`) provides a natural starting point for connecting critical-line zeros to disk geometry.

### Direction 1: Selberg Zeta Function and Hyperbolic Prime Duality

**Conjecture**: For a cofinite Fuchsian group Γ acting on the Poincaré disk, the Selberg zeta function Z_Γ(s) and the hyperbolic lattice zeta function ζ_H(s) = Σ_{z ∈ Γ·0} ‖z‖_H^{-2s} share a common set of "spectral zeros" determined by the eigenvalues of the Laplacian on Γ\𝔻.

**Test**: For the modular group Γ = PSL(2,ℤ), compute the first 50 zeros of the partial ζ_H(s) using 10,000 orbit points and compare with the known eigenvalues of the Laplacian on the modular surface (λ_1 ≈ 91.14, λ_2 ≈ 148.43, ...). If the zeros cluster near s = 1/2 ± i√(λ_k − 1/4), the conjecture is supported.

**Impact**: If true, this would establish a *spectral–arithmetic duality* where prime counting in hyperbolic space is equivalent to an eigenvalue problem. The Selberg trace formula would then give an exact formula for π_H(R), analogous to Riemann's explicit formula for π(x). This could provide a geometric proof strategy for RH-type results.

**Catalog References**: `Algebra/HyperbolicNumberTheory.lean` (cayley_maps_disk_to_uhp, partialHypZeta), `Algebra/Foundations.lean` (critical_line_implies_unit_disk)

**Proof Strategy**: (1) Formally define the Selberg zeta function using `Algebra/HyperbolicNumberTheory.lean`'s Möbius maps and hyperbolic distance. (2) Prove that the Selberg zeta function has an Euler product over primitive closed geodesics. (3) Use the Cayley bridge to translate between disk and half-plane representations. (4) Connect to the spectral decomposition of the Laplacian via the Selberg trace formula.

**Domain Bridges**: Hyperbolic Geometry <-> Analytic Number Theory, Spectral Theory <-> Arithmetic

**Lineage**: Builds on `cayley_maps_disk_to_uhp` and `mobius_preserves_disk` from this cycle.

**Ambition**: grand_challenge

### Direction 2: Unique Factorization in Hyperbolic Lattices

**Conjecture**: For a hyperbolic lattice Λ_G generated by G = {g_1, ..., g_k} ⊂ 𝔻, the monoid of finite Möbius compositions is a *free monoid* on G (i.e., every lattice point has a unique expression as a sequence of generator applications), provided the generators are "sufficiently separated" in hyperbolic distance (d_H(g_i, g_j) > C for all i ≠ j, where C is an explicit constant depending on k).

**Test**: Generate lattices with k = 3 generators at varying separations. For each lattice, check whether any point z has two distinct generator-sequences producing z. If collisions appear below a threshold separation but not above it, the conjecture is supported. The critical separation C can be estimated numerically.

**Impact**: Unique factorization is the cornerstone of classical number theory (Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic). Establishing it for hyperbolic integers would validate the analogy between ℤ_H and ℤ and open the door to a hyperbolic analog of the Fundamental Theorem. Failure would mean the hyperbolic integers have a richer, non-unique factorization structure — potentially analogous to number rings with class number > 1.

**Catalog References**: `Algebra/HyperbolicNumberTheory.lean` (HyperbolicLattice, isHypPrime, mobiusMap)

**Proof Strategy**: (1) Formalize the free monoid on generators using Lean's `FreeMonoid`. (2) Show that sufficiently separated Möbius maps satisfy a "ping-pong" condition (Klein's combination theorem). (3) Deduce freeness from the ping-pong lemma. (4) Translate freeness into unique factorization.

**Domain Bridges**: Hyperbolic Geometry <-> Abstract Algebra, Combinatorial Group Theory <-> Number Theory

**Lineage**: Builds on `HyperbolicLattice`, `isHypPrime`, and `mobius_denom_ne_zero` from this cycle.

**Ambition**: extension

### Direction 3: Hyperbolic Lattice Embeddings for Machine Learning

**Conjecture**: A k-ary tree of depth d can be isometrically embedded into a hyperbolic lattice Λ_G with k generators, achieving zero distortion in hyperbolic distance, and the embedding can be computed in O(k^d) time.

**Test**: Implement the embedding algorithm for binary trees (k=2) of depths d = 5, 10, 15. Measure the distortion metric D = max_{u,v} |d_tree(u,v) - d_H(embed(u), embed(v))| / d_tree(u,v). If D < 0.01 for all tested depths, the conjecture is supported.

**Impact**: This would provide the first *exact* (zero-distortion) discrete embedding of trees into hyperbolic space, improving on the approximate embeddings used in current Poincaré embedding methods. The lattice structure would enable exact arithmetic on embeddings, potentially improving the numerical stability of hyperbolic neural networks.

**Catalog References**: `Algebra/HyperbolicNumberTheory.lean` (hypDist, mobiusMap, HyperbolicLattice), potential bridge to `MachineLearning/` directory

**Proof Strategy**: (1) Map the root to origin 0. (2) Map child i of node z to φ_{-z}(g_i) (translate generator g_i to neighborhood of z). (3) Prove that this preserves tree distance using the isometry property of Möbius maps and the additive structure of hyperbolic distance along geodesics. (4) The induction is on tree depth.

**Domain Bridges**: Hyperbolic Geometry <-> Machine Learning, Metric Geometry <-> Data Science

**Lineage**: Builds on `mobius_preserves_disk`, `hypDist`, and `hypNorm_strict_mono` from this cycle. Connects to `MachineLearning/` Catalog domain.

**Ambition**: extension

### Direction 4: Noncommutative Hyperbolic Zeta and Random Matrix Theory

**Conjecture**: The nearest-neighbor spacing distribution of the zeros of the hyperbolic zeta function ζ_H(s) follows the Gaussian Unitary Ensemble (GUE) statistics, rather than the Gaussian Orthogonal Ensemble (GOE) predicted for the Riemann zeta function. The noncommutativity of Möbius composition breaks the time-reversal symmetry that leads to GOE.

**Test**: Compute the first 200 zeros of ζ_H(s) numerically for a fixed lattice with 5,000 orbit points. Compute the nearest-neighbor spacing distribution and compare the level repulsion exponent β: GOE has β = 1, GUE has β = 2. If the observed β ≈ 2, the conjecture is supported.

**Impact**: This would establish the first concrete link between the noncommutativity of a number-theoretic structure and its random matrix universality class. It would predict that hyperbolic L-functions belong to a different symmetry class than classical L-functions — a new phenomenon with implications for quantum chaos and the BGS conjecture.

**Catalog References**: `Algebra/HyperbolicNumberTheory.lean` (partialHypZeta, hypPNT_conjecture), `Speculative/IdempotentCollapse/TheoreticalExtensions.lean` (RH_via_fixed_points)

**Proof Strategy**: (1) Numerically compute zeros of ζ_H(s) using Newton's method on the partial sum. (2) Unfold the spacing distribution and fit to GUE/GOE curves. (3) If GUE is confirmed, investigate whether the noncommutative Möbius group structure induces a unitary (rather than orthogonal) symmetry in the associated quantum system via the Cayley bridge.

**Domain Bridges**: Number Theory <-> Random Matrix Theory, Hyperbolic Geometry <-> Quantum Physics

**Lineage**: Builds on `partialHypZeta_nonneg` and `hypPNT_conjecture` from this cycle. Extends `RH_via_fixed_points` from `Speculative/IdempotentCollapse/TheoreticalExtensions.lean`.

**Ambition**: grand_challenge

### Direction 5: Higher-Dimensional Hyperbolic Arithmetic (3-Manifolds)

**Conjecture**: The hyperbolic lattice construction generalizes to the 3-dimensional hyperbolic ball model 𝔹³, with Möbius maps replaced by Clifford–Möbius transformations. The lattice point counting function satisfies N(R) ~ C·R³ for finitely generated lattices, and there exist "hyperbolic primes" whose density exhibits a 3D analog of the PNT: π_H^{(3)}(R) ~ R³/(3 log R).

**Test**: Implement Clifford–Möbius transformations in ℝ³ (using quaternion arithmetic), generate a lattice with 4 generators, and count points up to hyperbolic radius R = 5. If N(R) grows cubically and the prime ratio stabilizes, the conjecture is supported. If growth is slower than cubic, the finite-generation constraint is more restrictive in higher dimensions.

**Impact**: Thurston's geometrization (proved by Perelman) shows that every closed 3-manifold decomposes into geometric pieces, eight types, of which hyperbolic is the most common. Understanding arithmetic on hyperbolic 3-space would connect number theory to 3-manifold topology — a completely unexplored territory. It could also connect to knot invariants via the hyperbolic volume of knot complements.

**Catalog References**: `Algebra/HyperbolicNumberTheory.lean` (all definitions generalize), potential connection to `Geometry/` Catalog domain

**Proof Strategy**: (1) Define Clifford algebra Cl(3,0) and its action on the 3-ball. (2) Formalize the Möbius group of 𝔹³ using 2×2 matrices over the quaternions (Ahlfors' construction). (3) Prove disk preservation in 3D by generalizing the normSq identity. (4) Define the 3D lattice and counting function. (5) Prove monotonicity and non-negativity properties.

**Domain Bridges**: Hyperbolic Geometry <-> 3-Manifold Topology, Number Theory <-> Knot Theory

**Lineage**: Builds on the entire foundation from this cycle, generalizing from 2D to 3D.

**Ambition**: extension
