# Future Directions: Periodic Orbit Theory and Cognitive Dynamics

## Synthesis

This research cycle established the **covering relation** as the core combinatorial engine underlying periodic orbit forcing in one-dimensional dynamics. We proved that a continuous map with a period-3 orbit generates specific interval covering relations — [b,c] f-covers [a,c] and [a,b] f-covers [b,c] — and that these covering relations force fixed points of all iterates via the Self-Covering Fixed Point Theorem. The topological conjugacy invariance of periodic structure connects this theory to algebra, while the logistic map analysis provides a concrete computational testbed.

The most promising cross-domain connection is the **Covering → Group Action bridge**: the covering graph of a continuous interval map encodes the same information as the representation of the cyclic group ⟨f⟩ on the interval's topology. The orbit-stabilizer theorem from group theory and the orbit cardinality theorem from dynamics are two faces of the same structural principle. This suggests that deeper algebraic tools (representation theory, homological algebra) could illuminate the fine structure of periodic orbit forcing.

The highest breakthrough potential lies in **Direction 1**: formalizing the complete Sharkovsky ordering. This would be the first machine-verified proof of Sharkovsky's full theorem, a landmark result in dynamics. The covering chain machinery developed in this cycle provides the foundation — what remains is the careful inductive construction of covering chains for each element of the Sharkovsky ordering.

### Direction 1: Complete Sharkovsky Ordering Formalization

**Conjecture**: The Sharkovsky ordering on ℕ (3 ◁ 5 ◁ 7 ◁ ... ◁ 2·3 ◁ 2·5 ◁ ... ◁ 4·3 ◁ ... ◁ 8 ◁ 4 ◁ 2 ◁ 1) has the property that if a continuous map f: [a,b] → ℝ has a periodic point of minimal period p, then f has periodic points of every minimal period q where p ◁ q. This can be formalized via covering chains: for each pair (p,q) with p ◁ q, construct an explicit covering chain of length q using the covering graph generated by the period-p orbit.

**Test**: Formalize the Sharkovsky ordering as a linear order on ℕ. Prove the key lemma: if f has a period-(2k+1) orbit for k ≥ 1, then f has a period-2 orbit. This is the first non-trivial step beyond period-3 → fixed point.

**Impact**: A complete formalization of Sharkovsky's theorem would be a landmark in formal mathematics — no existing proof assistant library contains it. It would also provide a template for formalizing other forcing results in dynamics.

**Catalog References**: `SharkovskyForcing.period3_implies_all_iterate_fixed_points` (DejaVuDynamics/SharkovskyForcing.lean), `IntervalCovering.self_covering_fixed_point` (DejaVuDynamics/IntervalCovering.lean), `IntervalCovering.two_step_covering_has_period2_point` (DejaVuDynamics/IntervalCovering.lean)

**Proof Strategy**:
1. Define the Sharkovsky ordering as a total order on ℕ.
2. For odd period p = 2k+1 ≥ 3, construct the covering graph with k+1 intervals.
3. Prove that the covering graph admits chains of every length q where p ◁ q.
4. Apply the Covering Chain Theorem (generalize two_step_covering to n-step chains) to extract periodic points.
5. Handle the 2^n tail separately via period-doubling.

**Domain Bridges**: Dynamics ↔ Combinatorics (covering graphs are directed graphs; the chain construction is a path-finding problem), Dynamics ↔ Order Theory (Sharkovsky ordering has a unique structure among total orders on ℕ)

**Lineage**: Builds on covering theory from this cycle (IntervalCovering.lean, SharkovskyForcing.lean)

**Ambition**: grand_challenge

### Direction 2: Semiconjugacy of Logistic Map and Bernoulli Shift

**Conjecture**: The logistic map at r = 4 is semiconjugate to the full shift on two symbols via the coding map. Specifically, define the symbolic dynamics space Σ₂ = {0,1}^ℕ with the shift map σ, and the coding map h: [0,1] → Σ₂ by h(x)_n = 0 if (logistic 4)^n(x) ∈ [0,1/2] and 1 otherwise. Then h ∘ (logistic 4) = σ ∘ h. Moreover, h is surjective, establishing that the dynamics of the logistic map at r=4 is at least as complex as the full shift.

**Test**: Prove that the coding map is well-defined and satisfies the semiconjugacy equation. As a first step, verify computationally that for 10,000 random initial conditions, the symbolic orbit under h agrees with the shift of the coding.

**Impact**: This would connect continuous dynamics on [0,1] to symbolic dynamics on Cantor space, bridging analysis to combinatorics and information theory. It would also provide a rigorous foundation for computing the topological entropy h_top(logistic 4) = log 2.

**Catalog References**: `IntervalCovering.conjugacy_preserves_periodic` (DejaVuDynamics/IntervalCovering.lean), `LogisticDynamics.logistic_4_surjective_on_unit` (DejaVuDynamics/LogisticDynamics.lean)

**Proof Strategy**:
1. Define the symbolic dynamics space Σ₂ and shift map σ in Lean.
2. Define the coding map h via the itinerary of x under [0,1/2] vs (1/2,1].
3. Prove the semiconjugacy equation by induction using the partition structure.
4. Surjectivity of h follows from the surjectivity of logistic 4 on each half-interval.

**Domain Bridges**: Analysis ↔ Combinatorics (continuous dynamics ↔ sequences), Dynamics ↔ Information Theory (topological entropy = information rate)

**Lineage**: Extends logistic map analysis from this cycle (LogisticDynamics.lean)

**Ambition**: grand_challenge

### Direction 3: Covering Graph Spectral Theory

**Conjecture**: The topological entropy of a continuous piecewise-monotone interval map equals the logarithm of the spectral radius of its covering graph's adjacency matrix. For a map with k monotone pieces, the covering graph has k vertices. The period-3 logistic map has a 2-vertex covering graph with adjacency matrix [[1,1],[1,0]] (or [[1,1],[1,1]] depending on the partition), whose spectral radius is the golden ratio φ ≈ 1.618, giving entropy log(φ) ≈ 0.481.

**Test**: Compute the covering graph for the logistic map at several parameter values. Verify that the spectral radius of the adjacency matrix matches the numerically computed Lyapunov exponent (which equals topological entropy for interval maps).

**Impact**: This would provide a purely algebraic/combinatorial method for computing topological entropy, reducing a dynamical question to a linear algebra computation. It bridges dynamics to graph theory and spectral theory.

**Catalog References**: `IntervalCovering.Covers` (DejaVuDynamics/IntervalCovering.lean), `IntervalCovering.period3_bc_covers_ac` (DejaVuDynamics/IntervalCovering.lean)

**Proof Strategy**:
1. Define the covering graph as a directed graph with intervals as vertices.
2. Prove that the number of n-step covering chains equals the (i,j) entry of A^n.
3. Relate n-step covering chains to periodic points of f^n.
4. Use the growth rate of |Fix(f^n)| to define topological entropy.
5. Show growth rate = log(spectral_radius(A)) by Perron-Frobenius theory.

**Domain Bridges**: Dynamics ↔ Graph Theory (covering graph), Dynamics ↔ Linear Algebra (spectral radius = entropy), Dynamics ↔ Number Theory (growth rates of periodic points)

**Lineage**: Extends covering theory from this cycle

**Ambition**: extension

### Direction 4: Periodic Orbit Density in Devaney Chaos

**Conjecture**: For a continuous map f: [0,1] → [0,1] with positive topological entropy, the set of periodic points is dense in [0,1]. More precisely, for every open interval (a,b) ⊆ [0,1] and every N, there exists a periodic point of period > N in (a,b).

**Test**: Prove that if f has a period-3 orbit, then for every subinterval [α,β] ⊆ [a,c] with α < β, f has a periodic point in [α,β]. This is a consequence of the covering theory: the self-covering of [b,c] can be refined to cover any subinterval.

**Impact**: Density of periodic points is one of the three ingredients of Devaney chaos (along with topological transitivity and sensitive dependence). Proving it from the covering theory would be a significant formalization milestone.

**Catalog References**: `SharkovskyForcing.Period3Config.f_image_I2_contains_full` (DejaVuDynamics/SharkovskyForcing.lean)

**Proof Strategy**:
1. Show that the self-covering relation on [b,c] implies f^n([b,c]) ⊇ [b,c] for all n.
2. By compactness, for any open subinterval U of [b,c], there exists n such that f^n(U) ⊇ [b,c].
3. Then f^n has a fixed point in U (by self-covering and Brouwer).
4. This fixed point of f^n is a periodic point of f with period dividing n.
5. For density in [a,c], use the covering [a,b] → [b,c] to transfer density.

**Domain Bridges**: Dynamics ↔ Topology (density of periodic orbits), Dynamics ↔ Ergodic Theory (equidistribution of periodic orbits)

**Lineage**: Extends Period-3 forcing theory from this cycle

**Ambition**: extension

### Direction 5: Higher-Dimensional Brouwer and Cognitive State Spaces

**Conjecture**: The Universal Period Divisor Theorem (every continuous self-map of [a,b] has periodic points of period dividing n) extends to continuous self-maps of the closed unit ball B^d ⊆ ℝ^d for all d ≥ 1, via the Brouwer fixed point theorem. However, the covering-based forcing results (period 3 ⟹ all periods) do NOT extend to d ≥ 2 — there exist continuous self-maps of B² with period-3 orbits but no period-2 orbits.

**Test**: Formalize the Brouwer fixed point theorem for B^d (available in Mathlib as `Brouwer.exists_fixed_point_of_mapsTo` or similar) and use it to prove the d-dimensional Universal Period Divisor Theorem. Then construct an explicit counterexample to Sharkovsky in 2D.

**Impact**: This clarifies the boundary of Sharkovsky-type results: they are fundamentally one-dimensional phenomena. Higher-dimensional cognitive state spaces (which are more realistic) obey different forcing rules, potentially explaining why déjà vu is intermittent rather than constant.

**Catalog References**: `IntervalCovering.exists_periodic_point_dividing` (DejaVuDynamics/IntervalCovering.lean)

**Proof Strategy**:
1. Find or formalize Brouwer FPT for B^d in Mathlib.
2. Show f^n: B^d → B^d is continuous and self-mapping.
3. Apply Brouwer to f^n to get a fixed point.
4. For the counterexample: use a rotation of the disk that has period-3 points but no period-2 points.

**Domain Bridges**: Dynamics ↔ Algebraic Topology (Brouwer FPT), 1D Dynamics ↔ Higher-Dimensional Dynamics (boundary of Sharkovsky)

**Lineage**: Generalizes Universal Period Divisor from this cycle

**Ambition**: extension
