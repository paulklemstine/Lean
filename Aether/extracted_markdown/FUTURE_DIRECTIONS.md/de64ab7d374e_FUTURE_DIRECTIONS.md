# Future Directions — Impossible Geometries via the Fibonacci Apparition Lattice

## Synthesis

This cycle reframed the divisibility structure of Fibonacci numbers as a *discrete
geometry* and proved that this geometry simultaneously exhibits the Euclidean and
the elliptic faces of the parallel postulate.

The organizing object is the **entry point** (rank of apparition) `α(m)`: the least
`k > 0` with `m ∣ F(k)`. The Entry-Point Characterization Theorem
(`FibEntryChar.fib_dvd_iff_entryPt_dvd`) shows that each divisibility "line"
`L(m) = {k | m ∣ F(k)}` is exactly the principal ideal `α(m)·ℕ`. Reading these
ideals geometrically:

* **Divergence (Euclidean face).** `ApparitionGeometry.apparition_step` proves that
  no index lies strictly between consecutive members of a line — they are spaced by
  *exactly* `α(m)`, evenly spaced parallel hyperplanes whose spacing grows linearly
  with `α(m)`. The complementary `apparition_succ` shows the next member is always
  `k + α(m)`, so a line is literally the arithmetic progression `α(m)·ℕ`.
* **Convergence (elliptic / "impossible" face).** `apparition_inter_eq_mul` and
  `apparition_lines_meet` prove that two lines are *forced to re-intersect*, and that
  their meeting set is again a line — generated by `lcm(α a, α b)`. Parallel families
  that converge.

The bridge between the two faces is the **lcm law**
`FibEntryChar.fibEntryPt_mul_coprime` (`α(a·b) = lcm(α a, α b)` for coprime `a, b`),
re-exported here through `apparition_product_generator`. Together with the
`(3,4,5)`-instantiation (`fibEntryPt_three`, `fibEntryPt_four`, `fibEntryPt_five`,
`apparition_profile_345`, giving the apparition profile `(4,6,5)`), this packages the
apparition lattice into a usable arithmetic-geometry dictionary.

## Direction 1 — The full apparition reconstruction functor `α` from prime powers

**Conjecture.** For every `m ≥ 1`, `α(m) = lcm` over prime powers `p^e ‖ m` of
`α(p^e)`, and `α(p^e) = p^{max(e - e₀, 0)} · α(p)` where `e₀` is the `p`-adic
valuation of `F(α(p))` (the "wall" exponent). Equivalently, `α` is a
fully-multiplicative-on-lcm functor from the divisor lattice of `m` to the index
lattice of `ℕ`.

**The key insight is** that `fibEntryPt_mul_coprime` (and its finite form
`FibEntryChar.fibEntryPt_prod_coprime`) already proves the *coprime gluing* half;
what remains is the single-prime *lifting law*, governed by the `p`-adic valuation
`v_p(F_k)` and the lifting-the-exponent lemma for Lucas sequences. Once both halves
are in place, `α` is computable from a factorization without ever touching a
Fibonacci number.

**Why now?** Mathlib has `Nat.factorization`, the multiplicative-function API, and
enough `padicValNat` machinery to state and attack the lifting law; the coprime half
is done, so this is the natural, immediately testable next theorem. Falsifiable: a
single `m` with `α(m) ≠ lcm_p α(p^{v_p m})` (search `m ≤ 10^5` computationally) kills
it.

## Direction 2 — A genuine metric making the "impossible geometry" a metric space

**Conjecture.** The map `d(j,k) = ` (the number of distinct primes `p` with
`α(p) ∣ j` XOR `α(p) ∣ k`), restricted to apparition indices, is an (ultra)metric,
and the re-intersection theorem `apparition_lines_meet` becomes the statement that
any two lines are at bounded Hausdorff distance — formally realizing "parallel lines
that both diverge (componentwise) and converge (in the metric)".

**The key insight is** that the convergence/divergence duality proved this cycle is
*not* metaphor: it is the difference between the product (componentwise) order on the
index lattice — captured by `apparition_step` — and the lcm-generated structure on it
— captured by `apparition_lines_meet`; a single symmetric-difference weight
reconciles them.

**Why now?** Mathlib's `MetricSpace`/`PseudoMetricSpace` and `Finset.symmDiff` APIs
make the triangle inequality a finite combinatorial check; the underlying lines are
already proven to be ideals (`apparition_product_generator`), so the carrier set is
fully specified. Falsifiable by exhibiting a triple of indices violating the
(ultra)triangle inequality.

## Direction 3 — Carmichael's primitive-divisor theorem, the infinite tail

**Conjecture (target sorry).** Every composite `n > 10000` (indeed every
`n ∉ {1,2,6,12}`) has a Fibonacci primitive prime divisor; this closes the remaining
`sorry` in `Catalog/Shared/CarmichaelProof.lean`.

**The key insight is** that, in the apparition language, a primitive divisor of
`F(n)` is exactly a prime with `α(p) = n` (`FibEntryChar.entryPt_eq_iff_primitive`),
so Carmichael's theorem is the surjectivity of `α` onto `ℕ \ {1,2,6,12}`; the proof
reduces to a lower bound `Φ_n(φ) > ` (largest non-primitive contribution) on the
`n`-th cyclotomic value at the golden ratio, i.e. a Zsygmondy-style size estimate.
The boundary obstruction is already isolated as `FibEntryChar.fib_twelve_no_primitive`.

**Why now?** The finite range `n ≤ 10000` is already discharged by `native_decide`
in the file, and the entry-point reformulation is now available; the only missing
piece is the analytic cyclotomic lower bound, a self-contained growth estimate
(`F(n) ≈ φ^n / √5`) that Mathlib's `Real`/`Polynomial.cyclotomic` API can support.
Falsifiable instantly by any composite `n` with no primitive divisor.

## Direction 4 — Entry points and primitive Pythagorean triples (Berggren bridge)

**Conjecture.** Along the Berggren tree of primitive Pythagorean triples (catalog:
`Catalog/Pythagorean/BerggrenTree.lean`), the multiset of apparition profiles
`(α a, α b, α c)` of triples at tree-depth `d` is governed by the lcm law of
Direction 1, with the hypotenuse `c` satisfying an `α(c) ∣ c`-type rigidity
infinitely often.

**The key insight is** that the `(3,4,5)` profile `(4,6,5)` proven here
(`apparition_profile_345`) is the depth-0 seed of a tree-recursive apparition
statistic: the Berggren matrices act linearly on legs while `α` acts by lcm
(`apparition_lines_meet`), so the two structures should commute up to the coprime
gluing law.

**Why now?** The Berggren tree is already formalized in the catalog and the coprime
gluing law is now available; pairing them is a cross-domain (Pythagorean ×
number-theory) experiment with immediate computational tests over the first few tree
levels. Falsifiable by computing profiles down to depth 5 and finding no lcm pattern.

## Direction 5 — Non-coprime collapse: the exact defect of the lcm law

**Conjecture.** For arbitrary `a, b` (not coprime), `α(a·b) ∣ lcm(α a, α b)` always,
and the *defect* `lcm(α a, α b) / α(a·b)` is supported only on primes dividing
`gcd(a,b)`, with an explicit formula in terms of shared apparition walls.

**The key insight is** that dropping coprimality cannot *increase* the entry point
(more divisibility constraints only add multiples), so the lcm becomes an upper
bound; the interesting content is exactly *how much* shared structure collapses the
meeting line — the failure mode that `apparition_lines_meet` already sidesteps (it
holds for the lcm form without coprimality) but `apparition_inter_eq_mul` and
`apparition_product_generator` require coprimality for.

**Why now?** The proof of `fibEntryPt_mul_coprime` localizes precisely where
coprimality was used (`Nat.Coprime.mul_dvd_of_dvd_of_dvd`), so the one-sided
divisibility `α(a·b) ∣ lcm` should follow by deleting that step; the defect formula
is then a clean `padicValNat` computation. Falsifiable by an `(a,b)` whose defect has
a prime factor not dividing `gcd(a,b)`.
