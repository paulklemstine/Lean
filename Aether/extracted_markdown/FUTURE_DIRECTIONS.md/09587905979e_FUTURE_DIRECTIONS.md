# Future Directions: Adelic Coordinate Independence

## Synthesis

The coordinate independence theorem establishes that the maximal compact of a restricted product of finite groups is a product probability space under the normalized counting measure. This opens five interconnected research directions:

1. **Measure-theoretic generalization** — extending from finite counting measure to Haar measure on locally compact groups, connecting to the existing `RestrictedProduct.IsLevelCompatible` definition.
2. **Information-theoretic number theory** — formalizing entropy additivity and zero mutual information as consequences of independence, creating new arithmetic invariants.
3. **Interacting models** — breaking independence by introducing prime-prime interactions, modeled as Gibbs measures on restricted products.
4. **Arithmetic statistics** — using independence to compute densities of arithmetically defined sets via Euler products.
5. **Probabilistic automorphic forms** — recasting adelic integration in automorphic representations as expectation computation on a probability space.

Each direction bridges at least two mathematical domains (probability, number theory, harmonic analysis, statistical mechanics, information theory) and is grounded in the formally verified infrastructure.

## Direction 1: Entropy Additivity for Adelic Local Laws

**Conjecture:** For any finite set S of indices and the uniform law on the maximal compact of a restricted product of finite groups, the Shannon entropy of the joint coordinate distribution equals the sum of the local entropies:

    H((πᵢ)ᵢ∈S) = Σᵢ∈S H(πᵢ)

This is equivalent to zero mutual information between any pair of coordinates.

**Test:** For G_p = (ℤ/p²ℤ)× with primes p ∈ {2, 3, 5, 7, 11}, compute both sides for 1000 random choices of subsets S. Any nonzero difference (beyond floating-point error ~10⁻¹²) disproves the conjecture.

**Impact:** Establishes entropy as an additive arithmetic invariant on restricted products. Opens the door to information-theoretic characterizations of arithmetic density theorems: the Euler product for ζ(s) becomes an entropy decomposition.

**Catalog References:**
- `Pythagorean/HaarRestrictedProduct/Probability.lean` — `finite_coordinate_independent`
- `Pythagorean/HaarRestrictedProduct/Defs.lean` — `maximalCompact`, `basicCylinder`

**Proof Strategy:** The conjecture follows immediately from Theorem 2 (coordinate independence). Independence implies H(X,Y) = H(X) + H(Y). Formalize Shannon entropy for discrete distributions in Lean, then apply the factorization theorem. The main technical work is defining entropy and connecting it to the combinatorial probability already formalized.

**Domain Bridges:** Probability ↔ Information Theory ↔ Number Theory

**Lineage:** Direct consequence of `prob_finiteCoordEvent_eq_prod`

**Ambition:** Extension — formalizing a well-known consequence of independence in the adelic setting.

## Direction 2: Haar Measure Factorization on Infinite Restricted Products

**Conjecture:** For a countable family of locally compact groups {Gᵢ} with compact open subgroups {Kᵢ}, the normalized Haar measure on the maximal compact ∏ Kᵢ factors as the product of normalized local Haar measures. The coordinate projections πᵢ are independent random variables in the measure-theoretic sense (with respect to the product σ-algebra generated by coordinate maps).

**Test:** Cannot be directly computed (infinite product), but can be tested on truncations: for increasing finite subsets S ⊂ I, verify the factorization on the S-truncated restricted product. The finite case is Theorem 1b; consistency under truncation is the test.

**Impact:** This would be the full measure-theoretic version of coordinate independence, applicable to the actual ring of adeles ℤ̂ = ∏ₚ ℤₚ. It would connect our formalization to Mathlib's probability infrastructure (`ProbabilityMeasure`, `iIndepFun`, `MeasureTheory.ProbabilityMeasure`).

**Catalog References:**
- `Pythagorean/HaarRestrictedProduct/Defs.lean` — `IsLevelCompatible`
- `Pythagorean/HaarRestrictedProduct/Theorems.lean` — `normalized_haar_value`, `haar_unique_of_eq_on_compact`
- `Pythagorean/HaarRestrictedProduct/Probability.lean` — all theorems

**Proof Strategy:**
1. Define the σ-algebra on the restricted product via the subtype measurable space (already in Defs.lean).
2. Show the maximal compact is measurable and has finite positive measure.
3. Define the normalized measure μ_K = (μ(K))⁻¹ · μ restricted to K.
4. Show μ_K is a probability measure.
5. Use `IsLevelCompatible` to show cylinder measures factor.
6. Apply Dynkin's π-λ theorem (in Mathlib as `MeasureTheory.induction_on_inter`) to extend from cylinders to the full σ-algebra.

**Domain Bridges:** Measure Theory ↔ Harmonic Analysis ↔ Number Theory

**Lineage:** Extends `finite_coordinate_independent` from finite to infinite index sets

**Ambition:** Grand challenge — requires substantial new Lean infrastructure connecting restricted products to Mathlib's measure theory.

## Direction 3: Interacting Gibbs Measures on Prime-Indexed Spaces

**Conjecture:** Define a family of probability measures on ∏ₚ Kₚ parametrized by an "interaction potential" φ : ι × ι → ℝ:

    μ_φ(x) ∝ exp(Σᵢ<ⱼ φ(i,j) · f(xᵢ, xⱼ))

For φ = 0, this recovers the product measure (independence). For φ ≠ 0, coordinates become dependent. **Conjecture:** There exists a critical interaction strength φ_c such that for |φ| < φ_c, the measure is uniquely determined, and for |φ| > φ_c, phase coexistence occurs (multiple Gibbs measures for the same interaction).

**Test:** For G_p = ℤ/pℤ with p ∈ {2, 3, 5, 7}, define φ(p,q) = β for nearest-neighbor primes and compute the mutual information I(πₚ; πq) as a function of β. Plot the curve; look for a singularity or rapid change indicating a phase transition.

**Impact:** Would establish arithmetic analogues of phase transitions in statistical mechanics. The "non-interacting" limit (β = 0) is our independence theorem; interactions model correlations between primes (e.g., from sieving or from automorphic L-functions).

**Catalog References:**
- `Pythagorean/HaarRestrictedProduct/Probability.lean` — independence as the β = 0 case

**Proof Strategy:** Start with finite models. Define the partition function Z(β) = Σₓ exp(β · H(x)). Show analyticity in β for finite systems. Study the thermodynamic limit (adding more primes). Use Dobrushin uniqueness conditions to establish uniqueness for small |β|.

**Domain Bridges:** Statistical Mechanics ↔ Number Theory ↔ Probability

**Lineage:** Extends independence theorem by parametric deformation

**Ambition:** Grand challenge — would be a genuinely new connection between statistical physics and number theory.

## Direction 4: Euler Product Density Formalization

**Conjecture:** For any "local property" Pₚ ⊆ Gₚ at each prime p, if the natural density of integers n satisfying all local conditions exists, then:

    d({n : Pₚ(n) for all p}) = ∏ₚ d_p(Pₚ)

where d_p(Pₚ) = |Pₚ|/|Gₚ| is the local density. This is the Euler product formula for arithmetic densities.

**Test:** Verify for:
- Square-free: Pₚ = {x ∈ (ℤ/p²ℤ)× : p ∤ x}; density = ∏(1-1/p²) = 6/π².
- Cube-free: density = ∏(1-1/p³) = 1/ζ(3).
- Simultaneously coprime to several primes: density = ∏(1-1/p).

Compute for 10-50 primes and compare to the empirical density up to N = 10⁶.

**Impact:** Formalizes the most common application of restricted-product independence in analytic number theory. Would provide machine-verified proofs of classical density results.

**Catalog References:**
- `Pythagorean/HaarRestrictedProduct/Probability.lean` — `prob_finiteCoordEvent_eq_prod`

**Proof Strategy:** Define "local property" as a predicate on each Gₚ. Use `finite_coordinate_independent` on truncations to finitely many primes. Show convergence of the truncated product to the infinite Euler product. The main challenge is connecting the finite formal setup to the asymptotic density of integers.

**Domain Bridges:** Number Theory ↔ Probability ↔ Analysis

**Lineage:** Direct application of `prob_finiteCoordEvent_eq_prod` to arithmetic

**Ambition:** Extension — standard application of independence, but novel in the formal setting.

## Direction 5: Expectation Factorization and Moment Methods

**Conjecture:** For bounded functions fᵢ : Kᵢ → ℝ depending on finitely many coordinates:

    E[∏ᵢ∈S fᵢ(πᵢ)] = ∏ᵢ∈S E[fᵢ(πᵢ)]

More generally, all mixed cumulants of order ≥ 2 involving functions of distinct coordinates vanish.

**Test:** For G_p = (ℤ/p²ℤ)× with p ∈ {2,3,5,7}, choose 500 random bounded functions fₚ : Gₚ → ℚ and verify the expectation factorization using exact rational arithmetic. Separately, compute the third and fourth cumulants and verify vanishing.

**Impact:** Extends independence from events to random variables. The expectation factorization is the standard tool for proving limit theorems (CLT, LDP) for sums of independent random variables. Formalizing it would enable formal proofs of arithmetic CLTs (e.g., Erdős–Kac theorem).

**Catalog References:**
- `Pythagorean/HaarRestrictedProduct/Probability.lean` — `FiniteCoordinateIndependent`

**Proof Strategy:** Define expectation as a sum over the maximal compact divided by its cardinality. Use `finiteCoordEvent_eq_piFinset` to decompose the sum into a product of local sums. The main technical step is rewriting a sum over a product space as an iterated sum.

**Domain Bridges:** Probability ↔ Harmonic Analysis ↔ Number Theory

**Lineage:** Direct consequence of `finite_coordinate_independent`, extending from indicator functions to general functions

**Ambition:** Extension — computationally verified, formally within reach.
