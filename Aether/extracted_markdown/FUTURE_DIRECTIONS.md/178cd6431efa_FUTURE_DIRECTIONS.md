# Future Directions: Reversible Computing and Thermodynamic Efficiency

## 1. Toffoli Gate Universality

The Toffoli gate generates all even permutations of `{0,1}^n` for `n ≥ 3`, and together with NOT generates all permutations. A formal proof would show that the subgroup of `Equiv.Perm (Fin (2^n))` generated by all Toffoli-like gates (acting on any three wires of an n-wire circuit) equals the alternating group `alternatingGroup (Fin (2^n))` when `n ≥ 3`.

The key insight is that the Toffoli gate, viewed as a permutation, is an even permutation (a product of an even number of transpositions), and the set of all wire-permuted Toffoli gates generates the full alternating group because they can simulate arbitrary 3-cycles.

Why now? We already have `toffoliEquiv` as a concrete permutation and Mathlib has `alternatingGroup`. The gap is formalizing wire-embedding (placing a 3-bit gate into an n-bit circuit) and proving generation, which requires the theory of transitive group actions on `Fin (2^n)`.

## 2. Quantitative Landauer Bound with Shannon Entropy

Our `landauer_erasure_identity` gives the total collision count `|α| - |β|`. A deeper result would relate the Shannon entropy `H(X)` of a random variable `X` on `α` to the conditional entropy `H(X | f(X))`: the entropy drop `H(X) - H(X | f(X))` is exactly the mutual information `I(X; f(X))`, which lower-bounds the thermodynamic cost in units of `kT ln 2`. Formalizing this requires connecting `Finset.card`-based combinatorics to `MeasureTheory.entropy`.

The key insight is that our combinatorial erasure identity is the uniform-distribution special case of the entropic Landauer bound; the general statement requires the data processing inequality for Shannon entropy.

Why now? Mathlib's `MeasureTheory.Measure.tilted` and `MeasureTheory.entropy` provide the measure-theoretic infrastructure. The bridge is showing that for counting measure on finite types, Shannon entropy reduces to logarithm of fiber sizes.

## 3. Circuit Complexity of Reversible Simulation

Bennett (1973) showed that any computation using time `T` and space `S` can be made reversible using time `O(T)` and space `O(S log T)`. The pebble game formalization would model this: define a DAG representing a computation, define "reversible pebbling" (each pebble placement/removal must be justified), and prove the `O(S log T)` space bound for the recursive pebbling strategy.

The key insight is that Bennett's construction is a recursive divide-and-conquer on the computation DAG: to reversibly simulate `T` steps, simulate the first `T/2`, checkpoint the midpoint, simulate the second `T/2`, then un-simulate the first `T/2` to clean up — yielding the `log T` space overhead.

Why now? The `reversibleEmbed` construction we proved gives the single-step case. Extending to multi-step computation requires formalizing the pebble game on DAGs, which is a well-studied combinatorial object amenable to inductive proof in Lean.

## 4. Fredkin Gate and Conservative Logic

The Fredkin (controlled-SWAP) gate is another universal reversible gate with the additional property of being *conservative*: it preserves the Hamming weight of its input. Formalizing Fredkin universality and proving that conservative logic can simulate any reversible computation would connect to the physics of billiard-ball computing models.

The key insight is that the Fredkin gate is universal for conservative reversible logic, and any reversible function can be decomposed into conservative gates with at most a constant-factor overhead in ancilla bits — this is because Fredkin can simulate Toffoli when given appropriate constant inputs.

Why now? We have the Toffoli gate formalized as an involutive equivalence. The Fredkin gate has the same algebraic structure (involutive permutation on `Bool³`) but with a different action. Proving that Fredkin simulates Toffoli (and vice versa with ancillae) would unify the two universal gate sets.

## 5. Entropy Production in Markov Chains and Detailed Balance

Generalizing beyond deterministic functions: for a stochastic map (Markov kernel) on a finite state space, the entropy production per step is non-negative and equals zero iff the kernel satisfies detailed balance. This is the stochastic generalization of "reversible ↔ zero erasure" and connects to the second law of thermodynamics for discrete systems.

The key insight is that our `landauer_erasure_identity` is the deterministic limit of the stochastic entropy production formula: when the Markov kernel is a delta function concentrated on `f(x)`, the entropy production reduces to `log(|fiber|)` summed over the stationary distribution, recovering the counting argument.

Why now? Mathlib has `MeasureTheory.Kernel` for Markov kernels and `PMF` for discrete distributions. The missing piece is the formalization of KL-divergence for finite PMFs and the proof that entropy production (= KL divergence of forward vs. time-reversed chain) is non-negative, which follows from Jensen's inequality applied to the convex function `-log`.
