# Future Directions: Metric Geometry of Decision Cells

This document outlines five concrete research directions opened by the ball-inclusion and inscribed-radius theorems for Lipschitz margin cells.

---

## 1. Tropical Chamber Inradius Theorem

**Hypothesis.** When score functions are max-plus affine (i.e., `s_i(x) = max_k (a_{ik} · x + b_{ik})`), the margin cell `marginCell s i` is a tropically convex polyhedron. Its Chebyshev radius admits a purely combinatorial lower bound in terms of coefficient gaps.

**Proof strategy.**
- Formalize max-plus affine maps and show that pairwise differences of such maps are piecewise-affine and hence Lipschitz.
- Prove that the margin cell is an intersection of tropical halfspaces.
- Derive an explicit formula for the inscribed ball radius from the vertex enumeration of the tropical polyhedron.

**Cross-domain connection.** This bridges certified deep learning (ReLU networks produce piecewise-affine scores) with tropical convexity and combinatorial optimization.

**Lean formalization target.** Define `TropicalAffineScore` and prove `marginCell_tropicallyConvex` and `chebyshevRadius_tropical_lower_bound`.

## 2. Robust Nerve Theorem for Decision Regions

**Hypothesis.** Given a collection of margin cells with certified pairwise inscribed-ball overlaps, the nerve complex of the collection is stable under bounded score perturbations. That is, if all Lipschitz certified radii exceed a threshold, the homotopy type of the nerve is preserved.

**Proof strategy.**
- Use the ball-inclusion theorem to guarantee that certified intersections of margin cells are nonempty.
- Apply a nerve lemma (good-cover theorem) to conclude that the nerve captures the topology of the union.
- Show that bounded perturbations of scores preserve the nonemptiness of intersections when the certified radii are large enough.

**Cross-domain connection.** This connects adversarial robustness certification to topological data analysis and persistent homology of classifiers.

**Lean formalization target.** Formalize `NerveLemma` for metric nerves and prove `nerve_stable_under_perturbation`.

## 3. Continuous-Depth Certification via Grönwall Estimates

**Hypothesis.** For score functions generated by ODE flows `ds/dt = F(s, t)` with a Lipschitz vector field, the margin gap satisfies a Grönwall-type differential inequality. The ball-inclusion theorem then extends to continuous-depth networks (neural ODEs) with Lipschitz constants determined by the flow time and the Lipschitz constant of F.

**Proof strategy.**
- Prove a Grönwall lemma for the pairwise gap function `g_j(t) = s_i(x, t) - s_j(x, t)`.
- Show that if `g_j(0) ≥ γ` and the vector field is `L`-Lipschitz, then `g_j(T) ≥ γ · e^{-LT}`.
- Apply `ball_subset_marginCell_of_pairwise_lipschitz` with `K = e^{LT}` times the spatial Lipschitz constant.

**Cross-domain connection.** This bridges neural ODEs, dynamical systems stability, and Hamilton–Jacobi theory with certified robustness.

**Lean formalization target.** Formalize `GronwallLipschitzBound` and prove `ball_subset_marginCell_neuralODE`.

## 4. Infinite-Class Kernel and Nearest-Neighbor Certification

**Hypothesis.** For kernel classifiers with an infinite hypothesis family (e.g., Gaussian process classifiers with continuous label spaces), the infimum-gap formulation of the margin provides a certified radius via our theorem, provided the gap function has a uniform Lipschitz bound.

**Proof strategy.**
- Define the uniform margin as `uniformMargin s i x = sInf ((fun j => s i x - s j x) '' {j | j ≠ i})`.
- Prove that if `uniformMargin > 0` and all pairwise gaps are `K`-Lipschitz, then `ball x (uniformMargin / K) ⊆ marginCell s i`.
- Instantiate for Gaussian process classifiers with Matérn kernels, where Lipschitz constants can be bounded from kernel smoothness.

**Cross-domain connection.** This extends certified robustness from finite-class neural networks to infinite-dimensional hypothesis spaces and functional analysis.

**Lean formalization target.** Define `uniformMargin` and prove `ball_subset_marginCell_of_infimumGap`.

## 5. Optimization Principle: Maximizing Decision-Cell Inscribed Radii

**Hypothesis.** Training a classifier to maximize `γ / K` (margin divided by Lipschitz constant) is equivalent to maximizing a lower bound on the Chebyshev radius of the decision cell at each training point. This provides a geometric foundation for adversarial training.

**Proof strategy.**
- Show that the certified radius `γ / K` is monotone in `γ` and anti-monotone in `K`.
- Formalize the training objective `maximize min_x (γ(x) / K)` and prove it provides a uniform lower bound on inscribed radii across all training points.
- Connect to spectral-norm regularization: bounding `K` by constraining weight matrix spectral norms.

**Cross-domain connection.** This bridges geometric measure theory (inscribed balls, Chebyshev centers) with robust optimization, adversarial training, and spectral regularization in deep learning.

**Lean formalization target.** Formalize `training_objective_bounds_chebyshevRadius` as a direct corollary of the inscribed-radius theorem.
