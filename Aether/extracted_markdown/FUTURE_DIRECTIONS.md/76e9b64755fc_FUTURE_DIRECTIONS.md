# Future Directions: Berggren Harmonic Computation

## Overview

The formalization of multiresolution analysis on bounded Berggren layers opens several concrete research directions. Each builds on the verified foundation of wavelet decomposition, spectral sparsity, and certified recovery.

---

## Direction 1: Infinite Berggren Boundary Analysis and Plancherel Theory

**Goal:** Extend the finite MRA to the infinite boundary of the Berggren tree and establish a Plancherel-type isometry.

**Context:** The current formalization works at fixed finite depth n. The Berggren tree, however, is infinite: every primitive Pythagorean triple corresponds to a unique infinite word in {A,B,C}^ℕ. The *boundary* of the tree — the set of all infinite words — is a Cantor-like space with a natural probability measure (product measure giving weight 1/3 to each branch).

**Concrete Steps:**
1. Define the boundary space Ω = (ℕ → Fin 3) with the product topology and product measure μ = (1/3, 1/3, 1/3)^ℕ.
2. Construct L²(Ω, μ) and define wavelet functions as measurable functions on Ω.
3. Prove the wavelet system forms an orthonormal basis of L²(Ω, μ).
4. Establish the Plancherel identity: ‖f‖² = |c₀|² + Σ_{k,u,j} |c_{k,u,j}|².
5. Formalize the convergence of finite-depth approximations to the boundary L² theory.

**Impact:** This would be the first formal Plancherel theorem for a number-theoretically defined wavelet system, connecting abstract harmonic analysis to Diophantine geometry.

**Formalization Challenge:** Requires measure theory (Mathlib's MeasureTheory), L² spaces, and conditional expectations in the measure-theoretic sense — all available in Mathlib but requiring careful integration with the combinatorial tree structure.

## Direction 2: Representation-Theoretic Block Decomposition of Transport Operators

**Goal:** Show that natural operators on the Berggren tree (averaging, shifting, permuting) decompose into block-diagonal form in the wavelet basis.

**Context:** The three child-shift operators S_A, S_B, S_C and the averaging operator Avg generate an algebra of endomorphisms on LayerFun(n). The wavelet decomposition should diagonalize (or block-diagonalize) this algebra, revealing its representation-theoretic structure.

**Concrete Steps:**
1. Define the Berggren transport algebra A_n ⊆ End_ℂ(LayerFun n) generated by {S_A, S_B, S_C, Avg}.
2. Prove that A_n preserves the scaling/detail decomposition: T(scalingSpace k) ⊆ scalingSpace k.
3. Show that in the wavelet basis, each T ∈ A_n acts blockwise on detail subspaces.
4. Classify the irreducible representations of A_n and relate them to the wavelet index structure.
5. For commutative subalgebras, prove simultaneous diagonalization — the true Fourier-theoretic statement.

**Impact:** This is the "Berggren Fourier analysis" in the representation-theoretic sense. It would transform the wavelet decomposition from a basis change into a spectral decomposition with algebraic meaning — analogous to the Peter-Weyl theorem for compact groups.

**Key Insight:** The transport algebra should be related to the monoid algebra ℂ[{A,B,C}*_n] of words of length ≤ n, and its representation theory should reflect the branching structure of the tree.

## Direction 3: Spectral Concentration and Distribution of Hypotenuse Residues

**Goal:** Prove quantitative bounds on wavelet coefficient concentration for modular observables, connecting spectral sparsity to equidistribution of hypotenuse residues.

**Context:** Our experiments show that hypotenuse mod 2 and mod 4 have *complete* sparsity (all detail coefficients vanish), reflecting the known fact that hypotenuses of primitive triples are always ≡ 1 (mod 4). For other moduli, partial sparsity is observed. A theorem relating the degree of sparsity to the equidistribution properties of residues would connect wavelet analysis to analytic number theory.

**Concrete Steps:**
1. Prove that hypotenuse mod 2 and mod 4 are 0-prefix-constant (complete sparsity).
2. For general q, bound the number of nonzero wavelet coefficients in terms of q and the depth n.
3. Show that as n → ∞, the wavelet energy spectrum of hypotenuse mod q converges to a distribution determined by the reduction of the Berggren monoid modulo q.
4. Connect to known results on equidistribution of Pythagorean triples in arithmetic progressions.

**Impact:** This would provide a new wavelet-based proof technique for equidistribution results in multiplicative number theory, and could yield quantitative rates of convergence that are difficult to obtain by other methods.

**Formalization Strategy:** Start with mod-2 and mod-4 cases (computationally decidable), then develop the general theory using the Chinese Remainder Theorem and the structure of the Berggren monoid modulo q.

## Direction 4: Hidden-Period Algorithm with Formal Complexity Bounds

**Goal:** Design and formally verify an algorithm that detects hidden periodicity in arithmetic signals on the Berggren tree, with provable time/space complexity bounds.

**Context:** The certified robust recovery theorem shows that period detection is stable under noise. The next step is to formalize this as an algorithm with explicit input/output specifications and complexity analysis.

**Concrete Steps:**
1. Define a formal model of computation on Berggren-indexed signals (e.g., RAM model or Turing machine).
2. Specify the DETECT-PREFIX-DEPTH algorithm formally in Lean.
3. Prove correctness: if the input is a k-prefix-constant signal plus bounded noise, the algorithm outputs k.
4. Prove complexity: the algorithm runs in O(3^n · n) time and O(3^n) space.
5. Compare to classical and quantum period-finding algorithms: show that the Berggren wavelet transform achieves the same structural advantage as quantum Fourier sampling for this specific class of periodic functions.

**Impact:** This would be, to our knowledge, the first formally verified period-finding algorithm on a number-theoretically structured domain. It would also clarify the precise relationship between wavelet analysis on trees and quantum Fourier analysis on groups.

**Extension:** For signals where the period is not aligned with the prefix structure but with an arithmetic quotient (e.g., hypotenuse mod q), design a hybrid algorithm that combines wavelet analysis with modular arithmetic.

## Direction 5: Berggren Spectral Blocks and Automorphic Structure

**Goal:** Relate the wavelet decomposition of the Berggren tree to automorphic forms and transfer operators on the modular group.

**Context:** The Berggren generators lie in O(2,1;ℤ), the integer orthogonal group of the Lorentzian form. This group is closely related to the modular group PSL₂(ℤ) and to the theory of automorphic forms. The spectral decomposition of transfer operators on such groups is a deep and active area of research.

**Concrete Steps:**
1. Formalize the embedding of the Berggren monoid into O(2,1;ℤ) and its relationship to PSL₂(ℤ).
2. Define the transfer operator L: LayerFun(n) → LayerFun(n) associated to the Berggren action.
3. Compute the spectrum of L in the wavelet basis and relate it to known spectral data of the Selberg/Ruelle zeta function.
4. For finite quotients (Berggren monoid mod p), relate the representation theory to modular forms mod p.
5. Explore whether wavelet coefficients of Berggren observables are related to Fourier coefficients of automorphic forms.

**Impact:** This is the most speculative but potentially most transformative direction. A connection between Berggren wavelet coefficients and automorphic forms would link finite combinatorial harmonic analysis to some of the deepest structures in modern number theory.

**Risk Assessment:** This direction requires substantial mathematical development beyond what is currently in Mathlib. However, even partial results (e.g., computing the transfer operator spectrum at small depths and comparing to known zeta values) would be significant.

## Priority Ranking

1. **Direction 1** (Boundary Plancherel) — Most natural extension, well-supported by Mathlib infrastructure.
2. **Direction 3** (Spectral Concentration) — Directly connects to verifiable number-theoretic phenomena.
3. **Direction 2** (Block Decomposition) — Core harmonic analysis, moderate difficulty.
4. **Direction 4** (Certified Algorithm) — High impact for verified computing, builds on existing work.
5. **Direction 5** (Automorphic Structure) — Highest risk/reward, requires new mathematical development.
