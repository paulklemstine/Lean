# Future Directions — Units, Localness, and the Differential Ring of Combinatorial Species

## Synthesis

The catalog's `Applications/CombinatorialSpecies.lean` established the exponential generating
function (EGF) `egf a = ∑ₙ (aₙ/n!) Xⁿ` as the bridge between Joyal's combinatorial species and
formal power series, proving it *additive* (`egf_add`), *multiplicative* over the binomial
(Day-convolution) product (`egf_mul`), *injective* (`egf_injective`), and compatible with the
shift and Euler operators (`egf_derivative`, `egf_pointing`). Those were a *list of separate
homomorphism identities*.

This cycle promotes that list to a **single structural object** and realizes the prior cycle's
own stated future direction ("Units, Localness, and the Differential Ring of Combinatorial
Species"). In the new file `Applications/SpeciesDifferentialRing.lean` we prove:

* `egf_surjective` + `egfEquiv` — the EGF is an **isomorphism of commutative rings**
  `CSeq ≃+* ℚ⟦X⟧`, where `CSeq` is the type of counting sequences equipped with the binomial
  convolution as multiplication and the delta sequence as unit. (`CombinatorialSpecies` had only
  injectivity; we add the explicit inverse `n ↦ n!·cₙ` and bundle everything as a `RingEquiv`.)
* `binConv_leibniz` — the **shift is a derivation** for the convolution: `(F·G)′ = F′·G + F·G′`,
  making `CSeq` a *differential ring*.
* `binPoint_leibniz` — the **Euler/pointing operator `X·d/dX` is also a derivation**
  `(F·G)• = F•·G + F·G•`, the categorified product rule for "distinguishing a label".
* `egf_isUnit_iff` and `cseq_isUnit_iff` — the **units / localness** theorems: a species is a
  unit of the convolution ring iff its empty-structure count `a 0` is nonzero. So `CSeq` is a
  *local ring* with maximal ideal the augmentation ideal `{a | a 0 = 0}`.

## Results Summary

All headline results in `Applications/SpeciesDifferentialRing.lean` are proved with **no `sorry`**
and depend only on the standard axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`. The file
imports and cites `Applications/CombinatorialSpecies.lean`, building on `egf`, `binConv`,
`egf_mul`, `egf_add`, `egf_injective`, `egf_derivative`, `egf_pointing`, and `binConv_comm`
rather than reproving them. The CommRing instance on `CSeq` and the `RingHom`/`RingEquiv`
packaging reduce every ring axiom to `egf_injective` plus the power-series axioms — the
"analytic shadow proves the combinatorial identity" pattern, now made structural.

Separately, the broken import `Shared/CarmichaelHelper` (a nonexistent module) was removed from
`Shared/CarmichaelProof.lean`, which now compiles; its one remaining `sorry` (Carmichael's
theorem for composite `n > 10000`) is analyzed as Direction 3 below.

## Research Directions

### 1. The species ring is a discrete valuation ring; valuation = empty-structure order

Define the **order** of a species, `ord F = inf {n | F[n] ≠ ∅}` (the smallest cardinality on
which `F` has a structure). The conjecture is that, transported across `egfEquiv`, `ord`
is exactly the `X`-adic valuation of `ℚ⟦X⟧`, so `CSeq` is a discrete valuation ring whose
maximal ideal is principal, generated by the **singleton species** `X` (one structure on a
1-element set). Concretely and falsifiably: `ord (F·G) = ord F + ord G`, `ord (F + G) ≥
min (ord F) (ord G)`, and `IsUnit F ↔ ord F = 0`. The last is already `cseq_isUnit_iff`; the
multiplicativity of `ord` is the new testable content.

The key insight is that `cseq_isUnit_iff` identifies the maximal ideal as the augmentation ideal
`{a | a 0 = 0}`, and in a local ring whose value group is `ℤ` this ideal must be principal — so
the abstract localness we proved *forces* a valuation, and the only candidate generator with
`a 0 = 0, a 1 ≠ 0` is the singleton species `X`.

Why now? With `egfEquiv : CSeq ≃+* ℚ⟦X⟧` in hand, every valuation-theoretic fact about `ℚ⟦X⟧`
(itself a DVR in Mathlib) can be pulled back mechanically; the only genuinely new lemma is that
`ord` corresponds to `PowerSeries.order`, a one-line `coeff`-comparison away.

### 2. Species composition, the chain rule, and the exponential formula

Extend the dictionary to **substitution** `F ∘ G` (insert `G`-structures into the blocks of an
`F`-structure), defined for `G` with `G[0] = ∅`, whose EGF is the analytic composition
`(EGF F) ∘ (EGF G)`. The falsifiable predictions: (i) the **chain rule**
`(F ∘ G)′ = (F′ ∘ G)·G′` holds at the level of counting sequences; (ii) the augmentation ideal
`{a | a 0 = 0}` is closed under substitution and forms a (non-unital) composition monoid;
(iii) the **exponential formula** `EGF(E ∘ G) = exp(EGF G)` counts disjoint unions of
`G`-structures, recovering set-partition statistics when `G = X`.

The key insight is that the two derivations we proved, the shift `binConv_leibniz` and the Euler
operator `binPoint_leibniz`, are precisely the pieces a chain rule is assembled from: `D(F∘G)`
expands by Leibniz into `(D F ∘ G)` reweighted by `D G`, so the chain rule is a *theorem about
derivations*, not a fresh computation.

Why now? The product rule and the ring isomorphism are the two prerequisites of a chain rule;
both now exist, so composition is the next and last basic species operation to close the
dictionary.

### 3. Carmichael's theorem for large composite n via the convolution/cyclotomic bridge

`Shared/CarmichaelProof.lean` proves that `F(n)` has a primitive prime divisor for all
`13 ≤ n ≤ 10000` by `native_decide`, but leaves the composite tail `n > 10000` as a `sorry`.
The conjecture (Carmichael 1913, here a concrete falsifiable target) is that
`primPart n > 1` for **every** composite `n > 10000`, where `primPart n = ∏_{d|n} F_d^{μ(n/d)}`
is the homogeneous-cyclotomic value `Φ_n(α,β)`, `α = (1+√5)/2`. The strategy: (a) prove
`primPart` equals the Möbius-convolution product above (a `binConv`-style Möbius inversion of
`log F`); (b) lower-bound `|Φ_n(α,β)| ≥ α^{φ(n)}/√5`; (c) bound the unique possible "intrinsic"
non-primitive prime by the largest prime factor of `n`, hence `≤ n`; (d) compare using
`φ(n) > log_α(n√5)` for `n > 10000`.

The key insight is that the Möbius inversion turning the multiplicative tower `F_n = ∏_{d|n}
Φ_d` into `Φ_n = ∏_{d|n} F_d^{μ(n/d)}` is the *same* incidence-algebra inversion that underlies
the binomial-convolution ring of Direction 1–2 — the species program and the Carmichael program
share one algebraic engine (Möbius inversion over a divisibility/incidence poset).

Why now? The arithmetic apparition-rank scaffolding already exists in `Shared/CarmichaelProof.lean`
(`bridge_lemma`, `primPart`, `primPart_implies_primitive`); what is missing is exactly the
analytic lower bound, which is isolated and self-contained once the cyclotomic identity (a) is
formalized. Mathlib currently has **no** Zsygmondy/primitive-divisor theory, so this would be a
genuinely new contribution rather than a wrapper.

### 4. The shift and Euler operators generate a faithful Weyl/`sl₂`-type action on species

Let `D` be the shift derivation (`binShift`) and `E` the Euler derivation (`binPoint`). The
conjecture is the commutator identity `[E, D] = -D` on `CSeq` (equivalently `E∘D − D∘E = −D`),
together with `[E, X·] = X·` for multiplication by the singleton, exhibiting a faithful action of
the **3-dimensional solvable Lie algebra** `⟨X·, E, D⟩` (the `ax+b`/Borel subalgebra of `sl₂`) on
the species ring. Falsifiable: the bracket relations must hold on every counting sequence, and
the representation must be faithful (no nonzero operator in the enveloping algebra annihilates all
species).

The key insight is that both `D` and `E` are *already known to be derivations* (this cycle), and
the commutator of two derivations is again a derivation; computing `[E,D]` on the basis sequences
`δ_k` reduces the whole Lie-bracket identity to the single scalar recurrence
`(n)(a_{n+1}) − (a_{n+1})(n+1) = −a_{n+1}`.

Why now? Derivations are the atoms of any Lie-algebra-of-operators statement; with two
independent derivations proved, their algebra is the immediate next structure to expose, and it
upgrades the "differential ring" to a "differential ring with symmetry".

### 5. Closed-form virtual inverses (antipode) of unit species

Because `cseq_isUnit_iff` says every species with `a 0 ≠ 0` is invertible in `CSeq`, each such
species has a *virtual* inverse `a⁻¹` with `binConv a a⁻¹ = δ`. The conjecture is a closed,
computable recursion for the inverse coefficients,
`a⁻¹_n = −a_0^{-1} ∑_{i=1}^{n} C(n,i) a_i a⁻¹_{n−i}` (with `a⁻¹_0 = a_0^{-1}`), and that for the
set species `E` (all `a_n = 1`) the inverse is the sequence of **signed derangement-like
numbers** `a⁻¹_n = (−1)^n`, i.e. `E⁻¹ = exp(−X)`. Falsifiable by direct computation of the first
several coefficients.

The key insight is that localness converts an existence statement (`IsUnit`) into an explicit
finite recursion: inverting `egf a = a_0 + (higher)` in `ℚ⟦X⟧` is Neumann-series summation, and
the binomial weights `C(n,i)` are exactly the `binConv` structure constants, so the antipode is
forced by the convolution combinatorics.

Why now? `cseq_isUnit_iff` guarantees the inverse *exists*; turning that into a formula is the
natural constructive follow-up and yields directly usable enumeration identities (e.g. inclusion–
exclusion as `E⁻¹`).
