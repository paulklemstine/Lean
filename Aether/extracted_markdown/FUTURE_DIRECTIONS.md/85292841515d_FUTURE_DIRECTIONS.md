# Future Directions: Hyperbolic Number Theory

## Synthesis

This research cycle established the algebraic foundations of arithmetic on the Poincaré disk, proving that Möbius addition is closed on the unit disk, possesses identity and inverse elements, and produces strictly monotone iterates. The key technical tool was the **Fundamental Identity** $(a+b)^2 - (1+ab)^2 = -(1-a^2)(1-b^2)$, which encodes the curvature of hyperbolic space in a single algebraic equation.

The most promising cross-domain connection emerging from this cycle is the bridge between **hyperbolic lattice theory** and **post-quantum cryptography**. The exponential compression of Möbius iteration — where computing $g^{\oplus n}$ is numerically easy but recovering $n$ from the result is hard due to boundary crowding — suggests a candidate one-way function rooted in geometry rather than number theory. This connects naturally to the Catalog's existing work on lattice cryptography (`Cryptography/BerggrenLatticeCryptography.lean`) and Berggren tree structures (`Cryptography/BerggrenFreeMonoid.lean`).

The second major connection is to **machine learning and embedding theory**. The Poincaré ball model is already used for hierarchical embeddings in AI, but lacks rigorous algebraic foundations. Our formalized Möbius addition, with proven closure and monotonicity, provides the mathematical bedrock for certified hyperbolic neural network layers — connecting to the Catalog's EML framework (`EML/EMLv17Core.lean`).

The direction with highest breakthrough potential is **Direction 1** (Hyperbolic Discrete Logarithm), because it could yield a genuinely new cryptographic primitive based on geometric hardness assumptions, independent of factoring, discrete log, or lattice problems.

### Direction 1: Hyperbolic Discrete Logarithm Problem

**Conjecture**: The hyperbolic discrete logarithm problem (HDLP) — given $g \in (0,1)$ and $y = g^{\oplus n}$, recover $n$ — is computationally hard in the following sense: for $g$ chosen uniformly from $(0.01, 0.99)$ and $n$ chosen uniformly from $\{1, \ldots, N\}$, any algorithm running in time $\text{poly}(\log N)$ succeeds with probability at most $O(1/N^{1/3})$.

**Test**: Implement a numerical HDLP solver using binary search on the Möbius iterate sequence (which is monotone by our Theorem 3.8). Measure the numerical precision required as a function of $n$. If $O(\log N)$ bits of precision suffice to distinguish $g^{\oplus n}$ from $g^{\oplus(n+1)}$, the problem is easy. If $\Omega(N)$ bits are needed near the boundary, it suggests genuine hardness.

**Impact**: A new hardness assumption for post-quantum cryptography based on hyperbolic geometry. Unlike lattice problems (LWE, SIS), the underlying structure is continuous and geometric, potentially enabling new protocol designs. If false (HDLP is easy), it reveals structural regularity in Möbius iteration useful for algorithm design.

**Catalog References**: `Cryptography/BerggrenLatticeCryptography.lean`, `Cryptography/TropicalPostQuantum.lean`, `Cryptography/BerggrenSpectralHash.lean`

**Proof Strategy**: (1) Establish the exact formula for $g^{\oplus n}$ in closed form (conjectured: $(g^{\oplus n}) = \tanh(n \cdot \text{arctanh}(g))$, which reduces HDLP to computing $\text{arctanh}(y)/\text{arctanh}(g)$ — a real division). If this formula holds, HDLP is easy and the direction pivots to multi-generator variants. (2) For multi-generator lattices with $k \geq 2$ generators, prove that the word problem (expressing a lattice point as a Möbius word) is NP-hard by reduction from subset sum.

**Domain Bridges**: Cryptography <-> Geometry, NumberTheory <-> Computation

**Lineage**: Builds on the closure theorem and monotonicity results from this cycle, plus the Catalog's Berggren cryptographic framework.

**Ambition**: grand_challenge

### Direction 2: Hyperbolic Zeta Function and Spectral Theory

**Conjecture**: For the hyperbolic lattice $\mathcal{L}_g$ generated by Möbius iteration of $g \in (0,1)$, the hyperbolic zeta function $\zeta_H(s) = \sum_{x \in \mathcal{L}_g, x \neq 0} \|x\|_H^{-2s}$ has a meromorphic continuation to $\mathbb{C}$ with a simple pole at $s = 1/2$ and satisfies a functional equation $\zeta_H(s) = \chi(s) \zeta_H(1-s)$ for an explicit factor $\chi(s)$.

**Test**: Compute $\zeta_H(s)$ for $s \in \{0.1, 0.2, \ldots, 2.0\}$ using 10000 lattice points. Fit the data to the functional equation form. Check whether $\zeta_H(s)$ and $\chi(s)\zeta_H(1-s)$ agree to 6 decimal places.

**Impact**: A provable analog of the Riemann zeta function on hyperbolic space. Since the lattice structure is explicit and the hyperbolic norms grow exponentially ($\|g^{\oplus n}\|_H \sim 3^n$ for $g=1/2$), the zeta function reduces to a Dirichlet series in $3^{-2ns}$, which is a geometric series. This makes the analytic continuation and functional equation potentially provable — giving a "Riemann Hypothesis" that we can actually prove.

**Catalog References**: `Cryptography/BerggrenDirichletSeries.lean`, `EML/ModularForms.lean`

**Proof Strategy**: (1) Derive the closed form $\zeta_H(s) = 2 \sum_{n=1}^\infty ((3^n - 1)/2)^{-2s}$ for $g = 1/2$. (2) Relate this to the Hurwitz zeta function via substitution $m = 3^n$. (3) Use known analytic continuation results for Dirichlet series to establish meromorphic continuation. (4) Prove the functional equation by exploiting the $3$-adic structure of the exponents.

**Domain Bridges**: NumberTheory <-> Analysis, Geometry <-> Cryptography

**Lineage**: Builds on the hyperbolic norm definition and lattice structure from this cycle.

**Ambition**: grand_challenge

### Direction 3: Certified Hyperbolic Neural Network Layers

**Conjecture**: A Möbius-addition-based neural network layer $f(x) = W \oplus_M x \oplus_M b$ (where $\oplus_M$ is Möbius addition in $\mathbb{R}^d$ and $W, b$ are weight/bias parameters in the Poincaré ball) is Lipschitz continuous with constant $L \leq C \cdot \|W\|_H / (1 - \|b\|^2)$ for an explicit constant $C$.

**Test**: Train a hyperbolic neural network on a hierarchical classification task (e.g., WordNet taxonomy). Compare the empirical Lipschitz constant (estimated by random perturbation) with the theoretical bound. The bound should hold within a factor of 2 on 95% of test inputs.

**Impact**: Formal Lipschitz bounds for hyperbolic neural networks enable certified robustness guarantees, adversarial defense proofs, and convergence rate analysis for hyperbolic gradient descent. This bridges geometric algebra with machine learning theory.

**Catalog References**: `EML/EMLv17Core.lean`, `MachineLearning/HyperbolicNumberTheory/Theorems.lean`

**Proof Strategy**: (1) Formalize the multi-dimensional Möbius addition in Lean 4, extending our 1D closure theorem to $\mathbb{R}^d$. (2) Compute the Jacobian of the Möbius addition map and bound its operator norm. (3) Apply the chain rule to compose layer-wise Lipschitz bounds. Key lemma: the Möbius addition map has differential $D_x(a \oplus x) = (1 - |a|^2) / (1 + \bar{a}x)^2 \cdot I$ at $x = 0$.

**Domain Bridges**: MachineLearning <-> Geometry, Algebra <-> Computation

**Lineage**: Builds on the complex Möbius addition definition and closure results from this cycle.

**Ambition**: extension

### Direction 4: Gyrogroup Factorization and Unique Decomposition

**Conjecture**: For a hyperbolic lattice $\mathcal{L}$ generated by two generators $g_1, g_2 \in (0,1)$ with $\text{arctanh}(g_1)/\text{arctanh}(g_2) \notin \mathbb{Q}$, every nonzero lattice element has a unique representation as a Möbius word $g_{i_1} \oplus g_{i_2} \oplus \cdots \oplus g_{i_k}$ (up to commutativity reordering) if and only if the generators are "hyperbolically independent" — meaning no finite Möbius word in $g_1, -g_1$ evaluates to a Möbius word in $g_2, -g_2$.

**Test**: For $g_1 = 0.3, g_2 = 0.5$ (incommensurable since $\text{arctanh}(0.3)/\text{arctanh}(0.5)$ is irrational), enumerate all Möbius words of length $\leq 8$ and check for collisions. Unique factorization predicts zero collisions for length $\leq 8$.

**Impact**: Establishes a hyperbolic analog of the fundamental theorem of arithmetic. Connects Möbius algebra to the theory of free groups and word problems in geometric group theory. Failure would reveal hidden "hyperbolic relations" analogous to relations in Berggren's tree structure.

**Catalog References**: `Cryptography/BerggrenFreeMonoid.lean`, `Cryptography/BerggrenRightCancellation.lean`, `Algebra/Berggren.lean`

**Proof Strategy**: (1) Prove that real Möbius addition corresponds to ordinary addition under the $\text{arctanh}$ map: $\text{arctanh}(a \oplus b) = \text{arctanh}(a) + \text{arctanh}(b)$. This isomorphism reduces the factorization question to unique representation in $\mathbb{R}$ as $n_1 \alpha + n_2 \beta$ with $\alpha/\beta \notin \mathbb{Q}$. (2) Apply the theory of $\mathbb{Z}$-linear independence to conclude uniqueness.

**Domain Bridges**: Algebra <-> NumberTheory, Cryptography <-> Geometry

**Lineage**: Builds on the Möbius addition properties and lattice definition from this cycle, plus the Berggren monoid theory in the Catalog.

**Ambition**: extension

### Direction 5: Hyperbolic Tessellation Counting via Selberg Trace Formula

**Conjecture**: For a hyperbolic lattice $\mathcal{L}$ associated to a Fuchsian group $\Gamma < \text{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ of cofinite volume $V$, the number $N(R)$ of lattice points within hyperbolic distance $R$ of the origin satisfies
$$N(R) = \frac{V}{4\pi} e^R + O(e^{2R/3})$$
and the error term cannot be improved to $O(e^{(1/2+\epsilon)R})$ without assuming the Selberg eigenvalue conjecture.

**Test**: For the modular group $\Gamma = \text{PSL}(2, \mathbb{Z})$ (with $V = \pi/3$), compute $N(R)$ exactly for $R = 1, 2, \ldots, 10$ by enumerating group elements and compare with the asymptotic formula. The ratio $N(R) / (V e^R / 4\pi)$ should converge to 1.

**Impact**: Connects our algebraic framework to the deep analytic number theory of automorphic forms. The Selberg trace formula provides the tool to relate point counting (geometry) to eigenvalues of the Laplacian (analysis), mirroring how the Riemann zeta function connects prime counting to complex analysis. This could enable new computational approaches to the Selberg eigenvalue conjecture.

**Catalog References**: `EML/ModularForms.lean`, `Computation/GravityOracle.lean`, `Bridges/AlgebraEMLClosureComputation.lean`

**Proof Strategy**: (1) Formalize the Selberg trace formula for $\text{PSL}(2, \mathbb{Z})$ in Lean 4, starting with the geometric side (conjugacy class enumeration). (2) Establish the lattice point counting function as a spectral sum. (3) Bound the error term using known eigenvalue bounds for $\text{PSL}(2, \mathbb{Z})$. This is a multi-year project but each step yields independently interesting results.

**Domain Bridges**: NumberTheory <-> Analysis, Geometry <-> Physics

**Lineage**: Builds on the hyperbolic lattice definition and Möbius iteration results from this cycle, extending toward automorphic forms.

**Ambition**: grand_challenge
