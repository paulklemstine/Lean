# Future Research Directions

## Synthesis

This research cycle established the metric geometry of pitch class set (PCS) spaces over ℤ/12ℤ, proving three isometry theorems (transposition, inversion, complementation), introducing the intervallic fingerprint as a novel transposition invariant, and verifying the hexachordal complementation theorem. The central discovery is that the symmetry group of Hamming chord space is remarkably large — generated by the cyclic group ℤ/12ℤ (transpositions), the involution I (inversion), and set complementation, these isometries form a group that preserves all metric and topological structure.

The most promising cross-domain connection from this cycle is between **coding theory** (Hamming distance, binary codes) and **music theory** (interval vectors, hexachordal theorem). The PCS space 𝒫(ℤ/12ℤ) is literally the Hamming cube {0,1}¹² with cyclic symmetry, connecting it to the Catalog's work on algebraic structures (`Algebra/Berggren.lean`), group actions (`Cryptography/BerggrenGroupoidOrbit.lean`), and metric/topological methods (`Bridges/HammingMetric.lean`). The hexachordal theorem, proved via exhaustive computation, awaits a structural proof via Fourier analysis — this connects to the Catalog's harmonic analysis work (`MachineLearning/FiniteAbelianHarmonicAnalysis`).

The highest breakthrough potential lies in **Direction 1 (Fourier-Analytic Hexachordal Proof)**, because it would establish the formal infrastructure for discrete Fourier analysis on finite abelian groups in Lean, a capability with broad applications beyond music theory — to coding theory, signal processing, and number theory.

### Direction 1: Fourier-Analytic Proof of the Hexachordal Theorem

**Conjecture**: The hexachordal complementation theorem can be proved structurally (without enumeration) via the discrete Fourier transform on ℤ/12ℤ, using the identity R_S(d) = R_{S^c}(d) for |S| = 6 and d ≠ 0, where R_S(d) = |{(a,b) ∈ S² : b - a = d}| is the autocorrelation function. Moreover, this proof generalizes to: for any even n, every (n/2)-element subset of ℤ/nℤ has the same interval content as its complement.

**Test**: Formalize the discrete Fourier transform on ℤ/nℤ in Lean 4 (as a function Finset(ℤ/nℤ) → (ℤ/nℤ → ℂ)), prove Parseval's identity, and derive the hexachordal theorem as a corollary for arbitrary even n. The test succeeds if the proof compiles without `native_decide` or `decide` on the key step.

**Impact**: If successful, this would (a) provide the first machine-verified structural proof of the hexachordal theorem, (b) establish formal DFT infrastructure usable for coding theory and signal processing, and (c) generalize beyond n=12 to arbitrary cyclic groups. If the approach fails, it would identify specific gaps in Mathlib's complex analysis and Fourier theory coverage.

**Catalog References**: `MachineLearning/FiniteAbelianHarmonicAnalysis`, `Bridges/HammingMetric.lean`, `EML/PersistentHarmony/PitchClass.lean`

**Proof Strategy**:
1. Define the DFT on ℤ/nℤ: F̂(k) = Σ_{x ∈ S} ω^{kx} where ω = e^{2πi/n}
2. Prove |F̂_S(k)|² + |F̂_{S^c}(k)|² = |F̂_{ℤ/nℤ}(k)|² - 2Re(F̂_S(k) · conj(F̂_{S^c}(k))) ... actually use F̂_{S^c}(k) = F̂_{ℤ/nℤ}(k) - F̂_S(k), and for k ≠ 0, F̂_{ℤ/nℤ}(k) = 0
3. So |F̂_{S^c}(k)|² = |F̂_S(k)|² for k ≠ 0
4. The autocorrelation R_S is the inverse DFT of |F̂_S|², so R_S = R_{S^c} for all nonzero lags

**Domain Bridges**: Coding theory (Hamming codes) ↔ Music theory (interval vectors) ↔ Signal processing (autocorrelation)

**Lineage**: Builds on this cycle's hexachordal_complementation theorem (verified by native_decide) and fingerprint_T_invariant

**Ambition**: grand_challenge

### Direction 2: Voice-Leading as Optimal Transport on ℤ/12ℤ

**Conjecture**: The voice-leading distance between two equal-cardinality PCS A, B ⊆ ℤ/12ℤ — defined as the minimum total semitone displacement under optimal bijection A → B — satisfies a triangle inequality and is related to the Hamming distance by: d_VL(A, B) ≤ 6 · d_H(A, B) / 2 for equal-cardinality sets. Moreover, the voice-leading distance is computable in O(k³) time via the Hungarian algorithm on the circular distance matrix.

**Test**: Formalize voice-leading distance as an optimal transport problem on ℤ/12ℤ (with circular metric d(a,b) = min(|a-b|, 12-|a-b|)). Prove the triangle inequality for d_VL. Verify the bound d_VL ≤ 6 · |A △ B|/2 computationally for all pairs of trichords. Implement the Hungarian algorithm and verify it produces optimal matchings.

**Impact**: If true, this establishes a precise quantitative relationship between two fundamental musical distances, enabling multi-parameter persistent homology with provably related filtrations. If the bound is tight, it reveals when Hamming distance and voice-leading distance give fundamentally different topological information.

**Catalog References**: `Bridges/CompositionalMusicSpec.lean`, `EML/PersistentHarmony/PitchClass.lean`

**Proof Strategy**:
1. Define circular distance on ℤ/12ℤ: d_circ(a,b) = min((a-b) mod 12, (b-a) mod 12)
2. Define voice-leading distance: d_VL(A,B) = min over bijections σ: A → B of Σ d_circ(a, σ(a))
3. Prove triangle inequality using the composition of bijections
4. Prove the Hamming bound by noting each disagreement contributes at most 6 to d_VL

**Domain Bridges**: Optimal transport theory ↔ Music theory (voice leading) ↔ Computational geometry

**Lineage**: Builds on T_isometry (transposition preserves Hamming distance) and the metric space structure established this cycle

**Ambition**: extension

### Direction 3: Persistent Homology of Diatonic Subspaces

**Conjecture**: The Vietoris-Rips persistent homology of the set of all diatonic triads (the seven triads of a major scale) in Hamming chord space exhibits a persistent H₁ cycle that is absent in the persistent homology of seven randomly chosen triads. Specifically, the cycle persists from ε = 2 to ε = 4 (persistence = 2), reflecting the circular structure of diatonic harmony (I → IV → vii° → iii → vi → ii → V → I).

**Test**: Compute the Vietoris-Rips filtration of the seven diatonic triads of C major ({C,E,G}, {D,F,A}, {E,G,B}, {F,A,C}, {G,B,D}, {A,C,E}, {B,D,F}) using Hamming distance. Compute H₁ persistence intervals. Compare with 1000 random samples of 7 triads from the 12 major + 12 minor triads. The conjecture is confirmed if the diatonic H₁ persistence exceeds the 95th percentile of the random distribution.

**Impact**: If true, this provides a topological signature that distinguishes diatonic harmony from arbitrary chord collections, with implications for music information retrieval and computational musicology. If false, it reveals that diatonic structure is metrically indistinguishable from random structure at the level of H₁, suggesting that higher-order invariants (H₂, etc.) or different metrics are needed.

**Catalog References**: `EML/PersistentHarmony/PitchClass.lean`, `Bridges/HammingMetric.lean`

**Proof Strategy**:
1. Implement Vietoris-Rips complex construction from a distance matrix
2. Implement persistent homology computation (boundary matrix reduction)
3. Compute for diatonic triads and compare with random baseline
4. If the H₁ feature is present, formalize the cycle as a concrete chain in Lean

**Domain Bridges**: Algebraic topology (persistent homology) ↔ Music theory (diatonic harmony) ↔ Statistics (random baseline comparison)

**Lineage**: Builds on hammingDist_triangle (metric space structure), ripsEdge_monotone (filtration monotonicity) from PitchClass.lean, and the isometry theorems from this cycle

### Direction 4: Z-Relation Classification via Intervallic Fingerprints

**Conjecture**: Two PCS S, S' ⊆ ℤ/12ℤ are *Z-related* if they share the same interval-class vector but are not related by transposition or inversion. The intervallic fingerprint (directed interval multiset) can distinguish some but not all Z-related pairs. Specifically, Z-related pairs that differ in their intervallic fingerprints have different directed interval distributions, providing a finer invariant than the classical interval vector. Conjecture: every Z-related pair of PCS in ℤ/12ℤ can be distinguished by some function of the form f(S) = Σ_{(a,b) ∈ S², a≠b} g(b-a) for an appropriate weight function g: ℤ/12ℤ → ℝ.

**Test**: Enumerate all Z-related pairs in ℤ/12ℤ (there are known to be exactly 23 Z-related pairs of set classes). For each pair, compute the intervallic fingerprint and check whether it distinguishes them. If some pairs remain indistinguishable, search for weight functions g that separate them.

**Impact**: If the fingerprint distinguishes all Z-related pairs, it provides a strictly finer invariant than the interval vector, with applications to music analysis. If some pairs remain indistinguishable, it reveals fundamental limits of pairwise interval information and motivates higher-order invariants (e.g., triplewise interval functions).

**Catalog References**: `EML/PersistentHarmony/PitchClass.lean`, `Speculative/AutoResearch/ChromaticTopology.lean`

**Proof Strategy**:
1. Enumerate all T/I equivalence classes of PCS in ℤ/12ℤ
2. Group classes by interval vector to find Z-related pairs
3. For each pair, compute the directed fingerprint
4. If fingerprints differ, prove this formally; if not, search for separating invariants

**Domain Bridges**: Combinatorics (set classification) ↔ Music theory (Z-relation) ↔ Representation theory (Fourier coefficients as invariants)

**Lineage**: Builds on fingerprint_T_invariant and intervalVector definitions from this cycle

**Ambition**: extension

### Direction 5: Tropical Geometry of Chord Progressions

**Conjecture**: The space of chord progressions (sequences of PCS) can be endowed with a tropical metric by replacing the standard Hamming distance with a min-plus algebra framework. Specifically, define the *tropical chord distance* d_trop(A,B) = min_{t ∈ ℤ/12ℤ} d_H(A, T_t(B)), the minimum Hamming distance over all transpositions of B. This quotient metric on 𝒫/ℤ₁₂ satisfies the triangle inequality and captures "closeness up to transposition." The persistent homology of this quotient metric space has non-trivial H₁ features corresponding to transposition cycles.

**Test**: Compute d_trop for all pairs of trichord classes (there are 12 classes up to transposition). Construct the Vietoris-Rips filtration and compute persistent homology. Check if the H₁ group is non-trivial at any filtration parameter.

**Impact**: If successful, this bridges tropical geometry (min-plus algebra) with music theory, connecting to the Catalog's extensive tropical mathematics coverage. The quotient metric captures "harmonic closeness" in a transposition-invariant way, which is musically more natural than the raw Hamming distance. If the tropical approach yields no new topological features, it suggests that transposition equivalence is too coarse a quotient.

**Catalog References**: `Tropical/`, `Bridges/MinPlusHarmonicAnalysis.lean`, `EML/PersistentHarmony/PitchClass.lean`

**Proof Strategy**:
1. Define d_trop as a quotient metric
2. Prove triangle inequality for d_trop using triangle inequality for d_H and properties of min
3. Enumerate the quotient space and compute the full distance matrix
4. Apply persistent homology algorithms to the finite quotient space

**Domain Bridges**: Tropical geometry (min-plus algebra) ↔ Music theory (transposition equivalence) ↔ Metric geometry (quotient metrics)

**Lineage**: Builds on T_isometry and the Hamming metric infrastructure from this cycle, plus the Catalog's tropical geometry foundations

**Ambition**: extension
