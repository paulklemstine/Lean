# Future Directions

## Synthesis

This cycle established the **differential algebra structure** of the EML class: closure under addition, multiplication, composition, differentiation, and pointwise inversion. The central discovery is the *self-referential differentiation property* — `∂/∂x eml(x,y) = eml(x,1)` — which means the EML primitive maps to a specialization of itself under differentiation. This structural self-reference is the deep reason why the EML class is differentially closed.

The most promising cross-domain connection is the **Lie subalgebra structure**: since EML is closed under both multiplication and differentiation, it is automatically closed under the Lie bracket of vector fields. This connects the algebraic EML closure operator (from the Catalog's `EML/ClosureOperator.lean`) to differential geometry and the theory of symmetry groups. The Wronskian of the generators (exp and log) was computed and shown to be nonzero at x=1, establishing linear independence.

The highest breakthrough potential lies in **Direction 1** (Complex EML Differential Algebra), because extending to ℂ would connect EML to Riemann surfaces, monodromy, and the full power of complex analysis — potentially leading to a unified theory of special functions as boundary phenomena of the complex EML class.

### Direction 1: Complex EML Differential Algebra and Monodromy

**Conjecture**: The complex EML class — functions built from `eml(z, w) = exp(z) - Log(w)` over ℂ using arithmetic and composition — forms a differential field that is *not* algebraically closed. Specifically, there exist polynomial equations P(y, eml-expressions) = 0 whose solutions require analytic continuation around branch points, producing monodromy that cannot be expressed as a single-valued EML function.

**Test**: Define `eml_C(z, w) = exp(z) - Log(w)` using the principal branch of complex logarithm. Prove:
1. The complex EML class is closed under ∂/∂z (same self-referential property holds).
2. The equation y² = eml_C(z, 1) = exp(z) has solution y = exp(z/2), which IS in the EML class.
3. The equation y² = eml_C(0, z) = 1 - Log(z) has solutions involving √(1 - Log(z)), which are NOT single-valued and hence NOT in the single-valued EML class.

**Impact**: If true, this identifies the precise boundary between the EML differential field and its algebraic closure, connecting to Riemann surface theory. The monodromy group of the algebraic closure would be a new invariant of the EML class. If false (i.e., if the complex EML class IS algebraically closed), this would be a remarkable structural result with implications for differential Galois theory.

**Catalog References**: `EML/ClosureOperator.lean` (closure axioms), `EML/ExtendedTheory.lean` (basic identities), `EML/DiffAlgebra.lean` (differentiation closure from this cycle)

**Proof Strategy**: 
1. Define complex EML using Mathlib's `Complex.exp` and `Complex.log`
2. Prove self-referential derivative: ∂/∂z eml_C(z, w) = exp(z) = eml_C(z, 1)
3. For the non-closure, show that √(1 - Log(z)) has nontrivial monodromy around z=0 by computing the monodromy matrix explicitly
4. Use the fact that single-valued EML functions have trivial monodromy to derive a contradiction

**Domain Bridges**: Complex Analysis ↔ Differential Algebra ↔ Algebraic Geometry (Riemann surfaces)

**Lineage**: Builds on `EML/DiffAlgebra.lean` (this cycle's differentiation closure) and extends the real EML framework to ℂ.

**Ambition**: grand_challenge

### Direction 2: EML Differential Galois Groups

**Conjecture**: For the linear ODE y'' - y = 0 over the EML differential field, the differential Galois group is GL(1, ℝ) (diagonal matrices), reflecting the fact that both solutions (exp(x) and exp(-x)) are in the EML class. For y'' + y = 0, the Galois group is SO(2, ℝ) (rotation matrices), reflecting the fact that sin and cos are NOT individually in the EML class but sin²+cos² = 1 IS.

**Test**: 
1. Prove that exp(x) and exp(-x) are in EMLFunctions (immediate from the term algebra).
2. Prove that sin(x) is NOT in EMLFunctions (this requires showing no EML term evaluates to sin).
3. Compute the Wronskian W(exp, exp(-x)) and show it equals -2 (constant, nonzero).
4. Show that the fundamental matrix [exp, exp(-x); exp, -exp(-x)] has the claimed Galois group.

**Impact**: If confirmed, this provides the first explicit computation of differential Galois groups using the EML framework, bridging differential algebra with group theory. The contrast between the solvable ODE y''-y=0 (solutions in EML) and y''+y=0 (solutions outside EML) would illuminate exactly what makes some differential equations "elementary" and others not.

**Catalog References**: `EML/DiffAlgebra.lean` (differential field structure), `EML/ClosureOperator.lean` (closure as Moore family)

**Proof Strategy**:
1. Define the differential Galois group as the automorphism group of the Picard-Vessiot extension
2. For y''-y=0, the Picard-Vessiot extension is generated by exp(x), which is already in EML, so the Galois group is trivial over EML
3. For y''+y=0, sin and cos generate a proper extension; the Galois group acts by rotation
4. The key lemma: sin(x) is not an EML function. Proof: sin is periodic while all nonconstant EML functions grow without bound (by the diagonal dominance theorem from ExtendedTheory.lean)

**Domain Bridges**: Differential Algebra ↔ Group Theory ↔ ODE Theory

**Lineage**: Extends the differential field structure from this cycle to study solutions of ODEs over the EML field.

**Ambition**: grand_challenge

### Direction 3: Syntactic Complexity of EML Differentiation

**Conjecture**: The syntactic differentiation operator `sdiff : EMLTerm → EMLTerm` satisfies `size(sdiff(t)) ≤ 3 · size(t)`, and this bound is tight. Moreover, for n-fold differentiation, `size(sdiff^n(t)) = Θ(3ⁿ · size(t))` — differentiation has exponential expression swell.

**Test**: 
1. Define `EMLTerm.size` recursively (count all nodes).
2. Prove `size(sdiff t) ≤ 3 * size(t)` by structural induction.
3. Find a specific term t₀ where `size(sdiff t₀) ≥ 2 * size(t₀)` (e.g., t₀ = mul var var).
4. Compute `size(sdiff^n (mul var var))` for n = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 using `#eval`.
5. Prove the exponential lower bound `size(sdiff^n t₀) ≥ 2ⁿ` for the specific t₀.

**Impact**: This quantifies the computational cost of symbolic differentiation in the EML framework. The exponential swell is well-known in computer algebra (it's why automatic differentiation is preferred over symbolic differentiation for large expressions), but proving it formally for the EML term algebra would be new. A tight bound would inform compiler optimization strategies for EML-based neural architectures.

**Catalog References**: `EML/Defs.lean` (EMLExpr.esize), `EML/DiffAlgebra.lean` (EMLTerm.sdiff), `Bridges/UniversalApproxComplexity.lean` (eml_composition_size_bound)

**Proof Strategy**:
1. Induction on EMLTerm structure for the upper bound
2. Explicit computation for the lower bound (mul case has the worst blowup because product rule doubles the tree)
3. For the iterated bound, induction on n using the single-step bound

**Domain Bridges**: Computational Complexity ↔ Differential Algebra ↔ Compiler Theory

**Lineage**: Extends the size analysis from `eml_composition_size_bound` to the new differentiation operator.

**Ambition**: extension

### Direction 4: EML Integration Decidability via Risch-like Algorithm

**Conjecture**: There exists a decidable predicate `IsEMLIntegrable : EMLTerm → Prop` such that `IsEMLIntegrable t` holds if and only if there exists an EMLTerm s with `sdiff s = t` (up to semantic equivalence). Moreover, this predicate can be computed by a Risch-like algorithm operating on the syntactic structure of EML terms.

**Test**:
1. Define `IsEMLIntegrable t := ∃ s : EMLTerm, ∀ x, (sdiff s).eval x = t.eval x`
2. Prove `IsEMLIntegrable expT` (since sdiff expT = expT, the antiderivative is expT itself)
3. Prove `IsEMLIntegrable (inv var)` (since sdiff logT = inv var, the antiderivative is logT)
4. State `¬ IsEMLIntegrable (comp expT (neg (mul var var)))` (Gaussian has no elementary antiderivative)
5. Attempt to prove the Gaussian non-integrability by reduction to Liouville's theorem

**Impact**: A decision procedure for EML integrability would be a constructive version of the Risch algorithm, specialized to the EML term algebra. This would connect symbolic computation with differential algebra in a formally verified setting. Even partial results (decidability for specific subclasses) would advance the state of verified computer algebra.

**Catalog References**: `EML/DiffAlgebra.lean` (sdiff), `EML/Defs.lean` (UExpr and EMLExpr evaluation)

**Proof Strategy**:
1. For simple cases (polynomials, exponentials), construct antiderivatives explicitly
2. For the Gaussian, formalize the Liouville-Rosenlicht theorem: if exp(-x²) had an elementary antiderivative, it would have the form g₀ + Σ cᵢ log(gᵢ) where gᵢ are elementary. Show this leads to a contradiction via degree analysis.
3. The full decidability result requires formalizing the Risch algorithm's structural recursion on EML terms

**Domain Bridges**: Computer Algebra ↔ Differential Algebra ↔ Decidability Theory

**Lineage**: Extends the differentiation closure (this cycle) to the question of its adjoint operation (integration).

**Ambition**: grand_challenge

### Direction 5: Tropical EML Differential Algebra

**Conjecture**: The "tropical EML" function `eml_trop(x, y) = max(x, -y)` (the tropicalization of exp(x) - log(y)) generates a tropical differential semiring under the tropical operations max and +. The tropical derivative d_trop/dx of eml_trop(x, y) = x (the identity, since max(x, -y) is piecewise linear with slope 1 in x). This tropical differential structure is the "skeleton" of the real EML differential algebra.

**Test**:
1. Define `eml_trop(x, y) = max(x, -y)` over ℝ ∪ {-∞}
2. Prove the tropical chain rule: d_trop(f ∘ g) = (d_trop f ∘ g) + d_trop g (where + is tropical multiplication)
3. Show that the tropical Wronskian W_trop(id, neg) = max(x + 0, 0 + (-x)) = max(x, -x) = |x|
4. Prove that tropical EML closure is tropically differentially closed
5. Show that the real EML differentiation "degenerates" to tropical differentiation under the valuation map x ↦ log|x|

**Impact**: If confirmed, this establishes a degeneration/tropicalization map from the real EML differential algebra to the tropical one, providing a combinatorial shadow of the analytic theory. This would connect to tropical geometry, Newton polygons, and the combinatorial approach to differential equations.

**Catalog References**: `Cryptography/TropicalMinPlusOWF.lean` (tropical orbit closure), `EML/EMLTropicalSemiring.lean`, `EML/DiffAlgebra.lean` (real EML differentiation)

**Proof Strategy**:
1. Define tropical operations using `max` and `+` on `WithBot ℝ` (ℝ ∪ {-∞})
2. Tropical differentiation is piecewise-linear slope computation
3. The degeneration map uses `Real.log ∘ abs` to send real functions to their tropical counterparts
4. Prove that this map intertwines real differentiation (via L'Hôpital-type arguments) with tropical differentiation

**Domain Bridges**: Tropical Geometry ↔ Differential Algebra ↔ Valuation Theory

**Lineage**: Bridges the EML differential algebra (this cycle) with the tropical semiring theory (`EML/EMLTropicalSemiring.lean`).

**Ambition**: extension
