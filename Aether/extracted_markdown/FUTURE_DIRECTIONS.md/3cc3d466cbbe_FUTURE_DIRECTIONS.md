# Future Directions — The symmetry group of the Hodge–Deligne E-polynomial

## Synthesis of this cycle

The catalog file `Catalog/Bridges/HodgeEPolynomial.lean` had isolated the *mirror*
involution `(p,q) ↦ (n-p, q)` of a `HodgeDiamond` and shown that it casts a shadow on the
two-variable Hodge–Deligne polynomial `E(X; u, v) = Σ_{p,q} (-1)^{p+q} h^{p,q} uᵖ vᵍ` as the
functional equation `E(mirror X; u, v) = (-1)ⁿ uⁿ E(X; 1/u, v)`, recovering the numerical
mirror sign `χ(mirror X) = (-1)ⁿ χ(X)` at `u = v = 1`.

The new file `Catalog/Bridges/HodgeEPolynomialSymmetries.lean` completes that picture by
exhibiting the **entire group of lattice involutions** acting on the index square
`{0,…,n}²` and their exact algebraic images under `E`:

* `transpose` `(p,q) ↦ (q,p)` (Hodge symmetry / complex conjugation) is the **variable swap**
  `E(transpose X; u, v) = E(X; v, u)` — `epoly_transpose`;
* `qmirror` `(p,q) ↦ (p, n-q)` is the **second mirror**
  `E(qmirror X; u, v) = (-1)ⁿ vⁿ E(X; u, 1/v)` — `epoly_qmirror_functional_equation`;
* the **Serre reflection** `(p,q) ↦ (n-p, n-q)` is the *composite* `mirror ∘ qmirror`
  — `serre_reflection_h`, with `mirror_qmirror_comm` showing the two factors commute;
* `qmirror = transpose ∘ mirror ∘ transpose` holds **definitionally** (`rfl`), and
  `transpose ∘ transpose = id` (`transpose_transpose`).

At `u = v = 1` each functional equation degenerates to a sign rule
(`eulerChar_transpose`, `eulerChar_qmirror_sign`), and a Hodge-symmetry hypothesis makes the
polynomial symmetric in its two variables (`epoly_symm_of_hodgeSymmetric`). A computational
section verifies all of this on the K3-surface diamond (`χ = 24`, with all reflections
preserving it because `n = 2` is even).

## Results summary

All theorems below are proved with `sorry = 0` and only the standard axioms.

| Result | Statement |
|---|---|
| `epoly_transpose` | `E(transpose X; u, v) = E(X; v, u)` |
| `eulerChar_transpose` | `χ(transpose X) = χ(X)` |
| `epoly_qmirror_functional_equation` | `E(qmirror X; u, v) = (-1)ⁿ vⁿ E(X; u, 1/v)` |
| `eulerChar_qmirror_sign` | `χ(qmirror X) = (-1)ⁿ χ(X)` |
| `serre_reflection_h` / `mirror_qmirror_comm` | `mirror ∘ qmirror` is the Serre reflection, and commutes |
| `qmirror_eq_transpose_mirror_transpose` | `qmirror = transpose ∘ mirror ∘ transpose` (definitional) |
| `epoly_symm_of_hodgeSymmetric` | Hodge symmetry ⟹ `E(X; u, v) = E(X; v, u)` |

Together these say: the abstract group `(ℤ/2)²` (generated by the variable swap and the
index reflection) acts on Hodge diamonds, and `E` is an **exact intertwiner** carrying that
action to an explicit action on `K[u, v, u⁻¹, v⁻¹]`.

## Direction 1 — Künneth multiplicativity: `E` is a semiring homomorphism

**Conjecture.** Define the product diamond `X × Y` by `(X×Y).n = X.n + Y.n` and the
convolution `h^{p,q}_{X×Y} = Σ_{p₁+p₂=p, q₁+q₂=q} h^{p₁,q₁}_X · h^{p₂,q₂}_Y` (with `h`
genuinely supported on `{0,…,n}²`). Then `E(X × Y; u, v) = E(X; u, v) · E(Y; u, v)`, and in
particular `χ(X × Y) = χ(X) · χ(Y)`.

**The key insight is** that the E-polynomial is not merely an invariant but a *ring map*
from the Grothendieck-style semiring of Hodge diamonds (disjoint union `+`, product `×`)
into `K[u,v]`: the reflections proved this cycle are exactly the units/automorphisms of the
target, so multiplicativity is the one remaining structural axiom needed to upgrade "`E` is
a symmetry-equivariant invariant" to "`E` is a *homomorphism* respecting symmetries."

**Why now?** The reflection toolkit (`Finset.sum_bij`, parity bookkeeping, support
discipline) is already in place, and the only genuinely new ingredient is a Cauchy-product
reindexing (`Finset.sum_mul_sum` + `Finset.Nat.sum_antidiagonal`), which Mathlib supports
directly. This is the natural next lemma and it makes the bridge a *functor*.

## Direction 2 — Package the four reflections as a genuine `(ℤ/2)²`-action

**Conjecture.** The maps `{id, mirror, qmirror, serre}` (on the *support-normalized*
diamonds where `h` vanishes off `{0,…,n}²`) form a group isomorphic to `(ℤ/2)²`, and `E`
induces a group homomorphism into the polynomial automorphism group generated by
`u ↦ 1/u`, `v ↦ 1/v` (with the `(-1)ⁿ uⁿ`, `(-1)ⁿ vⁿ` cocycle prefactors).

**The key insight is** that the prefactors `(-1)ⁿ uⁿ` are a **1-cocycle**, not noise: the
group law `mirror ∘ qmirror = serre` forces the prefactors to multiply consistently
(`(-1)ⁿuⁿ · (-1)ⁿvⁿ = (uv)ⁿ`), which is exactly the Serre prefactor proved in the parent
file. Making this a Lean `MulAction` / `MonoidHom` turns scattered functional equations into
one equivariance statement.

**Why now?** `transpose_transpose`, `mirror_qmirror_comm`, and
`qmirror_eq_transpose_mirror_transpose` already supply every group relation needed; only the
"support-normalized" quotient (to kill the padding obstruction noted in the Lab Notebook) is
missing, and that is a one-definition fix (`def normalize : HodgeDiamond → HodgeDiamond`).

## Direction 3 — Palindromy: Serre-dual diamonds give reciprocal (palindromic) E-polynomials

**Conjecture.** If `X` is Serre-dual (`SerreDual`, from the parent file) then the
single-variable specialization `P_X(t) := E(X; t, t)` is **palindromic of degree `2n`**:
`t^{2n} P_X(1/t) = P_X(t)`, i.e. its coefficient vector reads the same forwards and
backwards. Equivalently the Poincaré polynomial of a compact Kähler manifold is palindromic
(Poincaré duality).

**The key insight is** that the parent's Serre functional equation
`E(X; u, v) = (uv)ⁿ E(X; 1/u, 1/v)` becomes, on the diagonal `u = v = t`, precisely the
classical palindromy relation `t^{2n} P(1/t) = P(t)` — so a deep duality theorem collapses
to a one-line corollary of work already done, and connects the bridge to the well-studied
theory of reciprocal/self-inversive polynomials.

**Why now?** The diagonal substitution `u = v = t` needs nothing beyond `epoly_serre_*`,
which is already proved; the only new content is recognizing `(uv)ⁿ|_{u=v=t} = t^{2n}` and
defining `P_X`. It is the cleanest possible cross-link between the bridge and Mathlib's
polynomial-reciprocity API (`Polynomial.reverse`, `Polynomial.IsSelfInversive`).

## Direction 4 — A reflection bridge to strong divisibility sequences

**Conjecture.** The very same "reflection engine" `j ↦ n - j` that governs the E-polynomial
governs the *companion reciprocal polynomials* of strong divisibility sequences in the
catalog's number-theory thread (e.g. the Fibonacci/Lucas world of
`Catalog/Bridges/StrongDivisibilitySequences.lean` and `Nat.fib_gcd`). Concretely: the
generating polynomial `Σ_{k≤n} F_k t^k` and its reflection satisfy a functional equation of
exactly the form proved here, and the resulting palindromy obstructs primitive-divisor
collisions.

**The key insight is** that `Finset.sum_range_reflect` is *domain-agnostic*: the parity
shift `(-1)^{n-j} = (-1)ⁿ(-1)^j` that produces the Hodge mirror sign is the identical
algebraic move underlying reciprocal polynomials of integer recurrences — so the geometry
bridge and the number-theory bridge are two faces of one combinatorial lemma.

**Why now?** Both threads already live in the catalog and both already rely on
reflection/`gcd` identities; unifying them needs only that we state the recurrence's
generating polynomial and apply the *exact same* `sum_bij` reflection proof, giving a
genuine cross-domain (geometry ↔ number theory) theorem at low marginal cost.

## Direction 5 — Stringy/orbifold E-functions and a numerical McKay shadow

**Conjecture.** Extend `HodgeDiamond` to `OrbifoldDiamond` carrying age-graded (rational)
shifts, define the stringy E-function `E_st(X; u, v) = Σ (uv)^{age} E(component)`, and prove
that the mirror/transpose reflections lift to `E_st`, yielding a numerical
`χ_st(mirror X) = (-1)ⁿ χ_st(X)` that matches the orbifold Euler number from the McKay
correspondence on the diagonal.

**The key insight is** that the stringy E-function is a *weighted sum of ordinary
E-polynomials*, so equivariance under the reflection group is inherited term-by-term from
`epoly_mirror_functional_equation` and `epoly_qmirror_functional_equation` — the hard
geometry (motivic integration) is invisible at the level of these functional equations.

**Why now?** With the full reflection group now formalized, the only new datum is the
rational age-grading (a `Finset (ℚ × …)` bookkeeping layer); the symmetry proofs port
verbatim. This is the most ambitious but also the most clearly *falsifiable* direction: a
single mismatched sign on a weighted-projective-space example would refute it immediately.
