# Future Directions: Tropical Low-Rank Cryptanalysis

## Overview

The tropical low-rank attack principle—that all powers of a rank-*r* matrix are governed by an *r* × *r* core—opens multiple breakthrough research directions across cryptography, algebra, complexity theory, and dynamical systems.

---

## Direction 1: Eventual Periodicity with Explicit Bounds

### Theorem Statement
Every *r* × *r* tropical matrix over a bounded entry range generates a finite semigroup, so its powers are eventually periodic. Prove explicit bounds on the pre-period length *N* and period *p* in terms of *r* and the entry range *B*.

### Proposed Lean Type Signature
```lean
theorem tropical_eventual_periodicity_bounded
    {r : ℕ} (H : Matrix (Fin r) (Fin r) (Tropical (WithTop ℤ)))
    (hB : ∀ i j, ∃ b : ℤ, H i j = Tropical.trop (some b) ∧ |b| ≤ B) :
    ∃ N p : ℕ, 0 < p ∧ p ≤ (2 * B + 1) ^ (r * r) ∧
      ∀ k ≥ N, H ^ (k + p) = H ^ k
```

### Proof Strategy
1. Model the set of *r* × *r* matrices with entries in {−B, …, B, +∞} as a finite set.
2. The semigroup generated by *H* under tropical multiplication is a subsemigroup of this finite set.
3. By the pigeonhole principle, the sequence H, H², H³, ... must eventually repeat.
4. The period divides the cardinality of the finite semigroup, which is at most $(2B+1+1)^{r^2}$.
5. Combine with our periodicity inheritance theorem to transfer bounds to the full matrix.

### Cross-Domain Significance
- **Cryptography**: Explicit period bounds give an upper limit on the search space for the hidden exponent.
- **Automata theory**: Connects to the Burnside problem for tropical matrix semigroups.
- **Discrete event systems**: Provides convergence guarantees for max-plus system evolution.

## Direction 2: Formal Cryptanalytic Key Recovery Theorem

### Theorem Statement
Given a tropical key-exchange protocol where the generator *G* has factorization rank *r*, formalize a complete key-recovery reduction: an algorithm that, given the factorization, recovers the hidden exponent in time polynomial in *r* and the period of the core.

### Proposed Lean Type Signature
```lean
theorem hidden_exponent_recovery
    {n r : ℕ}
    (G : TropMat n n)
    (U : TropMat n r) (V : TropMat r n)
    (hG : U * V = G)
    (P : TropMat n n) (a : ℕ) (ha : 1 ≤ a)
    (hP : G ^ a = P) :
    ∃ e : ℕ, e = a - 1 ∧
      U * (V * U) ^ e * V = P
```

### Proof Strategy
1. Substitute the factorization: P = G^a = U * (VU)^(a-1) * V.
2. Set e = a - 1 and verify the equality directly.
3. For the algorithmic version, iterate over candidate exponents e and check U * H^e * V = P.
4. Bound the search space using eventual periodicity bounds from Direction 1.

### Cross-Domain Significance
- **Cryptography**: Provides the first formally verified attack on tropical key-exchange.
- **Complexity theory**: Reduction shows the tropical DLP is fixed-parameter tractable in rank.
- **Formal methods**: Demonstrates that cryptanalytic reductions can be machine-verified.

## Direction 3: Tropical Spectral Radius Compression

### Theorem Statement
The **tropical spectral radius** (critical graph cycle mean) of *G* equals that of the core *H*, up to a bounded correction from the sandwich factors *U* and *V*.

### Proposed Lean Type Signature
```lean
theorem tropical_spectral_radius_compression
    {n r : ℕ}
    (U : TropMat n r) (V : TropMat r n)
    (λ_G : WithTop ℤ) (λ_H : WithTop ℤ)
    (hG : IsTropicalEigenvalue (U * V) λ_G)
    (hH : IsTropicalEigenvalue (V * U) λ_H) :
    λ_G = λ_H
```

### Proof Strategy
1. Define the tropical eigenvalue as the minimum cycle mean: λ = min over cycles c of (weight(c) / length(c)).
2. Show that cycles in G correspond bijectively to cycles in H (via the factorization), up to entry/exit costs that cancel in the mean.
3. Use the compression theorem to relate powers: the growth rate of (G^a)_{ii} equals that of (H^{a-1}) sandwiched, and the sandwich contributes a bounded additive term that vanishes in the limit.

### Cross-Domain Significance
- **Control theory**: The tropical eigenvalue determines the throughput of a timed event system; compression shows that throughput is determined by the bottleneck core.
- **Graph theory**: Connects tropical rank to the cycle structure of weighted digraphs.
- **Optimization**: Links to the max-plus spectral theory of Gaubert, providing a rank-parameterized version.

## Direction 4: Decidability of Tropical Rank

### Theorem Statement
The problem of determining whether a tropical matrix has factorization rank at most *r* is decidable (though potentially of high complexity).

### Proposed Lean Type Signature
```lean
theorem tropical_rank_decidable
    {n : ℕ} (r : ℕ) :
    DecidablePred (fun G : TropMat n n => HasTropFactRank G r)
```

### Proof Strategy
1. For matrices with entries in a finite range, the problem reduces to searching over all U ∈ T^{n×r} and V ∈ T^{r×n} with bounded entries.
2. Show that if G = UV, then entries of U and V can be bounded in terms of entries of G.
3. This gives a finite search space, making the problem decidable.
4. For unbounded entries, use a completeness argument: tropical rank is characterized by the non-existence of certain tropical linear dependences, which is decidable over ordered fields with decidable theory.

### Cross-Domain Significance
- **Computational algebra**: Fills a gap in the decidability landscape of tropical linear algebra.
- **Cryptography**: Enables automated security auditing of tropical protocol parameters.
- **Complexity theory**: The exact complexity class (NP-complete, in P, etc.) is a major open question.

## Direction 5: Low-Rank Structure in Random Tropical Matrices

### Theorem Statement
A random *n* × *n* tropical matrix with i.i.d. entries from {0, 1, ..., B} has tropical factorization rank *n* with high probability as *n* → ∞ (for fixed *B*).

### Proposed Lean Type Signature
```lean
theorem random_tropical_full_rank
    {n B : ℕ} (hB : 0 < B) :
    ∀ ε > 0, ∃ N : ℕ, ∀ n ≥ N,
      Prob[HasTropFactRank (random_tropical_matrix n B) (n - 1)] < ε
```

### Proof Strategy
1. Characterize rank deficiency via tropical determinants: a matrix has rank < n iff its tropical determinant is "achieved" by multiple permutations.
2. For random entries, the probability that two permutations achieve the same minimum decreases exponentially in n.
3. Use a union bound over the (n!)² pairs of permutations.
4. This shows that random tropical matrices are generically full-rank, implying the low-rank attack targets a measure-zero subset.

### Cross-Domain Significance
- **Cryptography**: Provides guidance on secure parameter selection—random matrices are safe, but structured ones may not be.
- **Probability**: Connects to the theory of random optimization problems (assignment problem).
- **Algebraic geometry**: Relates to the geometry of the tropical Grassmannian and the distribution of tropical rank.

## Research Team Directive

Each direction above should be pursued by a dedicated team with:
1. **Hypothesis**: The precise conjecture to prove or disprove.
2. **Computational validation**: Python/SageMath experiments to test the conjecture on examples before formalization.
3. **Formalization plan**: A breakdown into 5-10 helper lemmas, each independently provable.
4. **Cross-domain audit**: At least two experts from different fields (e.g., one algebraist and one cryptographer) review each result.
5. **Iteration cycle**: Weekly cycles of conjecture → experiment → formalize → review.

Priority ordering: Direction 2 (immediate cryptanalytic impact) > Direction 1 (enables quantitative bounds) > Direction 3 (deepest mathematical content) > Direction 4 (foundational) > Direction 5 (probabilistic, hardest).
