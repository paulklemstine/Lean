# Future Directions: Galois Theory of Cellular Automata

## Synthesis

This research cycle established the formal group-theoretic foundation for studying reversible cellular automata: the **reversibility subgroup** of the symmetric group, consisting of all shift-equivariant permutations of the configuration space. We proved four core theorems (group closure, locality implies equivariance, proper subgroup, shift-complement commutativity) and discovered computationally that the 6 universally reversible elementary CA rules generate a group of order 6 (isomorphic to $S_3$), which is much smaller than the centralizer of the shift (order 36 for period 3).

The most promising cross-domain connection is the **necklace-centralizer bridge**: the size of the reversibility group is determined by the cycle structure of the shift operator, which in turn is governed by the combinatorics of binary necklaces and the Euler totient function. This creates a direct link between CA reversibility (dynamical systems/computation) and analytic number theory. The period-dependent reversibility phenomenon — where rules like 45 and 105 are reversible only for specific periods related to their number-theoretic properties — is the richest unexplored territory.

The highest breakthrough potential lies in Direction 1 (characterizing which rules are reversible for which periods via de Bruijn graph theory), as it could yield a complete classification connecting CA dynamics to algebraic graph theory.

### Direction 1: De Bruijn Graph Characterization of Period-Dependent Reversibility

**Conjecture**: An elementary CA rule $R$ is reversible on $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ if and only if the $n$-th power of its de Bruijn transition matrix has determinant $\pm 1$. Equivalently, $R$ is reversible on period $n$ iff all eigenvalues of the de Bruijn matrix are $n$-th roots of unity (of modulus 1).

**Background**: Each elementary CA rule $R$ defines a directed graph on 4 vertices (the 2-bit overlaps $\{00, 01, 10, 11\}$), called the de Bruijn graph. The edge from $ab$ to $bc$ exists and is labeled $R(a,b,c)$. A configuration of period $n$ corresponds to a closed walk of length $n$ in this graph. The global map is reversible on period $n$ iff the induced map on closed walks of length $n$ is bijective.

**Test**: Compute the de Bruijn matrices for all 256 elementary CA rules. For each rule, compute the eigenvalues of the matrix and check whether reversibility on period $n$ correlates with all eigenvalues being $n$-th roots of unity. Specifically, test rules 45, 105, and 27 which show non-trivial period dependence.

**Impact**: A complete spectral characterization would reduce the reversibility question to linear algebra over finite fields, making it decidable in polynomial time for any given $(R, n)$ pair and potentially yielding closed-form conditions for "reversible for all $n$."

**Catalog References**: `Geometry/CellularAutomataGalois.lean` (reversibility subgroup definition and group structure), `Algebra/Advanced.lean` (iterated maps)

**Proof Strategy**: (1) Formalize de Bruijn graphs for CA rules. (2) Prove that closed walks correspond to periodic configurations. (3) Show the global map on period-$n$ configurations is the map on closed walks of length $n$. (4) Connect bijectivity of this map to the matrix determinant. Key lemma: the number of closed walks of length $n$ with a given output label sequence equals a matrix permanent/determinant.

**Domain Bridges**: Cellular automata dynamics ↔ Algebraic graph theory ↔ Linear algebra over finite fields

**Lineage**: Builds on the period-dependent reversibility computations from this cycle and the de Bruijn graph theory of Hedlund (1969).

**Ambition**: grand_challenge

### Direction 2: Higher-Radius Reversibility and Wreath Product Structure

**Conjecture**: For binary CAs of radius $r$ on $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ with $n > 2r$, the reversibility subgroup $\mathcal{R}_n^{(r)}$ generated by all reversible radius-$r$ CAs satisfies $\mathcal{R}_n^{(1)} \subsetneq \mathcal{R}_n^{(2)} \subsetneq \cdots$, and $\bigcup_r \mathcal{R}_n^{(r)}$ equals the full centralizer of the shift.

**Background**: This cycle showed that radius-1 CAs generate only $S_3$ (order 6) inside the centralizer (order 36) for period 3. Higher-radius rules should access more of the centralizer. The conjecture states that every shift-equivariant permutation can be realized as a composition of finite-radius CA rules — a sort of "density" theorem for local rules in the centralizer.

**Test**: For period 3, enumerate reversible radius-2 CAs (local rule $f: \{0,1\}^5 \to \{0,1\}$, so $2^{32}$ rules to check). Compute the group generated by radius-$\leq 2$ reversible CAs and check if it equals the centralizer (order 36). If not, try radius 3.

**Impact**: If true, this would show that increasing the CA radius eventually captures all reversible dynamics — locality is not a fundamental barrier to reversibility, only a quantitative constraint on the "complexity" of the reversible transformation.

**Catalog References**: `Geometry/CellularAutomataGalois.lean` (applyLocal, LocalRule, ReversibilitySubgroup)

**Proof Strategy**: (1) Implement radius-$r$ CA enumeration. (2) For small periods, compute generated groups for $r = 1, 2, 3$. (3) If the union equals the centralizer, prove it using a constructive argument: show that any permutation of shift orbits can be realized by a sufficiently large-radius rule. Key technique: construct explicit "permutation CAs" that permute specific orbits while fixing others.

**Domain Bridges**: Group theory (wreath products) ↔ Cellular automata ↔ Combinatorics (necklace permutations)

**Lineage**: Directly extends the reversibility group computations from this cycle.

**Ambition**: extension

### Direction 3: Undecidability Boundary — From 1D Decidability to 2D Undecidability

**Conjecture**: There exists a formally provable reduction from the halting problem to 2D CA reversibility, constructable as a Lean 4 proof, using the Kari (1994) construction. Furthermore, the borderline case — CAs on strips $\mathbb{Z} \times \{0, \ldots, k\}$ — has a decidability threshold at a specific finite width $k_0$.

**Background**: Kari proved that reversibility of 2D CAs is undecidable by reducing from the tiling problem. In 1D, reversibility is decidable via de Bruijn graphs. The strip case interpolates between these extremes. The exact width at which decidability breaks down is unknown.

**Test**: (1) Formalize the de Bruijn graph construction for 1D CA reversibility and prove decidability. (2) Formalize the Kari reduction (or a simplified version) showing 2D undecidability. (3) For strips of width $k = 2, 3, 4$, determine whether reversibility is decidable by attempting to construct decision procedures.

**Impact**: A formal proof of the decidability/undecidability boundary would be a landmark result in computability theory, connecting cellular automata to the foundations of mathematics.

**Catalog References**: `Computation/GravityOracle.lean` (oracle computability), `Logic/` (computability theory infrastructure)

**Proof Strategy**: For 1D decidability: formalize de Bruijn graph → show bijectivity ↔ matrix condition → show matrix condition is decidable. For 2D undecidability: formalize Wang tiles → reduce to CA reversibility → invoke undecidability of the tiling problem.

**Domain Bridges**: Cellular automata ↔ Computability theory ↔ Formal verification

**Lineage**: Extends the reversibility group framework to questions of decidability.

**Ambition**: grand_challenge

### Direction 4: Reversible CAs as Cryptographic Primitives

**Conjecture**: For binary CAs of radius $r \geq 3$ on $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ with $n \geq 2^{10}$, there exist reversible CA rules whose global map has the mixing properties required for a pseudorandom permutation: specifically, the maximum bias $\max_c |\Pr[F(x)_0 = 1 \mid x_c = 1] - 1/2|$ over all cells $c$ decreases exponentially in $r$.

**Background**: Reversible CAs are natural candidates for symmetric-key encryption: they are efficiently computable, invertible, and parallelizable. The question is whether they provide sufficient mixing. The 6 universally reversible elementary CAs are far too simple (they're just shifts and complements), but higher-radius rules may achieve cryptographic strength.

**Test**: For radius $r = 3, 4, 5$ and period $n = 16$, sample random reversible CA rules and measure the bias of the output bit as a function of each input bit. Plot the maximum bias vs. radius $r$ to check for exponential decay.

**Impact**: If confirmed, this would provide a theoretically grounded family of lightweight block ciphers suitable for constrained environments, with security guarantees derived from the algebraic structure of the reversibility group.

**Catalog References**: `Cryptography/BerggrenDiophantineLattice.lean` (cryptographic primitives), `Geometry/CellularAutomataGalois.lean` (reversibility group)

**Proof Strategy**: (1) Define mixing/diffusion metrics for CA rules. (2) Prove that composition of independent reversible CAs increases mixing. (3) Use the group structure to bound the mixing time. Key lemma: if $F$ and $G$ are reversible CAs with "orthogonal" local dependencies, then $F \circ G$ has strictly better mixing than either alone.

**Domain Bridges**: Cellular automata ↔ Cryptography ↔ Probability theory

**Lineage**: Applies the reversibility group theory to a concrete application domain.

**Ambition**: extension

### Direction 5: Topological Dynamics of the Reversibility Group Action

**Conjecture**: The reversibility group $\mathcal{R}_n(\{0,1\})$ acts on the set of binary necklaces of length $n$, and this action is faithful for $n$ prime. Furthermore, the kernel of this action for composite $n$ is generated by CAs whose local rules factor through the divisors of $n$.

**Background**: The shift operator partitions configurations into orbits (necklaces). Any shift-equivariant permutation must map orbits to orbits of the same size. For prime $n$, all non-fixed orbits have size $n$, creating a clean action. For composite $n$, orbits of different sizes create a richer structure.

**Test**: For $n = 6$ (composite, with orbits of sizes 1, 2, 3, 6), compute the action of the reversibility group on orbits. Identify elements that act trivially on orbits but non-trivially within orbits (the kernel). Check if these kernel elements arise from "sub-period" CAs.

**Impact**: Understanding how the reversibility group acts on necklaces would connect CA theory to the representation theory of wreath products and provide tools for analyzing the long-term dynamics of reversible CAs (ergodicity, orbit structure, entropy).

**Catalog References**: `Geometry/CellularAutomataGalois.lean` (ReversibilitySubgroup, shiftPerm), `Algebra/Basic.lean` (group actions)

**Proof Strategy**: (1) Define the orbit map $\mathcal{R}_n \to \text{Perm}(\text{Necklaces}_n)$. (2) For prime $n$, show the kernel is trivial by proving any CA that fixes all necklaces must fix all configurations. (3) For composite $n$, characterize the kernel using sub-lattice CAs.

**Domain Bridges**: Cellular automata ↔ Combinatorics (necklaces/Lyndon words) ↔ Representation theory (wreath products)

**Lineage**: Directly extends the necklace connection discovered in this cycle.

**Ambition**: extension
