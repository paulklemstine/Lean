# Future Directions: Arithmetic Semigroups as Pseudorandom Generators

## 1. Formalize Explicit Berggren Spectral Gap on Finite Quotients

**Hypothesis**: The Berggren semigroup acting on orbits of Pythagorean triples mod q has spectral radius ρ = 1/√3 ≈ 0.577 for all primes q ≥ 3 not dividing a specific discriminant.

**Proof Strategy**: 
- The computational evidence strongly suggests ρ = 1/√3 is universal for "good" primes.
- This likely follows from the representation theory of the Berggren group (which is a subgroup of O(2,1,ℤ)).
- The orbit structure modulo q is governed by the quadratic form x² + y² - z², so the spectral analysis reduces to character sums over the light cone.
- Formally verify the eigenvalue computation for small primes (q = 3, 5, 7) as base cases, then prove the general bound using automorphic methods.

**Cross-domain Connections**: Automorphic forms on O(2,1), Selberg's eigenvalue conjecture for arithmetic quotients, property (τ) for arithmetic lattices.

**Impact**: Would give the first formally verified spectral gap for an explicit arithmetic semigroup, enabling concrete PRG seed-length bounds.

## 2. Extend Fooling from Polynomial Tests to Low-Depth Algebraic Circuits

**Hypothesis**: The spectral-gap-to-fooling theorem extends to algebraic circuits of bounded depth, not just bounded-degree polynomials.

**Proof Strategy**:
- Use the existing `bounded_circuit_degree_bound` theorem (degree ≤ 2^depth) to reduce circuit tests to polynomial tests.
- The key new ingredient is a norm bound: show that the L∞ norm of a circuit-computed function on the orbit is controlled by the circuit size.
- Formalize: if a depth-d circuit computes a function f on the orbit, then TestComplexityNorm(f) ≤ poly(size) * max_coefficient.
- Combined with the spectral gap theorem, this gives: n = O(d * log(size/ε) / log(1/ρ)) steps suffice to fool depth-d circuits.

**Cross-domain Connections**: Algebraic circuit complexity (Valiant), PIT derandomization (Kabanets-Impagliazzo), Schwartz-Zippel.

**Impact**: Would establish the first formal link between arithmetic expansion and algebraic circuit derandomization.

## 3. Instantiate the Framework for Apollonian Group mod q

**Hypothesis**: The Apollonian group (generated by four reflections preserving the Descartes quadratic form) acting on Descartes quadruples mod q produces an expander for most primes q.

**Proof Strategy**:
- Define the Apollonian generators as 4×4 integer matrices preserving Q(x,y,z,w) = 2(x²+y²+z²+w²) - (x+y+z+w)².
- Compute orbits mod q and verify spectral gaps computationally.
- The strong approximation results of Fuchs (2011) and Bourgain-Kontorovich (2014) imply that the Apollonian group surjects onto (Z/qZ)⁴/Q for almost all q, which should give spectral gap.
- Formalize the orbit structure using the Descartes circle theorem.

**Cross-domain Connections**: Apollonian gaskets, thin groups, strong approximation, Bourgain-Gamburd expansion machine.

**Impact**: Would demonstrate that the framework handles non-Pythagorean arithmetic dynamics.

## 4. Representation-Theoretic Decomposition of Test Spaces

**Hypothesis**: Polynomial test functions on arithmetic orbits decompose into irreducible representations of the acting group, with each component decaying at a rate determined by its representation-theoretic type.

**Proof Strategy**:
- For the Berggren group mod q, the orbit carries a representation of the finite group O(2,1,Z/qZ).
- Decompose L²(orbit) into irreducible representations.
- Show that degree-d polynomial tests lie in a bounded number of irreducible components.
- The spectral gap gives the worst-case decay rate, but individual components may decay faster.
- Formalize using Mathlib's representation theory (RepresentationTheory.Basic).

**Cross-domain Connections**: Representation theory of finite groups of Lie type, automorphic forms, harmonic analysis on symmetric spaces.

**Impact**: Would refine the fooling bound from a single ρ to a spectrum of rates, giving tighter PIT parameters.

## 5. Derive Extractor and Hitting-Set Constructions from Arithmetic Walks

**Hypothesis**: Arithmetic walks on expanding orbits yield deterministic extractors and hitting sets for structured polynomial sources.

**Proof Strategy**:
- An extractor maps a "weakly random" source to a "nearly uniform" output. The arithmetic walk theorem says: if the input distribution is supported on the orbit with min-entropy k, then n = O((log(orbit_size) - k) / log(1/ρ)) steps of the walk produce a distribution that is ε-close to uniform.
- For hitting sets: if a polynomial p is nonzero on a fraction δ of the orbit, then a walk of length n = O(log(1/δε)/log(1/ρ)) starting from any point hits a nonzero of p with probability ≥ 1-ε.
- Formalize the extractor property using the Leftover Hash Lemma adapted to expander walks.

**Cross-domain Connections**: Randomness extractors (Trevisan), hitting set generators (Nisan-Wigderson), expander-based derandomization (Reingold-Vadhan-Wigderson).

**Impact**: Would complete the bridge from arithmetic dynamics to practical derandomization tools.

## 6. Markov Surface Dynamics mod q

**Hypothesis**: The Markov group (generated by Vieta involutions on the surface x² + y² + z² = 3xyz) acting on solutions mod q produces expanders for most primes.

**Proof Strategy**:
- The Markov surface is an affine cubic with remarkable dynamical properties.
- Recent work of Bourgain-Gamburd-Sarnak (2016) establishes strong approximation for the Markov group.
- Formalize the three Vieta involutions as generators and compute orbits mod q.
- Verify spectral gaps computationally, then formalize.

**Cross-domain Connections**: Cluster algebras, Teichmüller theory, trace-free character varieties, quantum topology.

**Impact**: Would demonstrate the framework on a surface of fundamental importance across multiple mathematical disciplines.

## 7. Langlands-to-Complexity Pipeline

**Hypothesis**: Automorphic spectral gap results (Selberg's theorem, Ramanujan conjecture) can be formalized as inputs to the fooling framework, yielding unconditional PIT results for specific circuit families.

**Proof Strategy**:
- Selberg's theorem gives a spectral gap for congruence subgroups of SL(2,ℤ).
- Formalize the SL(2,ℤ) action on P¹(Z/qZ) with spectral gap from Selberg.
- Show that evaluation of a bounded-degree polynomial on P¹(Z/qZ) along an SL(2,ℤ)-walk has exponentially decaying bias.
- This gives an unconditional PIT statement: bounded-degree polynomials can be tested using O(d log q) group elements.

**Cross-domain Connections**: Langlands program, Selberg's 3/16 theorem, Ramanujan graphs, automorphic forms.

**Impact**: This is the deepest potential connection — it would show that the entire Langlands-style spectral theory can be imported as a "black box" for derandomization.
