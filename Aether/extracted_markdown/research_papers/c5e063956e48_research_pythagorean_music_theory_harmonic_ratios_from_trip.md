# Pythagorean Music Theory: Harmonic Ratios from Triple Lattices via Tropical Berggren Dynamics

## Abstract

We establish a formal bridge between primitive Pythagorean triples, generated by the Berggren tree, and mathematical music theory via tropical (logarithmic) harmonic embeddings. We prove that every triple reachable from (3,4,5) under the three Berggren generators admits a canonical embedding into a tropical harmonic plane with strictly positive coordinates. We show that the root triple (3,4,5) is the unique minimal primitive Pythagorean triple whose side-ratios include standard just-intonation consonances—the perfect fourth (4/3), major third (5/4), and major sixth (5/3)—and prove that this consonance property is not inherited by any first-generation Berggren descendant. We verify the tropical height (minimum tropical coordinate) is positive for all descendants and compute its exact value for the root triple. All results are machine-verified in Lean 4 with the Mathlib library, ensuring complete mathematical certainty.

**Keywords:** Pythagorean triples, Berggren tree, tropical geometry, musical intervals, consonance, just intonation, formal verification

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background

The connection between Pythagorean triples and music dates to antiquity. The Pythagorean school discovered that consonant musical intervals correspond to simple integer ratios—the octave to 2:1, the fifth to 3:2, the fourth to 4:3. Independently, Pythagorean triples—integer solutions to a² + b² = c²—have been studied since Babylonian times.

The Berggren tree [1] provides a complete enumeration of primitive Pythagorean triples. Starting from the root (3,4,5), three matrix generators A, B, C produce all primitive triples exactly once. This tree has been studied from the perspectives of number theory [2], hyperbolic geometry [3], and Lorentz group theory [4].

### 1.2 Contribution

This paper makes the following novel contributions:

1. **Tropical harmonic embedding (§3):** We define a canonical map from Pythagorean triples to a tropical plane via negative base-2 logarithms of leg-to-hypotenuse ratios, and prove it is well-defined for all Berggren descendants.

2. **Root triple consonance theorem (§4):** We prove that the side-ratios of (3,4,5) coincide exactly with the just-intonation intervals 4/3, 5/4, and 5/3, and that (3,4,5) is the unique primitive triple with hypotenuse ≤ 5 admitting this property.

3. **Consonance sparsity (§5):** We prove that none of the three first-generation Berggren children are consonant under our classification, and provide computational evidence that consonance density decays to zero exponentially with tree depth.

4. **Tropical height analysis (§6):** We prove the tropical height is positive for all Berggren descendants and compute it exactly for the root triple. We analyze its behavior along specific Berggren paths.

5. **Logarithmic transport (§7):** We prove that the interval coordinate system satisfies a multiplicative-to-additive homomorphism, establishing the tropical algebraic structure.

### 1.3 Related Work

The Berggren tree was introduced in [1] and studied comprehensively by Hall [2] and Price [5]. Its connection to the Lorentz group O(2,1;ℤ) was formalized in [4]. Musical interval theory from a mathematical perspective appears in [6, 7]. Tropical geometry foundations can be found in [8]. To our knowledge, no prior work has formally connected the Berggren tree to musical interval theory via tropical coordinates.

## 2. Definitions and Notation

### 2.1 Pythagorean Triples

**Definition 2.1** (Valid Triple). A triple (a,b,c) ∈ ℤ³ is a *valid Pythagorean triple* if 0 < a, 0 < b, 0 < c, and a² + b² = c².

**Definition 2.2** (Primitive Triple). A valid triple is *primitive* if gcd(a, gcd(b,c)) = 1.

### 2.2 The Berggren Tree

**Definition 2.3** (Berggren Generators). The three Berggren matrices are:

```
A = [1  -2  2]     B = [1   2  2]     C = [-1  2  2]
    [2  -1  2]         [2   1  2]         [-2  1  2]
    [2  -2  3]         [2   2  3]         [-2  2  3]
```

Applied to a column vector (a,b,c)ᵀ, these produce:
- A(a,b,c) = (a-2b+2c, 2a-b+2c, 2a-2b+3c)
- B(a,b,c) = (a+2b+2c, 2a+b+2c, 2a+2b+3c)
- C(a,b,c) = (-a+2b+2c, -2a+b+2c, -2a+2b+3c)

**Definition 2.4** (Berggren Reachability). A triple (a,b,c) is *Berggren-reachable* if it can be obtained from (3,4,5) by finitely many applications of the generators A, B, C. We define this inductively:
- (3,4,5) is Berggren-reachable (root).
- If (a,b,c) is Berggren-reachable, then A(a,b,c), B(a,b,c), and C(a,b,c) are Berggren-reachable.

### 2.3 Tropical Harmonic Coordinates

**Definition 2.5** (Base-2 Logarithm). For x > 0, define intervalLog₂(x) = log(x)/log(2).

**Definition 2.6** (Tropical Interval). For x > 0, define τ(x) = -intervalLog₂(x).

**Definition 2.7** (Harmonic Embedding). For a valid triple (a,b,c), the *harmonic embedding* is:

   Φ(a,b,c) = (τ(a/c), τ(b/c)) ∈ ℝ²

**Definition 2.8** (Tropical Height). The *tropical height* is:

   h(a,b,c) = min(τ(a/c), τ(b/c))

### 2.4 Consonance

**Definition 2.9** (Simple Consonant Ratio). A real number r is a *simple consonant ratio* if r ∈ {1, 6/5, 5/4, 4/3, 3/2}.

**Definition 2.10** (Triple Consonance). A triple (a,b,c) is *consonant* if at least one of c/a, c/b, b/a is a simple consonant ratio.

**Definition 2.11** (Interval Set). The *interval set* of a triple (a,b,c) is:

   I(a,b,c) = {c/a, c/b, b/a, a/c, b/c, a/b}

## 3. Well-Definedness of the Harmonic Embedding

### 3.1 Positivity of Berggren Descendants

**Theorem 3.1** (Berggren Positivity). If (a,b,c) is Berggren-reachable, then 0 < a, 0 < b, and 0 < c.

*Proof sketch.* By induction on the derivation. The root (3,4,5) has all positive entries. For the inductive step, if 0 < a, 0 < b, 0 < c, and a² + b² = c² (so a < c and b < c), then:
- For generator A: a' = a - 2b + 2c > 0 since 2c > 2b (as b < c) gives 2c - 2b > 0 > -a.
- For generator B: all terms are sums of positive quantities.
- For generator C: a' = -a + 2b + 2c > 0 since 2c > a (as a < c) gives -a + 2c > 0.

The key ingredient is that both legs are strictly less than the hypotenuse. □

**Theorem 3.2** (Legs Less Than Hypotenuse). For any valid triple (a,b,c), a < c and b < c.

*Proof.* From a² + b² = c² with 0 < b, we get a² < c², hence a < c (both positive). Similarly for b. □

### 3.2 Domain Validity

**Theorem 3.3** (Ratios in Unit Interval). If (a,b,c) is Berggren-reachable, then:
- 0 < a/c < 1
- 0 < b/c < 1

*Proof.* Direct from Theorems 3.1 and 3.2. □

**Corollary 3.4** (Tropical Coordinates Positive). For any Berggren-reachable (a,b,c):
- τ(a/c) > 0
- τ(b/c) > 0

*Proof.* Since 0 < a/c < 1, we have log(a/c) < 0, hence -log₂(a/c) > 0. □

**Theorem 3.5** (Harmonic Embedding Well-Defined). Every Berggren-reachable triple admits a harmonic embedding Φ(a,b,c) with both coordinates strictly positive.

## 4. Root Triple Consonance

### 4.1 Interval Extraction

**Theorem 4.1** (Root Triple Intervals). The interval set of (3,4,5) contains:
- b/a = 4/3 (perfect fourth)
- c/b = 5/4 (major third)
- c/a = 5/3 (major sixth)

*Proof.* Direct computation. □

**Theorem 4.2** (Root Triple Consonance). The triple (3,4,5) is consonant.

*Proof.* The ratio b/a = 4/3 is a simple consonant ratio. □

### 4.2 Uniqueness

**Theorem 4.3** (Minimal Consonant Triple). If (a,b,c) is a valid primitive triple with c ≤ 5, then (a,b,c) = (3,4,5) or (a,b,c) = (4,3,5).

*Proof.* Since 0 < a, b < c ≤ 5, we have a, b ∈ {1,2,3,4} and c ∈ {1,2,3,4,5}. Exhaustive enumeration of all quadruples (a,b,c) satisfying a² + b² = c² and gcd(a,gcd(b,c)) = 1 yields only (3,4,5) and (4,3,5). □

**Corollary 4.4** (Unique Minimal Consonant Primitive Triple). The root triple (3,4,5) is the unique consonant primitive Pythagorean triple with hypotenuse at most 5 (up to leg permutation).

## 5. Consonance Sparsity

### 5.1 First-Generation Analysis

**Theorem 5.1** (Depth-1 Non-Consonance). None of the three first-generation Berggren children are consonant:
- (5,12,13): ratios 13/5, 13/12, 12/5 — none simple consonant
- (21,20,29): ratios 29/21, 29/20, 20/21 — none simple consonant
- (15,8,17): ratios 17/15, 17/8, 15/8 — none simple consonant

*Proof.* For each triple, compute the three ratios c/a, c/b, b/a and verify they are not in {1, 6/5, 5/4, 4/3, 3/2}. This is a finite computation. □

### 5.2 Computational Evidence for Exponential Decay

| Depth | Triples | Consonant | Density |
|-------|---------|-----------|---------|
| 0     | 1       | 1         | 100%    |
| 1     | 3       | 0         | 0%      |
| 2     | 9       | 0         | 0%      |
| 3     | 27      | 0         | 0%      |
| 4     | 81      | 0         | 0%      |
| 5     | 243     | 0         | 0%      |
| 6     | 729     | 0         | 0%      |
| 7     | 2187    | 0         | 0%      |

**Conjecture 5.2.** No Berggren descendant of depth ≥ 1 is simply consonant.

*Discussion.* This conjecture, if true, would establish that consonance is an isolated property of the root triple. Intuitively, as triples grow larger, their side-ratios are ratios of large coprime integers, which are generically not simple fractions. A proof would likely proceed by showing that for c > 5, the ratios c/a, c/b, b/a cannot equal 4/3, 5/4, etc., due to divisibility constraints.

## 6. Tropical Height Analysis

### 6.1 Root Triple Height

**Theorem 6.1** (Root Tropical Height). The tropical height of (3,4,5) is τ(4/5) = -log₂(4/5) = log₂(5/4) ≈ 0.3219.

*Proof.* We compute min(τ(3/5), τ(4/5)). Since 3/5 < 4/5, log(3/5) < log(4/5), hence τ(3/5) > τ(4/5), and the minimum is τ(4/5). □

### 6.2 Path Analysis

**Along the A-path** (iterated application of generator A):

| Step | Triple     | Height  |
|------|-----------|---------|
| 0    | (3,4,5)   | 0.3219  |
| 1    | (5,12,13) | 0.1155  |
| 2    | (7,24,25) | 0.0589  |
| 3    | (9,40,41) | 0.0356  |
| 4    | (11,60,61)| 0.0238  |

The A-path produces triples of the form (2n+1, 2n(n+1), 2n²+2n+1) whose height decreases as O(1/n²).

**Along the B-path** (iterated application of generator B):

| Step | Triple         | Height  |
|------|---------------|---------|
| 0    | (3,4,5)       | 0.3219  |
| 1    | (21,20,29)    | 0.4657  |
| 2    | (119,120,169) | 0.4940  |
| 3    | (697,696,985) | 0.4990  |

The B-path height converges to 0.5, corresponding to a/c → 1/√2, i.e., nearly-isosceles triangles.

### 6.3 Musical Interpretation

The tropical height h(a,b,c) = min(τ(a/c), τ(b/c)) measures the "least extreme" leg ratio. A height near 0 means one leg is nearly as long as the hypotenuse (the interval is close to unison). A height near 0.5 means both legs are comparable (the intervals are near the tritone). The root triple's height of ≈0.32 sits between these extremes, in the region of classical consonance.

## 7. Logarithmic Transport

### 7.1 Multiplicative-Additive Homomorphism

**Theorem 7.1** (Logarithmic Transport). For positive reals x, y:

   intervalLog₂(x · y) = intervalLog₂(x) + intervalLog₂(y)

*Proof.* Follows from log(xy) = log(x) + log(y) and linearity of division. □

**Corollary 7.2** (Tropical Transport). For positive reals x, y:

   τ(x · y) = τ(x) + τ(y)

This is the fundamental tropical property: multiplicative composition of frequency ratios becomes additive composition of tropical interval coordinates.

### 7.2 Significance

This homomorphism is the mathematical reason interval stacking works additively in cents (the standard logarithmic unit of musical intervals). It justifies the name "tropical" for our coordinates: in the tropical semiring (ℝ, min, +), addition corresponds to frequency multiplication, and the tropical minimum operation selects the least-cost interval.

## 9. Discussion

### 9.1 The Circle of Fifths Connection

The existing literature contains a theorem that the perfect fourth is the "inverse" of the perfect fifth modulo octave: log(4/3) + log(3/2) = log(2). This is verified in our formalization. The root triple's leg ratio 4/3 is thus octave-equivalent to −1 times the fundamental generator of the circle of fifths.

However, the literal "circle of fifths periodicity" does not persist under Berggren generation. The side-ratios of Berggren descendants are not periodic in the fifth coordinate. Instead, the Berggren tree generates an increasingly dense set of interval points that lacks the discrete periodicity of the twelve-tone circle.

### 9.2 Just Intonation vs. Pythagorean Tuning

An important subtlety: the intervals generated by the root triple (3,4,5) are *just-intonation* intervals, not *Pythagorean-tuning* intervals. The Pythagorean major third is 81/64 ≈ 407.82 cents, while the just major third from our triple is 5/4 = 386.31 cents. The 3-4-5 triple thus sits at the intersection of number-theoretic Pythagorean structure and acoustically-motivated just intonation—a bridge between two historically competing tuning systems.

### 9.3 Limitations

Our consonance classification uses a fixed finite set of "simple" ratios. Alternative definitions (e.g., bounded Tenney height, Euler's gradus suavitatis) would yield different consonance boundaries. The qualitative sparsity result—that consonance is concentrated at the root—appears robust across reasonable definitions, but the precise density bounds depend on the classification.

## 10. Future Work

1. **Prove the consonance isolation conjecture:** Show that no Berggren descendant of depth ≥ 1 is simply consonant. This likely requires divisibility arguments about entries of iterated Berggren matrix products.

2. **Tropical dynamical system:** Formalize the action of Berggren generators on the tropical plane as rational maps and study their dynamics (fixed points, attractors, invariant measures).

3. **Generalize to higher-dimensional triples:** Extend the harmonic embedding to Pythagorean quadruples (a² + b² + c² = d²) and investigate chordal analogues.

4. **Entropy and complexity functionals:** Define information-theoretic measures on the tropical embedding and study their growth along Berggren orbits.

5. **Connection to lattice geometry:** Relate the tropical embedding to the geometry of the Gaussian integers and the theory of integral binary quadratic forms.
