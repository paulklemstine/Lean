# Proof-Complexity Holography: Geometric Duals of Formal Derivations

## Abstract

We develop a discrete *holographic* correspondence in proof complexity. Working
with implicational theories — sets of atoms equipped with one-step inference
axioms — we equip each theory with a length-graded derivability relation
`DerivOfLen T a b k` ("there is a derivation of `b` from `a` using exactly `k`
axiom applications") and a ℕ-valued **proof quasi-metric**
`minDerivLen T a b = inf { k : DerivOfLen T a b k }`. We define a **proof
translation** between theories as a map of atoms together with a single
*one-step stretch certificate*: a number `L` bounding the target-derivation
length needed to realize each source axiom. Our central result is that this
purely *local* datum propagates *holographically* to a *global* metric
statement. Concretely:

1. **Holographic propagation** (`translate_deriv`): a stretch-`L` translation
   sends every length-`k` source derivation to a target derivation of length at
   most `L·k`.
2. **Lipschitz proof metric** (`minDerivLen_translate_le`): consequently the
   proof metric is `L`-Lipschitz under translation, which is exactly the
   Cook–Reckhow p-simulation condition read inside the ℕ-valued metric.
3. **Composition law** (`translate_comp_step`): composing translations
   multiplies their stretches, derived as a corollary of holographic propagation
   rather than reproved.
4. **Holographic exactness** (`chain_doubling_isometry`): on the chain theory the
   Lipschitz bound is attained exactly — the doubling embedding `n ↦ 2n` scales
   every proof distance by exactly 2 — so geodesic rigidity ("zero proof slack")
   coincides with attainment of the Lipschitz constant.

All results are fully formalized with no `sorry` and depend only on the standard
foundational axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`. The
contribution is conceptual as much as technical: it identifies the geometric
content shared by the proof-metric program and the Cook–Reckhow simulation
preorder, exhibiting the latter as the boundary shadow of the former.

## 1. Introduction

Proof complexity studies how *large* proofs must be. Its organizing concept,
introduced by Cook and Reckhow, is *p-simulation*: a proof system `P`
p-simulates a system `Q` if every `Q`-proof can be translated into a `P`-proof
of the same theorem with at most polynomial blow-up in size. P-simulation is a
preorder; its quotient is the poset of *p-degrees*, and proving non-simulation
results (separations) is equivalent to proving super-polynomial size lower
bounds.

A complementary, finer-grained viewpoint treats proofs as *paths*. Length-graded
derivability `DerivOfLen T a b k` records not merely *that* `b` follows from `a`
but the exact number of inference steps used, and the minimal length
`minDerivLen` turns the atoms of a theory into a directed, ℕ-valued metric space.
This *proof metric* satisfies a directed triangle inequality and admits geodesics
(realized exactly on the chain theory), giving proof complexity a genuinely
geometric texture.

This paper unifies the two viewpoints. We introduce a **proof translation** — the
metric-level analogue of a Cook–Reckhow simulation — whose entire data is a *one-
step* stretch certificate, and we prove that this local certificate determines
the global Lipschitz behaviour of the proof metric. We call the phenomenon
**proof-complexity holography**: a bulk fact about individual derivations
(propagation of a per-step bound) encodes, and is encoded by, a boundary fact
about distances (Lipschitz distortion), with the rigid chain geometry saturating
the correspondence. P-simulation, in this light, is precisely contraction in the
proof metric, and transitivity of simulation is precisely the composition of
bounded-distortion maps.

## 2. Background: theories, graded derivations, and the proof metric

Throughout, `α, β, γ` denote types of *atoms*.

### 2.1 Implicational theories and derivability

**Definition 2.1 (Implicational theory).** An *implicational theory* on `α` is a
binary relation `T : α → α → Prop`. We read `T a b` as "the axiom `a → b`."

**Definition 2.2 (Derivability).** *Derivability* `Derivable T` is the reflexive–
transitive closure of `T`. Thus `Derivable T a b` holds iff there is a finite
(possibly empty) chain of axioms from `a` to `b`.

**Definition 2.3 (Chain theory).** The *chain theory* `chainT` on `ℕ` is defined
by `chainT a b ⟺ b = a + 1`: the only axioms are the unit increments `n → n+1`.

### 2.2 Length-graded derivability

**Definition 2.4 (Graded derivability).** `DerivOfLen T : α → α → ℕ → Prop` is the
inductive predicate generated by two rules:

- `refl`: `DerivOfLen T a a 0` for every `a` (the empty derivation);
- `tail`: from `DerivOfLen T a b n` and `T b c`, conclude `DerivOfLen T a c (n+1)`
  (extend a derivation by one axiom).

So `DerivOfLen T a b k` asserts the existence of a derivation of `b` from `a` using
*exactly* `k` axiom applications.

**Lemma 2.5 (One-step derivation, `derivOfLen_one_of_step`).** If `T a b` then
`DerivOfLen T a b 1`.

*Proof.* Apply `tail` to the empty derivation `refl a` and the axiom `T a b`. ∎

**Theorem 2.6 (Additive composition, `derivOfLen_comp`).** If
`DerivOfLen T a b m` and `DerivOfLen T b c n`, then `DerivOfLen T a c (m+n)`.

*Proof.* Induct on the second derivation. The base case (`n = 0`, so `b = c`)
returns the first derivation, and `m + 0 = m`. In the inductive step the second
derivation ends in a `tail` step `T c' c`; applying the induction hypothesis to
the truncated derivation and re-attaching the final axiom yields a derivation of
length `(m + n') + 1 = m + (n' + 1)`. Inducting on the *second* derivation keeps
the `+1`s accruing on the right so that the arithmetic is definitional. ∎

Additive composition is the structural fact that proof length behaves like a
length: concatenation of derivations adds their lengths. It is the engine behind
every subsequent result.

### 2.3 The proof metric

**Definition 2.7 (Minimal proof length).**
`minDerivLen T a b := sInf { k | DerivOfLen T a b k }` (with the convention that
the infimum of the empty set is 0).

When `a ⊢ b` is derivable the defining set is nonempty, and `minDerivLen` enjoys
the metric properties one expects: `minDerivLen T a a = 0`, and the directed
triangle inequality `minDerivLen T a c ≤ minDerivLen T a b + minDerivLen T b c`
holds whenever both legs are derivable (a direct consequence of Theorem 2.6 via
`Nat.sInf_mem` on the two nonempty length-sets and `Nat.sInf_le` on their sum).
Thus `(α, minDerivLen T)` is an asymmetric, ℕ-valued premetric space — the *proof
geometry* of `T`.

### 2.4 The chain as a ruler

**Theorem 2.8 (Graded boundary of the chain, `chain_derivOfLen_iff`).** For all
`a, b, k : ℕ`, `DerivOfLen chainT a b k ⟺ b = a + k`.

*Proof.* (⇒) Induct on `k`; each `tail` step increments the endpoint by exactly 1.
(⇐) Induct on `k`, building `a → a+1 → ⋯ → a+k` by repeated `tail`. ∎

**Theorem 2.9 (Chain metric, `minDerivLen_chain_eq`).** For `a ≤ b`,
`minDerivLen chainT a b = b - a`.

*Proof.* By Theorem 2.8 the length-set `{ k | DerivOfLen chainT a b k }` is the
singleton `{ b - a }` (since `b = a + k ⟺ k = b - a` when `a ≤ b`). The infimum of
a singleton is its element. Formally: `Nat.sInf_le` gives `≤ b - a` from the
explicit length-`(b-a)` derivation, and `le_csInf` gives the reverse bound since
every length `k` in the set satisfies `k = b - a`. ∎

The chain is the extremal *geodesic* geometry: between any two ordered points
there is exactly one derivation, whose length is forced. There is no proof slack.

## 3. Proof translations

We now introduce the morphisms of proof geometries.

**Definition 3.1 (Proof translation, `Translation`).** A *proof translation* from
a source theory `T` on `α` to a target theory `S` on `β` is a structure
consisting of:

- `map : α → β`, a function on atoms;
- `stretch : ℕ`, the advertised per-axiom cost; and
- `step`, a *one-step stretch certificate*: for every source axiom `T a b`, there
  exists `j ≤ stretch` with `DerivOfLen S (map a) (map b) j`.

The certificate is *local*: it constrains the translation only on individual
axioms of the source. It is the metric-level abstraction of a Cook–Reckhow
simulation, where the role of polynomial blow-up is played by the single constant
`stretch`. We write `L := φ.stretch` for a translation `φ`.

**Remark 3.2 (Why a `≤` bound, not an exact length).** A single source axiom may
admit several target realizations of differing lengths; carrying the certificate
as `∃ j ≤ L, …` (rather than fixing `j`) is what keeps the propagation induction
of §4 definitional. Inducting on the target side, or tracking exact lengths,
breaks because the realizing length is not a function of the source axiom.

**Remark 3.3 (Degenerate stretch).** Stretch `0` is harmless: the certificate then
forces `map a = map b` along axioms (the only length-0 derivation is `refl`),
consistent with `0 · k = 0`. The identity translation has `map = id` and
`stretch = 1`.

## 4. Holographic propagation (the bulk engine)

**Theorem 4.1 (Holographic propagation, `translate_deriv`).** Let `φ` be a
translation from `T` to `S` with stretch `L`. Then for all `a, b : α` and `k : ℕ`,

> `DerivOfLen T a b k  ⟹  ∃ j ≤ L · k, DerivOfLen S (map a) (map b) j`.

*Proof.* Induct on the source derivation `DerivOfLen T a b k`.

*Base (`refl`, `k = 0`).* The empty source derivation maps to the empty target
derivation: take `j = 0`, with `0 ≤ L · 0 = 0` and `DerivOfLen S (map a) (map a) 0`.

*Step (`tail`).* Suppose the derivation is `DerivOfLen T a b n` extended by an
axiom `T b c`, giving length `n + 1`. By the induction hypothesis there is
`j ≤ L · n` with `DerivOfLen S (map a) (map b) j`. By the one-step certificate
(`φ.step` applied to `T b c`) there is `i ≤ L` with
`DerivOfLen S (map b) (map c) i`. Concatenating via additive composition
(Theorem 2.6) yields `DerivOfLen S (map a) (map c) (j + i)`, and

> `j + i ≤ L · n + L = L · (n + 1)`,

so `j + i` witnesses the claim at length `n + 1`. ∎

This is the bulk statement: the per-axiom bound `L` propagates, additively along
the derivation, into the per-derivation bound `L · k`. The two ingredients are
exactly the local certificate (one `L` per axiom) and the additivity of length
(Theorem 2.6); their interaction *is* the holographic propagation.

## 5. The boundary shadow: a Lipschitz proof metric

**Theorem 5.1 (Lipschitz proof metric, `minDerivLen_translate_le`).** Let `φ` be a
translation from `T` to `S` with stretch `L`. If `Derivable T a b`, then

> `minDerivLen S (map a) (map b) ≤ L · minDerivLen T a b`.

*Proof.* Since `Derivable T a b`, the set `{ k | DerivOfLen T a b k }` is
nonempty (the reflexive–transitive closure unwinds to a graded derivation of some
length), so `m := minDerivLen T a b` is *attained*: by `Nat.sInf_mem`,
`DerivOfLen T a b m`. Apply Theorem 4.1 to this minimal derivation to obtain
`j ≤ L · m` with `DerivOfLen S (map a) (map b) j`. Then by `Nat.sInf_le`,

> `minDerivLen S (map a) (map b) ≤ j ≤ L · m = L · minDerivLen T a b`. ∎

**Interpretation.** Theorem 5.1 says a translation of stretch `L` is an
`L`-Lipschitz map of proof geometries: it distorts proof distance by at most a
factor `L`. This is precisely the Cook–Reckhow p-simulation condition — "target
proofs are at most a bounded blow-up of source proofs" — transported from the
*size order* into the *ℕ-valued proof metric*. Where the simulation preorder
records existence of a polynomial blow-up, the proof metric records the sharp
multiplicative constant. Simulation is contraction.

## 6. Composition: stretches multiply

**Theorem 6.1 (Composition law, `translate_comp_step`).** Let `φ` be a
translation from `T` to `S` with stretch `L`, and `ψ` a translation from `S` to
`R` with stretch `M`. Then for every source axiom `T a b` there is
`j ≤ M · L` with

> `DerivOfLen R (ψ.map (φ.map a)) (ψ.map (φ.map b)) j`.

*Proof.* By `φ.step` applied to `T a b`, there is `i ≤ L` with
`DerivOfLen S (φ.map a) (φ.map b) i`. Now apply *holographic propagation*
(Theorem 4.1) to `ψ` and this length-`i` `S`-derivation: there is `j ≤ M · i`
with `DerivOfLen R (ψ.map (φ.map a)) (ψ.map (φ.map b)) j`. Finally
`j ≤ M · i ≤ M · L`. ∎

**Remark 6.2.** The composite map `ψ.map ∘ φ.map` with stretch `M · L` is again a
translation `T → R`; Theorem 6.1 is precisely the verification of its one-step
certificate. Crucially the proof *reuses* Theorem 4.1 rather than reproving
propagation: composition of translations is a corollary of holographic
propagation. This unifies two facts previously proved separately — additive
composition of derivations (the metric side) and transitivity of simulation (the
order side, where transitivity is closure of the blow-up class under composition)
— under a single engine. Multiplicativity of stretch is what makes the
assignment `φ ↦ φ.stretch` a (lax) functor into the multiplicative monoid
`(ℕ, ·)`.

## 7. Holographic exactness on the chain

The Lipschitz bound of Theorem 5.1 is an inequality; we show it is attained.

**Definition 7.1 (Doubling translation, `doubling`).** Define a translation from
`chainT` to `chainT` by `map n = 2n` and `stretch = 2`. The one-step certificate
holds: a source axiom `n → n+1` has translated endpoints `2n` and `2n+2`, and
`DerivOfLen chainT (2n) (2n+2) 2` holds (the derivation `2n → 2n+1 → 2n+2`), with
`2 ≤ 2`.

**Theorem 7.2 (Holographic exactness, `chain_doubling_isometry`).** For `a ≤ b`,

> `minDerivLen chainT (2a) (2b) = 2 · minDerivLen chainT a b`.

*Proof.* By Theorem 2.9 (applicable since `a ≤ b ⟹ 2a ≤ 2b`),
`minDerivLen chainT (2a) (2b) = 2b - 2a` and `minDerivLen chainT a b = b - a`. The
identity `2b - 2a = 2(b - a)` for `a ≤ b` is elementary natural-number arithmetic
(`omega`). ∎

**Interpretation.** Doubling realizes the Lipschitz constant `L = 2` of its
stretch *exactly* on every pair `a ≤ b`: not "≤ 2," but "= 2." Hence on the chain
the inequality of Theorem 5.1 is saturated. This is the extremal, rigid case: the
chain has no shortcuts (Theorem 2.9 makes every length-set a singleton), so there
is no slack to absorb the stretch. Thus *geodesic rigidity* ("zero proof slack,"
the chain realizes geodesics) is identified with *attainment of the Lipschitz
constant*. The two phrases describe one phenomenon, viewed from the bulk and from
the boundary respectively.

## 8. The holographic dictionary

We summarize the correspondence the four theorems establish.

| Bulk (derivations) | Boundary (metric) |
|---|---|
| One-step certificate: each axiom costs ≤ `L` (Def. 3.1) | — |
| Holographic propagation: length-`k` ↦ length-`≤ L·k` (Thm. 4.1) | `L`-Lipschitz distance (Thm. 5.1) |
| Concatenation adds lengths (Thm. 2.6) | Directed triangle inequality (§2.3) |
| Propagation through composite (Thm. 4.1 reused) | Stretches multiply (Thm. 6.1) |
| Chain has unique derivations (Thm. 2.8) | Chain is rigid; bound attained (Thm. 7.2) |

The pattern is the holographic slogan made precise for proofs: *local bulk data
(a per-axiom certificate) determines global boundary data (Lipschitz distances),
and the rigidity of the boundary geometry reflects the absence of bulk shortcuts.*

## 9. Relationship to the Cook–Reckhow program

A Cook–Reckhow propositional proof system is a surjective, polynomial-time map
from proofs to the tautologies they certify, with a size measure. System `P`
p-simulates `Q` when `Q`-proofs translate to `P`-proofs of the same theorem with
polynomial blow-up; the relation is a preorder whose transitivity is exactly
closure of the polynomial blow-up class under composition, and whose separations
are super-polynomial size lower bounds.

Our framework abstracts this in two coordinated moves. First, we replace the
*size order* (existence of a polynomial blow-up) with the *proof metric*
(`minDerivLen`, the sharp minimal length). Second, we replace a simulation by a
*translation* whose blow-up datum is a single constant `stretch`. Under this
dictionary:

- p-simulation ⟷ existence of a stretch-bounded translation (Lipschitz map);
- transitivity of simulation ⟷ multiplicativity of stretch under composition
  (Theorem 6.1);
- a separation (non-simulation) ⟷ a pair of points whose target distance exceeds
  `L` times the source distance for every admissible `L` — a *quantitative*
  Lipschitz-violation, sharper than mere super-polynomial growth.

Thus the simulation preorder is the *boundary shadow* of the proof-metric
geometry, and the constant `stretch` is the metric refinement of the polynomial
blow-up degree.

## 10. Algorithms

The constructive content of the theorems yields direct algorithms on finitely
presented theories (atoms drawn from a finite or effectively enumerable set, with
decidable axiom relation).

**Algorithm A (Proof distance via BFS).** To compute `minDerivLen T a b`,
breadth-first search the directed graph whose vertices are atoms and whose edges
are axioms `T x y`, starting from `a`; the level at which `b` is first dequeued is
`minDerivLen T a b`. Correctness is Definition 2.7: BFS returns the shortest
directed path length, which is the minimal derivation length. Complexity is
`O(V + E)` in the explored component.

**Algorithm B (Translate a derivation).** Given a translation `φ` and an explicit
source derivation `a = x₀ → x₁ → ⋯ → xₖ = b`, emit, for each source axiom
`xᵢ → xᵢ₊₁`, a witnessing target derivation `map xᵢ ⇒ map xᵢ₊₁` of length `≤ L`
(from the certificate), and concatenate. This is the algorithmic content of
Theorem 4.1; the output has length `≤ L · k`.

**Algorithm C (Certified Lipschitz bound).** Combine A and B: compute the source
distance `m = minDerivLen T a b` and a realizing source derivation, translate it
with B to obtain a target derivation of length `≤ L · m`, and (optionally) run A
in the target to obtain the *exact* target distance `≤ L · m`. This certifies the
bound of Theorem 5.1 and measures the actual slack `L · m − minDerivLen S …`.

## 11. Applications

- **Comparing proof systems quantitatively.** The sharp constant `stretch`
  upgrades the binary "does `P` p-simulate `Q`?" to "by what factor does the best
  translation distort proof length?", a finer invariant than the p-degree.
- **Slack as a separation signal.** Algorithm C measures the gap between `L · m`
  and the true target distance. Persistent zero slack (as on the chain) signals
  rigidity; growing slack signals that the translation is far from optimal, a
  computable proxy for hardness of simulation.
- **Modular construction of translations.** Theorem 6.1 lets one build complex
  translations by composing simple, individually certified ones, with the global
  stretch read off as the product — useful for assembling multi-stage proof
  compilers with provable size guarantees.
- **Benchmark geometries.** The chain (Theorem 7.2) provides an exactly solvable
  testbed where every distance and every translation cost is known in closed
  form, ideal for validating implementations of Algorithms A–C.

## 12. Discussion

The conceptual payoff is the identification of a single mechanism — additive
composition of graded derivations — beneath three superficially different facts:
the triangle inequality of the proof metric, the transitivity of the simulation
preorder, and the propagation of a per-axiom bound to a per-proof bound. Naming
this mechanism "holographic" is not mere analogy: the formal pattern (local bulk
certificate ⟹ global boundary bound, with boundary rigidity ⟺ bulk shortcut-
freeness) is structurally the boundary-encodes-bulk pattern, instantiated in a
finite, fully constructive logical setting.

A subtlety worth emphasizing is the role of the `≤`-bound in the certificate
(Remark 3.2). Insisting on an *exact* per-axiom length would make translations
brittle and would break the propagation induction, because the realizing length
is genuinely a relation, not a function, of the source axiom. The inequality is
not a weakness but the correct level of generality; the chain (Theorem 7.2) then
shows that this generality loses nothing in the extremal case.

## 13. Future work

The composition law (Theorem 6.1) already supplies an associativity-compatible
composition with multiplicative stretch and an identity translation of stretch 1.
This strongly suggests promoting translations to a genuine *category*: objects are
implicational theories, morphisms are translations, identity has stretch 1, and
composition has stretch `M · L`. Theorem 5.1 would then exhibit the assignment
`(T, a, b) ↦ minDerivLen T a b` as a *lax functor* into the multiplicative monoid
`(ℕ, ·)` acting on the metric — formalizing the slogan that the entire simulation
preorder is the shadow of a category whose hom-data is a single natural number
(the stretch). The claim is falsifiable in the strongest sense: were stretches to
*add* rather than multiply, the functor law would fail outright, and no consistent
categorical structure of this shape could exist. Establishing the identity and
associativity coherence — largely bookkeeping atop the present results — is the
natural next step. Further directions include: random/structured theories where
the triangle inequality is strict (genuine shortcuts) and loop-length sets carry
nontrivial numerical-semigroup (Frobenius) structure; quantitative separation
theorems phrased as Lipschitz-violation lower bounds; and decidability/complexity
analysis of computing optimal stretch for finitely presented theories.

---

## 14. Conclusion

We have shown that a single local datum — a one-step stretch certificate — governs
the global metric behaviour of proof translations. Holographic propagation
carries a per-axiom bound `L` to a per-derivation bound `L·k`; its boundary shadow
makes the proof metric `L`-Lipschitz, recovering Cook–Reckhow p-simulation as
contraction; composition multiplies stretches as a corollary of the same engine;
and the chain saturates the bound exactly, equating geodesic rigidity with
attainment of the Lipschitz constant. Proof complexity, usually phrased in sizes
and polynomials, thereby acquires a holographic geometry in which translation is
a bounded-distortion map and the boundary remembers the bulk.
