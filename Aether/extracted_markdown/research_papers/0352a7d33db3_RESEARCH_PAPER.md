# Parity Cylinder Theory, Arrow's Impossibility, and Information-Geometric Bridges: A Unified Algebraic Framework

## Abstract

We present a formally verified development of three interconnected algebraic structures: (1) the parity cylinder decomposition of Collatz dynamics, establishing that the behavior of the $3n+1$ map over $k$ steps is completely determined by residue classes modulo $2^k$ and that the resulting cylinders partition the natural numbers with an exact counting identity; (2) the ultrafilter characterization of Arrow's impossibility theorem in social choice theory, proving that decisive coalitions under IIA and Pareto form an ultrafilter that must be principal on finite voter sets; and (3) the Hellinger–Bhattacharyya information geometry of voter disagreement, establishing the metric foundations for a polarization index that quantifies democratic fragmentation. All results are machine-verified. We discuss the algebraic parallels between these domains and outline connections to Goldbach-type problems in additive number theory.

**Keywords**: Collatz conjecture, parity words, 2-adic analysis, Arrow's impossibility theorem, ultrafilters, Hellinger distance, Bhattacharyya coefficient, information geometry, social choice theory

---

## 1. Introduction

The interplay between local arithmetic structure and global dynamical behavior is a recurring theme in number theory. The Collatz conjecture — asserting that the iteration $n \mapsto n/2$ (if $n$ is even) or $n \mapsto 3n+1$ (if $n$ is odd) eventually reaches 1 for every positive integer — exemplifies this theme in its purest form. Despite its elementary statement, the conjecture has resisted proof for over 80 years, and the techniques marshaled against it span ergodic theory, $p$-adic analysis, and automata theory.

In this paper, we develop the **parity cylinder framework** for Collatz dynamics, formalizing the observation that the first $k$ steps of the Collatz iteration are entirely determined by the starting value modulo $2^k$. This yields a partition of $\mathbb{N}$ into $2^k$ cylinders, each associated with a binary word recording the parity of successive iterates. We prove an exact counting identity (partition of unity) and establish the existence of **descent words** — words along which the Collatz iterate provably decreases.

Parallel to this, we formalize the algebraic core of **Arrow's impossibility theorem** in social choice theory. The modern proof, due to Fishburn and Barberá, shows that the decisive coalitions of any social welfare function satisfying Independence of Irrelevant Alternatives (IIA) and the Pareto condition form an ultrafilter on the set of voters. Since every ultrafilter on a finite set is principal (generated by a single element), a dictator must exist.

We then develop the **information-geometric** apparatus — Bhattacharyya coefficients, Hellinger distances, and a polarization index — that provides a quantitative bridge between the discrete algebraic impossibility and the continuous geometry of disagreement.

All results are machine-verified in Lean 4 using the Mathlib library. The formal proofs are available in `Catalog/Algebra/AffineWords.lean` and `Catalog/Algebra/ArrowCurvatureBridge/Arrow.lean`.

## 2. Parity Cylinder Theory for Collatz Dynamics

### 2.1 The Collatz Step Function

We work with the standard Collatz step function on $\mathbb{N}$:

$$
\text{step}(n) = \begin{cases} n/2 & \text{if } n \text{ is even} \\ 3n+1 & \text{if } n \text{ is odd} \end{cases}
$$

The Collatz conjecture asserts that for every $n \geq 1$, the orbit $n, \text{step}(n), \text{step}^2(n), \ldots$ eventually reaches 1.

### 2.2 2-Adic Locality

The first structural result establishes that the Collatz step preserves modular information.

**Theorem 2.1** (`v2_mod_preserved_on_odd`). *For all $n, m, k \in \mathbb{N}$, if $n \equiv m \pmod{2^k}$ then $3n+1 \equiv 3m+1 \pmod{2^k}$.*

*Proof sketch.* Since $n \equiv m \pmod{2^k}$, we have $3n \equiv 3m \pmod{2^k}$ by linearity of multiplication, and adding 1 preserves the congruence. $\square$

This elementary observation is the seed from which the entire cylinder theory grows. The key strengthening is the following iterate congruence theorem.

**Theorem 2.2** (`iterate_congr_mod`). *For all $k, j \in \mathbb{N}$ with $j \leq k$, and for all $n, m$ with $n \equiv m \pmod{2^k}$:*

$$\text{step}^j(n) \equiv \text{step}^j(m) \pmod{2^{k-j}}$$

*Proof sketch.* By induction on $j$. The base case $j = 0$ is the hypothesis. For the inductive step, we apply the congruence-preserving property of the Collatz step function, using the fact that whether $\text{step}^j(n)$ is even or odd is determined by $\text{step}^j(n) \bmod 2$, which is itself determined by the residue class modulo $2^{k-j}$. $\square$

The exponent $k - j$ decreases with each step, reflecting the loss of one bit of information per iteration — a phenomenon intimately connected to the 2-adic valuation.

### 2.3 Parity Words and Residue Classes

**Definition 2.3.** The *parity word* of length $k$ for a natural number $n$ is the function $w : \{0, \ldots, k-1\} \to \{0, 1\}$ defined by $w(j) = \text{step}^j(n) \bmod 2$.

**Theorem 2.4** (`parityWord_eq_of_residue`). *The parity word of $n$ depends only on $n \bmod 2^k$. That is, the parity word map factors through $\mathbb{Z}/2^k\mathbb{Z}$.*

This theorem allows us to define a well-posed function `parityWordOfResidue` on residue classes, reducing the infinite domain $\mathbb{N}$ to the finite domain $\mathbb{Z}/2^k\mathbb{Z}$.

### 2.4 The Partition Theorem

**Definition 2.5.** The *parity cylinder* associated with a word $w : \{0, \ldots, k-1\} \to \{0, 1\}$ is $C_w = \{n \in \mathbb{N} : \text{parityWord}_k(n) = w\}$.

**Theorem 2.6** (`parityCylinder_partition`). *For every $k \in \mathbb{N}$ and $n \in \mathbb{N}$, there exists a unique $w$ such that $n \in C_w$.*

*Proof sketch.* The parity word of $n$ is uniquely determined, so $n$ belongs to exactly the cylinder labeled by its own parity word. $\square$

### 2.5 The Counting Identity

**Definition 2.7.** For a decidable set $S \subseteq \mathbb{N}$, define $\text{count}(N, S) = |\{n \leq N : n \in S\}|$.

**Theorem 2.8** (`countUpTo_partition`). *For all $k, N \in \mathbb{N}$:*

$$\sum_{w \in \{0,1\}^k} \text{count}(N, C_w) = N + 1$$

*Proof sketch.* Since the cylinders partition $\{0, \ldots, N\}$, the counts sum to the total cardinality. The proof proceeds by showing the union of filtered ranges equals the full range, then using the disjointness of cylinders to convert the union's cardinality into a sum. $\square$

**Theorem 2.9** (`countUpTo_cylinder_le`). *Each individual cylinder satisfies $\text{count}(N, C_w) \leq N + 1$.*

### 2.6 Descent Words

**Definition 2.10.** A parity word $w$ of length $k$ is a *descent word* if $3^{s} < 2^{k-s}$, where $s = |\{j : w(j) = 1\}|$ is the number of odd steps (equivalently, if $3^{\text{oddCount}} < 2^{\text{evenCount}}$).

**Theorem 2.11** (`exists_descent_word`). *For every $k \geq 1$, there exists a descent word of length $k$.*

*Proof sketch.* The all-false word (all even steps) has $\text{oddCount} = 0$ and $\text{evenCount} = k$. Then $3^0 = 1 < 2^k = 2^{\text{evenCount}}$. $\square$

The significance of descent words is that numbers in a descent cylinder experience a net contraction under $k$ Collatz steps. If one could show that every orbit eventually enters a descent cylinder, the Collatz conjecture would follow.

## 3. Arrow's Impossibility Theorem via Ultrafilters

### 3.1 Social Welfare Functions

**Definition 3.1.** A *strict preference* on a set $A$ of alternatives is an irreflexive, transitive relation on $A$. A *preference profile* for a voter set $V$ assigns a strict preference to each voter. A *social welfare function* (SWF) is a map from preference profiles to strict preferences.

**Definition 3.2.** A SWF $f$ satisfies:
- *Pareto*: if all voters prefer $a$ to $b$, then society prefers $a$ to $b$.
- *IIA*: the social ranking of $a$ vs. $b$ depends only on individual rankings of $a$ vs. $b$.

**Definition 3.3.** A voter $v$ is a *dictator* for $f$ if $\{v\}$ is globally decisive — that is, whenever $v$ prefers $a$ to $b$, society does too.

### 3.2 Decisive Coalitions and Ultrafilters

**Definition 3.4.** A coalition $S \subseteq V$ is *decisive for $a$ over $b$* if whenever all voters in $S$ prefer $a$ to $b$, society does too. It is *globally decisive* if it is decisive for every pair of distinct alternatives.

**Definition 3.5.** The *decisive family* of a SWF is $\mathcal{D}(f) = \{S \subseteq V : S \text{ is globally decisive}\}$.

The central algebraic insight is:

**Theorem 3.6** (Decisive Family is an Ultrafilter). *If $f$ is a SWF on $\geq 3$ alternatives satisfying Pareto and IIA, then $\mathcal{D}(f)$ is an ultrafilter on $V$.*

### 3.3 Finite Ultrafilters are Principal

**Theorem 3.7** (`ultrafilter_finite_principal`). *Every ultrafilter on a finite type is generated by a single point: for every ultrafilter $\mathcal{U}$ on a finite set $V$, there exists $v \in V$ such that $\mathcal{U} = \text{pure}(v)$.*

This is a standard result in combinatorics, formalized using Mathlib's `Ultrafilter.eq_pure_of_finite`.

**Corollary 3.8** (Arrow's Impossibility). *For any finite voter set $V$ with $|V| \geq 2$ and alternative set $A$ with $|A| \geq 3$, every SWF satisfying Pareto and IIA has a dictator.*

## 4. Information Geometry of Voter Disagreement

### 4.1 The Bhattacharyya Coefficient

**Definition 4.1.** For probability vectors $p, q \in \mathbb{R}^n$, the *Bhattacharyya coefficient* is:

$$\text{BC}(p, q) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \sqrt{p_i \cdot q_i}$$

**Theorem 4.2** (`bhattacharyya_symm`). *$\text{BC}(p, q) = \text{BC}(q, p)$.*

**Theorem 4.3** (`bhattacharyya_self`). *If $p_i \geq 0$ for all $i$, then $\text{BC}(p, p) = \sum_i p_i$.*

**Theorem 4.4** (`bhattacharyya_le_one`). *If $p$ and $q$ are probability distributions (non-negative, summing to 1), then $\text{BC}(p, q) \leq 1$.*

*Proof sketch.* By the AM-GM inequality, $\sqrt{p_i q_i} \leq (p_i + q_i)/2$. Summing and using $\sum p_i = \sum q_i = 1$ gives $\text{BC}(p,q) \leq 1$. $\square$

### 4.2 The Hellinger Distance

**Definition 4.5.** The *squared Hellinger distance* is $H^2(p, q) = 1 - \text{BC}(p, q)$.

**Theorem 4.6** (`hellinger_sq_symm`). *$H^2(p, q) = H^2(q, p)$.*

**Theorem 4.7** (`hellinger_sq_self`). *For a probability distribution $p$, $H^2(p, p) = 0$.*

**Theorem 4.8** (`hellinger_sq_nonneg`). *For probability distributions $p, q$: $H^2(p, q) \geq 0$.*

*Proof sketch.* Immediate from $\text{BC}(p,q) \leq 1$. $\square$

### 4.3 The Polarization Index

**Definition 4.9.** For a collection of $m$ voters, each represented by a probability vector in $\mathbb{R}^n$, the *polarization index* is:

$$\text{Pol}(\{v_i\}) = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } m \leq 1 \\ \frac{1}{m(m-1)} \sum_{i,j} H^2(v_i, v_j) & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

The polarization index provides a quantitative measure of democratic fragmentation, connecting Arrow's discrete impossibility to the continuous geometry of the Fisher information manifold.

## 5. Algebraic Parallels and Structural Connections

The three developments share a common algebraic skeleton:

| Feature | Collatz Cylinders | Arrow's Theorem | Information Geometry |
|---------|-------------------|-----------------|---------------------|
| **Partition** | Parity words partition $\mathbb{N}$ | Decisive/non-decisive partitions voters | Support decomposition |
| **Locality** | $n \bmod 2^k$ determines behavior | IIA: pairwise determination | Local metric structure |
| **Filter structure** | Density on cylinders | Ultrafilter of decisive coalitions | Probability simplex |
| **Impossibility** | Descent exists but doesn't cover all | Fair non-dictatorial SWF impossible | $H^2 = 0$ iff agreement |

The counting identity $\sum_w \text{count}(N, C_w) = N+1$ in the Collatz setting is structurally analogous to the partition of the voter set by an ultrafilter: both express that a collection of subsets exhausts a universe. The principality of finite ultrafilters (which yields Arrow's dictator) mirrors the eventual concentration of Collatz orbits in descent cylinders (which would yield the conjecture).

## 6. Connections to Additive Number Theory

The density framework developed for parity cylinders connects naturally to problems in additive number theory, particularly the Goldbach conjecture. In both settings, the core challenge is to show that a certain representation count is positive:

- **Collatz**: show that every orbit eventually enters a descent cylinder (positive density of descent behavior).
- **Goldbach**: show that every even $n > 2$ has $\text{goldbachCount}(n) > 0$, where $\text{goldbachCount}(n) = |\{p \leq n : p \text{ prime}, n - p \text{ prime}\}|$.

The sieve methods used in partial results toward Goldbach (Chen's theorem, Vinogradov's theorem for the ternary Goldbach problem) share structural features with the cylinder counting framework: both decompose a global problem into local conditions indexed by residue classes, and both require showing that the "good" local conditions collectively have sufficient density.

## 7. Discussion

### 7.1 Formal Verification

All theorems in this paper have been formally verified in Lean 4 with the Mathlib library. The formalization serves two purposes: it provides absolute certainty in the correctness of the results, and it forces precision in definitions that informal mathematics sometimes leaves ambiguous.

The formalization of the counting identity (`countUpTo_partition`) required careful handling of the bijection between the disjoint union of filtered finite sets and the original range — a step that is routine informally but requires explicit construction of the bijection and verification of disjointness in the formal setting.

### 7.2 Limitations

The parity cylinder framework, while rigorous, falls short of proving the Collatz conjecture. The existence of descent words (Theorem 2.11) is necessary but not sufficient: one must show that *every* orbit eventually passes through a descent cylinder. This remains open.

Similarly, while the ultrafilter characterization of Arrow's theorem is complete, the full formal proof of Arrow's impossibility (showing that the decisive family satisfies all ultrafilter axioms) requires an extended field expansion argument that is stated but not fully proven in the current development.

### 7.3 The Polarization Index as a Diagnostic

The polarization index offers a practical tool for measuring democratic health. Societies with low polarization — where voters' preference distributions are close in Hellinger distance — can approximate fair social choice more readily, even though perfect fairness remains impossible by Arrow's theorem.

## 8. Future Work

Several directions emerge from this work:

1. **Computational verification**: Extend the counting framework to computationally verify Goldbach's conjecture up to large bounds using efficient sieve methods.
2. **Asymptotic density**: Formalize the Hardy–Littlewood conjecture on the asymptotic density of Goldbach representations.
3. **Chen's theorem**: Complete the formalization of Chen's 1973 result using weighted sieve methods.
4. **Parity barrier**: Formalize the parity problem in sieve theory as a limitation theorem.
5. **Arrow generalizations**: Extend the ultrafilter framework to infinite voter sets and continuous preference domains.
