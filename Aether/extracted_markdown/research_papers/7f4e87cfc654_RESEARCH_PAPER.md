# Finite and Canonical Generation of Elimination Congruences for Boolean Polynomials

## Abstract

We study variable elimination in the algebra of **Boolean polynomials**: the
free idempotent commutative semiring whose elements are finite sets of
monomials, with addition given by union (so that $p + p = p$) and multiplication
given by the union product of monomials (so that $x_i^2 = x_i$). This semiring
is, up to notation, the algebra of monotone Boolean formulas in disjunctive
normal form, with $+$ as logical OR and $\times$ as logical AND.

Given a finite set $R$ of polynomial equations, we form the **generated semiring
congruence** $\equiv_R$ — the least equivalence relation containing $R$ and
compatible with $+$ and $\times$ — and we study its image under the operation of
eliminating (projecting away) the last variable. We call this image the
**elimination congruence**.

Our two main results are:

1. **Finite generation (bounded).** For any finite universe $V$ of monomials,
   the elimination congruence restricted to polynomials supported on $V$ is
   itself a finitely generated congruence.

2. **Canonical atomic generation.** That bounded elimination congruence is
   generated by its **join-irreducible witnesses** — congruent pairs whose
   support difference is join-irreducible in the inclusion lattice, equivalently
   a *single monomial*. Thus every projected equality, however large the gap
   between its two sides, decomposes into a chain of single-monomial moves.

We also establish the supporting structural fact that the join-irreducible
elements of the inclusion lattice of finite sets are exactly the singletons, and
we give soundness and completeness statements for an algorithmic witness
enumerator. All results have been formally verified. We close with proof
sketches, an explicit algorithm with complexity analysis, applications to
quantifier elimination and database dependency projection, and directions for
future work.

---

## 1. Introduction

Elimination — projecting a system of relations onto a subset of its variables
while preserving every consequence expressible over that subset — is a unifying
theme across mathematics and computer science. Gaussian elimination, resultants,
Gröbner-basis elimination ideals, Fourier–Motzkin elimination, and logical
quantifier elimination are all instances of the same aspiration: to convert an
apparently unbounded process of cancellation into a finite, mechanical one.

A recurring obstacle is that the *projection* of a finitely presented structure
need not be finitely presented, and even when it is, a canonical minimal
presentation can be elusive. This paper resolves both questions in a concrete,
combinatorially transparent setting: the idempotent semiring of Boolean
polynomials. We show that elimination there is not merely finitely generated but
admits a *canonical atomic* generating set built from single-monomial witnesses,
and we identify those atoms precisely through the lattice-theoretic notion of
join-irreducibility.

The setting is far from artificial. Boolean polynomials with idempotent addition
are the algebraic shadow of monotone DNF formulas, the everyday currency of SAT
solving, hardware synthesis, knowledge compilation, and database dependency
theory. Variable elimination in this algebra is exactly the projection /
quantifier-elimination step those fields rely on.

## 2. The Boolean polynomial semiring

### 2.1 Monomials and polynomials

Fix $n \in \mathbb{N}$ variables $x_0, \dots, x_{n-1}$. Because the algebra is
Boolean, $x_i^2 = x_i$, so an exponent vector degenerates to a *presence set*.

**Definition 2.1 (Monomial).** A *monomial* over $n$ variables is a finite set
of variable indices; we write $\mathrm{BPolyExp}\, n$ for the type of monomials.
The monomial $\varnothing$ denotes the constant $1$.

**Definition 2.2 (Boolean polynomial).** A *Boolean polynomial* is a finite set
of monomials. We write $\mathrm{BPoly}\, n$ for the type of polynomials, and
$\operatorname{supp}(p)$ for the underlying finite set of monomials of $p$ (its
*support*).

### 2.2 Operations

**Definition 2.3 (Semiring structure).** For $p, q \in \mathrm{BPoly}\, n$:

$$
p + q \;=\; \operatorname{supp}(p) \cup \operatorname{supp}(q),
\qquad
p \cdot q \;=\; \{\, m \cup k \;:\; m \in \operatorname{supp}(p),\; k \in \operatorname{supp}(q)\,\},
$$

with $0 = \varnothing$ (the empty set of monomials) and $1 = \{\varnothing\}$
(the single empty monomial).

These operations make $\mathrm{BPoly}\, n$ a commutative semiring. The defining
algebraic features used throughout are:

- **Idempotent addition:** $p + p = p$ (formalized as `BPoly.add_idem`).
- **Support of a sum is a union:** $\operatorname{supp}(p + q) =
  \operatorname{supp}(p) \cup \operatorname{supp}(q)$ (formalized as
  `BPoly.support_add`).
- **Commutativity** of $+$ and $\times$ (`BPoly.add_comm`, `BPoly.mul_comm`).

This is the *free idempotent commutative semiring* on $n$ generators, isomorphic
to the algebra of monotone Boolean functions in DNF under (OR, AND).

### 2.3 The lifting map

To speak of eliminating the last variable we need to embed $\mathrm{BPoly}\, n$
into $\mathrm{BPoly}(n+1)$.

**Definition 2.4 (Lifting).** The map $\mathrm{liftBPoly} : \mathrm{BPoly}\, n
\to \mathrm{BPoly}(n+1)$ reinterprets each monomial over $\{0,\dots,n-1\}$ as a
monomial over $\{0,\dots,n\}$ that does not use the new variable $x_n$. It is a
semiring homomorphism: $\mathrm{liftBPoly}(f+g) = \mathrm{liftBPoly}(f) +
\mathrm{liftBPoly}(g)$ (`lift_add`) and likewise for products (`lift_mul`).

Thus $\mathrm{liftBPoly}$ identifies $\mathrm{BPoly}\, n$ with the subsemiring of
$\mathrm{BPoly}(n+1)$ of polynomials independent of $x_n$.

## 3. Generated congruences

### 3.1 Definition

**Definition 3.1 (Generated congruence).** Let $R \subseteq \mathrm{BPoly}\, n
\times \mathrm{BPoly}\, n$ be finite. The *generated congruence*
$\mathrm{GeneratedCong}\, R$ is the relation defined inductively by the rules:

- *(gen)* $(a,b) \in R \;\Rightarrow\; a \equiv_R b$;
- *(refl)* $a \equiv_R a$;
- *(symm)* $a \equiv_R b \;\Rightarrow\; b \equiv_R a$;
- *(trans)* $a \equiv_R b,\; b \equiv_R c \;\Rightarrow\; a \equiv_R c$;
- *(add\_left)* $a \equiv_R b \;\Rightarrow\; a + c \equiv_R b + c$;
- *(mul\_left)* $a \equiv_R b \;\Rightarrow\; a \cdot c \equiv_R b \cdot c$.

From left compatibility and commutativity one derives right compatibility
(`add_right`, `mul_right`) and full two-sided compatibility (`add_compat`,
`mul_compat`): if $a \equiv_R b$ and $c \equiv_R d$ then $a + c \equiv_R b + d$
and $a \cdot c \equiv_R b \cdot d$.

### 3.2 Basic properties

**Proposition 3.2 (Monotonicity).** If $R \subseteq S$ then
$a \equiv_R b \Rightarrow a \equiv_S b$ (`generatedCong_mono`).

**Proposition 3.3 (Triviality of the empty relation).**
$a \equiv_{\varnothing} b \Rightarrow a = b$ (`generatedCong_empty`).

**Theorem 3.4 (Universal property, `generatedCong_le`).** Let $C$ be any
relation on $\mathrm{BPoly}\, n$ that is reflexive, symmetric, transitive,
compatible with $+$ and $\times$ on the left, and contains $R$ (i.e.
$C(p_1, p_2)$ holds for every $(p_1,p_2) \in R$). Then $a \equiv_R b
\Rightarrow C(a,b)$. That is, $\mathrm{GeneratedCong}\, R$ is the *least* such
congruence.

*Proof sketch.* Induction on the derivation of $a \equiv_R b$, discharging each
constructor with the corresponding hypothesis on $C$. $\square$

The universal property is the workhorse: to show $\equiv_R$ is contained in some
relation, it suffices to verify that relation is a congruence containing the
generators.

## 4. The elimination congruence

**Definition 4.1 (Elimination congruence).** Given a relation $C$ on
$\mathrm{BPoly}(n+1)$, define the *elimination congruence*
$\mathrm{eliminationCong}\, C$ on $\mathrm{BPoly}\, n$ by

$$
(\mathrm{eliminationCong}\, C)(f, g) \;:=\; C\big(\mathrm{liftBPoly}\, f,\; \mathrm{liftBPoly}\, g\big).
$$

Intuitively, two $x_n$-free polynomials are elimination-equivalent iff their
liftings are $C$-equivalent in the larger algebra. When $C = \mathrm{GeneratedCong}\, R$,
this is precisely the projection of the rulebook $R$ onto the $x_n$-free world.

**Proposition 4.2 (Elimination is a congruence).** For any finite $R \subseteq
\mathrm{BPoly}(n+1)^2$, the relation $\mathrm{eliminationCong}(\mathrm{GeneratedCong}\, R)$
is reflexive, symmetric, transitive, and compatible with $+$ and $\times$ on
$\mathrm{BPoly}\, n$.

*Proof sketch.* Reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity are inherited directly
from $\equiv_R$ (`eliminationCong_refl/symm/trans`). For compatibility one uses
that lifting is a homomorphism: from $\mathrm{liftBPoly}(a+c) = \mathrm{liftBPoly}\,a
+ \mathrm{liftBPoly}\,c$ and `add_left`/`mul_left` of $\equiv_R$ one obtains
`eliminationCong_add_left` and `eliminationCong_mul_left`. $\square$

### 4.1 Idempotent absorption lemmas

The idempotent law yields two pivotal "absorption" lemmas that let one move
freely between a polynomial and its union with a congruent partner.

**Lemma 4.3 (`elimCong_self_union`).** If $f \equiv g$ (in the elimination
congruence) then $f \equiv f + g$.

*Proof sketch.* From $f \equiv g$ and right-compatibility,
$\mathrm{liftBPoly}\,f + \mathrm{liftBPoly}\,f \equiv \mathrm{liftBPoly}\,f +
\mathrm{liftBPoly}\,g$. The left side collapses by `add_idem` to
$\mathrm{liftBPoly}\,f$, and the right side equals $\mathrm{liftBPoly}(f+g)$ by
`lift_add`. $\square$

**Lemma 4.4 (`elimCong_union_self`).** If $f \equiv g$ then $f + g \equiv g$.
*(Symmetric proof.)*

**Lemma 4.5 (Single-monomial step, `elimCong_add_singleton`).** If $f \equiv g$
and $e \in \operatorname{supp}(g)$, then $f \equiv f + \{e\}$, where $\{e\}$ is
the polynomial with the single monomial $e$.

*Proof sketch.* Adding $\{e\}$ to both sides of $f \equiv f+g$ and using that
$e \in \operatorname{supp}(g)$ makes $(f+g) + \{e\} = f + g$ (idempotent
absorption of $e$), one chains $f \equiv f+g$ with $f + \{e\} \equiv f + g$ and
concludes by symmetry and transitivity. $\square$

These lemmas are what make the projected congruence *decomposable into
single-monomial moves*.

## 5. Bounded universes and finite generation

### 5.1 Bounded polynomials

**Definition 5.1 (Bounded polynomials).** For a finite universe of monomials
$U \subseteq \mathrm{BPolyExp}\, n$, let

$$
\mathrm{boundedPolys}(U) \;=\; \{\, p \in \mathrm{BPoly}\, n \;:\; \operatorname{supp}(p) \subseteq U \,\}.
$$

**Proposition 5.2 (`mem_boundedPolys`, `boundedPolys_card_le`).** A polynomial
$p$ lies in $\mathrm{boundedPolys}(U)$ iff $\operatorname{supp}(p) \subseteq U$,
and $|\mathrm{boundedPolys}(U)| \le 2^{|U|}$ (with equality, since the map
$s \mapsto \langle s\rangle$ from subsets of $U$ to polynomials is a bijection
onto the bounded set).

The supporting universe of a relation collects all monomials occurring in it:
$\mathrm{supportUniverse}(R) = \bigcup_{(p,q)\in R}
\operatorname{supp}(p) \cup \operatorname{supp}(q)$.

### 5.2 Finite generation

**Definition 5.3 (Bounded elimination generators, `elimPairsBounded`).** For a
finite monomial universe $V$ over $n$ variables and $R \subseteq
\mathrm{BPoly}(n+1)^2$, let $\mathrm{elimPairsBounded}(V, R)$ be the set of all
pairs $(f, g) \in \mathrm{boundedPolys}(V) \times \mathrm{boundedPolys}(V)$ that
are elimination-congruent.

**Theorem 5.4 (Finite generation, `elim_finitely_generated_bounded`).** There
exists a finite set $S \subseteq \mathrm{BPoly}\, n \times \mathrm{BPoly}\, n$
such that for all $f, g$ with $\operatorname{supp}(f) \subseteq V$ and
$\operatorname{supp}(g) \subseteq V$,

$$
\mathrm{eliminationCong}(\mathrm{GeneratedCong}\, R)(f, g)
\;\Longleftrightarrow\;
\mathrm{GeneratedCong}\, S\,(f, g).
$$

*Proof sketch.* Take $S = \mathrm{elimPairsBounded}(V, R)$.

- *(⇒)* If $f$ and $g$ are bounded and elimination-congruent, then $(f,g) \in S$
  by construction, so $f \equiv_S g$ by the *(gen)* rule.
- *(⇐)* Apply the universal property (Theorem 3.4) with $C =
  \mathrm{eliminationCong}(\mathrm{GeneratedCong}\, R)$. By Proposition 4.2 this
  $C$ is a congruence, and every generator in $S$ is, by definition, an
  elimination-congruent pair, i.e. lies in $C$. Hence $f \equiv_S g \Rightarrow
  C(f,g)$. $\square$

The finiteness of $S$ is immediate because $S \subseteq
\mathrm{boundedPolys}(V)^2$, a finite set of size at most $2^{2|V|}$. The content
of the theorem is that this brute-force generating set is *correct* — it
captures exactly the projected congruence on the bounded vocabulary.

## 6. Join-irreducible witnesses and the main theorem

Theorem 5.4 gives a finite, but enormous and redundant, generating set. The main
theorem replaces it by a canonical one built from atomic moves.

### 6.1 Join-irreducibility

**Definition 6.1 (`IsJoinIrredFinset`).** A finite set $s$ is *join-irreducible*
if it is nonempty and cannot be written as the union of two strictly smaller
subsets:

$$
s \neq \varnothing \quad\wedge\quad \forall a, b \;(a \subsetneq s,\; b \subsetneq s \;\Rightarrow\; a \cup b \neq s).
$$

**Theorem 6.2 (Atoms are singletons, `isJoinIrredFinset_singleton`).** Every
singleton $\{x\}$ is join-irreducible.

*Proof sketch.* The only proper subset of $\{x\}$ is $\varnothing$, and
$\varnothing \cup \varnothing = \varnothing \neq \{x\}$. $\square$

Conversely, any set with $\ge 2$ elements $a, b$ is the union of the proper
subsets $s \setminus \{a\}$ and $s \setminus \{b\}$, hence reducible. So in the
inclusion lattice of finite sets, **the join-irreducible elements are exactly the
singletons** — the atomic, indivisible witnesses.

### 6.2 Join-irreducible witnesses

**Definition 6.3 (`elimJoinIrredWitnesses`).** For $V$ and $R$ as above, the
*join-irreducible elimination witnesses* are the pairs $(f, g) \in
\mathrm{boundedPolys}(V)^2$ such that

1. $f \neq g$;
2. $\operatorname{supp}(f) \setminus \operatorname{supp}(g)$ is join-irreducible
   (equivalently a single monomial); and
3. $f$ and $g$ are elimination-congruent.

**Proposition 6.4 (Soundness, `elimJoinIrredWitnesses_sound`).** Every
join-irreducible witness $(f,g)$ is elimination-congruent.

*Proof.* Immediate from clause (3) of the definition. $\square$

### 6.3 The subset lemma

The crux of the main theorem is the following inductive lemma, which handles the
case where one support contains the other.

**Lemma 6.5 (Subset case, `subset_elim_gen_by_ji`).** Suppose
$\operatorname{supp}(f) \subseteq V$, $\operatorname{supp}(g) \subseteq V$,
$\operatorname{supp}(f) \subseteq \operatorname{supp}(g)$, and $f$ is
elimination-congruent to $g$. Then $f \equiv_W g$, where $W =
\mathrm{elimJoinIrredWitnesses}(V, R)$.

*Proof sketch.* Strong induction on $|\operatorname{supp}(g) \setminus
\operatorname{supp}(f)|$.

- *Base case:* if the difference is empty then $f = g$ (since
  $\operatorname{supp}(f) \subseteq \operatorname{supp}(g)$ and the reverse holds
  vacuously), and reflexivity applies.
- *Inductive step:* pick a monomial $e \in \operatorname{supp}(g) \setminus
  \operatorname{supp}(f)$ and set $h = f + \{e\}$. Then
  $\operatorname{supp}(f) \subseteq \operatorname{supp}(h) \subseteq
  \operatorname{supp}(g)$ and $|\operatorname{supp}(g) \setminus
  \operatorname{supp}(h)| < |\operatorname{supp}(g) \setminus
  \operatorname{supp}(f)|$. By Lemma 4.5, $f$ is elimination-congruent to $h$,
  and the support difference $\operatorname{supp}(h) \setminus
  \operatorname{supp}(f) = \{e\}$ is a singleton, hence join-irreducible
  (Theorem 6.2). Therefore $(h, f) \in W$, giving $f \equiv_W h$ via *(gen)* and
  symmetry. Transitivity of elimination-congruence gives $h$
  elimination-congruent to $g$, so the induction hypothesis yields $h \equiv_W
  g$. Chain $f \equiv_W h \equiv_W g$. $\square$

### 6.4 Main theorem

**Theorem 6.6 (Main theorem, `elim_eq_generate_joinIrred_witnesses`).** For all
$f, g$ with $\operatorname{supp}(f) \subseteq V$ and $\operatorname{supp}(g)
\subseteq V$,

$$
\mathrm{eliminationCong}(\mathrm{GeneratedCong}\, R)(f, g)
\;\Longleftrightarrow\;
\mathrm{GeneratedCong}\,\big(\mathrm{elimJoinIrredWitnesses}(V, R)\big)\,(f, g).
$$

*Proof sketch.*

- *(⇒)* Suppose $f$ is elimination-congruent to $g$. By Lemma 4.3, $f$ is
  elimination-congruent to $f + g$, and by Lemma 4.4, $f + g$ is
  elimination-congruent to $g$. Now $\operatorname{supp}(f) \subseteq
  \operatorname{supp}(f+g)$ and $\operatorname{supp}(g) \subseteq
  \operatorname{supp}(f+g)$ (since support of a sum is a union), and
  $\operatorname{supp}(f+g) \subseteq V$. Apply Lemma 6.5 to the pair $(f, f+g)$
  and to the pair $(g, f+g)$ (using symmetry), obtaining $f \equiv_W f+g$ and
  $g \equiv_W f+g$. Conclude $f \equiv_W g$ by transitivity and symmetry.
- *(⇐)* Apply the universal property (Theorem 3.4) with $C =
  \mathrm{eliminationCong}(\mathrm{GeneratedCong}\, R)$, which is a congruence by
  Proposition 4.2; every generator in $W$ is elimination-congruent by soundness
  (Proposition 6.4). $\square$

The factorization through $f + g$ is the key trick: it reduces the general case
(two arbitrary supports) to two *subset* cases, each handled by peeling off one
monomial at a time. The result is that **every projected equality decomposes
into a chain of single-monomial witnesses** — a canonical atomic presentation of
the elimination congruence.

### 6.5 Algorithmic interface

The witness construction is packaged as an enumerator with matching soundness
and completeness theorems.

**Definition 6.7 (`enumerateProjectedWitnesses`).**
$\mathrm{enumerateProjectedWitnesses}(V, R) := \mathrm{elimJoinIrredWitnesses}(V, R)$.

**Theorem 6.8 (Soundness, `enumerateProjectedWitnesses_sound`).** Every
enumerated pair is elimination-congruent.

**Theorem 6.9 (Completeness, `enumerateProjectedWitnesses_complete`).** For
bounded $f, g$, if $f$ is elimination-congruent to $g$ then $f \equiv g$ in the
congruence generated by the enumerated witnesses.

Together these say the enumerator is *exactly right*: it outputs a generating
set that is sound (no spurious equalities) and complete (no missing ones) for
the bounded elimination congruence.

## 7. Algorithm and complexity

We make the construction explicit. Let $V$ be a monomial universe with $|V| = m$,
and let $N = 2^m = |\mathrm{boundedPolys}(V)|$.

**Algorithm `EliminateVariable(V, R)`** (computes the join-irreducible witness
set and, equivalently, decides bounded elimination-congruence):

1. Enumerate the carrier $\mathcal{B} = \mathrm{boundedPolys}(V)$, of size $N =
   2^m$.
2. Compute the generated congruence $\equiv_R$ on the *full* universe $V \cup
   \{x_n\}$ by congruence closure (union-find seeded with $R$, then iterated
   merges under $+c$ and $\cdot c$ for all carrier elements $c$ until a
   fixpoint).
3. For each ordered pair $(f, g) \in \mathcal{B}^2$ with $f \neq g$ that is
   elimination-congruent (i.e. $\mathrm{liftBPoly}\,f \equiv_R
   \mathrm{liftBPoly}\,g$) and whose support difference
   $\operatorname{supp}(f)\setminus\operatorname{supp}(g)$ is a singleton, emit
   $(f, g)$ as a witness.

**Complexity.** The carrier has $N = 2^m$ polynomials, where $m = |V|$. The
congruence-closure fixpoint touches $O(N^2)$ pairs per round with $O(N)$
compatibility checks each, over $O(N)$ rounds in the worst case, giving a
polynomial-in-$N$ (hence exponential-in-$m$) bound. Witness extraction in step 3
is $O(N^2)$ pair tests, each an $O(m)$ singleton-difference check. The output —
the join-irreducible witnesses — has size $O(N^2)$ in the worst case but is the
*canonical minimal* atomic generating set, dramatically smaller than the
$O(N^2)$ brute-force set of *all* congruent bounded pairs in practical instances,
because every non-atomic equality is recovered as a derived chain rather than
stored explicitly.

The exponential dependence on $m$ is intrinsic: the carrier of a Boolean
polynomial algebra over $m$ monomials genuinely has $2^m$ elements, mirroring the
$\mathrm{coNP}$-hardness of Boolean reasoning in general. The theorems guarantee
*correctness and canonicity*; the bounded universe is the lever that turns an a
priori infinite projection into a finite computation.

## 8. Applications

**Quantifier elimination for monotone Boolean theories.** Under the (OR, AND)
dictionary, eliminating the last variable is existential projection of a system
of monotone Boolean constraints. Theorem 6.6 says the projected theory has a
canonical presentation by single-monomial implications — directly relevant to
model checking and automated reasoning, where minimal projected presentations
control both termination and output size.

**Database dependency projection.** Boolean polynomials model sets of attribute
combinations; idempotent addition models the union of such combinations.
Projecting away an attribute while preserving all induced dependencies is exactly
elimination, and the join-irreducible witnesses give a compact, canonical cover
of the projected dependencies.

**Circuit and DNF simplification.** Monotone DNF formulas are Boolean polynomials
verbatim. Reducing variable count while preserving all logical consequences is a
synthesis primitive; the atomic-witness theorem provides a principled,
minimal-move simplification target.

**A combinatorial template for elimination.** More broadly, the result is a clean
template: in any idempotent semiring whose addition is a lattice join, the
join-irreducible elements furnish the atoms of an elimination calculus. The
identification "atoms = singletons" here is the simplest nontrivial instance, and
the proof architecture (absorption lemmas + subset induction + factorization
through the join) is reusable.

## 9. Discussion

The two theorems answer the two classic worries about elimination in this
algebra. Finite generation (Theorem 5.4) dispels the fear that a finite rulebook
could cast an irreducibly infinite shadow. Canonical atomic generation (Theorem
6.6) goes further: it shows the shadow has a *smallest, most elementary*
description, where every equality is a chain of single-monomial steps.

Three features of the proof deserve emphasis. First, **idempotence is the engine**:
the law $p + p = p$ is what permits adding a monomial to one side without
disturbing the other, enabling the one-at-a-time peeling in Lemma 6.5. Second,
**the lattice viewpoint supplies the atoms**: recognizing single monomials as the
join-irreducible elements of the inclusion lattice is what makes "atomic witness"
a precise, structural notion rather than an ad hoc one. Third, **the universal
property does the heavy lifting** for every "reverse" containment, reducing it to
checking that elimination-congruence is a congruence containing the chosen
generators.

## 10. Future directions

The formalization that anchors this paper sits within a broader program. The
adjacent development on *persistence stability* (the "Boltzmann Bridge" line of
work) closes the gap between combinatorial filtration machinery and the
robustness theory that makes persistent homology usable on noisy data. There,
fully formalized and free of gaps, one now has: functoriality of the inclusion
preorder on abstract simplicial complexes and the connecting maps of the
sublevel persistence module; the algebraic stability theorem as a
$\delta$-interleaving of sublevel families, its additive composition law
(triangle inequality), and the symmetric two-sided form; and lattice
compatibility of sublevel / Vietoris–Rips complexes with `min`. These turn the
filtration calculus into a genuine functor and equip the space of filtrations
with a sub-additive interleaving pseudometric.

Two concrete next steps stand out.

**(1) The interleaving pseudometric is a genuine pseudometric.** The additive
composition law is exactly the triangle inequality, and the two-sided form gives
symmetry, for the candidate distance
$d(F, G) = \inf\{\delta \ge 0 : \forall \sigma,\ |F.\mathrm{weight}\,\sigma -
G.\mathrm{weight}\,\sigma| \le \delta\}$. Defining this as a weighted sup-metric
$\sup_\sigma |F.\mathrm{weight}\,\sigma - G.\mathrm{weight}\,\sigma|$ and proving
the pseudometric axioms reduces the metric obligations to elementary facts about
suprema of absolute values — no homological computation required. With stability
and composition already in hand, the remaining obligations are pure order /
supremum lemmas.

**(2) A stable Euler-characteristic curve.** Generalize the Euler characteristic
of a full simplex to an arbitrary finite complex $K$ via $\mathrm{eulerChar}\,K =
\sum_\sigma (-1)^{|\sigma| - 1}$ over nonempty faces, then define the Euler curve
$t \mapsto \mathrm{eulerChar}(F.\mathrm{sublevelComplex}\,t)$ of a filtration.
The goals are (a) right-continuity as a step function with finitely many jumps
for finitely supported filtrations, and (b) a stability bound: if $d(F, G) \le
\delta$ then the Euler curves agree outside a set of measure $\le 2\delta \cdot
(\text{number of faces})$.

For the elimination theory specifically, natural extensions include: lifting the
*bounded* finite-generation result to an *unbounded* (still finitely presented)
statement; characterizing the minimal witness set up to redundancy; generalizing
the "atoms = singletons" identification to arbitrary idempotent semirings whose
addition is a lattice join; and connecting the single-monomial witness calculus
to incremental SAT and to database chase procedures.

## 11. Conclusion

We have shown that variable elimination in the idempotent semiring of Boolean
polynomials is finite and canonical: the projection of a finitely generated
congruence onto a bounded vocabulary is again finitely generated (Theorem 5.4),
and is generated by its join-irreducible witnesses — the single-monomial moves
(Theorem 6.6). The supporting lattice fact that the join-irreducible finite sets
are exactly the singletons (Theorem 6.2) pins down what "atomic" means, and the
soundness/completeness of the witness enumerator (Theorems 6.8, 6.9) turns the
mathematics into an algorithm. Together these results recast elimination from an
unbounded algebraic cancellation problem into a finite combinatorial engine
powered by join-irreducible witness extraction.
