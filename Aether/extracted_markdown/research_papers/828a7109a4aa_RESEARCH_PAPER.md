# Phantom Topologies: Observer-Dependent Topological Spaces

## Abstract

We introduce **phantom topologies**, a framework in which the topological structure of a space depends on an observer parameter. A phantom system on a type $X$ indexed by observers $O$ assigns to each observer $o \in O$ a topology $T(o)$ on $X$. The **consensus topology** — the coarsest topology whose open sets are exactly those open in every observer's view — captures the "shared reality" visible to all observers simultaneously.

We establish fundamental structural results: (1) the consensus topology is fully characterized by the universal quantifier over observers; (2) continuous maps between phantom systems lift to consensus; (3) the $T_1$ separation axiom is preserved by consensus while $T_2$ (Hausdorff) is conjectured not to be; (4) phantom systems satisfy an idempotence principle — systems where all observers already see the consensus are necessarily collapsed; (5) the consensus operation distributes over the supremum of phantom systems.

All results are formalized and machine-verified in Lean 4 with Mathlib, building on the complete lattice structure of `TopologicalSpace X`.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Motivation

In classical topology, a topological space $(X, \tau)$ consists of a set $X$ and a fixed topology $\tau$. The topology is an intrinsic property of the space, independent of any observer or measurement process.

However, in many applications — from quantum mechanics to distributed systems to social epistemology — the relevant notion of "nearness" or "openness" depends on the observer. Different instruments may resolve different features; different agents may have access to different information. The question arises: can we formalize a notion of topology that is inherently observer-dependent?

### 1.2 Related Work

The lattice structure of topologies on a fixed set has been studied extensively since Birkhoff (1936). The complete lattice $\text{Top}(X)$ of all topologies on $X$ is well-understood: the infimum is the topology generated by the union of open set families, while the supremum is the intersection of open set families.

Our work builds on this lattice structure but shifts the focus from individual topologies to indexed families, with the consensus (supremum in the Mathlib convention) as the primary object of study.

### 1.3 Lattice Convention

In Mathlib (Lean 4), the order on `TopologicalSpace X` is defined such that $t_1 \leq t_2$ iff every open set in $t_2$ is also open in $t_1$ — that is, $t_1$ is **finer** than $t_2$. Under this convention:
- $\bot$ = discrete topology (finest, all subsets open)
- $\top$ = indiscrete topology (coarsest, only $\emptyset$ and $X$ open)
- $\sup$ = intersection of open set families = consensus
- $\inf$ = topology generated by union of open set families

The consensus of a family corresponds to $\sup$ (supremum) in this lattice.

## 2. Definitions

### 2.1 Phantom System

**Definition 2.1.** A *phantom system* on a type $X$ indexed by an observer type $O$ is a function
$$T : O \to \text{Top}(X)$$
assigning to each observer $o \in O$ a topological space structure on $X$.

In Lean 4:
```
structure PhantomSystem (O : Type*) (X : Type*) where
  observe : O → TopologicalSpace X
```

### 2.2 Consensus Topology

**Definition 2.2.** The *consensus topology* of a phantom system $T$ is
$$\tau_{\text{cons}} = \bigsqcup_{o \in O} T(o)$$
where $\bigsqcup$ denotes the supremum in the lattice of topologies (equivalently, the intersection of open set families).

A set $U \subseteq X$ is open in the consensus iff $U$ is open in $T(o)$ for every $o \in O$.

### 2.3 Phantom Representation

**Definition 2.3.** A *phantom representation* of a topology $\tau$ on $X$ with observer set $O$ is a phantom system $T : O \to \text{Top}(X)$ such that $\tau_{\text{cons}} = \tau$.

### 2.4 Agreement and Disagreement

**Definition 2.4.** For observers $o_1, o_2 \in O$:
- The *agreement* $A(o_1, o_2) = \{U \subseteq X \mid U \in T(o_1) \cap T(o_2)\}$
- The *disagreement* $D(o_1, o_2) = \{U \subseteq X \mid U \in T(o_1) \setminus T(o_2)\}$

### 2.5 Phantom Spectrum

**Definition 2.5.** The *phantom spectrum* of a set $U$ is
$$\text{Spec}(U) = \{o \in O \mid U \in T(o)\}$$

### 2.6 Phantom Number

**Definition 2.6.** The *phantom number* of a topology $\tau$ is
$$\text{pn}(\tau) = \inf\{|O| : \exists T : O \to \text{Top}(X),\ \tau_{\text{cons}} = \tau,\ |O| < \infty\}$$

## 3. Main Results

### 3.1 Consensus Characterization

**Theorem 3.1** (Fundamental Characterization). *A set $U$ is open in the consensus topology if and only if every observer sees it as open:*
$$U \in \tau_{\text{cons}} \iff \forall o \in O,\ U \in T(o)$$

*Proof.* Immediate from `isOpen_iSup_iff` in Mathlib, which characterizes the open sets of a supremum of topologies. □

**Corollary 3.2.** *The consensus topology is coarser than each individual observer's topology.*

**Theorem 3.3** (Spectrum Characterization). *$U \in \tau_{\text{cons}}$ if and only if $\text{Spec}(U) = O$.*

### 3.2 Continuous Map Lifting

**Theorem 3.4** (Consensus Continuous Lift). *Let $P, Q$ be phantom systems on $X, Y$ respectively, both indexed by $O$. If $f : X \to Y$ is continuous with respect to $T_P(o)$ and $T_Q(o)$ for every observer $o$, then $f$ is continuous with respect to the consensus topologies.*

*Proof.* Let $U$ be open in $Q$'s consensus. Then $U$ is open in $T_Q(o)$ for all $o$. Since $f$ is continuous for each observer, $f^{-1}(U)$ is open in $T_P(o)$ for all $o$. Hence $f^{-1}(U)$ is open in $P$'s consensus. □

This theorem shows that the consensus construction is **functorial**: it preserves morphisms (continuous maps).

### 3.3 Separation Axiom Transfer

**Theorem 3.5** ($T_1$ Preservation). *If every observer's topology is $T_1$ and $O$ is nonempty, the consensus topology is $T_1$.*

*Proof sketch.* A space is $T_1$ iff every singleton $\{x\}$ is closed, equivalently $\{x\}^c$ is open. Since each $T(o)$ is $T_1$, $\{x\}^c$ is open in every $T(o)$, hence open in the consensus. □

**Remark 3.6.** The $T_2$ (Hausdorff) axiom is **conjectured** not to be preserved by consensus in general. The key difference: $T_1$ requires each singleton complement to be open (a condition on individual sets), while $T_2$ requires the *existence* of disjoint open neighborhoods (a condition on pairs of sets), and the required neighborhoods for different observers may be incompatible.

### 3.4 Idempotence and Collapse

**Definition 3.7.** A phantom system *collapses* if all observers see the same topology.

**Theorem 3.8** (Phantom Idempotence). *If every observer sees the consensus topology ($T(o) = \tau_{\text{cons}}$ for all $o$), then the system collapses.*

*Proof.* For any $o_1, o_2$: $T(o_1) = \tau_{\text{cons}} = T(o_2)$. □

**Theorem 3.9** (Collapse Characterization). *If a system collapses and $O$ is nonempty, the consensus equals any single observer's topology.*

**Theorem 3.10** (Collapsed Disagreement). *A collapsed system has empty disagreement between all observer pairs.*

### 3.5 Monotonicity Principles

**Theorem 3.11** (Refinement Monotonicity). *If each observer in system $P_1$ sees a finer topology than in $P_2$, then $P_1$'s consensus is finer than $P_2$'s.*

*Proof.* Monotonicity of $\sup$. □

**Theorem 3.12** (Observer Anti-Monotonicity). *Restricting to a subset of observers produces a finer consensus. More observers → coarser consensus.*

*Proof.* The supremum over a subset is bounded by the supremum over the full set. □

### 3.6 Algebraic Structure

**Theorem 3.13** (Two-Observer Characterization). *The consensus of a 2-observer system with topologies $\tau_1, \tau_2$ is $\tau_1 \sqcup \tau_2$.*

**Theorem 3.14** (Two-Observer Open Sets). *$U \in \tau_1 \sqcup \tau_2$ iff $U \in \tau_1$ and $U \in \tau_2$.*

**Theorem 3.15** (Consensus Sup Distribution). *For phantom systems $P, Q$ on the same observer and base types:*
$$\text{cons}\big(\{T_P(o) \sqcup T_Q(o)\}_{o \in O}\big) = \text{cons}(P) \sqcup \text{cons}(Q)$$

*Proof.* This is `iSup_sup_eq` from Mathlib. □

### 3.7 Disagreement Structure

**Theorem 3.16** (Disagreement Disjointness). *$D(o_1, o_2)$ and $D(o_2, o_1)$ are disjoint.*

*Proof.* A set cannot simultaneously be in $T(o_1) \setminus T(o_2)$ and $T(o_2) \setminus T(o_1)$. □

**Theorem 3.17** (Agreement Symmetry). *$A(o_1, o_2) = A(o_2, o_1)$.*

**Theorem 3.18** (Self-Agreement). *$A(o, o) = T(o)$.*

### 3.8 Phantom Morphisms

**Definition 3.19.** A *phantom morphism* $\varphi : P_1 \to P_2$ consists of a map $\varphi_O : O_1 \to O_2$ such that $T_1(o) = T_2(\varphi_O(o))$ for all $o$.

**Theorem 3.20** (Surjective Morphisms Preserve Consensus). *If $\varphi_O$ is surjective, then $\text{cons}(P_1) = \text{cons}(P_2)$.*

### 3.9 Boundary Results

**Theorem 3.21** (Empty Observers). *The consensus of the empty phantom system is the discrete topology ($\bot$).*

**Theorem 3.22** (Phantom Number Bound). *Every topology has phantom number $\leq 1$.*

## 4. Algorithms

### 4.1 Finite Phantom Number Computation

For a finite set $X$ with $|X| = n$, the phantom number can be computed by:

1. Enumerate all topologies on $X$ (the number of topologies on $\{1, \ldots, n\}$ grows rapidly: 1, 4, 29, 355, 6942, ...)
2. For $k = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$, check all $k$-tuples of topologies
3. Return the first $k$ for which some tuple has consensus equal to the target

**Complexity.** Let $T(n)$ be the number of topologies on $n$ points. The algorithm has complexity $O(T(n)^k \cdot 2^n)$ for checking $k$ observers. This is practical only for $n \leq 4$.

### 4.2 Consensus Computation

Given $m$ topologies on $X$, each represented as a set of open sets:
- **Input**: $T_1, \ldots, T_m \subseteq \mathcal{P}(X)$
- **Output**: $T_1 \cap \cdots \cap T_m$
- **Complexity**: $O(m \cdot |\text{max topology}|)$ using hash set intersection

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 The T₁/T₂ Boundary

The most intriguing open question from this work concerns the boundary between separation axioms that transfer through consensus and those that don't. We proved that $T_1$ transfers; we conjecture that $T_2$ does not (for infinite spaces).

The structural reason: $T_1$ is defined by conditions on individual sets ($\{x\}^c$ is open), which transfer through intersection. $T_2$ requires the *simultaneous existence* of compatible open sets (disjoint neighborhoods), which may not survive intersection.

### 5.2 Connections to Other Frameworks

**Quantum mechanics.** The phantom topology framework mirrors the quantum measurement problem: each "observer" corresponds to a measurement basis, and the consensus corresponds to the decoherence-free subspace — the states that all measurement bases agree on.

**Distributed systems.** In distributed computing, different nodes may have different views of the network topology. The consensus topology corresponds to the guaranteed connectivity — edges that all nodes agree exist.

**Category theory.** Phantom systems are functors $O \to \text{Top}(X)$ where $\text{Top}(X)$ is viewed as a thin category. The consensus is the colimit. This suggests a categorical generalization to phantom structures in arbitrary categories.

### 5.3 The Proper Phantom Number

The phantom number as defined is always $\leq 1$ (trivially). A more interesting invariant is the **proper phantom number**: the minimum number of *strictly finer* topologies whose consensus is the given topology. This measures the genuine "decomposability" of a topology in the lattice.

Computing proper phantom numbers for topologies on $\mathbb{R}$ would require understanding the sublattice structure of $\text{Top}(\mathbb{R})$ far more deeply than is currently known.
