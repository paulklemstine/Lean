# Idempotent Spectral Duality for EML Semiring Operators via Max-Plus Perron Characters

## Abstract

We develop a formally verified theory of tropical (max-plus) spectral analysis for finitely generated EML (Exp-Minus-Log) semiring modules. Working in Lean 4 with Mathlib, we prove the tropical Perron–Frobenius theorem: every n×n real matrix admits a max-plus eigenvector with eigenvalue equal to the maximal cycle mean. We then establish the **EML eigencharacter theorem**, which lifts this spectral data to finitely generated invariant subsemimodules of EML algebras. The eigencharacter grows exactly linearly under iteration, with slope given by the tropical spectral radius. These results constitute a formally verified spectral backbone for idempotent operator theory, connecting three domains: max-plus linear algebra, EML function algebras, and computational graph optimization.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The Problem

Classical spectral theory — the study of eigenvalues and eigenvectors — is one of the most powerful tools in mathematics. The Perron–Frobenius theorem for nonneg­ative matrices underpins applications from Google's PageRank to population dynamics. But what happens when we replace the familiar arithmetic of real numbers with the *tropical* (max-plus) arithmetic, where "addition" is maximum and "multiplication" is ordinary addition?

This question, far from being a mere curiosity, lies at the heart of:
- **Discrete event systems**: manufacturing, transportation networks, digital circuits
- **Optimization**: shortest/longest path problems, dynamic programming
- **Algebraic geometry**: tropical varieties and their connections to classical geometry
- **Neural networks**: ReLU activations naturally create piecewise-linear (tropical) maps

### 1.2 Our Contribution

We provide the first (to our knowledge) formally verified proof of the **tropical Perron–Frobenius theorem** in Lean 4, and establish a **spectral duality bridge** to finitely generated EML semiring modules. Our main results:

1. **Tropical Perron Theorem** (`exists_tropEigenpair_eq_maxCycleMean`): For any n×n real matrix M with n ≥ 1, there exists v : Fin n → ℝ such that
   $$\max_j (M_{ij} + v_j) = \mu + v_i \quad \text{for all } i$$
   where μ is the maximal cycle mean of M.

2. **EML Eigencharacter Theorem** (`exists_eigencharacter_of_presentation`): For any finitely generated invariant presentation of an EML operator, there exists a tropical character χ with eigenvalue μ = maxCycleMean(coeff).

3. **Iterate Growth Formula** (`eigencharacter_iterate_growth`): Under the eigencharacter, the k-th tropical power satisfies
   $$\max_j ((M^{k+1})_{ij} + \chi_j) = (k+1)\mu + \chi_i$$

## 2. Mathematical Framework

### 2.1 The Max-Plus Semiring

The **max-plus semiring** (ℝ_max, ⊕, ⊗) is defined on ℝ ∪ {−∞} with:
- a ⊕ b = max(a, b)  (tropical addition)
- a ⊗ b = a + b      (tropical multiplication)

The additive identity is −∞ and the multiplicative identity is 0. We work primarily over ℝ (without −∞) since all our matrices have finite entries.

### 2.2 Tropical Matrix Operations

For an n×n matrix M over ℝ, the **max-plus matrix-vector product** is:
$$(M \otimes v)_i = \bigoplus_j M_{ij} \otimes v_j = \max_j (M_{ij} + v_j)$$

The **max-plus matrix product** is:
$$(A \otimes B)_{ij} = \bigoplus_k A_{ik} \otimes B_{kj} = \max_k (A_{ik} + B_{kj})$$

These operations are associative (proved as `maxPlusMM_assoc` in our formalization).

### 2.3 Walks, Cycles, and the Spectral Radius

A **directed walk** of length ℓ in the weighted digraph of M visits vertices v₀, v₁, ..., v_ℓ with total weight Σᵢ M(vᵢ, vᵢ₊₁). Entry (M^ℓ)ᵢⱼ equals the maximum weight walk of length ℓ from i to j.

A **simple cycle** is a nonempty list of distinct vertices traversed cyclically. The **cycle mean** is the total weight divided by the cycle length. The **maximal cycle mean** μ = maxCycleMean(M) is the supremum of all cycle means — the tropical analogue of the spectral radius.

We prove that this supremum is always achieved by some simple cycle (`exists_critical_cycle`), that every simple cycle has mean ≤ μ (`cycleMean_le_maxCycleMean`), and that the set of cycle means is bounded above (`cycleMeanSet_bddAbove`).

## 3. The Tropical Perron–Frobenius Theorem

### 3.1 Statement

**Theorem** (Tropical Perron). For any n×n real matrix M with n ≥ 1, there exists v : Fin n → ℝ such that for all i:
$$\max_j (M_{ij} + v_j) = \mu + v_i$$
where μ = maxCycleMean(M).

### 3.2 Proof Strategy

Our proof follows the **critical graph potential method**:

1. **Find a critical cycle** c* achieving cycleMean = μ, with a vertex s on this cycle.

2. **Construct the eigenvector**: v(j) = max_{k=0}^{n-1} ((M^{k+1})_{j,s} − (k+1)μ)

3. **Verify the eigenvector equation** by showing two finite maxima over shifted index ranges are equal:
   - LHS = max_{k=1}^{n} ((M^k)_{i,s} − kμ)
   - RHS = max_{k=0}^{n-1} ((M^k)_{i,s} − kμ)

4. The **upper bound** (k=n term ≤ others) uses cycle removal via the pigeonhole principle: walks of length n+1 visit n+2 vertices, so some vertex repeats, forming a cycle with mean ≤ μ.

5. The **lower bound** (k=0 term ≤ others) uses the critical cycle: prepending the critical cycle of length lc and weight lc·μ improves the k=0 value.

### 3.3 Formalization Notes

In Lean 4, we represent:
- Matrices as `Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℝ`
- The max-plus product using `Finset.sup'` over `Finset.univ`
- Cycles as `List (Fin n)` with the `IsSimpleCycle` predicate (nonempty + nodup)
- The maximal cycle mean via `sSup` of the image set

The `[NeZero n]` typeclass ensures n ≥ 1, guaranteeing nonempty finsets for the sup operations. We proved 15+ lemmas building up to the main theorem, all verified by the Lean kernel.

## 4. The EML Bridge

### 4.1 Finitely Generated Invariant Presentations

An EML algebra A equipped with an endomorphism T admits a **finitely generated invariant presentation** when there exist generators g₁, ..., gₙ such that each T(gᵢ) can be expressed as a tropical linear combination of the generators:
$$T(g_i) = \bigoplus_j c_{ij} \otimes g_j$$

The coefficient matrix C = (cᵢⱼ) encodes the action of T on the generated subsemimodule.

### 4.2 Tropical Characters

A **tropical character** χ assigns a real value χ(gᵢ) to each generator, extending to tropical linear combinations by:
$$\chi\left(\bigoplus_j c_j \otimes g_j\right) = \max_j (c_j + \chi(g_j))$$

### 4.3 The Eigencharacter Theorem

**Theorem** (EML Eigencharacter). Given a finitely generated invariant presentation with coefficient matrix C, there exists a tropical character χ such that:
$$\chi(T(g_i)) = \mu + \chi(g_i) \quad \text{for all } i$$
where μ = maxCycleMean(C).

This follows immediately from the tropical Perron theorem: the eigenvector v of C defines χ(gᵢ) = vᵢ.

### 4.4 Iterate Growth

**Theorem** (Iterate Growth). Under the eigencharacter, the k-th iterate satisfies:
$$\chi(T^k(g_i)) = (k+1)\mu + \chi(g_i)$$

This is proved by induction on k, using the associativity of max-plus multiplication and the eigenvector equation.

## 5. Applications

### 5.1 Discrete Event Systems

In manufacturing, each "event" (machine processing, material transport) has a duration. The system's throughput — the maximum sustainable production rate — equals the reciprocal of the maximal cycle mean of the system's transition matrix. Our theorem guarantees the existence of a steady-state operating mode (the eigenvector) achieving this throughput.

### 5.2 Network Optimization

In communication or transportation networks, max-plus algebra models the "bottleneck" routing problem: find the path maximizing the minimum link capacity. The eigenvalue μ characterizes the network's capacity, and the eigenvector gives optimal routing potentials.

### 5.3 Neural Network Analysis

ReLU neural networks compute piecewise-linear functions, which are tropical rational maps. The tropical spectral radius of the weight matrices bounds the network's Lipschitz constant and controls gradient flow during training.

### 5.4 Program Analysis

Weighted automata track resource consumption (time, space, energy) of programs. The maximal cycle mean gives the worst-case asymptotic growth rate per loop iteration — a key metric for program verification and optimization.

## 6. Discussion: A Bridge Between Worlds

### For the General Reader

Imagine you're managing a factory with three machines. Machine A takes 2 hours and feeds into Machine B. Machine B takes 3 hours and feeds into Machine C. Machine C takes 1 hour and feeds back into Machine A. What's the fastest this factory can produce one unit?

You might think: add up all the times (6 hours total, 3 machines, so 2 hours per unit). But what if there are parallel paths? What if some machines can work simultaneously? The classical approach — linear algebra with multiplication and addition — breaks down because the factory's throughput is determined by the **slowest cycle**, not the average.

This is where tropical mathematics enters. In the tropical world, "addition" is maximum (the bottleneck determines the rate) and "multiplication" is ordinary addition (times accumulate along a path). The **maximal cycle mean** — the heaviest cycle divided by its length — gives the exact bottleneck rate. And the **eigenvector** tells you how to stagger the machines' schedules for optimal throughput.

Our theorem proves that this optimal schedule always exists, no matter how complex the factory. More remarkably, it shows that this scheduling principle extends to a much richer class of mathematical objects — EML semiring algebras — where exponentials and logarithms intertwine with the tropical structure.

### Historical Context

The max-plus Perron–Frobenius theorem was first established in the 1960s–70s by Romanovsky, Cuninghame-Green, and Vorobyev, with the definitive treatment by Baccelli, Cohen, Olsder, and Quadrat (1992). The critical graph approach we formalize follows Gaubert's thesis (1992). Our contribution is the first machine-verified proof and the explicit bridge to EML operator theory.

### The Significance of Formal Verification

Why formalize a theorem that's been known for decades? Because:

1. **Correctness guarantees**: The proof is checked by the Lean kernel — there are no gaps, handwaving, or "left to the reader" steps.

2. **Composability**: Formally verified theorems can be composed into larger verified systems. Our EML bridge theorem chains the Perron theorem with operator theory, and the computer ensures the chain is valid.

3. **Foundation for computation**: Our definitions are computable in principle, enabling verified algorithms for tropical optimization.

## 7. Conclusion

We have established the formal foundations of tropical spectral theory in Lean 4, proving the max-plus Perron–Frobenius theorem and its lift to EML semiring modules. The key innovation is the **eigencharacter bridge**: a finitely generated EML operator inherits a canonical spectral observable from its coefficient matrix, growing linearly at the tropical spectral radius.

This opens several directions: tropical Jordan decomposition, spectral decomposition by critical components, Collatz–Wielandt duality, and tropical Koopman theory for dynamical systems. Each direction benefits from the rigorous foundation we have laid.
