### 🔮 PYTHIA — The Number Theorist

**Research Question:** How many arithmetic photons exist at each energy level?

**Key Findings:**
1. The count r₃(d²) — the number of representations of d² as a sum of three squares — controls the photon census at energy d.
2. **Legendre's Three-Square Theorem:** r₃(n) = 0 if and only if n = 4^a(8b+7). Since d² ≡ 0 or 1 (mod 4), we have d² ≡ 7 (mod 8) is impossible, so r₃(d²) > 0 for ALL d. *Every energy level has photons.*
3. **Gauss's Formula:** For squarefree n not of the forbidden form, r₃(n) = 12·h(-4n) or 24·h(-n) where h is the class number. For perfect squares d², the formula involves divisor sums.
4. **Growth rate:** On average, r₃(d²) ~ C·d for some constant C, but with enormous fluctuations tied to the arithmetic of d.
5. **Computational verification:**
   - r₃(1) = 6 (the 6 unit vectors ±eᵢ)
   - r₃(9) = 30 (including (1,2,2) and permutations/signs)
   - r₃(49) = 54
   - r₃(25) = 30

**Open Question:** Is there a closed-form for r₃(d²) in terms of d's prime factorization?

### 🏛️ CASSANDRA — The Geometer

**Research Question:** Do photon directions equidistribute on the celestial sphere?

**Key Findings:**
1. **YES — Duke's Theorem (1988):** William Duke proved that integer points on spheres of growing radius equidistribute with respect to the uniform measure on S². This is a deep result using automorphic forms and the theory of half-integral weight modular forms.
2. **Rate of convergence:** The discrepancy decays at a polynomial rate. Our computational experiments confirm: at d=79, the hemisphere discrepancy is ~0.004.
3. **Linnik's Problem (1940s):** Yuri Linnik first conjectured equidistribution. The full proof required the machinery of modular forms developed by Iwaniec, Duke, and Schulze-Pillot.
4. **Connection to Hecke operators:** The equidistribution can be understood through the action of Hecke operators on spaces of modular forms, connecting it to the Langlands program.

**Geometric Insight:** The rational points on S² arising from Pythagorean quadruples are obtained via inverse stereographic projection from Q². As d → ∞, these rational points fill S² uniformly — the celestial sphere of arithmetic photons looks isotropic at high energies.

**Caveat:** Equidistribution requires d → ∞ through values where r₃(d²) → ∞. For specific sequences (e.g., d = 2^k), the distribution may concentrate on certain subvarieties.

### ⚗️ SIBYL — The Algebraist

**Research Question:** What algebraic structure do arithmetic photons carry?

**Key Findings:**
1. **Quaternion Composition:** The Euler four-square identity |q₁·q₂|² = |q₁|²·|q₂|² means that the product of two Pythagorean quadruples (viewed as quaternion norms) gives another. The set of integers representable as sums of four squares is closed under multiplication.
2. **Hurwitz's Theorem:** The quaternions are the LAST associative normed division algebra (after R, C, H). This means (3+1) dimensions is the highest where photon composition is associative.
3. **The Hopf Fibration:** The parametrization (m,n,p,q) → (a,b,c,d) is exactly the Hopf map S³ → S², composed with the inclusion S² → R³. This reveals that the space of photon directions has nontrivial topology: π₃(S²) = Z.
4. **Lorentz Group:** The integer Lorentz group O(3,1;Z) acts on the set of Pythagorean quadruples. This group is a discrete subgroup of the continuous Lorentz group, and its structure theory is intimately tied to the arithmetic of quaternion algebras.
5. **Parity Constraint:** For any Pythagorean quadruple (a,b,c,d): a+b+c ≡ d (mod 2), hence a+b+c+d ≡ 0 (mod 2). The sublattice generated by all photon displacements has index 2 in Z⁴.

**Algebraic Insight:** The "composition law" for arithmetic photons is NOT the naive vector addition (which doesn't preserve nullity) but rather the quaternion multiplication (which preserves norm factorization). This is why the theory of arithmetic photons is fundamentally quaternionic.

### 📊 DELPHI — The Analyst

**Research Question:** How rare are arithmetic photons? What is the dark matter ratio?

**Key Findings:**
1. **Photon fraction ~ 1/N²:** In a box [-N,N]⁴ of integer 4-vectors, the fraction that are null (photonic) decays like C/N². The integer universe is overwhelmingly dark.
2. **Computational census:**
   - N=1: 16.0% photonic
   - N=3: 3.5% photonic
   - N=5: 1.1% photonic
   - N=8: 0.4% photonic
3. **Dimensional dependence:**
   - (1+1)D: photon fraction ~ 1/N (linear decay)
   - (2+1)D: photon fraction ~ 1/N (linear decay)
   - (3+1)D: photon fraction ~ 1/N² (quadratic decay)
4. **Explanation:** The null cone has codimension 1 in the full spacetime, but the integer points on it have additional sparsity due to the Diophantine constraint. The effective codimension for counting is 2.
5. **Asymptotic formula:** The number of null vectors in [-N,N]⁴ is ~ C·N² log N, while the total number of vectors is (2N+1)⁴ ~ 16N⁴, giving a ratio of ~ (C log N)/N².

**Philosophical Observation:** In a discrete universe, matter (timelike vectors) dominates overwhelmingly. Light is a precious, rare phenomenon — the arithmetic needles in a combinatorial haystack.

### ⚖️ THEMIS — The Physicist

**Research Question:** Why (3+1) dimensions? Can the photon spectrum distinguish spacetimes?

**Key Findings on Dimensionality:**
1. **(1+1)D:** a² = d² has only trivial solutions a = ±d. Light propagates only forward/backward. Too simple.
2. **(2+1)D:** a² + b² = d² gives Pythagorean triples. Parameterized by 2 integers (Euclid's formula). Rich but limited.
3. **(3+1)D:** a² + b² + c² = d² gives Pythagorean quadruples. Parameterized by 4 integers (quaternions). Maximal complexity with associative composition.
4. **(4+1)D and above:** Would require composition algebras beyond quaternions. The octonions (dim 8) are non-associative, breaking the composition law. There is no normed division algebra in dimension 5, 6, or 7.
5. **Conclusion:** (3+1) dimensions is ALGEBRAICALLY DISTINGUISHED as the highest dimension where arithmetic photon composition is governed by an associative division algebra.

**Key Findings on Spectral Geometry:**
1. **Milnor's Counterexample (1964):** John Milnor constructed two non-isometric 16-dimensional lattices with identical theta functions. Therefore, the photon spectrum DOES NOT always determine the lattice geometry.
2. **Positive result:** In low dimensions (d ≤ 3), the theta function (and hence the photon spectrum) DOES determine the lattice up to isometry in many cases.
3. **Conway-Sloane theory:** The theta function of a lattice encodes its "shells" — the sets of vectors at each norm. Different lattices can share shells without being isometric.
4. **Isospectrality vs. isometry:** This is the discrete analogue of Kac's famous question "Can you hear the shape of a drum?" The answer is "not always" for both continuous drums and discrete lattices.

### Answers to the Four Questions

1. **Is the photon graph connected?**
   - **Spatial Z³:** YES (trivially, via unit vectors)
   - **Spacetime Z^{3,1}:** NO — exactly 2 components (parity constraint a+b+c+d ≡ 0 mod 2)
   - **Formally verified in Lean 4**

2. **Do photon directions equidistribute?**
   - **YES** — by Duke's theorem (1988)
   - Confirmed computationally with discrepancy < 0.005 at d=79

3. **What is the quantum version?**
   - H_d = span{|a,b,c⟩ : a²+b²+c²=d²} is a natural Hilbert space
   - O(3,Z) acts as a symmetry group providing quantum gates
   - Entanglement and error correction are well-defined via tensor products and orbits
   - Connection to quantum computing is structural, not yet computational

4. **Can we hear the shape of discrete spacetime?**
   - **Generally NO** — Milnor's counterexample shows non-isometric lattices with identical spectra
   - But the spectrum encodes rich arithmetic information (class numbers, divisor sums)
   - In low dimensions, the answer is often YES
