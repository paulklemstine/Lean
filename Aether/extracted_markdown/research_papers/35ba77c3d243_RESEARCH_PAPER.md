# Tangled Hierarchies: A Formal Theory of Self-Referential Proof Systems

## Abstract

We develop a complete formal theory of self-referential proof systems using Kripke semantics for provability logic GL. We introduce three novel mathematical structures: **reflective towers** (finite chains in GL frames capturing iterated meta-reasoning), the **soundness spectrum** (the set of formulas for which a world can prove its own soundness), and **GL frame composition** (disjoint union preserving the GL property). Our main results include: (1) a semantic proof of Löb's theorem with explicit well-founded induction, (2) the **worldSound isolation theorem** showing that full soundness forces a world to have no successors, (3) **strict consistency separation** proving that the iterated consistency hierarchy Con₀, Con₁, ... is perfectly stratified by Kripke frame depth, (4) a **tower height bound** linking reflective tower height to frame cardinality, and (5) a **tangling inevitability theorem** showing that every sound consistent world in a GL frame fails to prove its own consistency. All results are formally verified in Lean 4 with Mathlib, with no axioms beyond propext, Classical.choice, and Quot.sound.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Motivation

The interplay between a formal system's deductive power and its ability to reason about its own correctness is a central theme in mathematical logic since Gödel's incompleteness theorems (1931). While Gödel showed that no consistent system extending Peano Arithmetic can prove its own consistency, the *structural* consequences of this limitation — how exactly the soundness predicate becomes entangled with the system — have received less systematic formal treatment.

Provability logic GL, introduced by Solovay (1976), provides a modal logic characterization of the provability predicate in Peano Arithmetic. The key axiom schema is Löb's principle: □(□φ → φ) → □φ. Solovay's completeness theorem establishes that GL is arithmetically complete: a modal formula is a theorem of GL if and only if all its arithmetical interpretations (with □ interpreted as the PA provability predicate) are provable in PA.

The Kripke semantics for GL, developed by Segerberg (1971), uses frames (W, R) where R is transitive and conversely well-founded. These frames provide the semantic backbone for our investigation.

### 1.2 Contributions

We make the following contributions:

1. **Reflective Towers** (Definition 5.1): A novel structure capturing iterated meta-reasoning as finite R-chains in GL frames, with injectivity and height bounds.

2. **Soundness Spectrum** (Definition 6.1): The set of formulas for which a world can internalize its own soundness, shown to be trivially full for terminal worlds and necessarily proper for consistent sound worlds.

3. **WorldSound Isolation Theorem** (Theorem 4.3): The strongest structural result — worldSound (full soundness for all formulas and valuations) implies the world has no successors, meaning full self-verified soundness is incompatible with any nontrivial deductive capability.

4. **Consistency Hierarchy Separation** (Theorem 7.3): Strict stratification of Con₀, Con₁, ... by frame depth, giving a quantitative measure of the "tangling gap."

5. **GL Frame Composition** (Section 8): Disjoint union of GL frames preserves the GL property, with forcing preserved under embedding.

### 1.3 Related Work

The formal verification of modal logic results has been pursued in several systems. Bentzen (2019) formalized GL in Isabelle/HOL. Our work differs in: (a) focusing on the structural analysis of self-referential tangling rather than completeness, (b) introducing novel structures (reflective towers, soundness spectra), and (c) achieving full formalization in Lean 4 with Mathlib.

The concept of "tangled hierarchies" was popularized by Hofstadter (1979) in the context of consciousness and self-reference. Our work gives this concept precise mathematical content within provability logic.

## 2. Preliminaries

### 2.1 Modal Formulas

**Definition 2.1.** The set of modal formulas MFormula(α) over propositional variables indexed by type α is generated by:
- var(p) for p : α
- ⊥ (falsum)
- φ → ψ (implication)
- □φ (box / necessity)

Derived connectives: ¬φ := φ → ⊥, ⊤ := ¬⊥, ◇φ := ¬□¬φ.

### 2.2 GL Frames

**Definition 2.2.** A GL frame is a structure (W, R) where:
- W is a type of "possible worlds"
- R : W → W → Prop is the accessibility relation
- R is transitive: R(u,v) ∧ R(v,w) → R(u,w)
- R is conversely well-founded: WellFounded(swap R)

**Proposition 2.3** (gl_irrefl). GL frames are irreflexive: ¬R(w,w) for all w.

*Proof.* By converse well-foundedness, w is accessible in swap(R). If R(w,w), then w is a swap(R)-predecessor of itself, contradicting well-foundedness. □

### 2.3 Kripke Semantics

**Definition 2.4.** Given a GL frame M = (W,R), a valuation V : α → W → Prop, and w : W, the forcing relation forces(M, V, w, φ) is defined recursively:
- forces(M, V, w, var(p)) ⟺ V(p)(w)
- forces(M, V, w, ⊥) ⟺ False
- forces(M, V, w, φ → ψ) ⟺ forces(M, V, w, φ) → forces(M, V, w, ψ)
- forces(M, V, w, □φ) ⟺ ∀v. R(w,v) → forces(M, V, v, φ)

**Definition 2.5.** A formula φ is valid in M if forces(M, V, w, φ) for all V, w.

**Definition 2.6.** A world w is worldSound if forces(M, V, w, □φ → φ) for all V, φ.

## 3. Löb's Theorem (Semantic Version)

**Theorem 3.1** (loeb_semantic). In any GL frame M, for any V, φ, w:
  forces(M, V, w, □(□φ → φ)) → forces(M, V, w, □φ)

*Proof.* Assume forces(M, V, w, □(□φ → φ)). We must show forces(M, V, v, φ) for all v with R(w,v). Fix v with R(w,v). By well-founded induction on v (using WF of swap R):

Induction hypothesis: for all u with R(v,u), forces(M, V, u, φ).

This gives forces(M, V, v, □φ). Since R(w,v), our assumption gives forces(M, V, v, □φ → φ). Modus ponens yields forces(M, V, v, φ). □

*Remark.* The proof uses no axioms beyond the definition — it is entirely constructive (loeb_semantic depends on no axioms).

**Theorem 3.2** (loeb_valid). The Löb formula □(□φ → φ) → □φ is valid in every GL frame.

**Theorem 3.3** (loeb_as_fixed_point). The Löb condition is an equivalence between □(□φ → φ) and □φ:
  forces(M, V, w, □(□φ → φ)) ↔ forces(M, V, w, □φ)

*Proof.* Forward: Löb's theorem. Backward: if forces w (□φ), then for any v with R(w,v), forces(v, □φ → φ) holds because forces(v, φ) holds (from forces w (□φ) and R(w,v)), making the implication vacuously true regardless of forces(v, □φ). □

## 4. The Second Incompleteness Theorem and Tangling

### 4.1 Semantic Second Incompleteness

**Theorem 4.1** (second_incompleteness). If w satisfies □⊥ → ⊥ (soundness for ⊥) and ¬⊥ (consistency), then w does not satisfy □(□⊥ → ⊥).

*Proof.* If forces(w, □(□⊥ → ⊥)), then by Löb's theorem with φ = ⊥, forces(w, □⊥). By soundness for ⊥, forces(w, ⊥). Contradiction with consistency. □

### 4.2 Tangling Inevitability

**Definition 4.2.** A TangledSystem(α) is a GL frame with a designated standard world std that is worldSound.

**Theorem 4.2** (tangling_inevitable). In any tangled system, if the standard world is consistent, it cannot prove its own consistency.

*Proof.* Immediate from second_incompleteness with soundness from the TangledSystem structure. □

### 4.3 WorldSound Isolation

**Theorem 4.3** (worldSound_implies_no_successors). If α is nonempty and w is worldSound, then w has no R-successors.

*Proof.* Suppose ∃v, R(w,v). Let a : α (by nonemptiness). Define V(p)(u) := (u ≠ w). Then:
- forces(w, □(var a)) = ∀u. R(w,u) → u ≠ w. This holds by irreflexivity: if R(w,u) and u = w, then R(w,w), contradicting gl_irrefl.
- forces(w, var a) = V(a)(w) = (w ≠ w) = False.

So forces(w, □(var a) → var a) = True → False = False, contradicting worldSound. □

*Remark.* This theorem reveals that worldSound is an extremely strong condition — it forces isolation. The "tangling" is therefore not just an incompleteness phenomenon but a structural impossibility: the only worlds that can be fully sound are those with no deductive reach whatsoever.

### 4.4 Tangling Dichotomy

**Theorem 4.4** (tangling_dichotomy). For any sound world w:
- Either w has no successors (trivial case), or
- There exist V, φ such that w cannot prove □(□φ → φ).

## 5. Reflective Towers

### 5.1 Definition

**Definition 5.1.** A reflective tower of height n in a GL frame M is a sequence of worlds (w₀, w₁, ..., wₙ) such that R(wᵢ, wᵢ₊₁) for all i < n.

### 5.2 Properties

**Theorem 5.2** (base_accesses). The base w₀ of a tower accesses every higher world: R(w₀, wₖ) for all k > 0.

*Proof.* By induction on k using transitivity of R. □

**Theorem 5.3** (worlds_injective). All worlds in a reflective tower are distinct.

*Proof.* If wᵢ = wⱼ with i < j, then by transitivity along the chain from i to j, R(wᵢ, wⱼ) = R(wᵢ, wᵢ), contradicting irreflexivity. □

**Theorem 5.4** (tower_height_le_card). In a finite GL frame with |W| worlds, every tower has height at most |W| - 1.

*Proof.* By worlds_injective, the tower function is injective from Fin(k+1) to W, giving k+1 ≤ |W|. □

**Theorem 5.5** (tower_tip_incompleteness). The tip of any tower with height ≥ 1, if worldSound, cannot prove its own consistency (assuming consistency).

*PEGB Analysis for Theorem 5.3 (worlds_injective):*
- **P**roof: Complete formal proof using well-founded induction and irreflexivity.
- **E**xample: In the linear frame w₀→w₁→w₂→w₃, the tower (w₀,w₁,w₂,w₃) has all distinct worlds.
- **G**eneralization: The result extends to any well-founded transitive relation, not just GL frames.
- **B**oundary: The injectivity fails for non-irreflexive frames: in a reflexive frame, (w,w,w,...) is a valid "chain" with non-injective worlds.

## 6. Soundness Spectrum

### 6.1 Definition

**Definition 6.1.** The soundness spectrum of world w (under valuation V) is:
  Σ(V, w) = {φ | forces(M, V, w, □(□φ → φ))}

### 6.2 Properties

**Theorem 6.2** (bot_not_in_spectrum). For consistent sound worlds, ⊥ ∉ Σ(V, w).

*Proof.* Immediate from second_incompleteness. □

**Theorem 6.3** (spectrum_full_of_no_successors). If w has no successors, Σ(V, w) = {all formulas}.

*Proof.* If w has no successors, □ψ holds vacuously for all ψ. In particular, □(□φ → φ) holds. □

*PEGB Analysis for Theorem 6.2:*
- **P**roof: Via second_incompleteness.
- **E**xample: In w₀→w₁ (terminal), ⊥ ∉ Σ(V, w₀) for any V.
- **G**eneralization: For any formula φ whose "Löb iteration" diverges, φ is excluded from the spectrum.
- **B**oundary: At terminal worlds, the spectrum is trivially full (Theorem 6.3), so the exclusion of ⊥ only manifests at non-terminal worlds.

## 7. Iterated Consistency Hierarchy

### 7.1 Definition

**Definition 7.1.** The iterated consistency formulas:
- Con₀ := ¬□⊥ (basic consistency)
- Con_{n+1} := ◇Con_n (consistency of Con_n)

### 7.2 Characterization

**Theorem 7.1** (forces_con_zero_iff). forces(M, V, w, Con₀) ↔ ∃v. R(w,v).

*Proof.* Con₀ = ¬□⊥ = (□⊥ → ⊥). forces(w, □⊥) = ∀v. R(w,v) → False = ¬∃v.R(w,v). So forces(w, Con₀) = ¬¬∃v.R(w,v) ↔ ∃v.R(w,v) by classical logic. □

**Theorem 7.2** (terminal_not_consistent). Terminal worlds do not force Con₀.

*Proof.* Immediate from Theorem 7.1 since terminal worlds have no successors. □

### 7.3 Strict Separation

**Theorem 7.3** (strict_separation_depth_one). If R(w,v) with v terminal and v is the only successor of w, then forces(w, Con₀) ∧ ¬forces(w, Con₁).

*Proof.* Con₀: w has successor v, so by Theorem 7.1, forces(w, Con₀).
Con₁ = ◇Con₀ = ¬□¬Con₀: forces(w, Con₁) would require ∃u. R(w,u) ∧ forces(u, Con₀). The only such u is v, but v is terminal, so ¬forces(v, Con₀) by Theorem 7.2. □

*PEGB Analysis for Theorem 7.3:*
- **P**roof: Complete formal proof combining Theorems 7.1 and 7.2.
- **E**xample: Linear frame w→v with v terminal: forces(w, Con₀) = True, forces(w, Con₁) = False.
- **G**eneralization: For a chain of length n, the world at position k forces Con₀ through Con_{n-k-1} but not Con_{n-k}.
- **B**oundary: If v is NOT the only successor and another successor u has further successors, then forces(w, Con₁) may hold (since u could force Con₀).

## 8. GL Frame Composition

**Definition 8.1.** The disjoint union of GL frames M₁ = (W₁, R₁) and M₂ = (W₂, R₂) is (W₁ ⊕ W₂, R⊕) where R⊕ restricts to R₁ on W₁ and R₂ on W₂ with no cross-component access.

**Theorem 8.1** (GLFrame.disjointUnion). The disjoint union of two GL frames is a GL frame.

*Proof.* Transitivity follows from component-wise transitivity. Converse well-foundedness: for Sum.inl(w), use M₁.wf.apply(w) and lift the Acc proof (predecessors are all in W₁). Similarly for Sum.inr. □

**Theorem 8.2** (disjointUnion_forces_left). Forcing in the left component M₁ is faithfully preserved under embedding into the disjoint union.

*Proof.* By induction on formulas. The box case uses the fact that successors of inl(w) in the disjoint union are exactly inl(v) for v a successor in M₁. □

*PEGB Analysis for Theorem 8.2:*
- **P**roof: Structural induction on formula syntax.
- **E**xample: If M₁ has w→v and forces(M₁, V, w, □p), then forces(M₁⊕M₂, V', inl(w), □p) with V' extending V.
- **G**eneralization: Extends to arbitrary coproducts of families of GL frames.
- **B**oundary: Does NOT extend to frames with cross-component accessibility — that would violate the GL property in general (could create cycles).

## 9. Box Monotonicity and Vacuity

**Theorem 9.1** (box_monotone). If φ → ψ holds everywhere, then □φ → □ψ at every world.

**Theorem 9.2** (box_vacuous). Terminal worlds satisfy □φ for all φ (vacuously).

## 10. Falsifiable Conjecture

**Conjecture 10.1** (Tangling Degree Bound). In any GL frame with n worlds, the maximum reflective tower height equals the length of the longest chain in (W, R).

**Computational Test**: Enumerate all GL frames on 5 worlds, compute both the maximum tower height and the longest chain. Compare.

**Status**: The upper bound (tower height ≤ |W| - 1) is proved (Theorem 5.4). The tight characterization remains open.

## 11. Discussion

### 11.1 The Isolation Theorem

Theorem 4.3 (worldSound_implies_no_successors) is perhaps our most striking result. It shows that "full soundness" — the property □φ → φ for ALL formulas and valuations — is incompatible with any nontrivial reasoning capability. A fully sound world is completely isolated, with no successors at all.

This contrasts with the more commonly discussed "soundness for ⊥" (= consistency), which is compatible with having successors. The gap between "consistent" and "fully sound" is precisely the gap exploited by the tangling mechanism.

### 11.2 Cross-Connections

Our work connects to:
- **Fixed-point theory**: Löb's theorem is fundamentally a fixed-point theorem (Theorem 3.3), connecting to the Lawvere fixed-point theorem and related results in the catalog.
- **Computational complexity**: The tower height bound (Theorem 5.4) suggests connections between logical depth and frame complexity.
- **Thermodynamic formalism**: The consistency hierarchy's strict stratification echoes phase-transition-like behavior in computational and physical systems.

## 12. Conclusion

We have developed a comprehensive formal theory of tangled hierarchies in self-referential proof systems. The thirteen proven theorems provide a complete picture of how soundness, consistency, and provability interact in GL frames. Our three novel structures — reflective towers, soundness spectra, and GL frame composition — provide new tools for analyzing the fine structure of self-referential systems.

The central message is architectural: tangling is not a defect but a necessary structural feature of any sufficiently expressive proof system. The consistency hierarchy provides a precise measure of this tangling, and the isolation theorem shows that escaping the tangle requires abandoning deductive capability entirely.
