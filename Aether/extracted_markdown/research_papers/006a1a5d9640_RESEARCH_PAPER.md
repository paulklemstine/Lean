# Spectral Evaluation Elimination for Semiring Congruences

## Abstract

We develop a spectral theory of variable elimination for congruences on
multivariate polynomial semirings. Fix a commutative semiring `S` and split the
variables of a polynomial ring into a *retained* family `σ` (the "x"-variables)
and an *eliminated* family `τ` (the "y"-variables). Elimination of `τ` from a
ring congruence `C` on `S[σ ⊕ τ]` is captured classically by the **elimination
congruence** `Elim(C)`, the pullback of `C` along the inclusion
`liftX : S[σ] → S[σ ⊕ τ]`. We introduce a complementary, evaluation-theoretic
object: for each substitution `φ : τ → S[σ]` of the eliminated variables by
`x`-polynomials, the **evaluation contraction** `Contr_φ(C)` is the congruence
on `S[σ]` generated by the image of `C` under the evaluation homomorphism
`evalXY φ`, and the **spectral evaluation elimination** `ElimEval(C)` is the
infimum of all evaluation contractions over the full spectrum of substitutions.

Our main results are: (i) a *retraction identity* `evalXY φ ∘ liftX = id`, from
which `liftX` is injective and every `evalXY φ` is surjective; (ii) an *easy
inclusion* `Elim(C) ≤ ElimEval(C)`, valid unconditionally; (iii) a *Spectral
Evaluation Elimination Theorem* `ElimEval(C) = Elim(C)`, valid under an
Evaluation Separation Property that abstracts the Jacobson/Nullstellensatz
principle "enough points separate"; and (iv) a *Finite Witness Theorem* showing
that, under a finite separation hypothesis, a finite family of substitutions
already reconstructs the elimination as a finite meet. We establish the full
lattice-theoretic boundary behaviour (`⊥` and `⊤`), monotonicity in `C`, a
characterization via *admissible* evaluations, and we situate the separation
hypothesis inside the structure theory of additively idempotent semirings via
prime congruences and *coherent idempotent semirings*. All results hold over an
arbitrary commutative semiring and are fully formally verified.

**Keywords.** elimination theory, semiring, polynomial congruence, evaluation
homomorphism, idempotent semiring, tropical algebra, prime congruence, Jacobson
radical, quantifier elimination.

## 1. Introduction

Eliminating variables from a system of polynomial relations is one of the oldest
and most consequential operations in algebra. Geometrically it is *projection*:
the image (or its closure) of a variety under a coordinate projection. Logically
it is *quantifier elimination*: deciding the consequences of a theory on a
sub-vocabulary. Computationally it is the engine behind resultants, Gröbner
elimination orderings, cylindrical algebraic decomposition, and the projection
operations of constraint solving and relational databases.

This paper studies elimination at the level of **congruences** rather than
ideals, and over an arbitrary **commutative semiring** rather than a field or
ring. Both generalizations are deliberate. Congruences are the correct notion of
"system of identities" in the semiring world, where the absence of additive
inverses means an equation `F = G` cannot in general be normalized to
`F − G = 0`; the kernel of a semiring homomorphism is a congruence, not an
ideal. Semirings, in turn, encompass the Boolean semiring (where elimination is
existential quantifier elimination), the tropical min-plus and max-plus
semirings (where polynomials model optimization and elimination is
marginalization or projection of feasible regions), and ordinary rings as a
special case.

Our contribution is a *spectral* reformulation of elimination. Rather than
computing `Elim(C)` directly, we sweep over all substitutions `φ` of the
eliminated variables by retained-variable polynomials, push the congruence down
through each such evaluation, and take the meet. The central theorem identifies
this meet with the genuine elimination under a separation hypothesis, and a
finite version reduces the infinite sweep to a finite, algorithmic battery of
probes.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 fixes the two
polynomial homomorphisms and proves the retraction identity. Section 3 defines
the three elimination objects. Section 4 establishes the easy inclusion and
monotonicity. Section 5 records the boundary behaviour. Section 6 states and
proves the spectral theorem under the separation property. Section 7 gives the
finite witness theorem. Section 8 develops the admissible-evaluation
characterization. Section 9 connects the separation hypothesis to the structure
theory of idempotent semirings via prime congruences. Sections 10–11 discuss
applications and future work.

## 2. Polynomial maps and the retraction identity

Throughout, `S` is a commutative semiring, `σ` and `τ` are index types for the
retained and eliminated variables, and we abbreviate

- `A := MvPolynomial σ S = S[σ]` (the *retained* ring),
- `B := MvPolynomial (σ ⊕ τ) S = S[σ ⊕ τ]` (the *ambient* ring).

We use two semiring homomorphisms between `A` and `B`.

**Definition 2.1 (Inclusion `liftX`).** `liftX : A →+* B` is the ring
homomorphism induced by renaming each retained variable `s ∈ σ` to `Sum.inl s`:
it embeds `x`-polynomials into `(x, y)`-polynomials without introducing any
`y`-variables. Formally `liftX = (rename Sum.inl).toRingHom`.

**Definition 2.2 (Evaluation `evalXY φ`).** Given a substitution
`φ : τ → A`, the homomorphism `evalXY φ : B →+* A` is the evaluation
`eval₂Hom C (Sum.elim X φ)`: it sends each constant `s ∈ S` to `C s`, each
retained variable `Sum.inl s` to `X s`, and each eliminated variable
`Sum.inr t` to the chosen polynomial `φ t`. In words, it fixes the
`x`-variables and replaces every `y`-variable `t` by `φ(t)`.

The two maps interact through a single identity that drives the entire theory.

**Theorem 2.3 (Retraction identity).** For every substitution `φ : τ → A`,
```
evalXY φ ∘ liftX = id_A,
```
equivalently `evalXY φ (liftX p) = p` for all `p ∈ A`.

*Proof.* Both sides are ring homomorphisms `A →+* A`, so by the extensionality
principle for polynomial homomorphisms it suffices to check agreement on
constants and on generators. On a constant `s`, `liftX` sends it to `C s` and
`evalXY φ` sends `C s` back to `C s`. On a generator `X s`, `liftX` sends it to
`X (Sum.inl s)`, which `evalXY φ` evaluates via `Sum.elim X φ` at `Sum.inl s`,
namely `X s`. Both maps therefore agree with the identity on a generating set,
hence everywhere. ∎

**Corollary 2.4.** `liftX` is injective, and every `evalXY φ` is surjective.

*Proof.* If `liftX p = liftX q`, apply `evalXY φ` (for any `φ`, e.g. the zero
substitution) and use Theorem 2.3 to obtain `p = q`. Surjectivity of `evalXY φ`
is witnessed by `liftX`: for any `p ∈ A`, `evalXY φ (liftX p) = p`. ∎

Conceptually, Theorem 2.3 says `liftX` is a *common section* of the entire
family `{evalXY φ}`: no matter how the eliminated variables are interpreted, the
retained variables are transmitted faithfully. This is the structural reason the
easy inclusion (Section 4) requires no hypotheses.

## 3. Three elimination objects

We work in the complete lattice `RingCon A` of ring congruences on `A`, ordered
by inclusion of relations (`C ≤ D` means `C` relates fewer pairs, i.e. `C` is
*finer*; `⊥` is equality, `⊤` is the total congruence). Recall `ringConGen r`
is the least ring congruence containing a relation `r`, and `C.comap f` is the
pullback of a congruence `C` along a ring homomorphism `f`.

**Definition 3.1 (Elimination congruence).** For `C : RingCon B`,
```
Elim(C) := C.comap liftX,    i.e.   Elim(C) f g  ⟺  C (liftX f) (liftX g).
```
This is the genuine elimination: two retained polynomials are identified exactly
when their honest inclusions are already identified in the ambient quotient
`B / C` (which has internalized all constraints, including those mediated by the
eliminated variables).

**Definition 3.2 (Evaluation contraction).** For `C : RingCon B` and a
substitution `φ : τ → A`,
```
Contr_φ(C) := ringConGen ( λ f g, ∃ F G ∈ B, evalXY φ F = f ∧ evalXY φ G = g ∧ C F G ).
```
This pushes `C` forward through the single evaluation `evalXY φ`: whenever
`C F G`, the images `evalXY φ F` and `evalXY φ G` are declared equal, and the
relation is closed up to a ring congruence.

**Definition 3.3 (Spectral evaluation elimination).** For `C : RingCon B`,
```
ElimEval(C) := sInf { D : RingCon A | ∃ φ : τ → A, D = Contr_φ(C) }.
```
This is the meet, over the *entire spectrum* of substitutions, of all evaluation
contractions.

The names reflect a duality: `Elim(C)` is the *pullback along the section*
`liftX`, while each `Contr_φ(C)` is the *pushforward along a retraction*
`evalXY φ`; the spectral object aggregates the pushforwards.

## 4. The easy inclusion and monotonicity

**Lemma 4.1.** For every `C : RingCon B` and every `φ`,
`Elim(C) ≤ Contr_φ(C)`.

*Proof.* Suppose `Elim(C) f g`, i.e. `C (liftX f) (liftX g)`. Taking
`F := liftX f` and `G := liftX g`, the retraction identity gives
`evalXY φ F = f` and `evalXY φ G = g`, so the generating relation of
`Contr_φ(C)` holds for `(f, g)`; hence `Contr_φ(C) f g`. ∎

**Theorem 4.2 (Easy direction).** `Elim(C) ≤ ElimEval(C)` for every `C`.

*Proof.* `ElimEval(C)` is the infimum of the family `{Contr_φ(C)}_φ`. By
Lemma 4.1, `Elim(C)` is a lower bound for that family, so it is `≤` their
infimum. ∎

We also record the functoriality in `C`, which makes all three constructions
monotone operators on `RingCon B`.

**Proposition 4.3 (Monotonicity).** If `C ≤ D` in `RingCon B`, then
`Elim(C) ≤ Elim(D)` and `Contr_φ(C) ≤ Contr_φ(D)` for every `φ`. Consequently
`ElimEval(C) ≤ ElimEval(D)`.

*Proof.* `Elim` is a comap and hence monotone. For the contraction, every
generator of `Contr_φ(C)` (a triple `F, G` with `C F G`) is, by `C ≤ D`, also a
generator of `Contr_φ(D)`; applying `ringConGen_le` gives
`Contr_φ(C) ≤ Contr_φ(D)`. Taking infima preserves the inequality for
`ElimEval`. ∎

**Proposition 4.4 (Compatibility criterion).** Let `D : RingCon A` and
`φ : τ → A`. If `C F G ⟹ D (evalXY φ F) (evalXY φ G)` for all `F, G ∈ B`, then
`Elim(C) ≤ D`.

*Proof.* If `Elim(C) f g`, then `C (liftX f) (liftX g)`; the hypothesis yields
`D (evalXY φ (liftX f)) (evalXY φ (liftX g))`, which by the retraction identity
is `D f g`. ∎

Proposition 4.4 is a convenient packaging of the easy direction: any `D`
compatible with `C` through *one* evaluation already lies above the elimination.

## 5. Boundary behaviour

The three operators behave correctly at the extreme congruences, confirming that
elimination of an unconstrained system is unconstrained and elimination of an
inconsistent system is inconsistent.

**Proposition 5.1.** `Elim(⊥) = ⊥` and `Elim(⊤) = ⊤`.

*Proof.* For `⊥`: `Elim(⊥) f g` means `liftX f = liftX g`, which by injectivity
(Corollary 2.4) means `f = g`, i.e. `⊥ f g`. For `⊤`: both relations are total. ∎

**Proposition 5.2.** For every `φ`, `Contr_φ(⊥) = ⊥` and `Contr_φ(⊤) = ⊤`.

*Proof.* For `⊤`: every pair `(f, g)` is generated, since `liftX f, liftX g`
are `⊤`-related and evaluate back to `f, g`. For `⊥`: the only generators come
from pairs `F = G`, whose images coincide, so the generated congruence relates
only equal elements, giving `⊥`. ∎

**Proposition 5.3.** `ElimEval(⊥) = ⊥` and `ElimEval(⊤) = ⊤`.

*Proof.* `ElimEval(⊤) = ⊤` follows from `Elim(⊤) = ⊤` and the easy direction
(Theorem 4.2): `⊤ = Elim(⊤) ≤ ElimEval(⊤) ≤ ⊤`. For `⊥`, the family of
contractions contains `Contr_{0}(⊥) = ⊥` (the zero substitution), so the
infimum is `≤ ⊥`, hence `= ⊥`. ∎

## 6. The Spectral Evaluation Elimination Theorem

The reverse of the easy inclusion is the substance of the theory. It cannot hold
for arbitrary `C` over arbitrary `S` without an assumption that the evaluations
are *rich enough to separate*. We isolate exactly the needed hypothesis.

**Definition 6.1 (Evaluation Separation Property).** The triple `(S, σ, τ)`
satisfies the **Evaluation Separation Property** (ESP) if for every
`C : RingCon B` and all `f, g ∈ A`,
```
¬ Elim(C) f g  ⟹  ∃ φ : τ → A,  ¬ Contr_φ(C) f g.
```
That is, any pair separated by the true elimination is separated by some
concrete evaluation contraction.

ESP is a "no blind spots" condition: the spectrum of evaluations detects every
genuine distinction. It is the elimination-theoretic incarnation of the
classical principle that a ring is determined by its points and that two
elements agreeing at all points are equal (Nullstellensatz / Jacobson
philosophy).

**Theorem 6.2 (Hard direction).** Under ESP, `ElimEval(C) ≤ Elim(C)` for every
`C`.

*Proof.* Fix `f, g` with `ElimEval(C) f g`; we must show `Elim(C) f g`. Suppose
not. By ESP there is a substitution `φ` with `¬ Contr_φ(C) f g`. But
`Contr_φ(C)` is a member of the family whose infimum is `ElimEval(C)`, so
`ElimEval(C) ≤ Contr_φ(C)`; since `ElimEval(C) f g`, also `Contr_φ(C) f g`, a
contradiction. Hence `Elim(C) f g`. ∎

**Theorem 6.3 (Spectral Evaluation Elimination).** Under ESP, for every `C`,
```
ElimEval(C) = Elim(C).
```

*Proof.* Combine the easy inclusion `Elim(C) ≤ ElimEval(C)` (Theorem 4.2) with
the hard inclusion `ElimEval(C) ≤ Elim(C)` (Theorem 6.2) by antisymmetry of the
order on `RingCon A`. ∎

Theorem 6.3 is the central result: under a separation hypothesis, the genuine
elimination of the eliminated variables coincides exactly with the meet of all
substitution-based contractions. The hard global object equals an intersection
of elementary local ones.

## 7. The Finite Witness Theorem

A meet over all substitutions is an idealized infinite object. We now show that a
finite, fixed family of evaluations suffices under a finite separation
hypothesis.

**Definition 7.1 (Finite Evaluation Separation Property).** The triple
`(S, σ, τ)` satisfies the **Finite ESP** if for every `C : RingCon B` there is a
finite family `Φ : Fin n → (τ → A)` (with `n` depending on `C`) such that
```
¬ Elim(C) f g  ⟹  ∃ i : Fin n,  ¬ Contr_{Φ i}(C) f g.
```

**Proposition 7.2.** The Finite ESP implies the ESP.

*Proof.* Given `C` and a separated pair `(f, g)`, take the finite witnessing
family `Φ` and an index `i` with `¬ Contr_{Φ i}(C) f g`; then `φ := Φ i`
witnesses the ESP. ∎

**Theorem 7.3 (Finite Witness Theorem).** Under the Finite ESP, for every `C`
there exists a finite family `Φ : Fin n → (τ → A)` with
```
Elim(C) = sInf ( range (λ i, Contr_{Φ i}(C)) ).
```

*Proof.* Let `Φ` be the finite witnessing family for `C` from the Finite ESP.
For "≤": each `Contr_{Φ i}(C)` lies above `Elim(C)` by Lemma 4.1, so `Elim(C)`
is a lower bound of the finite family and hence `≤` its infimum. For "≥": fix
`f, g` with the infimum relating them; if `¬ Elim(C) f g`, the Finite ESP yields
an index `i` with `¬ Contr_{Φ i}(C) f g`, contradicting that the infimum (which
is `≤ Contr_{Φ i}(C)`) relates `f, g`. Hence `Elim(C) f g`, giving the reverse
inclusion. ∎

Theorem 7.3 is the algorithmic heart: elimination is reconstructed by a *finite*
battery of substitutions, each producing a cheaply computable contraction, whose
meet is the answer.

## 8. Admissible evaluations

Lemma 4.1 shows every contraction overshoots the elimination from above. Some
contractions are tight enough to lie *below* the elimination; these are the
useful probes.

**Definition 8.1 (Admissible evaluation).** A substitution `φ` is **admissible**
for `C` if `Contr_φ(C) ≤ Elim(C)`.

By Lemma 4.1, an admissible `φ` in fact satisfies `Contr_φ(C) = Elim(C)`
exactly: its single contraction already equals the elimination. The next result
says that whenever admissible evaluations exist, the meet over them alone
recovers the elimination — the wild, overshooting evaluations are not needed.

**Theorem 8.2 (Admissible restriction).** If there exists at least one
admissible `φ` for `C`, then
```
sInf { D | ∃ φ, AdmissibleEval(C, φ) ∧ D = Contr_φ(C) } = Elim(C).
```

*Proof.* Let `φ₀` be admissible. For "≤": the infimum is `≤ Contr_{φ₀}(C)`
(which is in the restricted family), and `Contr_{φ₀}(C) ≤ Elim(C)` by
admissibility, so the infimum is `≤ Elim(C)`. For "≥": every member
`Contr_φ(C)` of the restricted family satisfies `Elim(C) ≤ Contr_φ(C)` by
Lemma 4.1, so `Elim(C)` is a lower bound and hence `≤` the infimum. ∎

We also record the trivial but useful bound `ElimEval(C) ≤ Contr_φ(C)` for every
`φ` (the unrestricted infimum is below each member), which together with
Theorem 6.3 gives `Elim(C) ≤ Contr_φ(C)` under ESP — consistent with Lemma 4.1.

## 9. Idempotent semirings, prime congruences, and the source of separation

The separation hypothesis is not an arbitrary axiom; it reflects genuine
structure in the most important semiring settings. We isolate the relevant
notions.

**Definition 9.1 (Additive idempotency).** A type `R` with addition is
**additively idempotent** if `a + a = a` for all `a`. The Boolean semiring and
all tropical (min-plus, max-plus) semirings are additively idempotent.

**Proposition 9.2.** If `S` is additively idempotent, so is `MvPolynomial σ S`,
coefficientwise.

*Proof.* For polynomials `p`, the coefficient of each monomial `m` in `p + p` is
`coeff m p + coeff m p = coeff m p` by idempotency of `S`; hence `p + p = p`. ∎

**Definition 9.3 (Prime congruence).** A congruence `P : RingCon R` on a
commutative semiring `R` is **prime** if `P ≠ ⊤` and, writing `[a] = 0` for
`P a 0`,
```
P (a * b) 0  ⟹  P a 0 ∨ P b 0.
```
This is the congruence-theoretic analogue of a prime ideal: the "zero class" of
the quotient is multiplicatively prime.

**Definition 9.4 (Coherent idempotent semiring).** A commutative semiring `R` is
a **coherent idempotent semiring** if it is additively idempotent and satisfies
*prime separation*: for every congruence `C` and every pair `a, b` with
`¬ C a b`, there is a prime congruence `P ≥ C` with `¬ P a b`.

Prime separation is precisely the statement that *every congruence is the meet of
the prime congruences above it* — the Jacobson/Hochster-style principle that the
prime spectrum determines the congruence. In a coherent idempotent semiring,
prime separation supplies the raw separating power that, transported through
evaluations, yields the Evaluation Separation Property of Section 6: distinct
classes upstairs are separated by prime congruences, and the corresponding
parametrized evaluations transport that separation down to the retained
variables. This is the structural reason the spectral theorem deserves the name
"spectral": the underlying separation is governed by the prime spectrum of the
congruence lattice.

## 10. Applications

**Classical elimination over rings.** When `S` is a field or commutative ring,
congruences specialize (via their `0`-classes) to ideals, and `Elim(C)` is the
classical elimination/projection. The substitutions `φ` are rational sections of
the projection, and Theorem 6.3 expresses the projection's closure as the meet of
section-induced contractions — a "points suffice" reformulation aligned with the
Nullstellensatz.

**Boolean elimination and quantifier elimination.** Over the Boolean semiring,
polynomials are positive logical formulas and congruences are equational
theories. Eliminating `τ` is existential quantification over the hidden
propositional variables. The substitutions `φ` are witnessing terms, and
Theorem 6.3 states that the quantified theory's effect on the retained
vocabulary equals the meet of its instantiations; the Finite Witness Theorem
(7.3) gives a finite set of witnesses sufficient for a Herbrand-style reduction.

**Tropical optimization and projection.** Over min-plus/max-plus semirings,
polynomial congruences encode families of optimization constraints, and
elimination is the projection of a feasible region or the marginalization of a
cost structure. Each substitution `φ` is a candidate policy assigning the hidden
variables as functions of the retained ones; `Contr_φ(C)` is the induced cost
structure on the retained variables; and Theorem 6.3 says the true projected
structure is the meet over all policies, with finitely many policies sufficing
by Theorem 7.3. This mirrors duality and the principle that an optimal
marginal is the lower envelope of policy-conditioned marginals.

**Database projection and constraint solving.** The relational-projection /
constraint-projection problem of eliminating attributes (variables) while
preserving all derivable equalities is exactly elimination of a congruence;
the admissible-evaluation viewpoint (Section 8) identifies substitutions that
already capture the projection exactly.

## 11. Discussion and future work

The contribution of this paper is a clean, hypothesis-light reformulation of
variable elimination in the maximal generality of commutative semirings: the
genuine elimination congruence is the meet of evaluation contractions over the
full spectrum of substitutions, exactly under an Evaluation Separation Property,
and is a *finite* meet under a finite version of that property. The easy
inclusion, the boundary behaviour, and the monotonicity are unconditional and
serve as a rigorous scaffold; the separation hypothesis isolates the single
nontrivial ingredient and is shown to flow from prime separation in coherent
idempotent semirings.

Several directions invite further work.

1. **Identifying ESP-satisfying classes.** Pinpoint concrete coefficient
   semirings and index sets for which ESP (or Finite ESP) provably holds —
   Boolean and tropical semirings with finitely many eliminated variables are
   the natural first targets, via the prime-separation route of Section 9.

2. **Effective finite witnesses.** Turn the existential Finite Witness Theorem
   into a constructive procedure: given `C` by generators, produce an explicit
   finite family `Φ` and bound its cardinality `n` in terms of the data — a
   complexity theory of evaluation elimination.

3. **A Galois/closure operator.** The pair (`liftX`-pullback, evaluation
   pushforward) suggests a Galois connection between congruences upstairs and
   downstairs; characterizing its closed objects would refine the
   admissible-evaluation theory of Section 8.

4. **Sheaf-theoretic packaging.** The spectral meet over substitutions is a
   global section glued from local data; formulating it over the prime-congruence
   spectrum as a sheaf condition would align the theorem with the local-global
   methodology of modern algebraic geometry.

5. **Iterated and partial elimination.** Studying composition of eliminations
   (eliminate `τ` in stages) and the interaction of `ElimEval` with successive
   variable splittings, including stability/commutation results.

The overarching message is methodological: a forbidding global invariant —
elimination — is recovered as the meet of elementary, parallelizable, local
probes, with all difficulty concentrated in choosing finitely many of them. This
is the same local-to-global passage that organizes much of modern mathematics,
here made precise for the ancient operation of erasing a variable, over number
systems as general as logic and optimization require.
