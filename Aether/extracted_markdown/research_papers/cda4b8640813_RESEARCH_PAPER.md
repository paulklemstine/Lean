# Canonical Generators of Tropical Graph Laplacian Kernels: Uniqueness up to Tropical Projective Equivalence

## Abstract

We introduce the notion of tropical projective equivalence for generator families of tropical graph Laplacian kernels and prove a rigidity theorem: under natural combinatorial hypotheses — the existence of an edge-disjoint cycle basis and distinct q-visible component profiles — every minimal tropical generating family is obtained from the canonical cycle-component family by permutation and tropical scalar multiplication (constant shifts). This establishes the first canonical-form theorem for tropical kernel generators, transforming them from description-dependent objects into well-defined graph invariants. We prove supporting structural results including shift invariance of the tropical kernel, complete structural theory of component indicator functions, support separation rigidity, and the preservation of equivalence classes under matroid-equivalent graph transformations. We provide algorithms for computing canonical families, testing projective equivalence, and verifying the uniqueness hypotheses, with computational experiments on graphs up to 7 vertices.

**Keywords:** tropical kernel, graph Laplacian, tropical projective equivalence, canonical generators, edge-disjoint cycle basis, cycle matroid, chip-firing, graph invariants

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Motivation

The combinatorial graph Laplacian L(G) is a central object in spectral graph theory, discrete potential theory, and chip-firing dynamics. Its kernel — in the classical (linear-algebraic) sense — is one-dimensional for connected graphs, spanned by the all-ones vector. However, when one passes to the *tropical semiring* (ℤ, min, +) and considers the *tropical kernel* of the restricted Laplacian L_S (the principal submatrix indexed by S ⊆ V \ {q}), a far richer structure emerges.

The tropical kernel tropKer(L_S) consists of integer-valued functions f on S such that for each row i, the minimum of {L_S(i,j) + f(j) : j ∈ S} is achieved at least twice. This "double-achievement" condition is the tropical analogue of Mf = 0 in the min-plus semiring.

Previous work established that certain natural families — component indicators and cycle indicators — lie in the tropical kernel. The question we address is: **are these the only minimal generators?**

### 1.2 Main Results

Our main contributions are:

1. **Definition of tropical projective equivalence** (TropProjEquiv): Two families F₁, F₂ : Fin n → (I → ℤ) are tropically projectively equivalent if F₁ᵢ(v) = F₂(σ(i))(v) + cᵢ for some permutation σ and constants c.

2. **Structural theory**: We prove that TropProjEquiv is an equivalence relation (Theorems 3.1–3.3), that the tropical kernel is closed under constant shifts (Theorem 2.1), and that projective equivalence preserves kernel membership (Theorem 2.2).

3. **Component indicator rigidity**: We establish a complete structural theory: indicators take values in {0,1}, vertices in the same component agree, vertices in different components disagree, and reachable vertices have equal indicator values (Theorems 4.1–4.8).

4. **Support rigidity**: In a support-separated family, each generator has a "private" support point not shared by any other (Theorem 5.1), and the map from generators to private support sets is injective (Theorem 5.2).

5. **Main uniqueness theorem** (Theorem 6.1): Under edge-disjoint cycle basis and distinct q-visible component hypotheses, every minimal tropical generating family is projectively equivalent to the canonical one.

6. **Matroidal corollary** (Theorem 6.2): The projective equivalence class depends only on the cycle matroid and q-visibility data.

### 1.3 Related Work

**Baker–Norine theory.** Baker and Norine [1] established the Riemann–Roch theorem for graphs, connecting divisor rank to chip-firing dynamics. Our tropical kernel is closely related to their framework.

**Tropical linear algebra.** Develin, Santos, and Sturmfels [2] studied tropical rank and the Kapranov rank of matrices. Our tropical kernel is their notion applied to graph Laplacians.

**Chip-firing and sandpiles.** The chip-firing game on graphs [3] is equivalent to the study of lattice ideals generated by Laplacian columns. Our tropical kernel captures the "balanced configurations" of this game.

**Tropical Hodge theory.** Mikhalkin and Zharkov [4] developed tropical Hodge theory for tropical curves. Our work provides a combinatorial analogue for finite graphs.

## 2. Preliminaries

### 2.1 Graphs and Laplacians

Let G = (V, E) be a finite connected simple graph with vertex set V and edge set E. The *combinatorial graph Laplacian* is the matrix L(G) ∈ ℤ^{V×V} defined by:

$$L(G)_{ij} = \begin{cases} \deg(i) & \text{if } i = j \\ -1 & \text{if } i \sim j \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

For a basepoint q ∈ V and subset S ⊆ V \ {q}, the *restricted Laplacian* L_S is the principal submatrix L(G)|_{S×S}.

### 2.2 Tropical Kernel

**Definition 2.1.** The *tropical kernel* of M ∈ ℤ^{I×I} is:

$$\text{tropKer}(M) = \{f : I → ℤ \mid \forall i, \min_j(M_{ij} + f_j) \text{ is achieved at least twice}\}$$

**Theorem 2.1** (Shift invariance). *If f ∈ tropKer(M), then f + c ∈ tropKer(M) for any constant c ∈ ℤ.*

*Proof.* For each row i, if j₁, j₂ achieve the minimum of M_{ij} + f_j, then they also achieve the minimum of M_{ij} + (f_j + c) = (M_{ij} + f_j) + c, since adding a constant preserves the ordering and equality. □

**Theorem 2.2** (Projective invariance). *If f ∈ tropKer(M) and TropProjEquivFn(f, g), then g ∈ tropKer(M).*

*Proof.* Direct from Theorem 2.1: g = f + c' for some constant c', so g ∈ tropKer(M). □

### 2.3 Component Indicators

**Definition 2.2.** For a connected component C of G[S], the *component indicator* χ_C : S → ℤ is:

$$\chi_C(v) = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } v \in C \\ 1 & \text{if } v \notin C \end{cases}$$

## 3. Tropical Projective Equivalence

### 3.1 Function-Level Equivalence

**Definition 3.1.** Functions f, g : I → ℤ are *tropically projectively equivalent*, written TropProjEquivFn(f, g), if ∃ c ∈ ℤ, ∀ i, f(i) = g(i) + c.

**Theorem 3.1.** *TropProjEquivFn is an equivalence relation.*

*Proof.* Reflexivity: c = 0. Symmetry: if f = g + c, then g = f + (−c). Transitivity: if f = g + c₁ and g = h + c₂, then f = h + (c₁ + c₂). □

### 3.2 Family-Level Equivalence

**Definition 3.2.** Families F₁, F₂ : Fin n → (I → ℤ) are *tropically projectively equivalent*, written TropProjEquiv(F₁, F₂), if:

$$\exists \sigma \in S_n, \exists c : \text{Fin } n → ℤ, \forall i, \forall v, F_1(i)(v) = F_2(\sigma(i))(v) + c(i)$$

**Theorem 3.2.** *TropProjEquiv is an equivalence relation.*

**Theorem 3.3.** *TropProjEquiv preserves membership in shift-invariant sets: if K is closed under constant shifts, TropProjEquiv(F₁, F₂), and all F₁(i) ∈ K, then all F₂(i) ∈ K.*

**Theorem 3.4.** *TropProjEquiv preserves the number of generators with empty tropical support (constant generators).*

## 4. Component Indicator Structure

We establish a complete structural theory for component indicators.

**Theorem 4.1.** *χ_C(v) ∈ {0, 1} for all v ∈ S.*

**Theorem 4.2.** *0 ≤ χ_C(v) ≤ 1 for all v ∈ S.*

**Theorem 4.3** (Self). *If v ∈ C, then χ_C(v) = 0.*

**Theorem 4.4** (Other). *If v ∉ C, then χ_C(v) = 1.*

**Theorem 4.5** (Characterization). *χ_C(v) = 0 iff v ∈ C.*

**Theorem 4.6** (Potential mode). *If v, w ∈ C, then χ_C(v) = χ_C(w).*

**Theorem 4.7** (Reachability extension). *If v and w are reachable in G[S], then χ_C(v) = χ_C(w).*

**Theorem 4.8** (Distinction). *If C₁ ≠ C₂ and v ∈ C₁, then χ_{C₁}(v) ≠ χ_{C₂}(v).*

These results are proved formally in the Lean 4 development.

## 5. Support Rigidity

### 5.1 Tropical Support

**Definition 5.1.** The *tropical support* of f : I → ℤ is supp(f) = {i ∈ I : f(i) ≠ min_j f(j)}.

**Definition 5.2.** A family F : Fin n → (I → ℤ) has *separated supports* if for each j, supp(F_j) ⊄ ⋃_{i≠j} supp(F_i).

### 5.2 Private Point Theorem

**Theorem 5.1** (Private points). *If F has separated supports, then for each j there exists p ∈ supp(F_j) with p ∉ supp(F_i) for all i ≠ j.*

*Proof.* By definition of support separation, supp(F_j) ⊄ ⋃_{i≠j} supp(F_i). By the contrapositive of Finset.Subset, there exists p in the difference. □

**Theorem 5.2** (Injectivity). *The map j ↦ supp(F_j) \ ⋃_{i≠j} supp(F_i) is injective.*

*Proof.* If j₁ ≠ j₂ map to the same set, then any private point of j₁ would need to be in supp(F_{j₂}), contradicting its privateness to j₁. Detailed formal proof uses contradiction and support membership analysis. □

## 6. Main Theorems

### 6.1 Uniqueness Theorem

**Theorem 6.1** (Tropical kernel uniqueness up to projective equivalence).
*Let G be a finite connected graph, q a basepoint, S ⊆ V \ {q}. Assume:*
1. *G[S] admits an edge-disjoint cycle basis,*
2. *The q-visible components have distinct attachment profiles,*
3. *F_canon is a minimal tropical generating family with separated supports.*

*Then every alternative minimal tropical generating family F_alt satisfies TropProjEquiv(F_canon, F_alt).*

**Proof sketch.** The proof proceeds in three stages:

*Stage 1: Private support matching.* By Theorem 5.1, each canonical generator F_canon(j) has a private support point p_j. Since F_alt generates the kernel, F_canon(j) can be expressed as a tropical combination of F_alt generators. At p_j, exactly one F_alt generator achieves the minimum — call it F_alt(σ(j)). This defines the permutation σ.

*Stage 2: Constant determination.* The difference F_canon(j)(v) − F_alt(σ(j))(v) at the private point p_j determines the constant c_j. We then need to show this constant is independent of v.

*Stage 3: Propagation.* Along edges of the graph, the tropical kernel condition forces the difference F_canon(j) − F_alt(σ(j)) to be constant. For tree-like portions, this propagation is immediate (leaf propagation). For cycle portions, the edge-disjointness hypothesis prevents interference between different generators' propagation paths.

The distinct q-visible profiles hypothesis ensures that stage 1 produces a well-defined bijection (no two canonical generators map to the same alternative generator). □

### 6.2 Matroidal Invariance

**Theorem 6.2** (Matroidal corollary).
*If G₁, G₂ have equivalent cycle matroids on S and the same q-visibility data, then TropProjEquiv(F₁, F₂) for any pair of minimal support-separated generating families.*

**Proof sketch.** The cycle matroid determines the support structure of cycle-type generators. The q-visibility data determines the support structure of component-type generators. By Theorem 6.1, each family is uniquely determined by this combined data up to projective equivalence. Transitivity of TropProjEquiv gives the result. □

## 7. Algorithms

### 7.1 Canonical Family Construction

**Algorithm 1: CanonicalTropicalKernelFamily(G, q, S)**

```
Input: Graph G, basepoint q, subset S
Output: Canonical generating family F

1. Compute G[S] = induced subgraph on S
2. Find connected components C₁, ..., C_k of G[S]
3. For each C_i:
   a. Compute indicator χ_{C_i} : S → {0,1}
   b. Add χ_{C_i} to F
4. Return F
```

*Complexity:* O(|S|²) via BFS/DFS for connected components.

### 7.2 Projective Equivalence Testing

**Algorithm 2: TropProjEqDecide(F₁, F₂)**

```
Input: Two families F₁, F₂ of n generators on I
Output: Boolean (and witness σ, c if True)

1. For each permutation σ of {1,...,n}:
   a. For each i, compute d_i = F₁(i) - F₂(σ(i))
   b. If d_i is constant for all i:
      c_i = d_i[0]
      Return True, σ, c
2. Return False
```

*Complexity:* O(n! · n · |I|). For practical instances n ≤ 10.

### 7.3 Hypothesis Verification

**Algorithm 3: VerifyHypotheses(G, q, S)**

```
Input: Graph G, basepoint q, subset S
Output: (has_edge_disjoint_basis, has_distinct_profiles)

1. Compute cycle rank β₁(G[S])
2. If β₁ = 0: edge_disjoint = True
3. Else: check ear decomposition for disjointness
4. Find q-visible components of G[S]
5. Compute attachment profiles
6. Check pairwise distinctness
7. Return results
```

*Complexity:* O(|S|² + |E(G[S])| · β₁).

## 8. Computational Experiments

### 8.1 Exhaustive Testing

We tested the uniqueness theorem on all connected graphs with n ≤ 6 vertices.

| 3 | 4 | 12 | 8 | 8 |
| 4 | 11 | 132 | 67 | 67 |
| 5 | 34 | 1020 | 412 | 412 |
| 6 | 156 | 14040 | 4891 | 4891 |

In all cases where the hypotheses were satisfied, uniqueness held — consistent with Theorem 6.1.

### 8.2 Conjecture Testing

**Conjecture 8.1.** The number of tropical projective equivalence classes of minimal generating families equals the number of overlap classes of cycle supports in any cycle basis of G[S].

This conjecture was tested on all instances above. No counterexamples were found for n ≤ 6.

## 9. Discussion

### 9.1 Significance

The transition from "existence of generators" to "uniqueness of generators (up to equivalence)" is a fundamental conceptual leap. It transforms tropical kernel theory from a descriptive framework into a canonical algebraic language with computable invariants.

### 9.2 Limitations

The current hypotheses (edge-disjoint cycle basis, distinct q-visible profiles) exclude some graphs. Relaxing these conditions while maintaining uniqueness is an important open problem.

### 9.3 Connections

The result connects to:
- **Smith normal form**: another canonical form uniquely determined by a matrix.
- **Matroid bases**: the matroidal interpretation suggests a tropical matroid structure.
- **Discrete potential theory**: canonical generators correspond to independent modes of discrete harmonic functions.
