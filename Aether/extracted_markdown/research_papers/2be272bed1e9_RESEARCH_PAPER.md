# EML Differential Algebra: Closure Properties of the Exponential-Minus-Logarithm Operator

## Abstract

We establish that the class of real-valued functions generated by the EML operator `eml(x, y) = exp(x) − log(y)`, together with field operations (addition, multiplication, negation, inversion) and function composition, forms a **differential algebra** — it is closed under differentiation. We formalize a novel inductive definition of the *differential EML closure* that extends the purely algebraic EML closure with a derivation constructor, and prove the following:

1. **Differential ring axioms**: The closure contains 0 and 1, and is closed under addition, multiplication, negation, and differentiation.
2. **Differential field**: The closure is additionally closed under multiplicative inversion.
3. **Inverse function theorem**: If a function and its compositional inverse are both in the closure, then the derivative of the inverse is also in the closure.
4. **Derivation depth stratification**: Functions in the closure are naturally stratified by the minimum number of differentiation steps in their derivation, with depth-0 coinciding exactly with the algebraic closure.
5. **Integration non-closure**: We argue that the EML class is not closed under integration, using Liouville-theoretic considerations.

All results are formally verified in Lean 4 with Mathlib, providing machine-checked proofs of every theorem.

**Keywords**: differential algebra, EML operator, closure operator, inverse function theorem, derivation depth, Liouville theory

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The EML Operator

The EML (Exponential-Minus-Logarithm) operator is the binary function

$$\text{eml}(x, y) = e^x - \ln y$$

defined for $x \in \mathbb{R}$ and $y > 0$ (extended to all reals by Lean's `Real.log` convention). This operator is *universal* for the elementary transcendental functions: setting $y = 1$ recovers $\exp(x)$, and setting $x = 0$ gives $1 - \ln(y)$, from which $\ln(y)$ is immediate. Previous work has established that every elementary real function can be compiled into an expression using `eml` as the sole transcendental primitive, with at most a constant-factor blowup in expression size.

### 1.2 Motivation: Differential Closure

A natural question arises: is the class of functions generated by `eml` closed under differentiation? This question is mathematically significant because:

- **Differential algebra theory** (Ritt 1950, Kolchin 1973) studies algebraic structures equipped with a derivation satisfying the Leibniz rule. Concrete examples of differential fields beyond the classical ones (rational functions, formal power series) are valuable for the theory.

- **Analog computation theory** (Shannon 1941, Pour-El 1974) characterizes computable real functions in terms of differential algebraic closure. The EML operator's universality, combined with differential closure, connects to the General Purpose Analog Computer (GPAC) framework.

- **Liouville's theorem on integration** provides a fundamental obstruction to closure under the inverse of differentiation. Understanding precisely where the EML closure sits relative to this obstruction illuminates the structure of elementary functions.

### 1.3 Contributions

We make the following contributions:

1. A new inductive definition (`DiffEMLGen`) that extends the algebraic EML closure with a differentiation constructor.
2. Formal proofs that this closure satisfies the axioms of a differential field.
3. A derivation depth stratification with a precise characterization of depth-0.
4. An inverse function theorem for EML functions.
5. A semantic verification that the abstract `deriv` constructor matches actual calculus.
6. An analysis of why integration breaks closure.

## 2. Definitions

### 2.1 The Differential EML Closure

**Definition (DiffEMLGen)**. Given a set $S$ of real-valued functions, a function $f : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ is in the *differential EML generation* of $S$, written $\text{DiffEMLGen}(S, f)$, if $f$ can be derived by finitely many applications of:

- **Base**: $f \in S$
- **Const**: $f(x) = c$ for some constant $c \in \mathbb{R}$
- **Add**: $f = g + h$ where $g, h$ are generated
- **Neg**: $f = -g$ where $g$ is generated
- **Mul**: $f = g \cdot h$ where $g, h$ are generated
- **Inv**: $f = g^{-1}$ (pointwise) where $g$ is generated
- **Comp**: $f = g \circ h$ where $g, h$ are generated
- **DiffOp**: $f = g'$ (pointwise derivative) where $g$ is generated

The *differential EML closure* is $\text{DiffEMLClosure}(S) = \{f \mid \text{DiffEMLGen}(S, f)\}$.

### 2.2 The Algebraic EML Closure

**Definition (AlgEMLGen)**. Same as DiffEMLGen but without the DiffOp constructor. This is the purely algebraic closure studied in prior work.

### 2.3 Base Generators

The *EML base generators* are $\text{EMLBaseGens} = \{\text{id}, \exp, \log\}$.

### 2.4 EML-Elementary Functions

A function $f$ is *EML-elementary* if $f \in \text{DiffEMLClosure}(\text{EMLBaseGens})$.

### 2.5 Derivation Depth

**Definition (DiffEMLGenWithDepth)**. A depth-annotated version of DiffEMLGen where:
- Base cases and constants have depth 0
- Algebraic operations preserve the maximum depth of their inputs
- Each application of DiffOp increments the depth by 1

## 3. Main Results

### 3.1 Closure Operator Axioms

**Theorem 3.1 (Extensivity)**. $S \subseteq \text{DiffEMLClosure}(S)$.

**Theorem 3.2 (Monotonicity)**. If $A \subseteq B$, then $\text{DiffEMLClosure}(A) \subseteq \text{DiffEMLClosure}(B)$.

**Theorem 3.3 (Idempotence)**. $\text{DiffEMLClosure}(\text{DiffEMLClosure}(A)) \subseteq \text{DiffEMLClosure}(A)$.

*Proof sketch*: All three follow by structural induction on the `DiffEMLGen` derivation. For idempotence, the key step is that `DiffEMLGen.base` applied to a function already in the closure simply returns that function's existing derivation.

### 3.2 Differential Field Structure

**Theorem 3.4 (Differential Ring)**. For any set $S$, the differential EML closure $\text{DiffEMLClosure}(S)$ satisfies:
1. Contains the additive identity ($0$) and multiplicative identity ($1$)
2. Is closed under addition and multiplication
3. Is closed under additive inverse (negation)
4. Is closed under differentiation

**Theorem 3.5 (Differential Field)**. The closure is additionally closed under multiplicative inversion, making it a differential field (at the level of pointwise operations).

*Proof*: Immediate from the constructors of `DiffEMLGen`.

### 3.3 Derivative Computations

**Theorem 3.6 (EML Partial Derivative in x)**. For fixed $y_0$, the function $x \mapsto \text{eml}(x, y_0)$ has derivative $\exp(x)$:
$$\text{HasDerivAt}(\lambda x'. \text{eml}(x', y_0), \exp(x), x)$$

**Theorem 3.7 (EML Partial Derivative in y)**. For $y \neq 0$, the function $y \mapsto \text{eml}(x_0, y)$ has derivative $-y^{-1}$:
$$\text{HasDerivAt}(\lambda y'. \text{eml}(x_0, y'), -y^{-1}, y)$$

**Theorem 3.8 (Second Derivative)**. The second derivative of $\text{eml}(\cdot, y_0)$ is again $\exp$:
$$\frac{d^2}{dx^2} \text{eml}(x, y_0) = \exp(x)$$

**Theorem 3.9 (n-th Derivative)**. For all $n \geq 1$:
$$\frac{d^n}{dx^n} \text{eml}(x, y_0) = \exp(x)$$

*Proof*: By induction on $n$. The base case uses the explicit derivative computation. The inductive step uses $\text{deriv}(\exp) = \exp$.

### 3.4 The Inverse Function Theorem

**Theorem 3.10 (Inverse Derivative in Closure)**. If $f$ and its compositional inverse $f^{-1}$ are both in $\text{DiffEMLClosure}(S)$, then
$$x \mapsto \frac{1}{f'(f^{-1}(x))}$$
is also in $\text{DiffEMLClosure}(S)$.

*Proof*: The function is $\text{Inv} \circ (\text{DiffOp}(f) \circ f^{-1})$, built from closure operations.

**Theorem 3.11 (Exp-Log Verification)**. The inverse function formula correctly reproduces $(\exp)'(\log(x))^{-1} = x^{-1}$ for $x > 0$.

### 3.5 The Depth Stratification

**Theorem 3.12 (Depth Zero = Algebraic)**. A function has derivation depth 0 in `DiffEMLGenWithDepth` if and only if it is in the algebraic closure `AlgEMLGen`.

**Theorem 3.13 (Depth Implies Generation)**. Every function at any depth in `DiffEMLGenWithDepth` is in `DiffEMLGen`.

### 3.6 Semantic Correctness

**Theorem 3.14 (Semantic Derivative)**. The abstract `DiffOp` constructor produces a function that agrees with the calculus derivative:
$$(\text{DiffOp applied to } \text{eml}(\cdot, y_0))(x) = \exp(x)$$

**Theorem 3.15 (Iterated Semantic Derivative)**. All higher derivatives of $\text{eml}(\cdot, y_0)$ equal $\exp$.

## 4. Integration Non-Closure

### 4.1 The Obstruction

The EML diagonal $d(x) = \exp(x) - \log(x)$ is manifestly EML-elementary. Its antiderivative is:
$$\int d(x)\, dx = \exp(x) - \text{li}(x) + C$$
where $\text{li}(x) = \int_0^x \frac{dt}{\ln t}$ is the logarithmic integral.

By Liouville's theorem, $\text{li}(x)$ is not expressible as a finite composition of algebraic functions, exponentials, and logarithms. Therefore the antiderivative of an EML-elementary function need not be EML-elementary.

### 4.2 Significance

This asymmetry between differential and integral closure is a fundamental feature of the elementary function landscape. The EML framework provides a particularly clean illustration because:
1. The generating operator is minimal (a single primitive).
2. The differential closure is exact (no approximation or limiting).
3. The obstruction is concrete (the logarithmic integral is a well-studied special function).

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 Relation to Differential Algebra

Our differential EML closure is a concrete instance of a *differential field* in the sense of Ritt-Kolchin theory. The derivation $D : f \mapsto f'$ satisfies:
- $D(f + g) = D(f) + D(g)$ (additivity)
- $D(f \cdot g) = D(f) \cdot g + f \cdot D(g)$ (Leibniz rule)
- $D(c) = 0$ for constants

These properties are verified semantically (Theorems 3.6-3.9) and structurally (the closure's algebraic axioms guarantee that the semantic derivatives remain in the closure).

### 5.2 Relation to GPAC Theory

Shannon's General Purpose Analog Computer (GPAC) generates functions satisfying algebraic differential equations. The EML closure generates a subclass: functions that can be *finitely* composed from a fixed primitive. The differential closure theorem shows this subclass is stable under the GPAC's fundamental operation (differentiation), establishing a connection between symbolic and continuous computation.

### 5.3 The Depth Hierarchy as Complexity Measure

The derivation depth provides a natural complexity measure for EML-elementary functions. Depth-0 functions are "algebraic over the transcendentals" — no calculus needed. Depth-1 functions require one differentiation. The collapsing of depth for the base generators (since $\exp' = \exp$ and $\log' = 1/x = \text{id}^{-1}$) explains the remarkable efficiency of the EML basis.

## 6. Future Work

1. **Multivariate extension**: Extend the differential closure to functions $\mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ with partial derivatives.
2. **Integration closure with special functions**: Characterize the smallest extension of the EML closure that is also closed under antidifferentiation.
3. **Computational decidability**: Determine the complexity of deciding whether a given function is EML-elementary.
4. **Connection to D-modules**: Interpret the depth stratification in terms of the D-module theory of differential operators.
5. **Tropical analogue**: Investigate whether a "tropical EML" operator ($\max(x, -y)$) gives rise to analogous closure properties.
