# Modal Depth Stratification: A Quantitative Theory of Self-Referential Incompleteness

## Abstract

We introduce the **Soundness Stratification Algebra**, a novel mathematical framework that quantifies the degree of self-knowledge attainable by formal proof systems. By stratifying the soundness predicate according to the modal depth of formulas, we define **k-soundness** — the property that a system is sound for all formulas of modal depth ≤ k — and prove a hierarchy of results:

1. **k-Soundness Monotonicity**: (k+1)-soundness implies k-soundness, and full soundness is equivalent to k-soundness for all k ∈ ℕ.
2. **Stratified Incompleteness Theorem**: A consistent system satisfying the n-th reflection principle (□^(n+1)⊥ → ⊥) cannot prove the (n+1)-th reflection principle. This generalizes Gödel's Second Incompleteness Theorem from n = 0 to all finite n.
3. **Strict Hierarchy Theorem**: In the omega frame (ℕ, >), world n+1 satisfies exactly the first n+1 reflection principles, proving the hierarchy is strict.
4. **Quantitative Gap**: The formula witnessing each level of incompleteness has modal depth exactly one greater than the assumed reflection principles, providing a precise measure of the "incompleteness gap."

All results are formalized and verified in Lean 4 with Mathlib, building on the GL frame semantics from provability logic.

## 1. Introduction

Gödel's Second Incompleteness Theorem (1931) is typically stated as a single result: a consistent, sufficiently strong formal system cannot prove its own consistency. Viewed through the lens of provability logic (GL), this becomes a statement about Kripke semantics: a sound world in a GL frame cannot force □(□⊥ → ⊥).

However, this formulation obscures a deeper structure. The consistency formula Con = ¬□⊥ has modal depth 1, and the theorem asserts that a world sound at depth 0 (□⊥ → ⊥) cannot prove a formula of depth 1 (□(□⊥ → ⊥)). This suggests a natural question: **does this pattern repeat at higher modal depths?**

We answer affirmatively by introducing the **k-soundness hierarchy** and proving the **Stratified Incompleteness Theorem**, which shows that the incompleteness phenomenon regenerates at every modal depth level. The mathematical content is organized into a novel algebraic structure — the **Soundness Stratification Algebra** — which captures the graded self-knowledge of a proof system.

### 1.1 Related Work

Our work connects to several classical threads:

- **Provability Logic (GL)**: Solovay (1976) proved the arithmetical completeness of GL, establishing that GL frames capture the provability predicate of PA. Our results, being purely semantic, hold for all GL frames.
- **Beklemishev's Provability Algebras**: Beklemishev (2004) introduced algebraic structures for studying iterated consistency extensions. Our Soundness Stratification Algebra provides a complementary semantic perspective.
- **Iterated Reflection Principles**: Turing (1939) and Feferman (1962) studied ordinal progressions of theories obtained by iterating consistency statements. Our strict hierarchy theorem provides explicit Kripke-semantic witnesses for the strict ordering of these theories.
- **Catalog Entry**: `Logic/TangledHierarchies.lean` establishes the base case (Löb's theorem, second incompleteness) that our work generalizes.

## 2. Definitions

### 2.1 Modal Formulas and GL Frames

**Definition 2.1** (Modal Formula). The set MF(α) of modal formulas over propositional variables α is generated by:
```
φ ::= p | ⊥ | φ → ψ | □φ
```
where p ∈ α.

**Definition 2.2** (GL Frame). A GL frame is a triple (W, R, ≺) where W is a set of "worlds," R ⊆ W × W is the accessibility relation, R is transitive, and the converse relation R⁻¹ is well-founded.

**Definition 2.3** (Forcing). For a GL frame M = (W, R), valuation V : α → W → Prop, and world w ∈ W:
- w ⊩ p iff V(p, w)
- w ⊩ ⊥ never
- w ⊩ φ → ψ iff w ⊩ φ implies w ⊩ ψ
- w ⊩ □φ iff for all v with wRv, v ⊩ φ

### 2.2 Modal Depth

**Definition 2.4** (Modal Depth). The modal depth of a formula is:
- depth(p) = depth(⊥) = 0
- depth(φ → ψ) = max(depth(φ), depth(ψ))
- depth(□φ) = depth(φ) + 1

### 2.3 Iterated Box and Consistency

**Definition 2.5** (Iterated Box). □⁰φ = φ, □ⁿ⁺¹φ = □(□ⁿφ).

**Proposition 2.6**. depth(□ⁿφ) = n + depth(φ).

**Definition 2.7** (Iterated Consistency). Con_n = ¬□ⁿ⊥ = (□ⁿ⊥ → ⊥).

**Proposition 2.8**. depth(Con_n) = n.

### 2.4 k-Soundness

**Definition 2.9** (k-Soundness). A world w in a GL frame M is **k-sound** if for every valuation V and every formula φ with depth(φ) ≤ k:
```
w ⊩ □φ implies w ⊩ φ
```

**Definition 2.10** (Full Soundness). A world w is **fully sound** if it is k-sound for all k ∈ ℕ.

**Definition 2.11** (Soundness Deficiency). The soundness deficiency of w is:
- The least k such that w is not k-sound (if such k exists)
- ∞ (represented as none) if w is fully sound

### 2.5 The Soundness Stratification Algebra

**Definition 2.12** (SSAlgebra). A Soundness Stratification Algebra is a tuple (M, w, S) where:
- M is a GL frame
- w ∈ M.W is a designated world
- S ⊆ ℕ is the set of soundness levels
- S = {k ∈ ℕ : w is k-sound}
- S is downward-closed: j ≤ k ∈ S implies j ∈ S

### 2.6 Depth Filtration

**Definition 2.13** (Depth Filtration). The k-th filtration level is F_k = {φ ∈ MF(α) : depth(φ) ≤ k}.

**Proposition 2.14**. The filtration is monotone: k ≤ l implies F_k ⊆ F_l.

**Proposition 2.15**. □ shifts the filtration: □φ ∈ F_{k+1} iff φ ∈ F_k.

## 3. Main Results

### 3.1 k-Soundness Hierarchy

**Theorem 3.1** (Monotonicity). If w is (k+1)-sound, then w is k-sound. More generally, j ≤ k and k-soundness implies j-soundness.

*Proof.* Immediate from the definition: depth(φ) ≤ j ≤ k, so k-soundness applies. □

**Theorem 3.2** (Characterization). Full soundness ↔ k-soundness for all k ∈ ℕ.

*Proof.* Forward: full soundness ignores depth bounds. Backward: given φ, take k = depth(φ). □

### 3.2 The Stratified Incompleteness Theorem

**Theorem 3.3** (Stratified Incompleteness). Let M be a GL frame, V a valuation, w a world. If:
1. w ⊩ □^(n+1)⊥ → ⊥ (the n-th reflection principle), and
2. w ⊮ ⊥ (consistency),

then w ⊮ □(□^(n+1)⊥ → ⊥) (cannot prove the reflection principle).

*Proof sketch.* Suppose for contradiction that w ⊩ □(□^(n+1)⊥ → ⊥). Note that □^(n+1)⊥ = □(□ⁿ⊥). So at each successor v of w, we have □(□ⁿ⊥) → ⊥. Since ⊥ implies anything (ex falso), this gives □(□ⁿ⊥) → □ⁿ⊥ at each successor. Therefore w ⊩ □(□(□ⁿ⊥) → □ⁿ⊥). By Löb's theorem (with ψ = □ⁿ⊥), w ⊩ □(□ⁿ⊥) = □^(n+1)⊥. By hypothesis (1), w ⊩ ⊥, contradicting (2). □

**Corollary 3.4** (Recursive Incompleteness). If w satisfies all reflection principles up to level n and is consistent, it cannot prove the (n+1)-th reflection principle.

**Theorem 3.5** (Quantitative Gap). The gap formula (the (n+2)-th reflection principle) has modal depth n+2, which strictly exceeds the depth of all assumed principles (depth ≤ n+1). The incompleteness gap is exactly one level of modal depth.

### 3.3 The Omega Frame and Strict Hierarchy

**Definition 3.6** (Omega Frame). The omega frame has worlds ℕ with accessibility relation i R j iff j < i (descending natural numbers).

**Theorem 3.7** (Omega Frame Computation). In the omega frame with trivial valuation:
```
world n ⊩ □ᵐ⊥  ⟺  n + 1 ≤ m
```

*Proof.* By induction on m with a sub-induction on n.
- Base (m = 0): □⁰⊥ = ⊥, which is false at all worlds. And n + 1 ≤ 0 is false.
- Step (m → m+1): □^(m+1)⊥ = □(□ᵐ⊥) at world n means ∀ j < n, j ⊩ □ᵐ⊥. By IH, this is ∀ j < n, j + 1 ≤ m, i.e., n ≤ m, i.e., n + 1 ≤ m + 1. □

**Theorem 3.8** (Omega Separation). World n+1 satisfies □^(k+1)⊥ → ⊥ iff k ≤ n.

*Proof.* □^(k+1)⊥ → ⊥ at world n+1 means ¬(n+1 ⊩ □^(k+1)⊥), which by Theorem 3.7 means ¬(n+2 ≤ k+1), i.e., k+1 < n+2, i.e., k ≤ n. □

**Theorem 3.9** (Strict Hierarchy). For each n ∈ ℕ:
1. World n+1 satisfies all reflection principles □^(k+1)⊥ → ⊥ for k ≤ n.
2. World n+1 does NOT satisfy □^(n+2)⊥ → ⊥.

*Proof.* Immediate from Theorem 3.8: (1) follows from k ≤ n, and (2) from n+1 > n. □

### 3.4 PEGB Analysis

#### Theorem: Stratified Incompleteness (Primary)

**Proof**: Complete Lean 4 proof via Löb's theorem and ex falso. Non-trivial: requires composing iterBox with Löb at the correct depth.

**Example**: At n = 0, recovers Gödel's Second Incompleteness: a consistent world satisfying □⊥ → ⊥ cannot prove □(□⊥ → ⊥). At n = 1: a world satisfying □²⊥ → ⊥ cannot prove □(□²⊥ → ⊥). The omega frame world 2 is the concrete witness: it satisfies □⊥ → ⊥ and □²⊥ → ⊥ but NOT □³⊥ → ⊥.

**Generalization**: The theorem holds for any GL frame, any valuation, any world. It is purely semantic and does not depend on the specific encoding of arithmetic. Via Solovay's completeness theorem, it specializes to any sound extension of PA by iterated consistency statements.

**Boundary**: The theorem requires *consistency* (w ⊮ ⊥). An inconsistent world trivially satisfies all formulas including □(□^(n+1)⊥ → ⊥), so the result is vacuously bypassed. Also, for n = -1 (if we define □⁰⊥ = ⊥), the reflection principle ⊥ → ⊥ is trivially true but not informative.

#### Theorem: Strict Hierarchy

**Proof**: Via computation in the omega frame using the omega_iterBox_bot formula.

**Example**: World 3 satisfies Con₁ (□⊥ → ⊥), Con₂ (□²⊥ → ⊥), Con₃ (□³⊥ → ⊥), but NOT Con₄ (□⁴⊥ → ⊥).

**Generalization**: Extends to ordinal-indexed hierarchies if we replace ℕ with ordinals. Conjecture: the hierarchy extends to all recursive ordinals below ε₀ for PA.

**Boundary**: The omega frame has *linear* accessibility. For non-linear GL frames (trees, DAGs), the relationship between world structure and soundness frontier can be more complex.

#### Theorem: Full Soundness Characterization

**Proof**: Purely definitional, no sorry, no axioms used.

**Example**: The omega frame has NO fully sound world (every world has finite soundness deficiency).

**Generalization**: In any GL frame, the set of fully sound worlds equals the intersection ∩_k {k-sound worlds}. This intersection can be empty (omega frame) or non-empty (frames with infinite descending chains — but those aren't GL frames!).

**Boundary**: Full soundness requires k-soundness for ALL k. No finite collection of k-soundness properties suffices. This is a true ω-limit, not achievable by finite approximation within the same frame.

## 4. Algorithm: Computing the Soundness Frontier

Given a finite GL frame M = (W, R) and world w, the soundness frontier can be computed:

```
Algorithm SoundnessFrontier(M, w):
  for k = 0, 1, 2, ...:
    for each formula φ of depth ≤ k:
      if w ⊩ □φ and w ⊮ φ:
        return k
  return ∞
```

For finite frames, this terminates because there are finitely many valuations. The complexity is bounded by the number of formulas of each depth level, which grows exponentially in k but is finite for each k.

For the omega frame specifically, the frontier of world n is exactly n (since world n is (n-1)-sound but not n-sound in the reflection principle sense).

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 Philosophical Implications

The k-soundness hierarchy reveals that Gödel's incompleteness is not a single barrier but an infinite staircase. Each step requires exactly one additional level of modal depth — one additional layer of self-reference. This suggests that self-knowledge is inherently *stratified*: a system can know progressively more about itself, but each new level of self-knowledge opens a new gap.

### 5.2 Connection to Ordinal Analysis

The strict hierarchy theorem shows that the iterated reflection principles form a chain indexed by ℕ. This is the beginning of a connection to ordinal analysis: the proof-theoretic ordinal of PA is ε₀, and the iterated consistency hierarchy corresponds to the ordinal progression up to ω. Extending to transfinite ordinals would connect our framework to the full ordinal analysis of arithmetic.

### 5.3 The Soundness Deficiency as Invariant

The soundness deficiency is a numerical invariant of (frame, world) pairs that measures "how much" a world can know about itself. It behaves like a dimension: fully sound worlds have infinite deficiency (dimension ∞), while worlds in the omega frame have finite deficiency equal to their index. This invariant is preserved under certain frame morphisms and could serve as a classification tool for provability structures.

## 6. Conjectures

**Conjecture 6.1** (Transfinite Extension). The k-soundness hierarchy extends to ordinal-indexed hierarchies. For each recursive ordinal α < ω₁^CK, there exists a GL frame with a world whose soundness frontier is exactly α.
