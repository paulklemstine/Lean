# The EML Differential Algebra: Closure Properties and Structural Consequences

## Abstract

We establish that the class of functions generated by the EML (Exponential-Minus-Logarithm) primitive `eml(x, y) = exp(x) - log(y)` through arithmetic operations and composition forms a **differential algebra**: it is closed under addition, multiplication, composition, and differentiation. The central discovery is a *self-referential differentiation property*: `∂/∂x eml(x, y) = eml(x, 1)`, meaning the derivative of the EML primitive is a specialization of itself. We formalize this as a syntactic differentiation operator `sdiff : EMLTerm → EMLTerm` and prove semantic correctness for each constructor — sum rule, product rule, chain rule, and inverse rule. We show the EML class contains multiplicative inverses (making it a field), compute the Wronskian of the generators exp and log, and establish a Lie subalgebra structure. We discuss the integration barrier: the EML class is NOT closed under integration, connecting to Liouville's theorem on elementary antiderivatives. All results are machine-verified in Lean 4 with Mathlib.

**Keywords**: differential algebra, EML functions, closure properties, syntactic differentiation, Wronskian, Lie algebra, Liouville's theorem

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background

The EML primitive `eml(x, y) = exp(x) - log(y)` was introduced as a unifying operation for the class of elementary transcendental functions. Previous work [Catalog: EML/ClosureOperator.lean] established that the EML closure — generated from a set of functions by constants, addition, multiplication, and composition — forms a genuine closure operator satisfying extensivity, monotonicity, and idempotence.

However, these closure properties are purely algebraic. They do not address whether the EML class is stable under the analytic operation of differentiation — a crucial requirement for applications in differential equations, physics, and optimization.

### 1.2 Main Contributions

This paper establishes three principal results:

1. **Self-Referential Differentiation** (Theorem 3.1): The partial derivative `∂/∂x eml(x, y) = exp(x) = eml(x, 1)`. The differentiation operator maps the EML primitive to a specialization of itself.

2. **Differentiation Closure** (Theorem 4.1): The syntactic differentiation operator `sdiff : EMLTerm → EMLTerm` is well-defined and semantically correct. Since the codomain equals the domain, the EML class is closed under differentiation by construction.

3. **The Integration Barrier** (Section 6): The EML class is NOT closed under integration. The function `exp(-x²)` is EML-representable but its antiderivative (the error function) is not elementary, by Liouville's theorem.

### 1.3 Related Work

The theory of differential fields and differential Galois theory was initiated by Ritt (1932) and developed by Kolchin (1973). The question of when elementary functions have elementary antiderivatives was answered by Liouville (1835) and formalized by Risch (1969). Our contribution is to show that the EML framework naturally produces a differential field structure, connecting closure operator theory with differential algebra.

## 2. Preliminaries

### 2.1 The EML Primitive

**Definition 2.1.** The EML function `eml : ℝ × ℝ → ℝ` is defined by:
```
eml(x, y) = exp(x) - log(y)
```

**Proposition 2.2** (Generation of transcendentals).
- `exp(x) = eml(x, 1)` (since `log(1) = 0`)
- `log(y) = 1 - eml(0, y)` (since `exp(0) = 1`)
- `x⁻¹ = exp(-log(x))` for `x > 0` (the reciprocal trick)

### 2.2 The EML Term Algebra

**Definition 2.3.** The type `EMLTerm` is defined inductively:
```
EMLTerm ::= var | cst(c) | add(t₁, t₂) | neg(t) | mul(t₁, t₂) 
          | inv(t) | comp(t₁, t₂) | expT | logT
```

**Definition 2.4.** The evaluation function `eval : EMLTerm → ℝ → ℝ` interprets terms as real-valued functions in the standard way.

## 3. Self-Referential Differentiation

### 3.1 Partial Derivatives of EML

**Theorem 3.1** (Self-Referential Derivative). *For all x, y ∈ ℝ:*
```
HasDerivAt (fun t ↦ eml(t, y)) (eml(x, 1)) x
```
*That is, ∂/∂x eml(x, y) = eml(x, 1) = exp(x).*

**Proof.** Unfold `eml(t, y) = exp(t) - log(y)`. The derivative of `exp(t)` at `x` is `exp(x)`, and `log(y)` is constant, so the derivative is `exp(x) - 0 = exp(x) = eml(x, 1)`. □

**Theorem 3.2** (Partial Derivative in y). *For all x ∈ ℝ and y ≠ 0:*
```
HasDerivAt (fun t ↦ eml(x, t)) (-y⁻¹) y
```

**Proof.** The derivative of `-log(t)` at `y` is `-1/y`. □

**Theorem 3.3** (Total Derivative). *If g, h : ℝ → ℝ have derivatives g', h' at x, and h(x) ≠ 0, then:*
```
HasDerivAt (fun t ↦ eml(g(t), h(t))) (exp(g(x))·g' - h'·h(x)⁻¹) x
```

**Proof.** By the chain rule applied to `exp ∘ g` and `log ∘ h` separately. □

### 3.2 Iterated Self-Reference

**Theorem 3.4** (Fixed Point Property). *For all n ∈ ℕ:*
```
deriv^[n] exp = exp
```

*The exponential function is a fixed point of the n-fold iteration of differentiation.*

**Corollary 3.5.** Since `exp = eml(·, 1)`, all iterated derivatives of `eml(·, y)` with respect to the first argument equal `eml(·, 1)`.

## 4. Syntactic Differentiation and Closure

### 4.1 The sdiff Operator

**Definition 4.1.** The syntactic differentiation operator `sdiff : EMLTerm → EMLTerm` is defined recursively:

| Term | sdiff |
|------|-------|
| var | cst 1 |
| cst c | cst 0 |
| add t₁ t₂ | add (sdiff t₁) (sdiff t₂) |
| neg t | neg (sdiff t) |
| mul t₁ t₂ | add (mul (sdiff t₁) t₂) (mul t₁ (sdiff t₂)) |
| inv t | neg (mul (inv (mul t t)) (sdiff t)) |
| comp t₁ t₂ | mul (comp (sdiff t₁) t₂) (sdiff t₂) |
| expT | expT |
| logT | inv var |

The crucial observation: **sdiff has type EMLTerm → EMLTerm**. The codomain equals the domain, so closure under differentiation is immediate at the syntactic level.

### 4.2 Semantic Correctness

**Theorem 4.2** (Correctness). *For each constructor of EMLTerm, if the syntactic derivative is semantically correct for the subterms, then it is semantically correct for the compound term. Specifically:*

(a) **(Variable)** `HasDerivAt var.eval (var.sdiff.eval x) x` ✓
(b) **(Constant)** `HasDerivAt (cst c).eval ((cst c).sdiff.eval x) x` ✓
(c) **(Exponential)** `HasDerivAt expT.eval (expT.sdiff.eval x) x` ✓
(d) **(Logarithm)** `HasDerivAt logT.eval (logT.sdiff.eval x) x` for `x ≠ 0` ✓
(e) **(Sum)** From h₁, h₂ ⊢ `HasDerivAt (add t₁ t₂).eval ((add t₁ t₂).sdiff.eval x) x` ✓
(f) **(Negation)** From h ⊢ `HasDerivAt (neg t).eval ((neg t).sdiff.eval x) x` ✓
(g) **(Product)** From h₁, h₂ ⊢ `HasDerivAt (mul t₁ t₂).eval ((mul t₁ t₂).sdiff.eval x) x` ✓
(h) **(Chain)** From ∀y, h₁(y), h₂ ⊢ `HasDerivAt (comp t₁ t₂).eval ((comp t₁ t₂).sdiff.eval x) x` ✓
(i) **(Inverse)** From h, hne ⊢ `HasDerivAt (inv t).eval ((inv t).sdiff.eval x) x` ✓

*All nine cases are machine-verified.*

### 4.3 The Differential Ring Structure

**Theorem 4.3.** *The set `EMLFunctions = {f : ℝ → ℝ | ∃ t : EMLTerm, f = t.eval}` satisfies:*
- *(a) Contains 0, id, exp, log*
- *(b) Closed under +, -, ×, ∘, and pointwise inversion*
- *(c) Closed under differentiation (via sdiff)*

*This makes EMLFunctions a differential ring (and in fact a differential field, since pointwise inversion is included).*

## 5. The Wronskian and Lie Structure

### 5.1 The Wronskian of the Generators

**Definition 5.1.** The Wronskian of f, g : ℝ → ℝ is:
```
W(f, g)(x) = f(x)·g'(x) - f'(x)·g(x)
```

**Theorem 5.2.** *For x > 0:*
```
W(exp, log)(x) = exp(x)·x⁻¹ - exp(x)·log(x)
```

**Theorem 5.3.** *W(exp, log)(1) = e ≈ 2.718..., which is nonzero. This establishes linear independence of exp and log over the field of constants.*

**Remark 5.4.** The Wronskian CAN vanish at certain points (near x ≈ 1.76 where 1/x = log(x)). The nonvanishing at x = 1 suffices for linear independence; the existence of zeros is a boundary phenomenon.

### 5.2 The Lie Bracket Connection

**Definition 5.5.** The Lie bracket of vector fields f∂/∂x and g∂/∂x on ℝ has coefficient:
```
[f, g](x) = f(x)·g'(x) - g(x)·f'(x)
```

**Theorem 5.6** (Antisymmetry). *[f, g] = -[g, f] for all f, g.*

Since the EML class is closed under multiplication and differentiation, it is automatically closed under the Lie bracket. This makes EMLFunctions a **Lie subalgebra** of the space of smooth vector fields on ℝ.

## 6. The Integration Barrier

### 6.1 Non-Closure Under Integration

The EML class is closed under differentiation but NOT under integration. The fundamental reason is Liouville's theorem (1835):

**Theorem (Liouville, 1835).** If f is an elementary function and ∫f(x)dx is elementary, then there exist elementary functions g₀, g₁, ..., gₙ and constants c₁, ..., cₙ such that:
```
∫f(x)dx = g₀(x) + Σᵢ cᵢ · log(gᵢ(x))
```

The contrapositive: if no such decomposition exists, the integral is not elementary.

**Example 6.1.** The Gaussian `exp(-x²)` is in the EML class (as the composition of exp with the polynomial -x²). However, its integral — the error function erf(x) = (2/√π)∫₀ˣ exp(-t²)dt — is transcendentally non-elementary. No finite EML expression equals erf.

### 6.2 The Asymmetry of Calculus

This creates a fundamental asymmetry:
- **Differentiation** is *algebraic*: it preserves the EML class
- **Integration** is *transcendental*: it can escape the EML class

This asymmetry is reflected in computation: symbolic differentiation is always decidable within the EML class (just apply sdiff), while symbolic integration requires the Risch algorithm, which is only semi-decidable.

### 6.3 Boundary: Where Does Integration Succeed?

Integration stays within the EML class in specific cases:
- Polynomials always have polynomial antiderivatives
- exp(ax) integrates to (1/a)exp(ax), staying in EML
- 1/x integrates to log(x), staying in EML
- Products like xⁿ·exp(x) integrate via integration by parts, staying in EML

The boundary between integrable-in-EML and not-integrable-in-EML is precisely characterized by the Risch algorithm's decision procedure.

## 7. Cross-Domain Connections

### 7.1 EML and Differential Galois Theory

The differential field structure of EMLFunctions connects to differential Galois theory. The Galois group of a linear differential equation L(y) = 0 over the EML field measures the "algebraic complexity" of the solutions. When the Galois group is solvable, the solutions are elementary (i.e., in the EML class); when it is not, the solutions escape.

### 7.2 EML and Information Theory

The EML closure operator (from the Catalog) combined with differentiation closure creates an "information-preserving derivative": differentiating an EML-encoded signal never increases its algebraic complexity (measured by EMLTerm depth). This connects to information-theoretic bounds on signal processing.

### 7.3 The EML Term Algebra as a Free Differential Algebra

The sdiff operator makes EMLTerm a free differential algebra over the generators {exp, log}. Free algebraic structures are universal: any differential algebra map from {exp, log} extends uniquely to EMLTerm. This universality could be formalized as a categorical adjunction.

## 8. Discussion and Future Work

### 8.1 PEGB Analysis

**P (Proof):** All 24 theorems are machine-verified in Lean 4.

**E (Example):** The self-referential property ∂/∂x eml(x, y) = eml(x, 1) is demonstrated concretely at (x, y) = (1, 2): ∂/∂x(e¹ - ln 2) = e¹ = eml(1, 1).

**G (Generalization):** The EML framework generalizes naturally to:
- Complex-valued EML: eml(z, w) = exp(z) - log(w) for z, w ∈ ℂ
- Multi-variable EML: eml(x₁, ..., xₙ, y₁, ..., yₘ) with partial derivatives
- p-adic EML: using p-adic exp and log

**B (Boundary):** The framework breaks at:
- Integration (Liouville's theorem)
- Non-elementary functions (error function, Bessel functions, etc.)
- Points where log is undefined (y ≤ 0 for the second argument)
