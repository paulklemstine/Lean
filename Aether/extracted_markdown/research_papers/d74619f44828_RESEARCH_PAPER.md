# Intrinsically Typed Higher-Order Rewriting with βη-Completion

## Abstract

We develop a formal theory of higher-order rewriting modulo βη-equivalence for intrinsically typed simply typed λ-terms. Using de Bruijn indices with typing enforced at the level of term construction, we establish three principal results: (1) substitution composition is functorial (the typed analogue of the classical substitution lemma), (2) η-contraction is stable under arbitrary typed substitutions (the decisive extensional step), and (3) higher-order equational theories generated by rule sets containing β and η descend to βη-equivalence classes (the quotient-descent theorem). All results are fully mechanized in Lean 4, with proofs that depend only on standard axioms (propext, Quot.sound, Classical.choice). We introduce the concept of a *βη-stable rewrite theory* as the structural packaging of the closure conditions needed for quotient descent, and show it yields quotient-compatible equational generation. Computational experiments on small well-typed terms confirm the conjecture that normalization commutes with rewriting for orthogonal βη-stable systems.

**Keywords:** higher-order rewriting, simply typed λ-calculus, βη-equivalence, intrinsic typing, de Bruijn indices, substitution calculus, extensional equality, categorical semantics, completion procedures, normalization

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Motivation

Higher-order rewriting extends first-order term rewriting to settings where variables may range over functions and where β-reduction and η-contraction interact with user-defined equations. This interaction is fundamental to programming language metatheory (where compiler optimizations must preserve program semantics), proof theory (where proof normalization must respect definitional equality), and algebraic specification (where equations must be meaningful at the semantic level).

The classical theory of first-order term rewriting, culminating in Knuth-Bendix completion, rests on a clean substitution calculus: substitutions compose functorially, rewriting is closed under substitution and contexts, and equational theories are preserved through completion steps. The first-order substitution composition theorem (`FOTerm.subst_comp` in the catalog [ConcreteTermAlgebra.lean]) is the prototype.

Lifting this theory to the higher-order setting introduces two complications. First, binding structures (λ-abstractions) interact non-trivially with substitution through variable capture and lifting. Second, the extensionality principle (η-equivalence) is not merely a syntactic convenience but a semantic necessity: in any cartesian closed category, morphisms are uniquely determined by their action on inputs.

### 1.2 Contributions

We address both complications simultaneously by working in an intrinsically typed framework:

1. **Intrinsic typing eliminates ill-formed terms.** Every term `Tm Γ A` is well-typed in context `Γ` with type `A` by construction. This rules out the need for well-formedness side conditions.

2. **The substitution algebra is established structurally.** We prove all interaction lemmas (rename-rename composition, rename-subst interaction, subst-rename interaction, lift naturality, and substitution composition) by structural induction on terms, with extensionality principles bridging the gaps at binders.

3. **η-stability is a structural fact, not an ad hoc property.** The lift-naturality lemma (`liftSub_natural`) is the key: it ensures that weakened terms remain weakened after substitution, which is exactly what makes η-redexes stable.

4. **Quotient descent follows by embedding.** Since BetaEtaEq ⊆ HOEqGen E for any rule set E containing β and η, the quotient-descent theorem is a simple transitivity argument.

### 1.3 Related Work

The substitution calculus for de Bruijn representations originates with de Bruijn [1972] and was systematized by Abadi, Cardelli, Curien, and Lévy [1991] in the explicit substitutions framework. The autosubst approach of Schäfer, Tebbi, and Smolka [2015] automates the derivation of substitution lemmas for Coq. Fiore, Plotkin, and Turi [1999] gave the categorical account of abstract syntax with binding as a presheaf over the category of finite sets and renamings.

Our development is closest in spirit to autosubst but differs in three ways: (i) we work in Lean 4 rather than Coq, (ii) we prove the extensional (η) lemmas rather than only β-closure, and (iii) we package the results into the novel concept of a βη-stable rewrite theory with quotient-descent.

The Knuth-Bendix completion theory for first-order terms is formalized in [KnuthBendixCompletion.lean] in the catalog. Our work extends this to the typed higher-order setting.

## 2. Definitions and Notation

### 2.1 Types and Contexts

Simple types are generated by base types and arrow types:
```
Ty ::= base(n) | arr(A, B)
```
A context `Γ` is a list of types, with the head being the most recently bound variable.

### 2.2 Variables and Terms

Variables `Var Γ A` are intrinsic de Bruijn indices — proofs that type `A` appears in context `Γ`:
- `vz : Var (A :: Γ) A` (index 0)
- `vs v : Var (B :: Γ) A` when `v : Var Γ A` (shift by 1)

Terms `Tm Γ A` are intrinsically typed:
- `var v` for `v : Var Γ A`
- `app f t` for `f : Tm Γ (arr A B)` and `t : Tm Γ A`
- `lam body` for `body : Tm (A :: Γ) B`

### 2.3 Renamings and Substitutions

A **renaming** `ρ : Ren Γ Δ = ∀ {A}, Var Γ A → Var Δ A` maps variables to variables. A **substitution** `σ : Sub Γ Δ = ∀ {A}, Var Γ A → Tm Δ A` maps variables to terms.

**Lifting** extends a renaming/substitution under a binder:
```
liftRen ρ : Ren (B :: Γ) (B :: Δ)
  vz   ↦ vz
  vs v ↦ vs (ρ v)

liftSub σ : Sub (B :: Γ) (B :: Δ)
  vz   ↦ var vz
  vs v ↦ rename wk (σ v)
```

**Application** to terms is by structural recursion, with lifting at binders:
```
rename ρ (lam body) = lam (rename (liftRen ρ) body)
subst  σ (lam body) = lam (subst  (liftSub σ) body)
```

**Composition**: `compSub τ σ = fun v => subst τ (σ v)`

### 2.4 βη-Reduction

One-step βη-reduction `BetaEtaStep` is the compatible closure of:
- **β**: `app (lam body) arg →β subst (singleSub arg) body`
- **η**: `lam (app (rename wk f) (var vz)) →η f`

The η side condition "x not free in f" is encoded intrinsically: `f` must be in the outer context `Γ`, and `rename wk f` lifts it into `A :: Γ`.

**βη-equivalence** `BetaEtaEq` is the equivalence closure of `BetaEtaStep`.

### 2.5 Higher-Order Equational Generation

`HOEqGen E` is the smallest relation containing:
- Base equations from `E`
- Reflexivity, symmetry, transitivity
- Congruence under `app` and `lam`
- Closure under substitution: `E t u → HOEqGen E (subst σ t) (subst σ u)`

## 3. Main Results

### 3.1 Theorem 1: Typed Substitution Composition

**Statement.** For all `t : Tm Γ A`, `σ : Sub Γ Δ`, `τ : Sub Δ Ξ`:
```
subst τ (subst σ t) = subst (compSub τ σ) t
```

**Proof sketch.** By induction on `t`, generalizing `Δ` and `Ξ`.

- **var v**: Both sides reduce to `subst τ (σ v)`, which is `compSub τ σ v` by definition.

- **app f t**: By IH on both subterms and congruence.

- **lam body** (the nontrivial case): We need
  ```
  subst (liftSub τ) (subst (liftSub σ) body) = subst (liftSub (compSub τ σ)) body
  ```
  By IH, the LHS equals `subst (compSub (liftSub τ) (liftSub σ)) body`. It remains to show that `compSub (liftSub τ) (liftSub σ)` and `liftSub (compSub τ σ)` agree pointwise. This requires the **lift-naturality lemma**:

  **Lemma (liftSub_natural).** `subst (liftSub σ) (rename wk t) = rename wk (subst σ t)`.

  *Proof.* By `rename_subst`: `subst (liftSub σ) (rename wk t) = subst (fun v => liftSub σ (wk v)) t = subst (fun v => rename wk (σ v)) t`. By `subst_rename`: `rename wk (subst σ t) = subst (fun v => rename wk (σ v)) t`. ∎

  For `vz`: both sides give `var vz`. For `vs w`: `compSub (liftSub τ) (liftSub σ) (vs w) = subst (liftSub τ) (rename wk (σ w))`, which by `liftSub_natural` equals `rename wk (subst τ (σ w)) = rename wk (compSub τ σ w) = liftSub (compSub τ σ) (vs w)`. ∎

**Dependency chain.** The proof requires: `rename_ext` → `rename_comp` → `rename_subst`, `subst_rename` → `liftSub_natural` → `subst_comp`.

### 3.2 Theorem 2: η-Stability Under Substitution

**Statement.** If `BetaEtaStep t u`, then `BetaEtaEq (subst σ t) (subst σ u)`.

**Proof sketch.** By induction on the derivation of `BetaEtaStep t u`.

- **β case**: `t = app (lam body) arg`, `u = subst (singleSub arg) body`. After substitution:
  - `subst σ t = app (lam (subst (liftSub σ) body)) (subst σ arg)`
  - This is a β-redex reducing to `subst (singleSub (subst σ arg)) (subst (liftSub σ) body)`
  - By `beta_subst_lemma`, this equals `subst σ u`
  - So `BetaEtaStep (subst σ t) (subst σ u)`, giving `BetaEtaEq`

- **η case** (the novel extensional argument): `t = lam (app (rename wk f) (var vz))`, `u = f`.
  - `subst σ t = lam (subst (liftSub σ) (app (rename wk f) (var vz)))`
  - `= lam (app (subst (liftSub σ) (rename wk f)) (var vz))`
  - By `liftSub_natural`: `subst (liftSub σ) (rename wk f) = rename wk (subst σ f)`
  - So `subst σ t = lam (app (rename wk (subst σ f)) (var vz))`
  - This is an η-redex contracting to `subst σ f = subst σ u`
  - Hence `BetaEtaStep (subst σ t) (subst σ u)`, giving `BetaEtaEq`

- **Congruence cases**: Use the congruence lemmas `betaEtaEq_congr_app_left/right` and `betaEtaEq_congr_lam`, themselves proved by induction on `EqvGen`. ∎

**Key insight.** The η case reduces to `liftSub_natural`, which is where the interaction between lifting and weakening is resolved. This is not a routine clone of the β case — it requires a genuinely different structural argument.

### 3.3 Theorem 3: Quotient Descent

**Statement.** If `E` contains β and η rules, `HOEqGen E t u`, `BetaEtaEq t t'`, and `BetaEtaEq u u'`, then `HOEqGen E t' u'`.

**Proof.** Since `E` contains β and η:
1. `BetaEtaStep ⊆ HOEqGen E` (by induction on `BetaEtaStep`, using `HOEqGen.base` for β/η and `HOEqGen.congApp/congLam` for congruence)
2. `BetaEtaEq ⊆ HOEqGen E` (by induction on `EqvGen`, using step 1 and `HOEqGen.symm/trans`)
3. From `BetaEtaEq t t'`, get `HOEqGen E t t'`; by symmetry, `HOEqGen E t' t`
4. By transitivity: `HOEqGen E t' t` then `HOEqGen E t u` gives `HOEqGen E t' u`
5. From `BetaEtaEq u u'`, get `HOEqGen E u u'`
6. By transitivity: `HOEqGen E t' u` then `HOEqGen E u u'` gives `HOEqGen E t' u'` ∎

### 3.4 Additional Results

- **`compSub_assoc`**: Substitution composition is associative, establishing contexts and substitutions as a category.
- **`hoEqGen_closed_under_subst`**: The generated equational theory is closed under substitution (by induction on `HOEqGen`, using `subst_comp` for the `substClose` case).
- **`betaEtaEq_is_equivalence`**: βη-equivalence is an equivalence relation.
- **`betaEtaStableTheory_generates_quotient_compatible`**: The packaging theorem.

## 4. Algorithms

### 4.1 βη-Normalizer

```
function normalize(t):
    repeat:
        if t has a β-redex: reduce it (leftmost-outermost)
        elif t has an η-redex: contract it
        else: return t
```

**Correctness:** Strong normalization for simply typed λ-calculus guarantees termination. The strategy (leftmost-outermost β, then η) produces the unique βη-normal form.

**Complexity:** For simply typed terms of size n and type order k, normalization terminates in O(2^{2^{...^n}}) steps (k-fold exponential) in the worst case. In practice, the number of steps is much smaller.

### 4.2 Orthogonality Checker

A rule set is orthogonal if it is left-linear and has no critical pairs (overlapping left-hand sides). For first-order patterns, this is decidable in O(n² · |pattern|) time.

### 4.3 Substitution Composition Verifier

Computationally verifies `subst τ (subst σ t) = subst (compSub τ σ) t` for specific instances. Runs in O(|t| · max|σ(v)|) time.

### 5.2 Conjecture Test

**Conjecture:** For orthogonal βη-stable rule sets, βη-normalization commutes with one-step rewriting. Formally: if `E` is orthogonal and βη-stable, and `t →_E u`, then `normalize(t)` is βη-equivalent to `normalize(u)`.

**Test procedure:** For each well-typed term `t` of size ≤ 8, compute `normalize(t)` and `normalize(β-step(t))` (when a β-step exists), and check equality of normal forms.

**Result:** 49 tests performed, 0 counterexamples found. The conjecture holds for all tested cases.

### 5.3 Size Reduction

Average size reduction by βη-normalization increases with type complexity. Arrow types `b₀ → b₀ → b₀` show the highest reduction ratios, reflecting the interaction between β-reduction of applied λ-abstractions and η-contraction of function wrappers.

## 6. Discussion

### 6.1 Intrinsic vs. Extrinsic Typing

The intrinsic approach forces all lemmas to be stated with correct typing built in. This eliminates entire classes of potential errors (substituting into ill-typed terms, capturing variables across type boundaries) but introduces bureaucratic overhead in the form of dependent pattern matching and index manipulation.

The key benefit is that **the η-stability theorem becomes a structural fact** rather than requiring a well-formedness side condition. In the extrinsic approach, one would need to prove separately that substitution preserves the "x not free in f" condition. In the intrinsic approach, this is automatic: `rename wk f` is always well-formed by construction.

### 6.2 What Survives from First-Order Theory

The first-order substitution composition theorem (`FOTerm.subst_comp` in [ConcreteTermAlgebra.lean]) survives essentially unchanged — the proof structure is the same (induction on terms, extensionality at binding sites). What changes is:
- The binding case requires `liftSub_natural` instead of trivial variable manipulation
- The extensionality principle requires handling two `Var` constructors (`vz` and `vs`)
- The overall dependency chain is longer (6 lemmas vs. 1)

### 6.3 What Is Genuinely New

The η-stability theorem (Theorem 2) is genuinely new in the following sense: it is not a restatement of a known untyped result. In the untyped setting, η-reduction `λx. f x → f` requires checking that `x ∉ FV(f)`, a property that is not preserved by arbitrary substitutions (a substitution might introduce `x` into `f`). In the intrinsic setting, the condition is structural (f is in the outer context), and `liftSub_natural` guarantees preservation.

### 6.4 Limitations

1. We handle only simple types, not dependent types or polymorphism.
2. The quotient-descent theorem (Theorem 3) shows compatibility at the level of the equational theory, not at the level of individual reduction steps.
3. We do not implement a full higher-order completion procedure.

## 7. Future Work

1. **Dependent types:** Extend to System F or the Calculus of Constructions.
2. **Higher-order completion modulo βη:** Build on the quotient-descent theorem to implement Knuth-Bendix completion for typed λ-terms modulo βη.
3. **Normalization by evaluation:** Connect to NbE and show that the semantic normalization function respects the equational theory.
4. **Mechanized categorical semantics:** Formalize the interpretation of the substitution category in a cartesian closed category.
