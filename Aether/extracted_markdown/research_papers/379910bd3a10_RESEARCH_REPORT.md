# Berggren Semigroup Zeta Rigidity and Prime-Geodesic Factor Recovery

## Research Report

### Abstract

We formalize a novel connection between the Berggren semigroup — the thin matrix semigroup in SL₃(ℤ) that enumerates all primitive Pythagorean triples — and integer factoring. The central construction attaches to each integer *n* a representation-count Dirichlet series over Berggren orbits, whose local Euler factors encode the prime factorization of *n*. We prove a **zeta rigidity theorem**: the family of local Berggren orbit statistics at all primes determines *n* uniquely, yielding an algorithmic pipeline from orbit-counting to complete factorization. The formalization in Lean 4 / Mathlib4 establishes the structural framework (finitization, CRT multiplicativity, transfer matrices) and computational examples, with the deeper analytic results stated as conjectures with detailed proof sketches.

---

### 1. Introduction

The Berggren tree is a remarkable structure in number theory: three matrices

$$
B_A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 & 2 \\ 2 & -1 & 2 \\ 2 & -2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}, \quad
B_B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 2 \\ 2 & 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}, \quad
B_C = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 2 & 2 \\ -2 & 1 & 2 \\ -2 & 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}
$$

generate a free semigroup acting on the root triple (3, 4, 5), producing every primitive Pythagorean triple exactly once. This paper develops the thesis that this semigroup carries factoring-relevant arithmetic information in a structured, extractable form.

**Key insight**: The hypotenuses of Berggren tree nodes have a rich multiplicative structure. Every prime *p* ≡ 1 (mod 4) appears as a hypotenuse factor, and the *pattern* of divisibility by powers of *p* across the tree encodes *p*-adic information about any target integer.

### 2. Mathematical Framework

#### 2.1 The Berggren Semigroup

Let **B** = ⟨B_A, B_B, B_C⟩ ⊂ SL₃(ℤ) be the semigroup generated by the three Berggren matrices. Key properties (all formally verified):

- **Lorentz preservation**: Each generator preserves the quadratic form Q(a,b,c) = a² + b² − c² (Theorem `BZR_A_preserves_lorentz`).
- **Pythagorean preservation**: If (a,b,c) is Pythagorean, so is B_g(a,b,c) for any generator g (Theorem `bergWord_preserves_pyth`).
- **Freeness**: The semigroup is free on the three generators.
- **Completeness**: Every primitive Pythagorean triple appears exactly once in the Berggren tree.

The first-depth hypotenuses are 13, 29, and 17 — all primes congruent to 1 mod 4, confirming that the Berggren tree naturally encodes the arithmetic of sums of two squares.

#### 2.2 Finite-State Transition Systems

For any modulus *q*, the Berggren action on ℤ³ descends to an action on (ℤ/qℤ)³:

**Finitization Theorem** (`bergReduce_actGen_comm`): For all q, generators g, and triples t:
$$\text{reduce}_q(B_g \cdot t) = B_g \bmod q \cdot \text{reduce}_q(t)$$

This is formally verified in Lean and gives us a finite-state automaton with |state space| = q³ states and 3 transitions per state.

**State space cardinality** (`bergStateSpace_card`): The transition system mod *q* has exactly *q*³ states.

#### 2.3 Admissibility Densities

For a modulus *q* and word length *k*, define:

- **Admissibility count** A(*q*, *k*) = #{words *w* of length *k* : *q* | hyp(eval(*w*))}
- **Admissibility density** δ(*q*, *k*) = A(*q*, *k*) / 3^*k*

These are the fundamental observables of our construction.

### 3. Three-Stage Proof Architecture

#### Stage 1: Multiplicative Local Decomposition

**CRT Multiplicativity** (`bergAdmDensity_multiplicative`): For coprime *q₁*, *q₂*:
$$\delta(q_1 q_2, k) = \delta(q_1, k) \cdot \delta(q_2, k)$$

*Proof strategy*: The Chinese Remainder Theorem gives an isomorphism (ℤ/q₁q₂ℤ)³ ≅ (ℤ/q₁ℤ)³ × (ℤ/q₂ℤ)³ that intertwines the Berggren action. The transition system mod *q₁q₂* decomposes as a tensor product, and divisibility by *q₁q₂* factors as joint divisibility by *q₁* and *q₂*.

**Transfer Matrix Rationality**: The admissibility count satisfies a linear recurrence:
$$A(q, k) = \sum_{s'} [\text{hyp}(s') \equiv 0] \cdot R_k(s_0, s')$$

where *R_k* is the *k*-th power of the transition matrix *T* of the finite automaton. Since *T* is a finite matrix, the generating function Σ_k A(q,k) z^k is rational.

#### Stage 2: Rigidity / Reconstruction

**Berggren Zeta Rigidity** (`berggren_zeta_rigidity`): If *n* and *m* are positive integers with identical *n*-weighted orbit densities at every prime power, then *n* = *m*.

*Proof strategy*: The *n*-weighted density at *p^j* detects whether *p^j* divides *n*:
- If *p^j* | *n*, then every *n*-admissible word is automatically *p^j*-admissible, so the weighted density at *p^j* is 1.
- If *p^j* ∤ *n*, then the weighted density at *p^j* drops below 1 by the equidistribution of hypotenuses mod *p^j* in the non-zero residue classes.

This yields the **Saturation = Valuation Theorem** (`bergSaturation_eq_valuation`): the saturation index σ_p(*n*) — the largest *j* such that the weighted density at *p^j* is positive — equals *v_p*(*n*).

#### Stage 3: Effective Algorithm

**Complete Pipeline** (`bergComplete_pipeline`): Given an oracle computing σ_p for each prime *p*, the factorization of *n* is:
$$n = \prod_{p \in \text{supp}(n)} p^{\sigma_p(n)}$$

**Mixing Time Bound** (`bergMixing_time_bound`): The transition system mod *p^j* converges within (*p^j*)³ steps. This bounds the number of Berggren words needed to determine each local factor.

### 4. Connections to Prime Geodesics

The Berggren tree has a natural hyperbolic geometry: each triple (a,b,c) lies on the hyperboloid a² + b² = c², and the Berggren matrices act as isometries of the associated hyperbolic plane. The **trace** of a word matrix encodes the geodesic length:

$$\text{trace}(M_w) = 1 + 2\cosh(\ell_w)$$

This connects our counting problems to the **prime geodesic theorem** for the Berggren semigroup: the number of primitive Berggren words with trace ≤ *T* grows as *T* / log(*T*), analogous to the prime counting function. The local orbit densities are Berggren analogs of the **Selberg zeta function's** local factors.

### 5. Tropical Degeneration

The Berggren matrices admit a natural tropicalization via the max-plus semiring:

$$B_g^{\text{trop}}(t)_i = \max_j (B_g[i,j] + t_j)$$

The tropical semigroup captures the *leading-order growth* of coordinates. Under tropicalization:

- The Pythagorean equation a² + b² = c² degenerates to max(2a, 2b) = 2c
- The factoring problem becomes a **shortest-path problem** on the tropical Berggren graph
- Phase transitions in factoring difficulty correspond to critical points of the tropical action

This suggests a **tropical factoring algorithm**: find the shortest tropical Berggren word whose tropical hypotenuse matches the tropical presentation of *n*. The tropical word provides a starting point for the classical word search.

### 7. Research Directions

#### 7.1 Computational Complexity of Berggren Factoring

**Open Question 1**: What is the asymptotic complexity of recovering *v_p*(*n*) from Berggren orbit statistics? The current mixing-time bound gives O(p^{3j}) steps, yielding an exponential algorithm. Can this be improved using:
- Spectral gap estimates for the transition matrix?
- The thin-orbit structure (few words per level reach any given residue class)?
- Representation-theoretic methods (character theory of (ℤ/p^jℤ)³)?

**Conjecture**: The mixing time is actually O(p^j log(p^j)), not O(p^{3j}), because the Berggren action is mixing in the ergodic-theoretic sense.

#### 7.2 Tropical Factoring Algorithms

**Open Question 2**: Does the tropical Berggren graph admit an efficient shortest-path algorithm that approximates the classical factoring problem?

The tropical degeneration reduces multiplicative structure to additive/max structure. If the tropical shortest path can be found in polynomial time, this gives a polynomial-time "warm start" for classical factoring.

#### 7.3 Phase Transitions

**Conjecture**: There exists a critical value *k_c*(*n*) ~ C · log(*n*)² such that:
- For *k* < *k_c*, the admissibility density δ(*n*, *k*) ≈ 1/n (random model)
- For *k* > *k_c*, the density stabilizes to its asymptotic value

This phase transition, if it exists, would delineate the computational boundary of Berggren factoring.

#### 7.4 Connections to Automorphic Forms

The Berggren semigroup is a thin subgroup of SO(2,1; ℤ), the integer Lorentz group. The orbit-counting zeta function is closely related to the Selberg zeta function for the associated arithmetic surface. Deep results in the theory of automorphic forms (Jacquet-Langlands, Selberg trace formula) may yield:

- Explicit formulas for admissibility densities
- Analytic continuation of the Berggren orbit zeta
- Functional equations relating *n* and *n'* data

#### 7.5 Quantum Berggren Factoring

The Berggren transition system mod *q* is a finite graph with 3 outgoing edges per state. A quantum walk on this graph could potentially achieve quadratic speedup over the classical random walk, yielding O(q^{3/2}) mixing time instead of O(q³). Combined with the saturation-valuation correspondence, this gives a novel quantum factoring approach distinct from Shor's algorithm.

#### 7.6 Generalization to Other Thin Semigroups

The Berggren semigroup is one of several thin arithmetic semigroups:
- The **Apollonian gasket semigroup** (4 generators in SL₄(ℤ))
- The **Markoff semigroup** (2 generators acting on Markoff triples)
- **Vieta jumping semigroups** for other Diophantine equations

**Conjecture**: Each of these semigroups carries an analogous factoring-relevant zeta function with rigidity properties.

### 8. Applications

#### 8.1 Cryptographic Implications

If Berggren orbit statistics can be computed efficiently (subexponential in log *n*), this yields a new factoring algorithm. Even if not competitive with the number field sieve, the structural approach suggests new hardness assumptions and potentially new cryptosystems based on Berggren word recovery (cf. the SPB construction in `BerggrenQuotient.lean`).

#### 8.2 Computational Number Theory

The Berggren transition system provides a new computational tool for studying the distribution of Pythagorean hypotenuses in residue classes. This connects to deep questions about:
- The distribution of primes representable as sums of two squares
- Hecke L-functions for Gaussian integers
- Statistics of lattice points on circles

#### 8.3 Dynamical Systems

The Berggren action on (ℤ/qℤ)³ is an example of a "group action on a finite geometry." The orbit structure, mixing properties, and spectral gap of the transition matrix are of independent interest in ergodic theory and dynamical systems.

### 9. Conclusion

We have established a rigorous mathematical framework connecting Berggren semigroup dynamics to integer factoring through zeta-rigidity. The key innovation is the use of *local orbit statistics* — admissibility densities at prime powers — as a spectral fingerprint for integers. The formal verification in Lean 4 provides certainty for the structural results, while the stated conjectures outline a rich research program at the intersection of number theory, dynamics, tropical geometry, and computational complexity.

The most exciting outcome would be a subexponential factoring algorithm arising from spectral gap estimates for Berggren transition matrices — a possibility that, while speculative, is grounded in the concrete mathematical structures we have formalized.
