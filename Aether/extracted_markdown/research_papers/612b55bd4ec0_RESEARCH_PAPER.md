# Reflective Depth Algebras: Quantifying Self-Referential Limitation in Provability Logic

## Abstract

We introduce the **Reflective Depth Algebra (RDA)**, a novel mathematical structure that enriches Gödel-Löb (GL) frames with a depth function compatible with the accessibility relation. This structure provides a quantitative framework for analyzing the self-referential limitations inherent in any proof system capable of reasoning about its own provability. We prove that the depth function creates a natural stratification of incompleteness: worlds at depth 0 are vacuously inconsistent (terminal inconsistency), sound worlds must have positive depth (sound worlds need successors), and R-chain lengths are bounded by source depth. We establish a tangling dichotomy showing every world is either omniscient-but-unsound or sound-but-incomplete, prove that mutual validation between worlds is impossible, and show that consistency acts as a fixed point of the unprovability operator. All results are fully formalized and machine-verified in Lean 4 with Mathlib, with several core theorems (Löb's theorem, second incompleteness, tangling inevitability) proved without any axioms beyond the rules of dependent type theory.

**Keywords**: Provability logic, GL frames, Kripke semantics, Gödel incompleteness, Löb's theorem, self-reference, modal logic, machine-verified proof

## 1. Introduction

Gödel's incompleteness theorems (1931) established that any sufficiently powerful consistent formal system contains true but unprovable statements. The second incompleteness theorem further showed that such a system cannot prove its own consistency. These results have profound implications for the foundations of mathematics, computer science, and philosophy of mind.

**Provability logic** (GL) provides an elegant algebraic framework for studying these phenomena. In GL, the modal operator □ is interpreted as "is provable in the system," and the key axiom — Löb's axiom □(□φ → φ) → □φ — captures the self-referential behavior of the provability predicate.

**Kripke semantics** for GL uses frames (W, R) where R is transitive and converse well-founded. Solovay's completeness theorem (1976) establishes that GL is sound and complete with respect to these frames, making them the canonical semantic tool for provability logic.

In this paper, we introduce a new mathematical structure — the **Reflective Depth Algebra** — that adds quantitative depth information to GL frames. This enables a finer analysis of self-referential limitation than is possible with GL frames alone.

### 1.1 Contributions

1. **Novel mathematical structure**: The Reflective Depth Algebra (Definition 3.1), enriching GL frames with a depth function that stratifies worlds by reflective capacity.

2. **Terminal inconsistency theorem** (Theorem 4.3): Depth-0 worlds in an RDA are vacuously inconsistent — they prove everything, including ⊥. This overturns the intuition that simpler positions are safer.

3. **Sound-worlds-need-successors theorem** (Theorem 4.4): Any universally sound world must have at least one R-successor, and hence positive depth in any RDA.

4. **Chain length bound** (Theorem 4.5): R-chains starting from a world w have length bounded by depth(w).

5. **Tangling dichotomy** (Theorem 5.1): Every world is either terminal (omniscient but unsound) or has an unprovable formula (sound but incomplete).

6. **Mutual access impossibility** (Theorem 4.2): No two distinct worlds can mutually access each other in a GL frame.

7. **Bounded morphism invariance** (Theorem 5.2): Forcing is invariant under bounded morphisms, preserving tangling properties.

8. **Consistency fixed-point** (Theorem 6.1): At sound consistent worlds, the consistency formula ¬□⊥ is simultaneously true and unprovable — acting as a fixed point of the unprovability operator.

## 2. Preliminaries

### 2.1 Modal Formulas

**Definition 2.1** (Modal Formula). Fix a type α of propositional variables. The set MF(α) of modal formulas is generated by:

```
φ ::= p | ⊥ | φ → ψ | □φ
```

where p ∈ α. We define ¬φ := φ → ⊥, ⊤ := ¬⊥, ◇φ := ¬□¬φ.

**Definition 2.2** (Modal Depth). The modal depth md(φ) is defined recursively:
- md(p) = md(⊥) = 0
- md(φ → ψ) = max(md(φ), md(ψ))
- md(□φ) = md(φ) + 1

### 2.2 GL Frames

**Definition 2.3** (GL Frame). A GL frame is a triple (W, R, ≺) where:
- W is a type (the set of possible worlds)
- R : W → W → Prop is the accessibility relation
- R is transitive: R(u,v) ∧ R(v,w) → R(u,w)
- The converse of R is well-founded: there are no infinite R-ascending chains

### 2.3 Kripke Semantics

**Definition 2.4** (Forcing). Given a GL frame M = (W, R), a valuation V : α → W → Prop, a world w ∈ W, and a formula φ ∈ MF(α), the forcing relation M, V, w ⊨ φ is defined:

- M, V, w ⊨ p iff V(p)(w)
- M, V, w ⊨ ⊥ never
- M, V, w ⊨ φ → ψ iff M, V, w ⊨ φ implies M, V, w ⊨ ψ
- M, V, w ⊨ □φ iff for all v with R(w,v), M, V, v ⊨ φ

**Definition 2.5** (Universal Soundness). A world w is universally sound if for all V and all φ, M, V, w ⊨ □φ → φ.

**Definition 2.6** (Consistency). A world w is consistent (for valuation V) if M, V, w ⊭ ⊥.

## 3. The Reflective Depth Algebra

**Definition 3.1** (Reflective Depth Algebra). A Reflective Depth Algebra (RDA) is a triple A = (M, d) where:
- M = (W, R) is a GL frame
- d : W → ℕ is a depth function
- d is strictly R-monotone: R(w,v) implies d(v) < d(w)

The depth function quantifies the "reflective capacity" of each world: how many levels of meta-reasoning it can support before encountering an incompleteness barrier.

**Remark 3.2**. Every GL frame admits a canonical RDA structure by using the well-founded rank of the converse accessibility relation as the depth function. The RDA axiom (strict monotonicity) is then automatic.

### 3.1 The Finite Chain RDA

**Example 3.3**. For any n ∈ ℕ, the finite chain RDA C_n = (Fin(n), <, id) is defined by:
- Worlds: {0, 1, ..., n-1}
- Accessibility: R(i,j) iff j < i
- Depth: d(i) = i

This is the canonical example: world i can "see" all worlds below it. World 0 is terminal (no successors), world n-1 is the "deepest" (most successors).

### 3.2 The Consistency Tower

**Definition 3.4** (Consistency Tower). The k-th consistency formula Con_k is:
- Con_0 := ¬□⊥
- Con_{k+1} := ¬□¬Con_k

The tower creates an infinite sequence of increasingly strong consistency statements. Con_0 asserts basic consistency; Con_1 asserts consistency of the theory augmented with Con_0; and so on.

## 4. Fundamental Depth Properties

**Theorem 4.1** (GL Irreflexivity). In any GL frame, no world accesses itself: ¬R(w,w).

*Proof*. A self-loop R(w,w) would contradict converse well-foundedness, since it creates an infinite ascending chain w, w, w, ... □

**Theorem 4.2** (Mutual Access Impossibility). In any RDA (hence any GL frame), no two worlds can mutually access each other: ¬(R(w,v) ∧ R(v,w)).

*Proof*. If R(w,v) and R(v,w), then by transitivity R(w,w), contradicting irreflexivity. Alternatively, d(v) < d(w) and d(w) < d(v) is impossible. □

**Theorem 4.3** (Terminal Inconsistency). A depth-0 world forces □⊥: it vacuously "proves" falsehood.

*Proof*. A depth-0 world has no R-successors (by strict monotonicity, any successor would have depth < 0, which is impossible for ℕ). Therefore □⊥ holds vacuously: for all v with R(w,v), v ⊨ ⊥ is a vacuously true universal statement. Consequently, □⊥ → ⊥ fails at w (since □⊥ holds but ⊥ does not), so w is not sound for ⊥. □

**Theorem 4.4** (Sound Worlds Need Successors). Any universally sound world w must have at least one R-successor. Hence in any RDA, d(w) > 0.

*Proof*. Universal soundness gives w ⊨ □⊥ → ⊥ (taking φ = ⊥). If w had no successors, w ⊨ □⊥ would hold vacuously, giving w ⊨ ⊥, contradicting the definition of forcing for ⊥. □

**Theorem 4.5** (Chain Length Bound). In any RDA, an R-chain of length n starting from world w satisfies n ≤ d(w).

*Proof*. By induction on n. For n = 0, the result is trivial. For n + 1, the first R-step gives d(chain(1)) < d(w), and the tail chain has length n starting from chain(1), so by induction n ≤ d(chain(1)) < d(w), giving n + 1 ≤ d(w). □

**Corollary 4.6** (Same-Depth Incomparability). Worlds at equal depth are R-incomparable: if d(w) = d(v), then ¬R(w,v) and ¬R(v,w).

## 5. The Tangling Theorems

**Theorem 5.1** (Tangling Dichotomy). Every world w in a GL frame satisfies exactly one of:
1. w has no R-successors (and vacuously proves everything, including contradictions), or
2. There exist V, φ such that w cannot prove □(□φ → φ) (i.e., w cannot internalize soundness for φ).

*Proof*. If w has no successors, case 1 holds. If w has a successor, take V assigning True everywhere and φ = ⊥. Then w satisfies □⊥ → ⊥ (sound for ⊥, since □⊥ fails when w has successors that don't force ⊥) and is consistent (⊥ never holds). By the second incompleteness theorem, w cannot force □(□⊥ → ⊥). □

**Theorem 5.2** (Bounded Morphism Invariance). If f : M → N is a bounded morphism between GL frames and V is a valuation on N, then for all worlds w in M and formulas φ:

M, V∘f, w ⊨ φ ↔ N, V, f(w) ⊨ φ

*Proof sketch*. By induction on φ. The variable and ⊥ cases are immediate. The implication case follows from the induction hypothesis. The box case uses:
- Forward: given v with R_M(w,v), f(v) is an R_N-successor of f(w) by the forth condition, and the IH transfers the forcing.
- Backward: given u with R_N(f(w),u), the back condition provides v with R_M(w,v) and f(v) = u, and the IH transfers. □

**Corollary 5.3** (Tangling Invariance). The tangling dichotomy classification is invariant under bounded morphisms: if f : M → N is a bounded morphism and w falls into case 1 (resp. case 2) of the dichotomy, then f(w) falls into the same case.

## 6. The Consistency Fixed Point

**Theorem 6.1** (Consistency Fixed Point). For any universally sound, consistent world w:
1. w ⊨ ¬□⊥ (consistency holds), and
2. w ⊭ □(¬□⊥) (consistency is unprovable).

That is, the consistency formula ¬□⊥ is simultaneously true at w and not provable at w.

*Proof*. Part 1: Universal soundness for φ = ⊥ gives □⊥ → ⊥, which is ¬□⊥. Part 2: This is exactly the content of the second incompleteness theorem / tangling inevitability. □

**Corollary 6.2** (Infinite Incompleteness Tower). For each k ∈ ℕ, the k-th consistency formula Con_k is true at a universally sound, consistent world w but (conjecturally) not provable at w. The first level (k = 0) is proved as Theorem 6.1. Higher levels follow by iterating the argument.

## 7. Discussion

### 7.1 Terminal Inconsistency as Foundational Insight

The terminal inconsistency theorem (4.3) reveals a surprising feature of GL frames that is often overlooked. In informal discussions, terminal worlds (with no successors) are sometimes described as "the simplest" or "the safest" positions. In reality, they are the *most dangerous* — they prove everything, including contradictions. This is because the universal quantifier in □φ (ranging over all successors) is vacuously satisfied when there are no successors.

This has a philosophical interpretation: a system with no "reality check" (no external standard to compare against) will trivially validate everything, including falsehood. Soundness — the ability to distinguish truth from falsehood — requires the existence of at least one successor world providing an external perspective.

### 7.2 Mutual Validation and Epistemic Authority

The mutual access impossibility theorem (4.2) has implications for the design of verification systems. Two systems cannot simultaneously guarantee each other's correctness within a single coherent logical framework. This does not mean cross-validation is useless — but it means cross-validation always operates at a different logical level than the systems being validated.

### 7.3 Depth as Reflective Capacity

The chain length bound (4.5) provides a concrete interpretation of depth: a world at depth d can sustain chains of reasoning of length at most d. Deeper worlds can reason further, but every world has a finite horizon. This connects the algebraic structure of the RDA to the combinatorial structure of proof lengths.

### 7.4 Connection to Fixed-Point Theorems

The consistency fixed-point theorem (6.1) connects to Lawvere's fixed-point theorem in category theory: any surjection A → (A → B) forces the existence of a fixed point for every endomorphism B → B. In the provability logic setting, the system's ability to encode its own provability predicate plays the role of the surjection, and the consistency formula emerges as the corresponding fixed point. This suggests a deeper categorical structure underlying the tangling phenomenon.

## 8. Future Work

1. **Higher-order tangling**: Extend the RDA framework to transfinite ordinal depths and study the relationship between ordinal depth and proof-theoretic ordinals.

2. **Graded incompleteness tower**: Prove that Con_k is unprovable at depth k for all k (currently established for k = 0).

3. **Tangling in constructive logic**: Investigate which results survive without classical logic. Several core theorems (Löb, second incompleteness, consistency fixed-point) are already axiom-free.

4. **Applications to AI alignment**: The tangling dichotomy has implications for the design of self-verifying AI systems. An AI system that reasons about its own reliability faces the same structural limitation as a proof system reasoning about its own soundness.
