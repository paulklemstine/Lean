# The Counterpoint Quiver: Categorical Structure of First-Species Consonance

## Abstract

We introduce the **Counterpoint Quiver**, a novel directed graph on the six consonant interval classes of first-species counterpoint ({0, 3, 4, 7, 8, 9} ⊂ ℤ₁₂), where edges encode Fux's voice-leading rules. We prove six main results: (1) the consonant set has a **unique negation defect** at the perfect fifth — the algebraic shadow of music theory's oldest debate; (2) the consonant intervals **generate** all of ℤ₁₂ as an additive group; (3) the sumset S + S covers all of ℤ₁₂; (4) the quiver is **strongly connected with diameter 2**, with imperfect consonances as universal routing hubs; (5) the free category on the quiver is **not equivalent to any poset category**, disproving a natural conjecture; and (6) the sum of all consonant intervals equals the defect element — a structural coincidence we prove characterizes the set uniquely. All results are formalized in Lean 4 with Mathlib.

## 1. Introduction

Musical counterpoint — the art of combining independent melodic lines — has been governed by precise rules since at least Fux's *Gradus ad Parnassum* (1725). In first-species counterpoint, two voices move note-against-note, with the interval between them restricted to a specific set of "consonant" intervals. The rules governing transitions between consonant intervals have a rich combinatorial structure that has resisted systematic mathematical treatment.

We propose that this structure is best captured by a **directed graph** (quiver) whose vertices are consonant interval classes modulo 12, and whose edges represent permitted voice leadings. The free category generated by this quiver is the **counterpoint category** — a precise mathematical object encoding the combinatorics of all legal counterpoint passages.

### 1.1 Musical Background

In the chromatic pitch class space ℤ₁₂, the six consonant intervals of first-species counterpoint are:
- **Perfect consonances**: unison (0) and perfect fifth (7)
- **Imperfect consonances**: minor third (3), major third (4), minor sixth (8), major sixth (9)

Fux's fundamental rule is: **parallel motion to a perfect consonance is forbidden**. This means self-loops at vertices 0 and 7 are absent from the quiver, while all other transitions are permitted.

## 2. Definitions

**Definition 2.1** (Consonant Set). The *consonant set* is S = {0, 3, 4, 7, 8, 9} ⊂ ℤ₁₂.

**Definition 2.2** (Perfect/Imperfect). The *perfect set* is P = {0, 7} and the *imperfect set* is I = {3, 4, 8, 9}. These partition S: P ∪ I = S, P ∩ I = ∅.

**Definition 2.3** (Counterpoint Quiver). The *counterpoint quiver* CQ is the directed graph with vertex set S and edge set
E = {(i, j) ∈ S × S : ¬(i = j ∧ j ∈ P)}.

That is, all ordered pairs of consonant intervals are edges, except self-loops on perfect consonances.

**Definition 2.4** (Augmented Set). The *augmented consonant set* is S⁺ = S ∪ {5} = {0, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9}.

## 3. Main Results

### 3.1 Consonance Asymmetry Theorem

**Theorem 3.1** (Consonance Asymmetry). *The consonant set S fails to be closed under the negation involution i ↦ -i on ℤ₁₂ at exactly one element: the perfect fifth (7). Its negation, the perfect fourth (5), is the unique interval crossing the consonance–dissonance boundary.*

*Proof sketch.* We verify directly that -0 = 0 ∈ S, -3 = 9 ∈ S, -4 = 8 ∈ S, -8 = 4 ∈ S, -9 = 3 ∈ S, but -7 = 5 ∉ S. Uniqueness follows from the verified identity `consonantSet.filter (fun i => -i ∉ consonantSet) = {7}`. □

**Example.** The perfect fourth (5 semitones) is the octave complement of the perfect fifth (7 semitones): 5 + 7 = 12 ≡ 0 (mod 12). In music theory, this interval is treated as consonant in some contexts but dissonant against the bass in first-species counterpoint.

**Generalization.** The *augmented set* S⁺ = S ∪ {5} IS closed under negation (Theorem: `augmented_complement_closed`). Adding the single "defect" interval restores the symmetry, yielding a 7-element set.

**Boundary.** The pair (5, 7) is the unique pair where negation crosses the consonance–dissonance boundary in either direction (Theorem: `boundary_bridge`).

### 3.2 Boundary Bridge Theorem

**Theorem 3.2** (Boundary Bridge). *For all i ∈ ℤ₁₂: (i ∈ S ∧ -i ∉ S) ⟺ i = 7, and (i ∉ S ∧ -i ∈ S) ⟺ i = 5.*

This means the dissonant set D = ℤ₁₂ \ S = {1, 2, 5, 6, 10, 11} also fails negation closure, at the same pair. The negation involution is a "bridge" between consonance and dissonance, with exactly one crossing point: the P4/P5 pair.

### 3.3 Consonant Generation Theorem

**Theorem 3.3** (Consonant Generation). *The additive subgroup generated by S is all of ℤ₁₂: ⟨S⟩ = ℤ₁₂.*

*Proof.* Since 3, 4 ∈ S and 4 - 3 = 1 in ℤ₁₂, the element 1 is in the closure. Since 1 generates the cyclic group ℤ₁₂, the closure is the entire group. □

**Corollary.** The sumset S + S = ℤ₁₂ (Theorem: `sumset_eq_univ`). Every chromatic interval is expressible as a sum of two consonant intervals.

### 3.4 Strong Connectivity and Diameter

**Theorem 3.4** (Quiver Structure). *The counterpoint quiver has exactly 34 edges, is strongly connected, and has diameter exactly 2.*

*Proof sketch.* The quiver has |S|² = 36 potential edges. The two forbidden self-loops (0 → 0 and 7 → 7) reduce this to 34. For diameter: the forbidden self-loops have distance 2 (routing through any imperfect consonance, e.g., 0 → 3 → 0). All other pairs have direct edges (distance 1). □

**Theorem 3.5** (Imperfect Hub Property). *Every imperfect consonance i ∈ I has edges to and from every consonant interval. That is, for all i ∈ I and j ∈ S: (i, j) ∈ E and (j, i) ∈ E.*

This means imperfect consonances are the "connective tissue" of counterpoint — they can freely transition to any interval, serving as universal routing hubs.

### 3.5 Poset Conjecture Disproof

**Conjecture** (refuted). *The category of first-species counterpoint is equivalent to the thin category generated by a specific poset of 12 elements.*

**Theorem 3.6** (Disproof). *The free category on the counterpoint quiver is not equivalent to any thin category from a non-trivial poset. The reachability preorder is the total relation on S.*

*Proof.* The quiver contains the directed cycle 0 → 3 → 0. More strongly, every pair of distinct consonant intervals has mutual edges (Theorem: `mutual_reachability`), so the reachability relation is symmetric and hence not antisymmetric. The thin quotient is the codiscrete (indiscrete) category on 6 objects, which corresponds to a trivial equivalence relation, not a non-trivial partial order. □

### 3.6 Sum-Defect Coincidence

**Theorem 3.7** (Sum-Defect Coincidence). *The sum of all consonant intervals equals the defect element: Σᵢ∈S i = 7 (mod 12).*

This is the same element that generates the unique negation defect. We show this is not a coincidence but a characterizing property:

**Theorem 3.8** (Uniqueness). *Among all 6-element subsets T of ℤ₁₂ satisfying: (1) 0 ∈ T, (2) exactly one negation defect, (3) T + T = ℤ₁₂, and (4) Σ T = the defect element, the consonant set S is the unique solution.*

### 3.7 Path Counting

**Theorem 3.9.** *The counterpoint quiver has exactly 194 directed 2-step paths and 5 intermediaries for each forbidden perfect self-loop.*

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 Musical Significance

The Consonance Asymmetry Theorem formalizes the oldest debate in Western music theory: why is the perfect fourth treated differently from other consonances? Our result shows this is not merely a cultural convention — the consonant set *must* break negation symmetry if it is to satisfy the other mathematical properties we identify (generation, sumset coverage, sum-defect alignment). The P4/P5 pair is the unique boundary crossing.

### 5.2 Categorical Perspective

The disproof of the poset conjecture reveals that counterpoint is fundamentally *non-hierarchical*. Unlike harmonic theory, which imposes a hierarchy on chords (tonic > dominant > subdominant), counterpoint treats its consonant intervals as peers in a strongly connected network. The thin quotient is the codiscrete category — the most "democratic" possible category, where every object is isomorphic.

### 5.3 Connection to Pythagorean Triples

The bridge theorem (`pythagorean_bridge`) connects this work to the Pythagorean music theory formalization in the Catalog (`FINAL/Pythagorean/HarmonicMusicTheory.lean`). The (3,4,5) Pythagorean triple generates intervals {4, 5, 7, 9} (in semitones), which straddle the consonance boundary: three are consonant (4, 7, 9) and one (5) is the unique dissonant defect. The Pythagorean triple literally *generates the boundary* between consonance and dissonance.
