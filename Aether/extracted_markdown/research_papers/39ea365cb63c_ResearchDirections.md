## Abstract

We extend the Factoring via Berggren Universal Parent research program by resolving
several open questions, correcting three previously stated claims, and discovering
new algebraic invariants of the ghost map. All results are machine-verified in
Lean 4 with Mathlib (0 sorries).

**Key findings:**
1. The **ghost trace identity** gp + gq + gh = a + b - c holds for all integer triples.
2. The **unit probe** (1, N, N) has deficit 1 and descends by 2 per step.
3. p | deficit(p, q, pq) if and only if p | q² (Deficit-Factor Iff).
4. The ghost map eigenvalues are -1, 3±2√2, giving a spectral framework.
5. The linear triplet (x, N, x+N) maps to (-x, -N, x+N), NOT to itself.
6. The σ-descent for quadruples introduces a -2σ² correction.
7. The ghost map linearity prevents information amplification.

## 1. Corrections to Prior Claims

### Correction 1: Linear Triplet Fixed Point

**Prior claim:** UP(x, N, x+N) = (x, N, x+N) (fixed point).  
**Correction:** The signed ghost map gives G(x, N, x+N) = (-x, -N, x+N).
The linear triplet is **not** a fixed point of the signed ghost map.
It is a fixed point only when taking absolute values of the first two components.
The squared map G²(x, N, x+N) = (-3x-4N, -4x-3N, 5x+5N) ≠ (x, N, x+N),
so it does not even have period 2 under the signed map.

**Lean verification:** See `linear_triplet_gp`, `linear_triplet_gq`, `linear_triplet_gh`
and `linear_triplet_sq_p/q/h` in `OpenResearchTheorems.lean`.

### Correction 2: Period-2 Claim for Signed Ghost Map

**Prior claim:** The factoring triplet exhibits period-2 oscillation.  
**Correction:** This is true only with absolute values (UP map), not the signed ghost map G.
Verified: G(3,4,5) = (1,0,1), G²(3,4,5) = (-1,0,1), G³(3,4,5) = (-3,-4,5).
The orbit never returns to (3,4,5) under G.

**Lean verification:** `period2_false_signed` in `OpenResearchTheorems.lean`.

### Correction 3: σ-Descent for Quadruples

**Prior claim:** The σ-descent preserves the Pythagorean equation for quadruples.  
**Correction:** For quadruples with σ = (a+b+c-d)/2:
(a-σ)² + (b-σ)² + (c-σ)² = (d-σ)² **- 2σ²**

The correction term -2σ² is non-zero for generic quadruples.
The correct transformation for k=4 without correction is:
(d-b-c)² + (d-a-c)² + (d-a-b)² = (2d-a-b-c)².

**Lean verification:** `quad_descent_correction` and `k4_algebraic_identity`.

### Correction 4: Ghost Map Characteristic Polynomial

**Prior claim:** Characteristic polynomial (λ-1)(λ²-4λ+1) = 0.  
**Correction:** The correct polynomial is λ³ - 5λ² - 5λ + 1 = (λ+1)(λ²-6λ+1),
giving eigenvalues -1, 3±2√2 (not 1, 2±√3).

**Lean verification:** `char_poly_factorization`, `ghost_matrix_det`, `ghost_matrix_trace_val`.

## 2. New Theorems (Machine-Verified)

### Theorem 1: Ghost Trace Identity
gp(a,b,c) + gq(a,b,c) + gh(a,b,c) = a + b - c for all (a,b,c) ∈ ℤ³.

### Theorem 2: Deficit Preservation
deficit(G(v)) = deficit(v), where deficit(a,b,c) = a² + b² - c².

### Theorem 3: Deficit-Factor Iff
p | deficit(p, q, pq) ↔ p | q².
**Corollary:** For distinct primes p, q: p ∤ deficit(p, q, pq).

### Theorem 4: Unit Probe Properties
- deficit(1, N, N) = 1 (constant)
- gp(1, N, N) = 1, gq(1, N, N) = 2-N, gh(1, N, N) = N-2
- |gq| = gh = N-2 for N ≥ 2
- gh < N for N ≥ 3 (descent)

### Theorem 5: Universal Gap
gq(a,b,c) - gp(a,b,c) = a - b. The factor gap is preserved exactly.

### Theorem 6: Ghost Map Linearity
The ghost map is ℤ-linear: G(αu + βv) = αG(u) + βG(v).

### Theorem 7: Two-Invariant Product Formula
2ab = (a+b-c)² + 2(a+b-c)c - (a²+b²-c²).

### Theorem 8: Linear Triplet Structure
G(x, N, x+N) = (-x, -N, x+N). Deficit = -2xN. N | deficit.

### Theorem 9: Divisor Triplet Analysis
deficit(d, e, de) = -(d²-1)(e²-1) + 1. Negative for d,e ≥ 2.
Ghost sum: gp + gq = 3(d+e) - 4de < 0 for d,e ≥ 2.
Ghost gap: gq - gp = d - e (encodes factor difference).

### Theorem 10: Quadruple Extension
- Parity: a²+b²+c²=d² ⟹ 2 | (a+b+c-d)
- σ-descent correction: -2σ²
- Correct k=4 identity: (d-b-c)²+(d-a-c)²+(d-a-b)² = (2d-a-b-c)²

### Theorem 11: Eigenstructure
- Eigenvalues: -1, 3+2√2, 3-2√2
- (1,-1,0) is eigenvector with eigenvalue -1
- det(G) = -1, tr(G) = 5

### Theorem 12: Ghost Squared
G²(a,b,c) = (9a+8b-12c, 8a+9b-12c, -12a-12b+17c).

## 3. Hypotheses and Future Directions

### HIGH PRIORITY

**Direction A: Lattice-Ghost Hybrid Method**

Combine the ghost map's linear structure with LLL lattice reduction.
The factoring triplets {(x, N, √(x²+N²)) : x ∈ ℤ} form a polynomial curve in ℤ³.
Reducing the lattice generated by ghost iterates of this curve might reveal short
vectors encoding factors.

*Formalization target:* Define the ghost lattice as a sublattice of ℤ³ and prove
structural properties (rank, determinant, short vector bounds).

**Direction B: Non-Linear Extensions**

The ghost map linearity (Theorem 6) prevents information amplification.
To overcome this barrier, we need non-linear operations. Candidates:
- GCD operations (already used in multi-triplet voting)
- Modular reduction (ghost map mod small primes)
- Rounding operations (nearest-integer maps on √(x²+N²))

*Key question:* Can any polynomial-time non-linear post-processing of ghost map
iterates yield a factoring algorithm?

**Direction C: Quantum Ghost Period Finding**

The ghost map G mod p acts on (ℤ/pℤ)³ with finite order.
Since the eigenvalues are -1 and 3±2√2, the order depends on the
multiplicative order of (3+2√2) in 𝔽_p[√2], which in turn depends
on whether 2 is a quadratic residue mod p.

*Formalization target:* Prove that the order of G mod p divides
p² - 1 when 2 is a QR mod p, and p² + 1 otherwise.

### MEDIUM PRIORITY

**Direction D: Multi-Channel Correlation Theory**

The multi-triplet voting strategy (Demo 4) achieves 100% success on small
semiprimes by aggregating GCD clues across triplet channels.

*Key question:* What is the expected number of triplets needed to factor N
with probability > 1/2? Does this grow as O(√N), O(N^{1/3}), or O(polylog N)?

**Direction E: Higher-Dimensional Berggren Descent**

The σ-descent works cleanly for k=5 (sextuple) since the correction term
(k-5)σ² vanishes. In dimension 6 (a₁²+...+a₅²=a₆²), there are 5 independent
ghost parameters, potentially enabling faster factoring through additional constraints.

*Formalization target:* Define the 6×6 ghost matrix, prove deficit preservation,
and analyze its eigenstructure.

**Direction F: Spectral Lower Bounds**

The ghost map linearity (Theorem 6) implies that any ghost-based factoring
algorithm requires Ω(√N) operations. Can we prove a tighter lower bound?

*Approach:* Use the eigenvalue structure to show that the factor gap
information (eigenvalue -1 direction) cannot be extracted faster than
by exhaustive search along the contracting eigenspace.

### EXPLORATORY

**Direction G: Elliptic Curve Connection**

The ghost map on the Lorentz cone a²+b²=c² is a rational automorphism of a
conic section. After stereographic projection, this becomes a Möbius
transformation on ℙ¹. Connecting to the ECM factoring literature
might yield hybrid factoring methods.

**Direction H: Tropical Ghost Map**

Replace (+,×) with (min,+) to obtain a tropical analogue. The tropical
ghost map preserves the tropical Lorentz norm min(2a,2b)-2c and might
have qualitatively different dynamics (finite orbits).

**Direction I: Error Correction**

The trace identity and deficit preservation provide two independent
linear/quadratic constraints on ℤ³ triples. This gives a rate-1/3
error-detecting code. Extending with additional invariants could
yield error-correcting capabilities.

**Direction J: Markoff-Berggren Hybrid**

The Markoff equation x²+y²+z²=3xyz has Vieta involutions analogous to
Berggren steps. Composing Markoff involutions with ghost map operations
might produce new arithmetic structures on lattice points.

## 6. Conclusion

The ghost map provides a rich algebraic framework with beautiful invariants
(deficit preservation, universal gap, trace identity), but its linearity
fundamentally limits its factoring power. The most promising extensions
involve combining ghost map structure with non-linear methods (lattice
reduction, GCD computations, quantum period finding) to leverage the
algebraic insights while overcoming the linearity barrier.
