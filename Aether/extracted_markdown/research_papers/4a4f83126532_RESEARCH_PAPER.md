# Sonic Mathematics: First-Species Counterpoint as a Voice-Leading Quiver

**Abstract.** We formalize the voice-leading rules of first-species counterpoint (after Fux, 1725) as a directed multigraph — the *Counterpoint Quiver* — whose vertices are the six consonant intervals modulo 12 semitones and whose edges are voice leadings permitted by the parallel-motion constraint. We prove five structural theorems: (1) the quiver is strongly connected; (2) permitted voice leadings are not closed under composition, so the quiver does not extend to a category; (3) perfect consonances admit exactly 1 self-loop versus 12 for imperfect consonances, exhibiting a bottleneck asymmetry; (4) the voice-swap involution on ℤ/12ℤ does not preserve consonance, formalizing the privileged role of the bass; and (5) perfect consonances receive exactly 61 incoming voice leadings versus 72 for imperfect consonances. The entire development is parameterized over a general `CounterpointSystem n` structure on ℤ/nℤ, enabling application to microtonal temperaments. All results are formally verified.

**Keywords:** counterpoint, voice leading, category theory, quiver, modular arithmetic, music theory, formal verification

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Motivation

The rules of first-species counterpoint, codified by J. J. Fux in *Gradus ad Parnassum* (1725), constitute one of the oldest and most enduring formal systems in Western art. Every note-against-note pairing must form a consonant interval, and *parallel motion into a perfect consonance is forbidden*. These constraints have been analyzed qualitatively for centuries, but a fully rigorous mathematical treatment — one that can enumerate all legal voice leadings and prove structural properties of the resulting combinatorial object — has been lacking.

Recent work in mathematical music theory, notably Tymoczko's *A Geometry of Music* (2011), Mazzola's *The Topos of Music* (2002), and Cohn's *Audacious Euphony* (2012), has brought geometric and algebraic tools to bear on voice leading. However, these approaches typically model voice-leading *spaces* (quotient orbifolds, simplicial complexes) rather than the *constraint structure* of specific rule systems. Our approach is complementary: we fix a rule system and study the directed graph it generates.

### 1.2 Main Contribution

We introduce the **Counterpoint System**, a parameterized algebraic structure `CounterpointSystem n` over ℤ/nℤ that captures the essential data of any counterpoint-like voice-leading constraint:

- A finite set of *consonant* intervals
- A subset of *perfect* consonances
- The rule that parallel motion into perfect consonances is forbidden

We instantiate this for the standard 12-tone equal temperament (12-TET) and prove five theorems that reveal the combinatorial architecture of first-species counterpoint.

### 1.3 Original Conjecture and Its Refutation

The project originated with the conjecture that the category of first-species counterpoint over the diatonic scale is equivalent to the thin category generated by a specific poset of 12 elements. Our formalization demonstrates that this conjecture is **false**: permitted voice leadings fail the composition axiom and therefore do not form a category at all. The actual structure — a strongly connected quiver with bottleneck asymmetries — is both richer and more musically meaningful than a poset.

## 2. Definitions

### 2.1 The Counterpoint System

**Definition 2.1** (CounterpointSystem). Let $n \geq 1$. A *counterpoint system* over $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ is a triple $(C, P, \varphi)$ where:

1. $C \subseteq \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ is a nonempty finite set of *consonant intervals*
2. $P \subseteq C$ is a nonempty set of *perfect consonances*
3. $C \setminus P \neq \emptyset$ (there exists at least one imperfect consonance)
4. $\varphi$ is the *parallel-motion restriction*: parallel voice leadings into elements of $P$ are forbidden

The formal definition in our development additionally records the subset inclusion $P \subseteq C$ and existence witnesses as structure fields.

**Definition 2.2** (Voice Leading). A *voice leading* over $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ is a pair $v = (b, s) \in (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^2$, where $b$ is the bass motion and $s$ is the soprano motion (both measured in semitones mod $n$).

**Definition 2.3** (Target Interval). Given a source interval $i \in \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ and a voice leading $v = (b, s)$, the *target interval* is:
$$\tau(i, v) = i + s - b$$

This formula has a clear musical meaning: the interval increases by however much the soprano rises and decreases by however much the bass rises.

**Definition 2.4** (Parallel Motion). A voice leading $v = (b, s)$ is *parallel* if $b = s$ and $b \neq 0$. That is, both voices move by the same nonzero amount.

**Definition 2.5** (Permitted Voice Leading). A voice leading $v$ from source $i$ to target $j$ is *permitted* in a counterpoint system $(C, P, \varphi)$ if:

1. $i \in C$ (source is consonant)
2. $j \in C$ (target is consonant)  
3. $\tau(i, v) = j$ (the voice leading actually maps $i$ to $j$)
4. $\neg(j \in P \wedge v\text{ is parallel})$ (no parallel motion into perfect consonances)

**Definition 2.6** (Composition of Voice Leadings). Given $v_1 = (b_1, s_1)$ and $v_2 = (b_2, s_2)$, the *composite* is $v_1 \cdot v_2 = (b_1 + b_2, s_1 + s_2)$.

### 2.2 The Standard 12-TET System

The standard system is the instance with $n = 12$:

- $C = \{0, 3, 4, 7, 8, 9\}$ (unison, minor third, major third, perfect fifth, minor sixth, major sixth)
- $P = \{0, 7\}$ (unison, perfect fifth)

These intervals correspond to the six intervals traditionally classified as consonant in Western music theory. The choice reflects acoustic properties: these intervals have frequency ratios close to simple fractions (1:1, 6:5, 5:4, 3:2, 8:5, 5:3).

### 2.3 The Counterpoint Quiver

**Definition 2.7** (Counterpoint Quiver). The *counterpoint quiver* of a system $(C, P, \varphi)$ is the directed multigraph $Q = (V, E)$ where:

- $V = C$ (vertices are consonant intervals)
- $E(i, j) = \{v \in (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^2 : v\text{ is permitted from } i \text{ to } j\}$ (edges are permitted voice leadings)

## 3. Main Results

### 3.1 Strong Connectivity

**Theorem 3.1** (`exists_permitted_voice_leading`). *For any two consonant intervals $i, j \in C$ in the standard 12-TET system, there exists a permitted voice leading from $i$ to $j$.*

*Proof sketch.* Define the *canonical voice leading* $\kappa(i,j) = (0, j - i)$: bass holds, soprano moves by $j - i$. Then $\tau(i, \kappa(i,j)) = i + (j-i) - 0 = j$, so the voice leading reaches the target. It remains to check the parallel-motion constraint. If $i \neq j$, then $\kappa(i,j) = (0, j-i)$ with $j - i \neq 0$, so bass and soprano move by different amounts — this is not parallel motion. If $i = j$, then $\kappa(i,i) = (0,0)$, the identity, which has $b = 0$, so it is not parallel (parallel requires $b \neq 0$). In both cases, the constraint is satisfied. The proof that $i, j \in C$ follows by hypothesis. $\square$

**Corollary 3.2.** The counterpoint quiver of the standard 12-TET system is strongly connected as a directed graph.

### 3.2 Non-Composability

**Theorem 3.3** (`non_composability`). *There exist consonant intervals $i, j, k$ and voice leadings $v_1, v_2$ such that $v_1$ is permitted from $i$ to $j$, $v_2$ is permitted from $j$ to $k$, but the composite $v_1 \cdot v_2$ is NOT permitted from $i$ to $k$.*

*Proof sketch.* We exhibit a concrete counterexample. Choose intervals and voice leadings such that both individual steps avoid parallel motion into perfect consonances, but the composite motion (summing bass and soprano motions) constitutes parallel motion into a perfect consonance. The composite has $b_1 + b_2 = s_1 + s_2 \neq 0$ with target $k \in P$, violating the constraint. $\square$

**Corollary 3.4.** The permitted voice leadings of first-species counterpoint do not form a category. In particular, the counterpoint quiver is not the underlying graph of any category whose morphisms are exactly the permitted voice leadings.

This refutes the original conjecture that first-species counterpoint is equivalent to a thin category generated by a poset.

### 3.3 Perfect Consonance Bottleneck

**Theorem 3.5** (`perfect_self_loop_unique`). *If $i \in P$ is a perfect consonance, then the only permitted voice leading from $i$ to $i$ is the identity $(0, 0)$.*

*Proof sketch.* A self-loop at $i$ is a voice leading $(b, s)$ with $\tau(i, (b,s)) = i$, i.e., $s = b$. If $b \neq 0$, this is parallel motion into $i \in P$, which is forbidden. Hence $b = s = 0$. $\square$

**Theorem 3.6** (`imperfect_self_loops_all`). *If $i \in C \setminus P$ is an imperfect consonance, then every voice leading $(b, s)$ with $s = b$ is a permitted self-loop at $i$. There are exactly 12 such self-loops.*

*Proof sketch.* A voice leading $(b, b)$ satisfies $\tau(i, (b,b)) = i + b - b = i$, so it maps $i$ to itself. Since $i \notin P$, the parallel-motion constraint does not apply regardless of whether the motion is parallel. There are 12 choices of $b \in \mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$, yielding 12 self-loops. $\square$

**Corollary 3.7** (Bottleneck Ratio). The ratio of self-loops at a perfect consonance to self-loops at an imperfect consonance is $1:12$.

### 3.4 Voice-Swap Asymmetry

**Theorem 3.8** (`voice_swap_breaks_consonance`). *The involution $\sigma: i \mapsto -i$ on $\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$ does not preserve the consonant set $C$. Specifically, $\sigma(7) = 5 \notin C$.*

*Proof sketch.* Direct computation: $-7 \equiv 5 \pmod{12}$, and $5 \notin \{0, 3, 4, 7, 8, 9\}$. The perfect fifth (7 semitones) maps to the perfect fourth (5 semitones), which is classified as dissonant in first-species counterpoint. $\square$

**Remark 3.9.** The full image of $C$ under $\sigma$ is $\{-0, -3, -4, -7, -8, -9\} = \{0, 9, 8, 5, 4, 3\} = \{0, 3, 4, 5, 8, 9\}$. This differs from $C = \{0, 3, 4, 7, 8, 9\}$ in exactly one element: $7 \leftrightarrow 5$. The consonant set is "almost" symmetric but the asymmetry at the fifth/fourth is structurally decisive.

### 3.5 Hom-Set Computation

**Theorem 3.10** (`total_permitted_to_perfect`). *For each perfect consonance $j \in P$, the total number of permitted voice leadings from all consonant sources to $j$ is exactly 61.*

**Theorem 3.11** (`total_permitted_to_imperfect`). *For each imperfect consonance $j \in C \setminus P$, the total number of permitted voice leadings from all consonant sources to $j$ is exactly 72.*

*Proof sketch.* For a fixed target $j$, and a fixed source $i$, the voice leadings mapping $i$ to $j$ are exactly those $(b, s)$ with $s - b = j - i$, a constraint that determines $s$ given $b$, yielding 12 candidates. If $j \in P$, we must subtract the parallel motions: those with $b = s \neq 0$ and $s - b = 0$, which requires $j = i$. So only the self-loop source loses motions: 11 of 12 self-preserving motions are forbidden, leaving 1. Over 6 sources: $5 \times 12 + 1 = 61$ for a perfect target, vs. $6 \times 12 = 72$ for an imperfect target. $\square$

**Corollary 3.12.** The constraint imposed by the parallel-motion rule reduces the incoming hom-set of a perfect consonance by $72 - 61 = 11$ voice leadings, a reduction of approximately 15.3%.

## 4. The CounterpointSystem Abstraction

### 4.1 Generality

The `CounterpointSystem n` structure is parameterized by any positive integer $n$, enabling the study of counterpoint-like constraints in arbitrary equal temperaments. For example:

- **19-TET** ($n = 19$): The just minor third ≈ 5.03 steps, just major third ≈ 6.12 steps, just fifth ≈ 11.10 steps. A counterpoint system could use $C = \{0, 5, 6, 11, 13, 14\}$, $P = \{0, 11\}$.
- **31-TET** ($n = 31$): Offers excellent approximations to 7-limit just intonation. Consonance sets could include septimal intervals.
- **24-TET** (quarter-tones, $n = 24$): Consonance depends on which quarter-tone intervals are deemed consonant.

The structural theorems (strong connectivity via canonical voice leadings, bottleneck asymmetry) generalize whenever the consonance and perfection sets satisfy the axioms of `CounterpointSystem`.

### 4.2 The Canonical Voice Leading Lemma

**Lemma 4.1** (`targetInterval_canonical`). For any $i, j \in \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$:
$$\tau(i, \kappa(i,j)) = j$$

**Lemma 4.2** (`canonical_not_parallel`). For $i \neq j$:
$$\kappa(i,j) = (0, j-i) \text{ is not parallel}$$

These two lemmas provide the universal connectivity mechanism: oblique motion (bass holds) always reaches the target without triggering the parallel-motion constraint.

## 5. Connections to Existing Theory

### 5.1 Tymoczko's Voice-Leading Geometry

Tymoczko (2006, 2011) models voice leadings as paths in quotient orbifolds $T^n / S_n$, where $T^n$ is the $n$-torus of pitch classes and $S_n$ acts by permuting voices. Our approach is complementary: rather than modeling the continuous geometry of voice-leading *space*, we study the discrete combinatorics of *permitted* voice leadings under a specific rule system. The non-composability result (Theorem 3.3) explains why Tymoczko's geometric approach, which works with unrestricted voice leadings, cannot directly capture rule-based counterpoint — the constraint surface is not closed under concatenation.

### 5.2 Mazzola's Topos of Music

Mazzola (2002) applies topos theory and category theory to music, including counterpoint. Our work intersects with his in the categorical framing, but our conclusion differs: whereas Mazzola constructs categories of musical objects (gestures, denotators), we show that the *rules of counterpoint themselves* resist categorical formalization — permitted voice leadings fail composition. This is a structural negative result that constrains which categorical frameworks can faithfully model counterpoint.

### 5.3 Cohn's Parsimonious Voice Leading

Cohn (2012) studies voice leadings that minimize total motion, particularly the neo-Riemannian L, P, R transformations. Our quiver includes *all* permitted voice leadings, not just parsimonious ones. The bottleneck results (Theorems 3.5–3.6) suggest that restricting to parsimonious voice leadings would exacerbate the asymmetry between perfect and imperfect consonances, since the identity is the only parsimonious self-loop at a perfect consonance.

### 5.4 Pythagorean Harmonics

The consonant intervals $\{0, 3, 4, 7, 8, 9\}$ arise from acoustic considerations rooted in Pythagorean ratios. Prior work in this project (`HarmonicMusicTheory.lean`) establishes consonance from the perspective of Pythagorean triples and simple frequency ratios. The present work studies the *dynamics* of consonance — how consonant intervals connect to each other through permitted motion — complementing the *statics* of consonance classification.

## 6. Computational Aspects

### 6.1 Decidability

All predicates in the development — consonance, perfection, parallel motion, permission — are decidable over $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$. This enables:

- **Enumeration**: All permitted voice leadings can be computed by exhaustive search over $(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^2 \times C \times C$.
- **Verification**: Structural properties (connectivity, composition closure) can be checked by finite computation, yielding `by decide` proofs.

### 6.2 Complexity

For a counterpoint system over $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ with $|C| = c$ consonances:

- **Voice leadings**: $n^2$ possible voice leadings
- **Source-target pairs**: $c^2$ pairs to check
- **Total permission checks**: $O(c^2 n^2)$
- **For standard 12-TET**: $6^2 \times 12^2 = 5184$ checks

### 6.3 Enumeration Results for Standard 12-TET

| Target type | Sources | VLs per source | Self-loop correction | Total incoming |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Perfect ($j \in P$) | 6 | 12 | $-11$ (at $j = i$) | 61 |
| Imperfect ($j \in C \setminus P$) | 6 | 12 | $0$ | 72 |

Total edges in the quiver: $2 \times 61 + 4 \times 72 = 122 + 288 = 410$.

## 7. Discussion

### 7.1 Why Non-Composability Matters

The failure of composition (Theorem 3.3) is the most mathematically significant result. It means that counterpoint rules are inherently *local*: they constrain adjacent beats but not multi-beat trajectories. A sequence of individually legal moves can lead to an illegal composite — a phenomenon familiar to composers as the difficulty of "planning ahead" in strict counterpoint. This locality is reminiscent of constraint satisfaction problems, where local consistency does not imply global consistency.

### 7.2 The Bottleneck as Musical Meaning

The 12:1 self-loop asymmetry (Theorems 3.5–3.6) provides a precise measure of why perfect consonances feel "exposed" in musical texture. With only the identity self-loop available, a voice at a perfect fifth can sustain itself only through complete stillness — any motion must change the interval. This creates a natural musical tension: perfect consonances demand resolution or stasis, while imperfect consonances allow fluid internal motion.

### 7.3 Voice-Swap and the Bass

The voice-swap asymmetry (Theorem 3.8) formalizes one of the oldest principles in music theory: the bass voice is special. The perfect fourth (5 semitones) is consonant between upper voices but dissonant against the bass — a rule that appears arbitrary until one sees it as a consequence of the consonant set's failure to be closed under negation mod 12. The asymmetry $\{7 \leftrightarrow 5\}$ is irreducible: it cannot be removed by any relabeling of pitch classes.

## 8. Future Work

1. **Higher species**: Extend to second, third, fourth, and fifth species counterpoint, where rhythm, suspensions, and passing tones introduce additional constraints.
2. **Multi-voice counterpoint**: Generalize from two voices to $n$ voices, where the consonance constraint applies to all $\binom{n}{2}$ pairs simultaneously.
3. **Microtonal exploration**: Systematically study counterpoint systems for $n = 19, 24, 31, 53$ and classify their quiver properties.
4. **Categorical repair**: Investigate whether a suitable quotient or completion of the counterpoint quiver yields a category — e.g., by identifying voice leadings that produce the same musical effect.
5. **Homological invariants**: Compute the homology of the counterpoint quiver (viewed as a simplicial complex via its cliques) and interpret the Betti numbers musically.
6. **Algorithmic composition**: Use the quiver structure to constrain random walks, generating counterpoint that is provably correct by construction.

---

## 9. Conclusion

We have demonstrated that the venerable rules of first-species counterpoint, when formalized as a directed multigraph over modular arithmetic, reveal a precise and beautiful combinatorial structure. The counterpoint quiver is strongly connected but not composable, asymmetric between perfect and imperfect consonances, and broken under voice exchange. These properties — proved with full formal rigor — bridge music theory, combinatorics, algebra, and category theory, suggesting that the mathematical structure of musical rules is deeper and more subtle than previously recognized.

The original conjecture that counterpoint forms a category equivalent to a poset is definitively refuted. In its place, we find something more interesting: a quiver whose local constraints generate global structure that resists algebraic closure. This tension between local rules and global behavior is, perhaps, the mathematical essence of what makes counterpoint an art.
