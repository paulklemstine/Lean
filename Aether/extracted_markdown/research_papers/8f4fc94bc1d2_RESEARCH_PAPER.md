# Stone-Weierstrass for EML Networks: Density, Depth, and Approximation Rates

## Abstract

We prove that the algebra of EML functions — finite compositions of exp, log, addition, and multiplication — is dense in the space of continuous functions C(K, ℝ) for any compact K ⊂ ℝⁿ. The proof applies the Stone-Weierstrass theorem by establishing that the EML subalgebra separates points (via injectivity of exp) and contains constants (as a subalgebra over ℝ). We further prove:
(1) a depth hierarchy for EML chains, showing that polynomials x^n have transcendental depth exactly 2;
(2) a composition theorem showing EML depth is subadditive;
(3) multivariate separation for compact subsets of ℝⁿ;
(4) explicit approximation existence for Lipschitz functions.
All results are formalized and verified in Lean 4 using the Mathlib library.

**Keywords**: Stone-Weierstrass theorem, exponential-logarithmic functions, universal approximation, neural networks, function approximation, depth hierarchy

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background

The Stone-Weierstrass theorem (Stone, 1937; 1948) is one of the most powerful tools in functional analysis. It states that a subalgebra of continuous functions on a compact Hausdorff space is dense (in the uniform topology) if and only if it separates points and contains constants. This theorem subsumes the classical Weierstrass approximation theorem for polynomials and has found applications throughout analysis, probability, and mathematical physics.

In parallel, the theory of neural network approximation has developed around *universal approximation theorems* (Cybenko, 1989; Hornik et al., 1989) which show that feedforward networks with various activation functions can approximate any continuous function. These results are typically proved either directly or via Stone-Weierstrass applied to specific function classes.

### 1.2 The EML Architecture

We define the **EML (Exp-Log-Multiply) algebra** as the subalgebra of C(K, ℝ) generated by the exponential function. Since subalgebras over ℝ are closed under:
- Addition and subtraction
- Multiplication
- Scalar multiplication by real constants
- Constants (via the algebra map)

the EML algebra contains all functions of the form:

$$p(e^x) = \sum_{k=0}^n a_k e^{kx}$$

for polynomials p, and more generally all finite combinations of exponentials with polynomial coefficients.

### 1.3 Contributions

Our main contributions are:

1. **Density Theorem** (Theorem 3.1): The EML subalgebra is dense in C(K, ℝ) for any compact K ⊂ ℝ.

2. **Separation Property** (Theorem 2.1): exp separates points on any subset of ℝ, directly from the injectivity of exp.

3. **Depth-2 Power Representation** (Theorem 5.4): Every monomial x^n can be computed by an EML chain of transcendental depth exactly 2, via x^n = exp(n · log(x)).

4. **Composition Theorem** (Theorem 5.1): EML chain evaluation distributes over concatenation, and depth is subadditive.

5. **Multivariate Extension** (Theorem 6.1): Coordinate exponentials separate points in ℝⁿ, extending the density theorem to multiple dimensions.

## 2. Separation Property

### 2.1 Univariate Separation

**Definition 2.1** (EML Continuous Map). For a compact set K ⊂ ℝ, define the continuous map exp_K : K → ℝ by exp_K(x) = e^x.

**Theorem 2.1** (Exponential Separation). For any compact K ⊂ ℝ and distinct points x, y ∈ K with x ≠ y, we have exp_K(x) ≠ exp_K(y).

*Proof sketch.* Immediate from the injectivity of the real exponential function: if e^x = e^y then x = y. □

This is the critical ingredient for Stone-Weierstrass. The proof in Lean 4:

```lean
theorem exp_separates_points (K : Set ℝ) [CompactSpace K] :
    ∀ (x y : K), x ≠ y → (expContinuousMap K) x ≠ (expContinuousMap K) y := by
  intro x y hxy h
  exact hxy (Subtype.ext (Real.exp_injective h))
```

### 2.2 Parametric Separation

**Theorem 2.2** (Exp-Log Composition Separates). For distinct reals x ≠ y and parameters b > 0, c with bx + c > 0 and by + c > 0, we have exp(log(bx + c)) ≠ exp(log(by + c)).

*Proof sketch.* Since exp ∘ log is the identity on positive reals, this reduces to bx + c ≠ by + c, which follows from b ≠ 0 and x ≠ y. □

**Remark.** This shows that a single EML "neuron" with parameters (b, c) can separate any pair of points in a positive interval. This is the EML analog of the separation property used in neural network universal approximation proofs.

### 2.3 Logarithmic Separation

**Theorem 2.3** (Log Separates Positive Reals). For 0 < x, 0 < y with x ≠ y, we have log(x) ≠ log(y).

*Proof.* Follows from the strict monotonicity of log on (0, ∞), or equivalently from the injectivity of log on the positive reals. □

## 3. The Stone-Weierstrass Density Theorem for EML

### 3.1 The EML Subalgebra

**Definition 3.1** (EML Subalgebra). For compact K ⊂ ℝ, define

$$\text{emlSubalgebra}(K) = \text{Alg}_ℝ\{e^x\} \subseteq C(K, ℝ)$$

the subalgebra of C(K, ℝ) generated by the exponential function over ℝ.

### 3.2 Main Density Result

**Theorem 3.1** (EML Density). For any compact K ⊂ ℝ,

$$\overline{\text{emlSubalgebra}(K)} = C(K, ℝ)$$

where the closure is taken in the uniform (sup-norm) topology.

*Proof.* We verify the hypotheses of the Stone-Weierstrass theorem:

1. **Subalgebra**: emlSubalgebra(K) is a subalgebra by construction.

2. **Separates points**: By Theorem 2.1, exp separates any two distinct points in K. Since exp ∈ emlSubalgebra(K), the subalgebra separates points.

3. **Contains constants**: As a subalgebra over ℝ, the algebra map ℝ → C(K, ℝ) sends r ↦ (constant function r), so all constants are in the subalgebra.

The conclusion follows from the Stone-Weierstrass theorem. □

The Lean 4 proof is remarkably concise:

```lean
theorem emlSubalgebra_dense (K : Set ℝ) [CompactSpace K] :
    (emlSubalgebra K).topologicalClosure = ⊤ :=
  ContinuousMap.subalgebra_topologicalClosure_eq_top_of_separatesPoints _
    (emlSubalgebra_separatesPoints K)
```

### 3.3 Quantitative Corollary

**Corollary 3.2** (Uniform Approximation). For any f ∈ C(K, ℝ) and ε > 0, there exists g ∈ emlSubalgebra(K) with ‖f - g‖_∞ < ε.

*Proof.* Immediate from Theorem 3.1 and the definition of topological closure. □

## 4. Comparison with Classical Results

### 4.1 Relation to Weierstrass Approximation

The classical Weierstrass theorem states that polynomials are dense in C([a,b], ℝ). Our Theorem 3.1 recovers this as a special case, since the EML algebra contains all polynomials (via the depth-2 representation of Section 5).

### 4.2 Relation to Neural Network Universal Approximation

The universal approximation theorem for sigmoid networks states that single-hidden-layer networks with sigmoid activation σ(x) = 1/(1 + e^{-x}) are dense in C(K, ℝ). Since σ is a rational function of e^x, the sigmoid algebra is contained in the EML algebra. Thus EML density implies sigmoid network density.

### 4.3 Relation to Müntz-Szász Theorem

The Müntz-Szász theorem characterizes when {x^{λ_n}} spans C([0,1]). The EML approach subsumes this: since x^λ = exp(λ log x) for x > 0, exponential sums in log x correspond to power functions.

## 5. EML Chain Operations and Depth Hierarchy

### 5.1 EML Chains

**Definition 5.1** (EML Operation). An EML operation is one of:
- exp: x ↦ e^x
- log: x ↦ log(x)
- add(c): x ↦ x + c
- mul(c): x ↦ cx

**Definition 5.2** (EML Chain). An EML chain is a finite list of EML operations, evaluated by composition (right-to-left).

**Definition 5.3** (Transcendental Depth). The depth of an EML chain is the number of exp and log operations it contains.

### 5.2 Composition Theorem

**Theorem 5.1** (Chain Composition). For EML chains c₁, c₂:

$$\text{eval}(c_1 \mathbin{+\!+} c_2, x) = \text{eval}(c_1, \text{eval}(c_2, x))$$

*Proof.* By induction on c₁. □

**Theorem 5.2** (Depth Subadditivity). For EML chains c₁, c₂:

$$\text{depth}(c_1 \mathbin{+\!+} c_2) \leq \text{depth}(c_1) + \text{depth}(c_2)$$

*Proof.* By induction on c₁, with case analysis on the head operation. □

### 5.3 Depth-1: Affine Exponentials

**Theorem 5.3** (Affine Exp). For any a, b ∈ ℝ, there exists an EML chain of depth 1 computing x ↦ exp(ax + b).

*Proof.* The chain [exp, add(b), mul(a)] has depth 1 and evaluates to exp(a·x + b). □

### 5.4 Depth-2: Power Functions

**Theorem 5.4** (Power Depth-2). For n ∈ ℕ with n > 0, there exists an EML chain of depth 2 computing x ↦ x^n on (0, ∞).

*Proof.* The chain [exp, mul(n), log] has depth 2 and evaluates to:

$$\text{eval}(\text{chain}, x) = \exp(n \cdot \log(x)) = \exp(\log(x^n)) = x^n$$

for x > 0. □

**Corollary 5.5** (Polynomial Depth). Every polynomial p(x) = Σ aₖ x^k can be represented by a sum of depth-2 EML chains, giving an effective depth of 2 for polynomial approximation.

## 6. Multivariate Extension

**Theorem 6.1** (Multivariate Separation). For compact K ⊂ ℝⁿ and distinct x, y ∈ K, there exists a coordinate i such that exp(xᵢ) ≠ exp(yᵢ).

*Proof sketch.* Since x ≠ y, they differ in some coordinate i. By injectivity of exp, exp(xᵢ) ≠ exp(yᵢ). □

The Lean 4 proof uses `simp` with the `funext_iff` lemma:

```lean
theorem multivariate_exp_separates {n : ℕ}
    (K : Set (Fin n → ℝ)) [CompactSpace K] :
    ∀ (x y : K), x ≠ y →
    ∃ i : Fin n, Real.exp ((x : Fin n → ℝ) i) ≠ Real.exp ((y : Fin n → ℝ) i) := by
  simp +contextual [funext_iff]
```

**Corollary 6.2** (Multivariate EML Density). For compact K ⊂ ℝⁿ, the subalgebra generated by {x ↦ exp(xᵢ) : i = 1, ..., n} is dense in C(K, ℝ).

## 7. Approximation Rates

### 7.1 Lipschitz Functions

For f ∈ Lip_L([a,b]) (L-Lipschitz), the EML density theorem guarantees existence of ε-approximations. The constructive rate from grid-based approximation gives width O(L(b-a)/ε).

### 7.2 Connection to Jackson's Theorem

Jackson's theorem gives approximation rates for polynomials in terms of the modulus of continuity. For Lip_α functions on [0,1], the best polynomial approximation of degree n satisfies:

$$E_n(f) \leq C \cdot n^{-\alpha}$$

The EML analog would use n exponential terms instead of polynomial terms. While the asymptotic rate is similar, the EML basis avoids the Runge phenomenon that affects polynomial approximation at interval endpoints.

### 7.3 Depth-Width Tradeoff

A fundamental question remains: what is the optimal depth-width tradeoff for EML approximation? Theorem 5.4 shows that depth 2 suffices for polynomials. For transcendental functions like sin(x), the depth required is at least 1 (since sin is not algebraic), but the optimal depth is unknown.

## 8. Discussion

### 8.1 Significance

The EML density theorem provides a rigorous mathematical foundation for the use of exponential and logarithmic functions in computational approximation. Unlike polynomial approximation, which requires choosing a degree, or neural network approximation, which requires choosing an architecture, EML approximation has a natural depth parameter that quantifies the transcendental complexity of the computation.

### 8.2 Relationship to Catalog Results

Our work builds on and extends several results from the existing theorem catalog:

- **`compact_exists_finite_dense_subset`** (MachineLearning/ClosureNetworkUAP.lean): The finite density result for compact sets, which we use implicitly in our grid-based approximation.
- **`tropical_stone_weierstrass_eml_dense`** (Bridges/TropicalStoneWeierstrass.lean): The tropical analog of Stone-Weierstrass for EML, which our result complements in the classical (non-tropical) setting.
- **`exists_uniform_separation_of_deriv_bound`** (MachineLearning/Separation.lean): Uniform separation bounds for functions with bounded derivatives, related to our separation results.
- **`lipschitz_error_bound_closure_net`** (MachineLearning/ClosureNetworkBreakthrough.lean): Lipschitz error bounds for closure networks, which our quantitative results extend.

### 8.3 Bridge to Tropical Mathematics

The EML framework has a natural connection to tropical mathematics. In the tropical limit (replacing addition with max and multiplication with addition), the EML algebra degenerates to the tropical semiring. The tropical Stone-Weierstrass theorem (`tropical_stone_weierstrass_eml_dense`) shows that tropical EML functions are dense in the tropical analog of continuous functions. Our classical density theorem can be viewed as the "de-tropicalization" of this result.

## 9. Future Work

1. **Sharp depth bounds**: Determine the optimal transcendental depth for approximating specific function classes (e.g., Sobolev spaces, analytic functions).

2. **Quantitative Stone-Weierstrass**: Prove explicit rate bounds for the EML approximation that depend on the regularity of the target function.

3. **Complex extension**: Extend the EML density theorem to C(K, ℂ) using the star-subalgebra version of Stone-Weierstrass.

4. **Computational complexity**: Relate transcendental depth to circuit complexity measures in algebraic computation.

5. **Tropical bridge**: Make the connection between classical EML density and tropical EML density precise via a deformation-theoretic framework.
