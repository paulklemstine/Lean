## Preface

What does it mean to compute? This question, deceptively simple, lies at the heart of
mathematics, philosophy, and engineering. From the ancient algorithms of Euclid to the
quantum circuits of tomorrow, computation is the thread that connects logic to action,
abstraction to mechanism, and thought to reality.

This book is an exploration of computation in its many forms — mathematical, mechanical,
and metaphysical. It is written in the language of Lean 4, a modern proof assistant and
programming language that embodies one of computation's deepest truths: that proofs *are*
programs, and propositions *are* types.

Each chapter pairs expository prose with formal Lean 4 code. The formalizations are not
mere illustrations; they are the substance. When we define a Turing machine, we *really*
define it — in a language where the definition must be precise enough for a computer to
check. When we prove that the halting problem is undecidable, we *really* prove it — with
every logical step verified by Lean's kernel.

The book is organized into three parts:

**Part I: Foundations** introduces the primitive notions of computation — finite automata,
formal languages, and the lambda calculus — building up to the Church–Turing thesis.

**Part II: The Landscape of Decidability** explores what can and cannot be computed,
including the halting problem, Rice's theorem, and the arithmetic hierarchy.

**Part III: Complexity and Beyond** examines the cost of computation, from P vs NP to
interactive proofs, and looks toward the horizons of quantum and hypercomputation.
