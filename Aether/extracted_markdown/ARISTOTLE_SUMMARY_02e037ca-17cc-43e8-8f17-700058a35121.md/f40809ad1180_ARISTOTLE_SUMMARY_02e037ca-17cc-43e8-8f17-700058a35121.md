# Summary of changes
## Assessment

The submitted article — *"The Photon as Epistemic Bridge: How Light Mediates the Relationship Between Observer and Observed"* — is a well-crafted conceptual physics essay proposing the "Local Knowledge Table" (LKT) framework. It reinterprets standard quantum electrodynamics and quantum information theory through an epistemic lens, treating photons as fundamental carriers of relational information between observer and observed.

**However, no formal mathematical proof is possible here**, for the following reasons:

1. **No formal theorems are stated.** The article contains qualitative hypotheses, interpretive reframings of existing physics, and proposed experiments — none of which constitute precise mathematical claims amenable to machine-verified proof.

2. **The framework is explicitly interpretation, not new formalism.** The authors themselves state: *"It does not propose modifications to established physical law, but rather a new way of understanding the epistemic significance of existing physics."* Since no new mathematical structure is introduced, there is nothing new to prove.

3. **The one quasi-formal equation** (Hypothesis 1: I(O:S) = Σᵢ I(γᵢ) − D) is schematic — the terms are not rigorously defined in a way that permits formal proof without first building an entire mathematical model specifying the Hilbert spaces, interaction Hamiltonians, and information measures involved.

4. **Consistency claims** (with QED, special relativity, thermodynamics) are true by construction, since the framework adopts the standard mathematical formalism unchanged.

**In summary:** This is a thought-provoking scientific essay, but it does not contain formalizable mathematical content. A Lean proof requires a precise mathematical statement — a proposition with well-defined terms in a formal logical system. Interpretive physics frameworks, however insightful, fall outside this scope. If specific mathematical lemmas were extracted and precisely stated (e.g., deriving the Heisenberg uncertainty relation from information-theoretic bounds on single-photon encoding capacity), those could potentially be formalized and proven.
