### Theorem A: finite generation / Cayley–Hamilton-type transfer theorem
For every finitely supported initial state `ψ₀`, there exists a finite-dimensional transfer submodule `M(ψ₀)` containing all reachable amplitude states, invariant under each Berggren step operator, such that every sufficiently long word action is a linear combination of actions of bounded length. Equivalently, the family of operators induced by Berggren words satisfies a finite dependence relation on the cyclic module generated by `ψ₀`.

A precise mathematical statement to formalize:

> For any finite-support initial state `ψ₀`, there exists `N : ℕ` and a finite set of reachable states from words of length at most `N` that spans every reachable state from arbitrary words. Hence the amplitude map `w ↦ evalWord ψ₀ w` factors through a finitely generated transfer submodule.

### Theorem B: Hankel-rank / observational equivalence theorem
Define the word amplitude functional
\[
H_{\psi}(u,v) := \mathrm{amplitude}(u ++ v,\psi)
\]
or the one-sided version
\[
A_\psi(w) := \mathrm{amplitude}(w,\psi).
\]
Then prove that two states are observationally equivalent if and only if they induce the same linear functional on the finite transfer semimodule/submodule. This is the arithmetic analogue of syntactic minimality for weighted automata.

Precise statement:

> For states `ψ, φ` in the reachable transfer submodule, the following are equivalent:
> 1. For every word `w`, `amplitude ψ w = amplitude φ w`.
> 2. For every element `m` of the finite transfer submodule, the induced observation functional agrees on `m`.
> 3. Their images in the quotient by the kernel of the observation map coincide.

### Theorem C: certified minimal realization theorem
This is the centerpiece. Show that the Berggren amplitude process has a **canonical smallest finite spectral model** obtained from the reachable submodule modulo observational equivalence, and that this model reconstructs all amplitudes. In finite truncation, it should reconstruct all amplitudes up to length `n`; under rational/unitary parameters and a boundedness/stability hypothesis, it should reconstruct all amplitudes in the limit.

Precise statement:

> There exists a finite-dimensional realization `(Vmin, α, {T_g}, ω)` such that  
> \[
> \mathrm{amplitude}(w) = \omega(T_{w_n}\cdots T_{w_1}\alpha)
> \]
> for all words `w`, and for any other realization of the same amplitude process there exists a unique surjective intertwiner onto `Vmin`. Hence `dim Vmin` equals the rank of the Berggren Hankel matrix.

This is not just “there exists a representation.” It is a **minimality theorem** identifying the exact compression dimension of arithmetic quantum walk data.

## Proof strategy options

### Strategy A: truncated Hankel matrix → rank stabilization → minimal realization
1. For each depth `n`, define a finite Hankel matrix indexed by words of bounded length, with entries `H(u,v) = amplitude (u ++ v)`.
2. Show that its row space equals the span of reachable observations, and its column space equals the co-reachable span.
3. When rank stabilizes, extract a basis and construct the minimal realization from shift operators induced by generator concatenation.

**Why promising:** this is the closest analogue to weighted automata minimization and gives the cleanest route to a certified algorithm. It also naturally produces finite data structures suitable for Lean.

### Strategy B: cyclic module + invariant submodule + quotient kernel
1. View the walk as the action of the free monoid on a cyclic module generated by `ψ₀`.
2. Define the reachable submodule as the cyclic module of `ψ₀`.
3. Quotient by the observational kernel and prove this quotient is finite-dimensional/minimal under boundedness or truncation hypotheses.

**Why promising:** conceptually elegant and probably the best route to the “canonical smallest model” statement. This is the right strategy for the final theorem, though it may depend on truncated finite-dimensional lemmas from Strategy A.

### Strategy C: matrix realization on finite frontier + recurrence extraction
1. Restrict to a finite frontier or depth-bounded tree and realize each generator as a finite matrix.
2. Use finite-dimensional linear algebra to derive a characteristic-polynomial recurrence (Cayley–Hamilton).
3. Lift this recurrence to amplitudes/hitting amplitudes and prove reconstruction from boundary values.

**Why promising:** easiest route to the hitting-amplitude theorem and likely the quickest way to produce concrete, executable Lean artifacts. Best for early wins and for filling sorries.

**Recommended overall plan:** use **Strategy C** to get the first nontrivial recurrence/reconstruction theorem, **Strategy A** to prove minimal realization on truncations, then **Strategy B** to package the canonical quotient theorem cleanly.

/-! ## Section 2: Berggren Quantum Walk -/

/-- A Berggren quantum walk of dimension `n` over the complex Hilbert space ℂⁿ.
    Consists of three unitary operators (one per Berggren generator),
    an initial state vector ψ₀, and an observation vector. -/
structure BerggrenQuantumWalk (n : ℕ) where
  /-- Unitary operator assigned to each Berggren generator -/
  U : BerggrenGen → Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℂ
  /-- Left unitarity: U†U = I -/
  hU_star_mul : ∀ g, (U g)ᴴ * (U g) = 1
  /-- Right unitarity: UU† = I -/
  hU_mul_star : ∀ g, (U g) * (U g)ᴴ = 1
  /-- Initial state vector -/
  psi0 : Fin n → ℂ
  /-- Observation vector -/
  obs : Fin n → ℂ

variable {n : ℕ}

/-- Extend the generator action to words via the free monoid universal property.
    `evalWord w` is the product of unitary matrices along the word `w`. -/
def BerggrenQuantumWalk.evalWord (Q : BerggrenQuantumWalk n) :
    BerggrenWord →* Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℂ :=
  FreeMonoid.lift Q.U

/-- Evaluate a word on the initial state vector: U(w) · ψ₀ -/
def BerggrenQuantumWalk.evalState (Q : BerggrenQuantumWalk n) (w : BerggrenWord) :
    Fin n → ℂ :=
  (Q.evalWord w).mulVec Q.psi0

/-- The amplitude kernel: K(u,v) = ⟨U(u)ψ₀, U(v)ψ₀⟩ where the inner product
    is the standard Hermitian inner product on ℂⁿ. -/
def BerggrenQuantumWalk.kernel (Q : BerggrenQuantumWalk n) (u v : BerggrenWord) : ℂ :=
  dotProduct (star (Q.evalState u)) (Q.evalState v)

/-- The amplitude function: amp(w) = ⟨obs, U(w)ψ₀⟩ -/
def BerggrenQuantumWalk.amplitude (Q : BerggrenQuantumWalk n) (w : BerggrenWord) : ℂ :=
  dotProduct (star Q.obs) (Q.evalState w)

/-- evalWord is multiplicative: U(w₁ · w₂) = U(w₁) · U(w₂) -/
theorem BerggrenQuantumWalk.evalWord_mul (Q : BerggrenQuantumWalk n)
    (w₁ w₂ : BerggrenWord) :
    Q.evalWord (w₁ * w₂) = Q.evalWord w₁ * Q.evalWord w₂ :=
  map_mul Q.evalWord w₁ w₂
