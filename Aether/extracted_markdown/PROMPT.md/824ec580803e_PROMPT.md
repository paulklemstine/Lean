## Core Vision

Compression complexity should behave functorially under equivalence and additively under independent composition. If Morita invariance says `κ` depends only on observational content, then product additivity would say independent observational systems contribute linearly to total complexity. That is the hallmark of a dimension-like invariant.

A proof of sub-additivity alone is already a theorem of substance:
\[
\kappa(F_1 \times F_2)\le \kappa(F_1)+\kappa(F_2).
\]
A proof of full additivity would be a breakthrough: it would make `κ` a valuation on the symmetric monoidal category of finite presheaf models, opening a new “compression geometry.”

## Exact Theorem Targets

You must prove at least 3 substantial theorems. The following is the minimum acceptable theorem suite.

### Theorem 1: Product sub-additivity

This is the foundational unconditional theorem.

```lean
theorem compression_prod_le
    (M₁ M₂ : FinitePresheafModel) :
    κ (FinitePresheafModel.prod M₁ M₂) ≤ κ M₁ + κ M₂
```

Mathematical statement:
\[
\forall M_1\,M_2,\quad \kappa(M_1\times M_2)\le \kappa(M_1)+\kappa(M_2).
\]

Why this matters:
This is the analogue of sub-additivity of entropy and capacity bounds for product systems. It says independent systems never require more than the sum of their optimal probe complexities.

### Theorem 2: Additivity under a structural separability hypothesis

You likely need a nontrivial hypothesis stronger than bare finiteness. Introduce a new concept that captures when each factor contributes irreducible probe information independently.

For example, define a predicate such as:

```lean
def ProbeIndependent (M₁ M₂ : FinitePresheafModel) : Prop := ...
```

or

```lean
def CompressionSeparated (M : FinitePresheafModel) : Prop := ...
```

Then prove:

```lean
theorem compression_prod_eq_of_independent
    (M₁ M₂ : FinitePresheafModel)
    (h : ProbeIndependent M₁ M₂) :
    κ (FinitePresheafModel.prod M₁ M₂) = κ M₁ + κ M₂
```

Mathematical statement:
\[
\forall M_1 M_2,\ \mathrm{ProbeIndependent}(M_1,M_2)\to
\kappa(M_1\times M_2)=\kappa(M_1)+\kappa(M_2).
\]

Why this matters:
Even if unconditional additivity is false, identifying the exact structural mechanism that forces additivity is field-opening. It turns a conjecture into a classification program.

### Theorem 3: Lower bound via factor embeddings or information projection

Prove a converse inequality in a form that is actually attainable:

```lean
theorem max_le_compression_prod
    (M₁ M₂ : FinitePresheafModel) :
    max (κ M₁) (κ M₂) ≤ κ (FinitePresheafModel.prod M₁ M₂)
```

or, if the catalog supports transport of probe families more directly:

```lean
theorem compression_le_prod_left
    (M₁ M₂ : FinitePresheafModel) :
    κ M₁ ≤ κ (FinitePresheafModel.prod M₁ M₂)

theorem compression_le_prod_right
    (M₁ M₂ : FinitePresheafModel) :
    κ M₂ ≤ κ (FinitePresheafModel.prod M₁ M₂)
```

These are important because they show product cannot erase complexity. Combined with sub-additivity they trap the invariant in a meaningful interval.

### Theorem 4: Cross-domain theorem — channel/information analogy

You must include at least one theorem connecting this compression theory to another domain. The best bridge here is information theory.

Define a finite observational channel extracted from a probe family, or a simpler combinatorial surrogate such as distinguishability number / separation profile. Then prove that product models induce multiplicative distinguishability and hence additive logarithmic complexity.

A concrete target:

```lean
def distinguishabilityCard (M : FinitePresheafModel) : Nat := ...

theorem distinguishabilityCard_prod
    (M₁ M₂ : FinitePresheafModel) :
    distinguishabilityCard (FinitePresheafModel.prod M₁ M₂) =
      distinguishabilityCard M₁ * distinguishabilityCard M₂
```

and derive, if your `κ` is bounded below by a logarithmic separation quantity:

```lean
theorem logDistinguishability_le_compression
    (M : FinitePresheafModel) :
    Nat.log2 (distinguishabilityCard M) ≤ κ M
```

This is a true cross-domain bridge:
- presheaf compression ↔ zero-error information theory,
- product of models ↔ tensor product / independent channel use,
- additive complexity ↔ logarithm of multiplicative capacity.

If exact `Nat.log2` machinery is awkward, use powers of two and inequalities instead.

## Proof Strategy Architecture

You must not rely on trivial automation. Use multi-step mathematical arguments with explicit witness construction, contradiction, and transport.

### Strategy A: Constructive upper bound via product probe families
Most promising for `compression_prod_le`.

1. Start from optimal or near-optimal probe families `P₁` for `M₁` and `P₂` for `M₂`.
2. Build a probe family on `M₁ × M₂` by probing the left coordinate with `P₁` and the right coordinate with `P₂`.
3. Prove separation in the product by case analysis on whether two product states differ in the left or right coordinate.
4. Count the resulting family size and derive the inequality.

Why this is promising:
It directly mirrors product-code constructions in information theory and should align well with the catalog’s existing definitions of probe family and transport. This is the theorem most likely to formalize cleanly.

Lean tactics likely needed:
- `rcases` to unpack product witnesses,
- induction over finite families / lists / finsets of probes if the complexity is defined combinatorially,
- `calc` chains for cardinality bounds,
- careful rewriting through product restriction maps.

### Strategy B: Lower bounds by projection/embedding and contradiction
Best for `compression_le_prod_left/right` and `max_le_compression_prod`.

1. Assume a too-small separating family on the product.
2. Fix a basepoint in the other factor and restrict to the slice `x ↦ (x, y₀)`.
3. Show this induces a separating family on the original factor.
4. Contradict minimality of `κ M₁` or `κ M₂`.

Why this matters:
This is the exact mechanism by which complexity of factors survives into the product. It is conceptually the “data processing monotonicity” side of the theory.

Lean tactics likely needed:
- `by_contra`,
- witness extraction via `classical`,
- slicing maps on product fibers,
- transport theorem from `ToposCompressionInvariant.lean` if factor inclusion can be packaged as a model morphism/equivalence/embedding.

### Strategy C: Characterize when full additivity holds
Most ambitious and potentially revolutionary.

1. Define a structural independence predicate saying no probe in one factor can simulate information from the other, or that optimal separating families decompose uniquely.
2. Prove under this hypothesis that any separating family on the product induces enough independent information to recover separating families on both factors.
3. Combine with sub-additivity to get equality.

Alternative route:
Define a combinatorial invariant like “separation rank” or “tensor irreducibility defect” and prove:
\[
\kappa(M_1\times M_2)=\kappa(M_1)+\kappa(M_2)-\delta(M_1,M_2),
\]
with additivity exactly when `δ = 0`.

Why this is the breakthrough path:
Even if the raw conjecture fails, a defect formula would be better than a yes/no answer. It would create a new research program around interaction defects, analogous to mutual information correcting entropy additivity.
