## Direction 1: Tropical cycle-gap lower bounds via max-plus eigenvalues

### Flagship theorem
Let `W : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℝ` be a weighted adjacency matrix over the tropical semiring interpretation, and let `λ(W)` denote the maximum cycle mean of the directed weighted graph encoded by `W`. Let `g(k)` denote the maximal weight of a length-`k` walk, i.e. the relevant tropical matrix power growth statistic extracted from `tropPow W k`. Prove that beyond the transient regime, walk growth is bounded below by linear drift with slope `λ(W)`; in its cleanest form:

\[
\forall k \ge 1,\quad g(k) \ge k \,\lambda(W),
\]
or, if the currently available formal definitions force a weaker statement,
\[
\forall k \in \mathbb N,\quad g(k + \tau) \ge g(\tau) + k\,\lambda(W)
\]
for an explicitly defined transient parameter `τ`, with equality on critical components in the irreducible case.

This is the theorem that turns cycle decomposition into spectral asymptotics.

### Lean 4 formalization target
You may need to introduce auxiliary definitions if they do not yet exist, but the target should look approximately like:

```lean

    {n : ℕ} (W : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℝ) :
    ∀ k : ℕ, 1 ≤ k → maxCycleMean W * k ≤ walkWeightGrowth W k := by
  ...

    {n : ℕ} (W : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℝ) :
    ∃ τ : ℕ, ∀ k : ℕ, walkWeightGrowth W (τ + k) ≥ walkWeightGrowth W τ + k * maxCycleMean W := by
  ...
```

If multiplication by naturals is awkward over `ℝ`, use `((k : ℝ) * maxCycleMean W)` or the additive `nsmul` form consistently.

### Stronger breakthrough variant
If you can formalize strongly connected components and critical SCCs, prove a componentwise sharpening:

\[
g(k) \ge \max_{C \in \mathrm{SCC}(W)} \bigl(a_C + k\,\lambda_C\bigr),
\]
where `λ_C` is the max cycle mean inside component `C`. This would be much more than a lower bound: it would be a tropical analogue of Perron–Frobenius growth stratified by SCC decomposition.

### Why this is a breakthrough
This would reframe tropical depth lower bounds as a spectral phenomenon. Instead of saying “long paths force large weight by pigeonhole repetition,” you would show that the asymptotic obstruction is exactly the tropical eigenvalue. That opens a route from circuit lower bounds to weighted automata, mean-payoff games, and tropical representation theory.

This is not just a graph lemma. It is the missing conceptual upgrade from combinatorial recurrence to tropical dynamics.

### Proof strategies

#### Strategy A: Direct cycle extraction from long walks
Most promising if the current cycle-gap theorem already gives repeated-vertex decompositions.

1. Define `walkWeightGrowth W k` using `tropPow` and extract a maximizing length-`k` walk.
2. Use pigeonhole/cycle decomposition to split any sufficiently long walk into a simple prefix plus cycles.
3. Bound the average contribution of each extracted cycle by `maxCycleMean W`, then show a critical cycle can be repeated to obtain the lower bound \(k\lambda(W)\) or eventual affine growth.

Why promising: this uses exactly the machinery already hinted in the catalog—`tropPow`, `tropMul`, and pigeonhole decomposition. It is the shortest path to a certified theorem.

#### Strategy B: Tropical Collatz–Wielandt style argument
More visionary, but may require more infrastructure.

1. Define a tropical subeigenvector inequality of the form
   \[
   W \otimes x \le \lambda \otimes x
   \]
   and characterize `maxCycleMean W` as the least such `λ`.
2. Show that iterating `tropPow` on a basis vector yields growth controlled by `λ`, while critical cycles force matching lower growth.
3. Derive eventual linear growth from tropical spectral extremality.

Why important: this would connect your theorem directly to tropical Perron–Frobenius theory and make later generalizations vastly easier.

#### Strategy C: SCC condensation plus local spectral bounds
Best for the stronger componentwise theorem.

1. Build the DAG of SCCs.
2. Prove growth inside each SCC is eventually affine with slope its local cycle mean.
3. Show global walk growth is the maximum over SCC-local affine contributions plus bounded entry/exit corrections.

Why useful: this is the right architecture if you want a future theorem about non-irreducible systems, weighted automata, or branching programs.

Recommended order: **A first, then C, then B**. A gets a certifiable theorem quickly. C produces the structural upgrade. B is the conceptual crown.

## Direction 2: Width-depth tropical tradeoffs for branching programs

### Goal
Generalize the cycle-gap principle from linear path models to DAG- or layered-graph computation. The conceptual theorem should say: bounded width limits the number of distinct tropical states, forcing cycle reuse or state merging, which in turn constrains achievable growth/region complexity unless depth increases.

This is the right next leap: from graph asymptotics to lower bounds for nontrivial computational models.

### Candidate theorem statement
Suppose a tropical branching program of width `w` and depth `d` computes a function whose tropical complexity measure at depth `d` is at least `R(d)`—for example, number of distinct affine pieces, number of distinguishable value states, or a certified growth statistic induced by repeated tropical transitions. Then

\[
R(d) \le F(w,d)
\]

for an explicit upper bound `F`, and conversely any target with `R(d) > F(w,d)` requires either larger width or larger depth.

A more concrete route, if you encode a width-`w` layered branching program as a sequence of tropical matrices `W₁,\dots,W_d`, is to prove that the growth/region complexity of the composed map is bounded by a function polynomial or exponential in `w` but only linearly in the critical cycle slope, yielding a width-depth lower bound.

### Lean 4 formalization target
You will likely need a minimal abstraction for layered tropical branching programs:

```lean

  layer : Fin d → Matrix (Fin w) (Fin w) ℝ

def bpComplexity {w d : ℕ} (P : TropBP w d) : ℕ := ...

theorem bp_complexity_width_depth_bound
    {w d : ℕ} (P : TropBP w d) :
    bpComplexity P ≤ someBound w d := by
  ...
```

If `bpComplexity` is too ambitious, replace it with a growth statistic directly extracted from `bpEval P`.

This would be a field-opening bridge between tropical complexity and automata/game theory.

### Why this is a breakthrough
Branching programs sit between automata and circuits. A tropical width-depth theorem would create a new lower-bound technology: use spectral obstructions to prove that narrow systems cannot simulate deep tropical behavior without paying an explicit complexity cost. That is a genuinely new angle on tropical computation, not a cosmetic extension of path arguments.

### Proof strategies

#### Strategy A: State-collision / pigeonhole on width
Most promising for a first theorem.

1. Encode each layer as a tropical transition on a finite width-`w` state space.
2. Show that any depth much larger than `w` forces repeated state configurations or repeated SCC visitation.
3. Apply the cycle-gap/eigenvalue theorem from Direction 1 to the induced repeated dynamics.

Why promising: it directly reuses your cycle decomposition machinery and only needs finite-state bookkeeping.

#### Strategy B: Region-count transfer from neural tropical bounds
Exploit `linear_regions_width_bound`.

1. Interpret each branching-program layer as a tropical piecewise-linear operator.
2. Transfer region-count upper bounds from tropical neural networks to branching programs.
3. Deduce that width constraints cap representational complexity unless depth compensates.

Why important: this creates a bridge from complexity theory to tropical deep learning.

#### Strategy C: Automata-theoretic reduction
1. View a branching program as a weighted automaton over a layered alphabet.
2. Use weighted automata growth theorems to derive bounds on distinguishable outputs.
3. Pull the result back into a width-depth lower bound.

Why exciting: this would connect formal language theory, quantitative verification, and tropical complexity in one theorem.

Recommended order: **A first**, then try **B** if the complexity measure naturally matches existing neural-network definitions.

## Concrete execution plan

1. **Define the right growth statistic**  
   Decide whether `walkWeightGrowth W k` is:
   - the maximum entry of `tropPow W k`,
   - the maximum diagonal entry,
   - or the maximum weight of a length-`k` closed walk.  
   The closed-walk version aligns most cleanly with `maxCycleMean`; the all-walk version may be easier to connect to existing code.

2. **Define maximum cycle mean carefully**  
   Keep it finite and combinatorial:
   - cycles as nonempty closed walks,
   - mean = total weight / length,
   - max over finite lengths up to `n` if you can prove the standard reduction to simple cycles.  
   This finite reduction is likely crucial for Lean.

3. **Exploit finite graph structure aggressively**  
   Over `Fin n`, every repeated-vertex argument is tractable. If needed, prove:
   - every closed walk decomposes into simple cycles,
   - the mean weight of a closed walk is bounded by the maximum of the means of its constituent simple cycles,
   - a maximizing simple cycle exists.

4. **Package SCC lemmas cleanly**  
   Do not hardwire SCC facts into one proof. Create reusable lemmas about:
   - condensation DAG,
   - cycle containment in SCCs,
   - global max cycle mean as max over SCC cycle means.

5. **Only then lift to branching programs**  
   The spectral theorem is the engine. The complexity theorem is the vehicle.
