## Assignment: Direction 2 — Tensor-Sorted Extension for Scientific Computing

**Mode:** prove

Build a genuinely new many-sorted rewrite theory for scientific computing by extending the catalog’s module-level optimizer to a **three-sorted tensor calculus** with sorts
\[
\{\mathrm{Scal},\mathrm{Vec},\mathrm{Mat}\},
\]
and prove nontrivial soundness, normalization-by-measure, and energy-invariance theorems that turn symbolic tensor simplification into certified mathematics rather than ad hoc algebra.

This should not be a cosmetic extension of `ModuleSig`. The goal is to isolate a mathematically meaningful rewrite fragment for linear-algebraic physics expressions and show that it supports **provably semantics-preserving simplification of bilinear energies**
\[
E(v,A) = \langle v, A v\rangle,
\]
with enough structure to suggest a future bridge to computational mechanics, quadratic optimization, and finite-dimensional quantum theory.

## Why this would be a breakthrough

The catalog already has a many-sorted convergent rewrite optimizer for module-like expressions. The breakthrough here is to prove that the same philosophy scales to a **typed tensor language** where matrix-vector interaction, scalar transport, and bilinear observables coexist. That matters because most real scientific symbolic computation lives exactly at this interface: not in pure modules, but in expressions mixing linear maps, states, and observables.

A successful formal theory here would open a path toward:

- certified simplification of stiffness/energy functionals in mechanics,
- verified preprocessing for quadratic optimization and numerical linear algebra,
- typed symbolic reductions for finite-dimensional quantum expectation values,
- a blueprint for higher-order tensor calculi and Einstein-style indexed rewriting.

This is the smallest setting where “symbolic physics” becomes mathematically serious.

## Exact theorem statements to target

At minimum, prove **3 substantial theorems**, each requiring real proof structure. The following are the recommended flagship results.

### Theorem 1: Soundness of tensor rewrite rules

Prove that each rewrite step preserves denotation in every tensor model.

Suggested Lean shape:

```lean
theorem tensorRewrite_sound
    {R : Type _} {n : Type _}
    [Fintype n] [DecidableEq n] [CommRing R] :
    ∀ {s : TensorSort} {t u : TensorTerm s},
      TensorRewrite t u →
      evalTensor (R := R) (n := n) t = evalTensor (R := R) (n := n) u
```

This should cover rules such as:

- `A • (v + w) ↦ A • v + A • w` for matrix-vector action,
- `(A + B) • v ↦ A • v + B • v`,
- `a • (A • v) ↦ (a • A) • v`,
- `dot (v + w) u ↦ dot v u + dot w u`,
- `dot v (A • w)` stability under semantic transport where appropriate,
- scalar/vector/matrix associativity-normalization rules if needed.

Use Mathlib lemmas about `Matrix.mulVec`, finite sums, and scalar actions.

### Theorem 2: Multi-step semantic preservation / normal-form invariance

Define reflexive-transitive closure of rewriting and prove denotation is invariant across any reduction sequence.

Suggested Lean shape:

```lean
theorem tensorRewrites_sound
    {R : Type _} {n : Type _}
    [Fintype n] [DecidableEq n] [CommRing R]
    {s : TensorSort} {t u : TensorTerm s} :
    ReflTransGen TensorRewrite t u →
    evalTensor (R := R) (n := n) t = evalTensor (R := R) (n := n) u
```

This theorem is scientifically important: it says optimization passes, not just individual algebraic rewrites, are semantics-preserving.

### Theorem 3: Energy invariance under vector/matrix normalization rewrites

This is the real conceptual payoff. Prove that if a vector expression `v` and matrix expression `A` are rewritten independently, then the induced quadratic energy is unchanged.

Suggested Lean shape:

```lean
theorem energy_invariant_of_rewrites
    {R : Type _} {n : Type _}
    [Fintype n] [DecidableEq n] [CommRing R]
    {v v' : TensorTerm .vec} {A A' : TensorTerm .mat} :
    ReflTransGen TensorRewrite v v' →
    ReflTransGen TensorRewrite A A' →
    energy (evalTensor (R := R) (n := n) A) (evalTensor (R := R) (n := n) v) =
    energy (evalTensor (R := R) (n := n) A') (evalTensor (R := R) (n := n) v')
```

This theorem transforms the rewrite system from “symbolic algebra” into “observable-preserving reduction,” which is exactly the language of physics and optimization.

### Theorem 4: Bilinearity of energy in a structured argument

If you define
\[
Q_A(v) = \langle v, A v\rangle,
\]
then prove a nontrivial expansion theorem such as
\[
Q_A(v+w)=Q_A(v)+\langle v,Aw\rangle+\langle w,Av\rangle+Q_A(w).
\]

Suggested Lean statement:

```lean
theorem energy_add
    {R : Type _} {n : Type _}
    [Fintype n] [DecidableEq n] [CommRing R]
    (A : Matrix n n R) (v w : n → R) :
    energy A (v + w) =
      energy A v + dot v (Matrix.mulVec A w) +
      dot w (Matrix.mulVec A v) + energy A w
```

This is mathematically richer than plain rewrite soundness and creates a bridge to polarization identities, quadratic forms, and mechanics.

### Theorem 5: Symmetric-matrix specialization

If you can define matrix transpose and symmetry assumptions, prove the cross terms collapse in the expected way over a commutative ring:

```lean
theorem energy_add_of_symmetric
    {R : Type _} {n : Type _}
    [Fintype n] [DecidableEq n] [CommRing R]
    (A : Matrix n n R)
    (hA : Aᵀ = A) (v w : n → R) :
    energy A (v + w) =
      energy A v + (dot v (Matrix.mulVec A w) + dot v (Matrix.mulVec A w)) + energy A w
```

or an equivalent statement identifying the two mixed terms. This links the rewrite system to quadratic-form theory.

## Proof strategy architecture

You must provide at least 2–3 serious proof routes and choose the most promising one.

### Strategy A: Direct semantic soundness via Mathlib linear algebra
1. Define syntax and evaluator for the three sorts.
2. Prove one-step soundness by induction on rewrite constructors.
3. Lift to many-step soundness via `Relation.ReflTransGen`.
4. Deduce energy invariance by rewriting both matrix and vector semantics.

**Why promising:** This is the shortest route to strong theorems and directly leverages `Matrix.mulVec`, finite sums, and additive algebra in Mathlib.

### Strategy B: Factor through the catalog’s many-sorted master theorem
1. Recast the new tensor signature as an instance/generalization of the catalog’s parametric many-sorted framework.
2. Reuse the generic soundness/convergence infrastructure.
3. Specialize the semantic interpretation to vectors and matrices over `R`.

**Why promising:** This best honors the lineage of `ManySortedConvergentRewriteOptimizer.lean` and may produce the cleanest architecture if the existing framework is sufficiently abstract.

**Why risky:** The catalog abstraction may fit module-like terms but not mixed matrix/vector operators without nontrivial refactoring.

### Strategy C: Interpret the system categorically as an action of endomorphisms on a module
1. Treat matrices as endomorphisms of a finite free module.
2. Regard rewrite rules as coherence laws for distributivity and scalar compatibility.
3. Derive energy invariance from bilinearity of the pairing.

**Why promising:** Conceptually elegant and opens future generalization beyond concrete matrices.

**Why risky:** Harder to formalize quickly unless the category-theoretic overhead is kept minimal.

### Recommendation
Start with **Strategy A**, but shape definitions so they can later be refactored into **Strategy B**. That gives immediate theorem production while preserving the possibility of a cleaner generic framework.

## Concrete rewrite fragment to formalize

A good first fragment is the following 6–8 rules:

1. `mulVec A (vAdd v w)  ↦ vAdd (mulVec A v) (mulVec A w)`
2. `mulVec (mAdd A B) v  ↦ vAdd (mulVec A v) (mulVec B v)`
3. `mulVec (smulMat a A) v ↦ smulVec a (mulVec A v)`
4. `smulVec a (vAdd v w) ↦ vAdd (smulVec a v) (smulVec a w)`
5. `smulMat a (mAdd A B) ↦ mAdd (smulMat a A) (smulMat a B)`
6. `dot (vAdd v w) u ↦ scalAdd (dot v u) (dot w u)`
7. `dot u (vAdd v w) ↦ scalAdd (dot u v) (dot u w)`
8. optionally, `dot (smulVec a v) w ↦ scalMul a (dot v w)`

Do not overload the system with too many associativity/commutativity rules unless you also define a measure or orientation carefully. A focused, terminating fragment is better than an overambitious but unmanageable one.
