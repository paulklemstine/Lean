## Central Vision

For a finite `d`-uniform hypergraph `H`, let `τ* (H)` denote its fractional transversal number. Define the edge-insertion response at edge `e` by
\[
\Delta \tau^*(H,e) := \tau^*(H \cup \{e\}) - \tau^*(H).
\]
The susceptibility of a random hypergraph ensemble should measure how strongly the LP optimum reacts to microscopic perturbations.

The scientific conjecture is:

> For random `d`-uniform hypergraphs with `m = ⌊cn⌋`, the expected maximal insertion response and related averaged response observables exhibit a sharp finite-size peak near a critical density `c*`, and this peak obeys a scaling law whose exponent depends only on `d`, not on microscopic details.

A full asymptotic power-law theorem may currently be beyond the catalog. But you can prove the rigorous finite-size skeleton now: monotonicity, boundedness, martingale variance identities, peak existence principles, and finite-size scaling inequalities that make the critical-exponent conjecture mathematically testable.

This would open a new field: **optimization criticality**, where LP observables on random discrete structures are treated as thermodynamic response functions.

## New Definitions You Must Introduce

You must define at least one genuinely new concept absent from the catalog. I recommend introducing **finite-size susceptibility observables** for edge-exposure processes.

### 1. Edge insertion susceptibility
For a finite hypergraph `H`, define
\[
\chi_{\max}(H) := \max_{e} |\Delta \tau^*(H,e)|
\]
where the maximum ranges over all admissible `d`-edges on the vertex set.

### 2. Mean insertion susceptibility
Define
\[
\chi_{\mathrm{avg}}(H) := \frac{1}{|E_d(V)|}\sum_e |\Delta \tau^*(H,e)|.
\]

### 3. Edge-exposure quadratic susceptibility
For an edge-exposure martingale `M_i = E[τ*(H_m)\mid \mathcal F_i]`, define the finite-size quadratic susceptibility
\[
\chi^{(2)}_{n,m,d} := \sum_{i=0}^{m-1} \mathbb E[(M_{i+1}-M_i)^2].
\]
By martingale isometry this is the variance of `τ*(H_m)` when the process is correctly set up.

This is the right formal object: in statistical mechanics, susceptibility is a variance / response quantity. Here, the LP optimum itself plays the role of an energy-like observable.

### Theorem 3: Variance-susceptibility identity for edge-exposure martingales
This is the conceptual breakthrough theorem. It is the first rigorous statement that the fluctuation of the LP optimum is exactly a sum of local response contributions.

#### Mathematical statement
Let `M_i = E[X | 𝓕_i]` be the Doob martingale for `X = τ*(H_m)` along the edge-exposure filtration of a random `d`-uniform hypergraph process. Then
\[
\operatorname{Var}(X)
=
\sum_{i=0}^{m-1} \mathbb E[(M_{i+1}-M_i)^2].
\]
If each increment is bounded by `1`, then
\[
\operatorname{Var}(X) \le m.
\]
More sharply, if you define the quadratic susceptibility
\[
\chi^{(2)}_{n,m,d} := \sum_i \mathbb E[(M_{i+1}-M_i)^2],
\]
then
\[
\chi^{(2)}_{n,m,d} = \operatorname{Var}(\tau^*(H_m)).
\]

If full probability formalization is too heavy, prove a finite discrete probability version over a finite sample space of edge-exposure sequences.

#### Why this matters
This is the theorem that transforms susceptibility from a metaphor into mathematics. It says the “critical response” is not hand-waving: it is literally the variance decomposition of the LP optimum across microscopic edge exposures. This opens a route to universality, scaling windows, and renormalization-style questions for combinatorial optimization.

## Recommended Proof Strategies

You asked for 2–3 proof strategy steps. Here are three distinct proof architectures.

### Strategy A: Direct LP monotonicity + bounded-difference lifting
**Best for Theorems 1 and 2.**

1. Use catalog results `fracTransversalNum_addEdge_le` and `fracTransversalNum_addEdge_abs_le'` to control the change under single-edge insertion.
2. Prove monotonicity by showing every fractional transversal feasible for `H ∪ {e}` is feasible for `H`, so the feasible region shrinks under edge addition and the minimization optimum cannot decrease.
3. Combine monotonicity with the catalog `abs_le` bound to obtain
   \[
   0 \le \Delta\tau^*(H,e) \le 1.
   \]
4. Lift pointwise inequalities to `max` and `avg` susceptibility via `Finset` arguments.

**Why promising:** It uses the catalog exactly where it is strongest and produces foundational response inequalities with minimal probabilistic overhead.

---

### Strategy B: Doob martingale + finite sample-space variance decomposition
**Best for Theorem 3. Most promising overall.**

1. Model the random hypergraph process by exposing edges one at a time from a finite list of candidate `d`-edges, or by choosing an `m`-subset from the finite edge universe.
2. Define the Doob martingale
   \[
   M_i = \mathbb E[X \mid \mathcal F_i],\quad X=\tau^*(H_m).
   \]
3. Use bounded-difference theorems from the catalog to show `|M_{i+1} - M_i| ≤ 1`.
4. Prove the variance decomposition by orthogonality of martingale differences in a finite probability space:
   \[
   \operatorname{Var}(X)=\sum_i \mathbb E[(M_{i+1}-M_i)^2].
   \]
5. Package the RHS as `finiteSizeQuadraticSusceptibility`.

**Why this is the most promising strategy:** It gives the deepest theorem with the broadest downstream consequences. Once formalized, it becomes a reusable engine for other optimization observables, not just fractional transversals.

---

### Strategy C: Extremal finite combinatorics + pseudocritical point extraction
**Best for Theorem 4 and computational theory.**

1. Show the susceptibility observable is a function on a finite index set `Fin (M+1)`.
2. Apply finite maximum existence (`Finset.exists_max_image` or equivalent) to get an optimizer `m*`.
3. Interpret `m*` as a pseudocritical index and define `c*_{n,d}=m*/n`.
4. If possible, prove stability lemmas showing that if susceptibility changes slowly, any two maximizers are close in value.

**Why promising:** This turns numerical experiments into theorem-driven science. The computation of pseudocritical points becomes a verified algorithm, not just a plot.

## Conjecture With Falsifiable Prediction

You must state at least one explicit conjecture and give a computational disproof criterion.

### Conjecture: finite-size scaling of quadratic susceptibility
For each fixed `d ≥ 2`, there exist constants `c*(d) > 0`, `γ(d) > 0`, and a scaling function `F_d : ℝ → ℝ_{\ge 0}` such that for random `d`-uniform hypergraphs with `m = ⌊cn⌋`,
\[
\chi^{(2)}_{n,m,d}
=
n^{\gamma(d)} F_d\!\left((c-c^*(d))n^{1/\nu(d)}\right)
+ o(n^{\gamma(d)}).
\]

### Testable prediction
Let
\[
m^*_{n,d} := \arg\max_m \chi^{(2)}_{n,m,d}.
\]
Then:
1. `m^*_{n,d}/n` converges as `n` increases.
2. The peak height `max_m χ^{(2)}_{n,m,d}` grows polynomially in `n`.
3. After rescaling the horizontal axis by `n^{1/ν}` and vertical axis by `n^{γ}`, the curves collapse.

### Disproof criterion
The conjecture is false if:
- the estimated exponent `γ(d)` drifts without stabilization as `n` increases from `50` to `500`,
- or no nontrivial curve collapse appears after optimized scaling,
- or the pseudocritical density fails to stabilize.

This is scientifically excellent because it can fail clearly.
