## Assignment: Direction 4: Quantum Orbit Sampling — Orbit-Order Duality and Quantum Dynamical Period-Finding

### Central Conjecture

The iterated squaring map $f_n: \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$, $f_n(x) = x^2 \bmod n$, has orbit structure that encodes the complete factorization of $n$. A quantum period-finding circuit applied to the squaring map (not to modular exponentiation as in Shor) can extract this orbit structure in time $O(\text{poly}(\log n))$, yielding a BQP primality test and factoring algorithm structurally distinct from Shor's algorithm.

### Key Mathematical Insight: Orbit-Order Duality

The fundamental bridge between dynamics and algebra is:

**Theorem (Orbit-Order Duality)**. Let $n > 1$ and $x \in (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^*$ with multiplicative order $d = \text{ord}_n(x)$. The period of $x$ under the squaring map $f(y) = y^2 \bmod n$ equals the multiplicative order of 2 modulo $d$:

$$\text{per}_{f}(x) = \min\{k > 0 : x^{2^k} \equiv x \pmod{n}\} = \text{ord}_d(2)$$

*Proof sketch*: $x^{2^k} \equiv x \pmod{n} \iff x^{2^k - 1} \equiv 1 \pmod{n} \iff d \mid (2^k - 1) \iff 2^k \equiv 1 \pmod{d} \iff \text{ord}_d(2) \mid k$. The minimal such $k$ is exactly $\text{ord}_d(2)$. $\square$

This transforms the dynamical question (orbit period under iteration) into an algebraic question (order of 2 in a quotient group), making it immediately amenable to quantum period-finding applied to the *squaring map itself* rather than to modular exponentiation.

### Proof Strategies

**Strategy A (Direct order computation — most elementary)**: Prove the orbit-order duality by establishing a Galois connection between the two order predicates. Show $\{k > 0 : x^{2^k} \equiv x \pmod{n}\} = \{k > 0 : 2^k \equiv 1 \pmod{\text{ord}_n(x)}\}$ via the chain $x^{2^k} \equiv x \iff \text{ord}_n(x) \mid (2^k - 1) \iff 2^k \equiv 1 \pmod{\text{ord}_n(x)}$. Since both sets are subgroups of $(\mathbb{Z}, +)$ generated by the respective orders, their minimal elements are equal. **Best for formalization**: Mathlib already has `orderOf` and the order divisibility lattice; this reduces to rewriting in that lattice.

**Strategy B (Group-theoretic lifting via endomorphism rings)**: Interpret the squaring map as the endomorphism $\varphi: G \to G$ where $G = \langle x \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}$ and $\varphi(g) = g^2$. The orbit period of $x$ under $\varphi$ equals the order of $\varphi$ in $\text{Aut}(G) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})^*$, which is exactly $\text{ord}_d(2)$. This leverages Mathlib's `IsCyclic`, `Aut`, and `MonoidHom` infrastructure. **Most promising for the composite CRT theorem**: the endomorphism ring decomposes as a product via CRT.

**Strategy C (Dynamical systems approach via functional graphs)**: Model the squaring map as a functional graph on $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$. The orbit period of $x$ is the cycle length of $x$ in this graph. Prove that the cycle structure is determined by the orders $\text{ord}_d(2)$ for divisors $d$ of $\text{ord}_n(x)$. This approach naturally extends to non-coprime $x$ (where $x$ enters a fixed point after the preperiodic tail). **Best for the primality criterion and orbit distribution theorems**: the functional graph perspective makes the counting arguments transparent.

### Cross-Domain Connections

1. **Dynamical Systems ↔ Number Theory**: The squaring map $x \mapsto x^2 \bmod n$ is the discrete analogue of the quadratic map on the circle. Its orbit structure (attracting fixed points at idempotents, periodic orbits of length $\text{ord}_d(2)$, pre-periodic tails entering cycles) is a **dynamical factorization invariant** — different factorizations of $n$ produce non-isomorphic functional graphs. This connects to Arnol'd's cat map and the broader theory of discrete dynamical systems on algebraic varieties.

2. **Quantum Computing ↔ Spectral Theory**: The quantum period-finding circuit for the squaring map implements QPE on the unitary $U_f |x\rangle = |x^2 \bmod n\rangle$. The eigenvalues of $U_f$ are $\exp(2\pi i \cdot j / \text{per}_f(x_j))$ where $x_j$ ranges over cycle representatives. The spectrum of $U_f$ is the **dynamical zeta function** $\zeta_{f_n}(s) = \exp\left(\sum_k \text{Fix}(f^k) s^k / k\right)$ evaluated at $s = e^{2\pi i\theta}$. This is a direct bridge between quantum phase estimation and dynamical systems theory.

3. **Cryptography ↔ Ergodic Theory**: RSA security relies on the classical hardness of computing $\text{ord}_n(x)$ for composite $n$. The orbit-order duality shows $\text{ord}_{\text{ord}_n(x)}(2)$ is equivalent information (classical poly-time reduction via order computation). If quantum orbit sampling efficiently computes these orders, it breaks RSA through a **fundamentally different route** from Shor — exploiting dynamical structure rather than hidden subgroup structure. The ergodic-theoretic question "how quickly does the squaring map mix on $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$?" directly controls the quantum circuit depth.

4. **Arithmetic Dynamics ↔ Tropical Geometry**: The orbit period $\text{ord}_d(2)$ tropicalizes: in the min-plus semiring, the iteration $x \mapsto 2 \otimes x = x + \log 2$ has "period" governed by the $p$-adic valuation of $2^k - 1$. This connects to $p$-adic dynamics and Silverman's dynamical height functions, opening a tropical arithmetic dynamics framework where orbit lengths satisfy product formulas analogous to the Weil conjectures.

5. **Statistical Mechanics ↔ Orbit Distributions**: The orbit type distribution $\mu_n(k) = |\{x \in (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^* : \text{per}_f(x) = k\}| / \phi(n)$ is a partition function on the "energy levels" $k$. The Boltzmann distribution $\exp(-\beta k)$ over orbit periods converges to a specific heat capacity that has phase transitions at values of $n$ with particular factorization structures — a number-theoretic analogue of the Kosterlitz-Thouless transition.

### Testable Conjecture with Computational Test

**Conjecture (Orbit Period Factoring)**: Given the multiset of squaring orbit periods $\{\text{ord}_{\text{ord}_n(x)}(2) : x \text{ uniform random in } (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^*\}$ for $O(\log^2 n)$ random starting points, one can classically factor $n$ in expected polynomial time via GCD post-processing.

**Falsification test**: For each semiprime $n = pq$ with $p, q$ prime, $p, q \in [100, 1000]$:
1. Sample 100 random units $x \in (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^*$.
2. Compute orbit periods $k_i = \text{ord}_{\text{ord}_n(x_i)}(2)$ for each.
3. For each $k_i$, compute $d_i = \gcd(2^{k_i} - 1, n)$.
4. Check: does any $d_i$ yield a non-trivial factor of $n$?

If the success rate (fraction of semiprimes where at least one $d_i$ is a non-trivial factor) is $< 1/2$, the conjecture is weakened. If $< 1/4$, it is likely false in its strong form.
