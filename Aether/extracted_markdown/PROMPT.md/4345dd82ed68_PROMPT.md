## Precise Theorem Targets

You need to define the right objects first. A strong formalization path is:

- `PrimitiveTriple := {t : ℤ × ℤ × ℤ // ... primitive Pythagorean conditions ...}`
- Berggren generators `A B C : Matrix (Fin 3) (Fin 3) ℤ`

- a canonical Gram map from triples to rank-2 lattices
- a finite family realization map from a finite subtree to a finite set / indexed family of lattices with certified short bases

The most promising canonical rank-2 Gram map is:
\[
G(a,b,c)=
\begin{pmatrix}
c+a & b\\
b & c-a
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For a primitive Pythagorean triple \(a^2+b^2=c^2\), this matrix is symmetric integral and has determinant
\[
(c+a)(c-a)-b^2=c^2-a^2-b^2=0,
\]
so as written it is degenerate. That is mathematically important: you must **not** ignore this. Use it to sharpen the program.

### Required correction / deeper insight
The degenerate matrix above is not a positive-definite Gram matrix; instead it should be treated as:

1. a **rank-1 boundary form** encoding the triple on the cone of semidefinite binary forms, or
2. an ingredient in a **rank-3 positive-definite lift**, e.g.
   \[
   \widetilde G(a,b,c)=
   \begin{pmatrix}
   c+a & b & 0\\
   b & c-a & 0\\
   0 & 0 & \delta(a,b,c)
   \end{pmatrix}
   \]
   with a canonical positive scalar \(\delta\), or
3. a replacement by a genuinely positive-definite rank-2 form such as
   \[
   G^+(a,b,c)=
   \begin{pmatrix}
   c & a\\
   a & c
   \end{pmatrix}
   \quad\text{or}\quad
   \begin{pmatrix}
   c & b\\
   b & c
   \end{pmatrix},
   \]
   whose determinant is \(c^2-a^2=b^2\) or \(c^2-b^2=a^2\), hence positive for primitive triples.

**Do not proceed with a false positive-definite claim.**  
A theorem proving that the Berggren invariants classify the positive-definite lift is stronger and cleaner.

## Main Theorem Statement

A robust theorem package would be:

### Theorem A: Canonical lattice realization from finite Berggren presentations
For every finite normalized Berggren subtree \(T\) of primitive triples, there exists a canonically associated finite family of integral positive-definite lattices \(L_T(v)\) indexed by vertices \(v \in T\), together with explicit basis certificates whose norm bounds are computable from Berggren invariants:
- hypotenuse \(c(v)\),
- leg-difference \(|a(v)-b(v)|\),
- 2-adic valuation \(v_2(a(v)-b(v))\) or parity profile,
- ancestry depth \(d(v)\).

Moreover, the shortest certified basis length is bounded above by an explicit function
\[
\beta(v) \le F(c(v), |a(v)-b(v)|, d(v)).
\]

### Theorem B: Rigidity / minimality
If \(T_1,T_2\) are finite normalized Berggren generating subtrees and the induced certified lattice families are isometric via a certificate-preserving isometry compatible with the chosen normalization, then \(T_1\) and \(T_2\) are equal up to signed Berggren tree automorphism. In particular, each realizable certified lattice family admits a unique minimal normalized Berggren presentation.

### Theorem C: Certified reconstruction
There exists an algorithm
\[
\mathsf{reconstruct} : \mathsf{LatticeCert} \to \mathsf{Option}\ \mathsf{BerggrenPresentation}
\]
such that whenever a lattice certificate satisfies explicit spectral, determinant, parity, and primitiveness constraints characterizing the image of the realization functor, the algorithm returns the unique minimal normalized Berggren presentation producing that certificate. The algorithm runs in time polynomial in the bit-size of the certificate data.

This is the finite realization/minimality duality you should formalize.

### 3. Positive-definite lattice lift
Choose a mathematically correct Gram construction. A very promising choice is:
\[
G^+(a,b,c)=\begin{pmatrix} c & a \\ a & c \end{pmatrix}
\]
with determinant \(b^2\), or symmetrically using \(b\). Then:
- trace \(= 2c\),
- determinant \(= b^2\),
- shortest obvious basis vector norm \(= c\),
- off-diagonal arithmetic records one leg exactly.

This is not enough by itself to distinguish all triples, so enrich with an auxiliary invariant or rank-3 lift.

A stronger rank-3 construction:
\[
\widetilde G(a,b,c)=
\begin{pmatrix}
c & a & 0\\
a & c & 0\\
0 & 0 & \phi(a,b,c)
\end{pmatrix}
\]
where \(\phi\) is chosen canonically from depth or leg-difference data, for example \(\phi = c + |a-b|\) or another invariant making reconstruction rigid.

Then prove positive-definiteness by Sylvester criteria.

Lean target:
```lean
theorem liftedGram_posdef
  (t : PrimitiveTriple) :
  PositiveDefinite (liftedGramFromTriple t)
```

## Proof Strategy Paths

### Strategy A: Tree-theoretic rigidity first, then lattice realization
1. Formalize normalized primitive triples and the Berggren parent uniqueness theorem.
2. Prove finite subsets of the primitive triple tree admit unique minimal generating subtrees.
3. Define the lattice realization functor and show it is faithful on normalized generators via explicit invariant formulas.

**Why promising:** this isolates the genuinely new combinatorial rigidity from the linear algebra. The tree part is mathematically canonical and should give the uniqueness theorem almost for free once the encoding is injective.

### Strategy B: Quadratic-form classification first, then pull back to Berggren ancestry
1. Choose a positive-definite Gram construction with determinant/trace formulas recovering triple invariants.
2. Prove a classification theorem: certificates satisfying the image constraints correspond bijectively to normalized primitive triples.
3. Extend from single triples to finite semimodule/subtree presentations by closure under parent maps and finite-generation minimality.

**Why promising:** if Mathlib already has stronger matrix and quadratic-form support than tree machinery, this may be easier to mechanize. It also foregrounds the cryptographic interpretation immediately.

### Strategy C: Semimodule/functorial language from the start
1. Define a Berggren action semimodule or closure system on primitive triples.
2. Construct a functor to certified lattices.
3. Prove essential injectivity and finite-image reconstruction categorically.

**Why less promising initially:** conceptually elegant, but likely heavier in Lean because you may spend time formalizing category-theoretic overhead instead of proving the core arithmetic theorem. Use this only if the relevant abstractions already exist in your local codebase.

**Recommended path:** Strategy A first, then fold in Strategy B for the reconstruction theorem. This gives the strongest chance of a finished, sorry-minimized artifact.

Your theorem would make this analogy mathematically exact.

### 2. Quadratic forms ↔ lattice reduction certificates

### 4. Semidefinite boundary geometry ↔ tropical and one-way paradigms
The initially proposed degenerate matrix lies on the boundary of positive-definite form space. That is not an obstacle but a clue: Berggren triples naturally live at the interface between rigid arithmetic and degenerating geometry. This resonates with tropicalization, cone boundaries, and one-way compression phenomena. Even if your final theorem uses a positive-definite lift, note this boundary geometry explicitly—it suggests a future “tropical-lattice trapdoor” program.

### 5. Complexity theory ↔ arithmetic reconstruction
