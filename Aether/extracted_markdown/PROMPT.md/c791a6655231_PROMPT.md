## Synthesis

This cycle laid down the *algebraic backbone* of p-curvature for rank-one Picard–Fuchs
operators, entirely from first principles over an arbitrary commutative ring `A` of
characteristic `p` (file `Catalog/Novelty/PCurvatureRigidity.lean`). The central objects are a
derivation `D` (the differentiation `d/dz` of a Picard–Fuchs operator reduced mod `p`) and the
rank-one connection `∇ = D + a`, the operator `Y ↦ DY + a·Y` whose horizontal sections solve the
first-order ODE `Y' = -aY`.

Three structural facts were proved and machine-verified:

1. **Iterated Leibniz** (`iterate_leibniz`): `Dⁿ(xy) = Σ_k C(n,k)·Dᵏx·Dⁿ⁻ᵏy`.
2. **Hochschild's theorem** (`hochschild_prime`): in characteristic `p`, the `p`-fold iterate
   `Dᵖ` is *again a derivation*. The middle binomials `C(p,k)` (`0<k<p`) all vanish mod `p`; this
   single fact is *the* reason the p-curvature is an `A`-linear "scalar" operator rather than a
   merely additive one — i.e. why it is an honest `F_p(z)`-linear invariant.
3. **Jacobson formulas + rigidity dichotomy** at `p = 2, 3` (`pCurvature_two`, `pCurvature_three`,
   `pCurv_two_dichotomy`, `pCurv_three_dichotomy`): `∇ᵖ = Dᵖ + ψ_p(a)·` where the p-curvature
   *scalar* is `ψ_p(a) = aᵖ + Dᵖ⁻¹(a)`, and the operator `∇ᵖ − Dᵖ` vanishes **iff** `ψ_p(a) = 0`.
   The vanishing condition is literally an Artin–Schreier equation `Dᵖ⁻¹(a) = −aᵖ`.

A cross-domain bridge to the catalog's number theory thread was established
(`pCurv_const_zmod_eq_self`): for the trivial (constant-coefficient) connection, the p-curvature
degenerates to the **Frobenius** `a ↦ aᵖ`, which over `ZMod p` is the identity by Fermat's little
theorem — exactly the `ZMod.pow_card`/`ZMod.pow_card_sub_one_eq_one` mechanism powering the
Korselt → Carmichael bridge in `Catalog/Novelty/KorseltCarmichael.lean`. The same Frobenius that
detects Carmichael numbers is the degenerate p-curvature of a differential operator.

## Research Directions

### 1. The general-`p` Jacobson formula (the missing keystone)

We proved `∇ᵖ = Dᵖ + ψ_p(a)·` only for `p = 2, 3`. The general statement
`(connection D a)^[p] Y = Dᵖ Y + (aᵖ + Dᵖ⁻¹(a))·Y` for every prime `p` is the natural next
target. Our failure analysis is itself a contribution: the *naive* closed form
`∇ⁿY = Σ_k C(n,k)·Dᵏ(aⁿ⁻ᵏ Y)` is **false** — its cross-term coefficients are not binomial,
already at `n = 2`. **The key insight is** that the correct collapse is not a binomial identity
but a char-`p` Lie-theoretic one: inside the additive-endomorphism ring `End(A)`, the operators
`δ = D` and `α = (·a)` satisfy `[δ, α] = (·Da)` with all multiplication operators commuting, and
`End(A)` inherits characteristic `p` from `A`; Jacobson's `(δ+α)ᵖ = δᵖ + αᵖ + (correction)`
specializes the correction to exactly `Dᵖ⁻¹(a)`. **Why now?** With `hochschild_prime` already
formalized (the char-`p` binomial vanishing), the only remaining ingredient is the operator-ring
`CharP` instance plus an induction on the iterated commutator — a self-contained, falsifiable
Lean goal that would upgrade `pCurv_two/three_dichotomy` to all primes at once.

### 2. Density of vanishing-p-curvature primes as an arithmetic discriminator

The headline conjecture is a density dichotomy: for an Apéry-type operator, the set
`{p : ψ_p(a) ≡ 0}` has natural density `0` unless the operator is an algebraic hypergeometric
motive (finite geometric monodromy), where it has positive density. A first formal milestone is
to *define* this density and prove the two extreme calibration points. **The key insight is** that
the constant-coefficient case `pCurv_const_zmod_eq_self` already pins one endpoint: a *genuinely
constant* `a ≠ 0` over `ZMod p` has `ψ_p(a) = a ≠ 0` for every `p`, giving density `0` (no
vanishing primes) — the transcendental archetype; whereas the trivial operator `a = 0` has
`ψ_p = 0` for all `p`, density `1` — the algebraic archetype. **Why now?** These two
machine-checked endpoints make the density statement a concrete, testable interpolation rather
than a vague heuristic, and they reuse exactly the Frobenius/Fermat lemma already proved.

### 3. Apéry numbers, supercongruences, and p-curvature nilpotency

Apéry's numbers `A_n = Σ_k C(n,k)²C(n+k,k)²` (irrationality of `ζ(3)`) satisfy the Beukers–Coster
supercongruences `A_{mp} ≡ A_m (mod p³)`, a shadow of p-curvature *nilpotency* of their
Picard–Fuchs operator. **The key insight is** that a Lucas-type congruence `A_{n} mod p` is the
combinatorial reflection of the Frobenius structure that `hochschild_prime` makes precise: the
operator's mod-`p` solution module carries a Frobenius descent whenever the coefficients obey a
Lucas congruence. **Why now?** Mathlib has the binomial machinery (`Nat.Prime.dvd_choose_self`,
`Nat.cast_choose`) and we have the iterated-Leibniz/Hochschild layer; a focused formalization of
the *order-1 Lucas congruence for central binomials* `C(2n,n)` (the algebraic `(1-4z)^{-1/2}`
prototype) is an achievable first rung connecting combinatorics to the p-curvature picture.

### 4. Cartier descent: vanishing p-curvature ⇔ full horizontal solution space

Our dichotomies say the *operator* `∇ᵖ − Dᵖ` vanishes iff the *scalar* `ψ_p` vanishes. The deeper
Cartier theorem says vanishing p-curvature is equivalent to the connection admitting a *full set
of horizontal (here: polynomial/rational) solutions* over the constants `F_p(z)^p`. **The key
insight is** that for the rank-one connection, a horizontal section is a solution of the
Artin–Schreier equation `Dᵖ⁻¹(a) = −aᵖ`, so Cartier descent in rank one is exactly the
solvability of an Artin–Schreier extension — a purely algebraic, formalizable statement. **Why
now?** The rank-one case sidesteps the full vector-bundle Cartier isomorphism (absent from
Mathlib) while capturing its arithmetic content, and it directly formalizes the
"polynomial-solutions ⇔ vanishing p-curvature" half of the headline conjecture.

### 5. Rank-2 and rank-3 operators: from scalars to p-curvature matrices

Picard–Fuchs operators of Calabi–Yau type are rank 3–4; their p-curvature is a *matrix*, not a
scalar. The next structural step is the rank-2 companion connection `∇ = D + M` for `M` a `2×2`
matrix over `A`, with p-curvature `ψ_p = (D + M)ᵖ − Dᵖ` valued in matrices. **The key insight is**
that Hochschild's theorem already guarantees `Dᵖ` acts entrywise as a derivation, so the rank-`r`
p-curvature is `A`-linear (a genuine matrix over `A`) for *every* rank — the scalar case is not
special, only simpler. **Why now?** With `iterate_leibniz` and `hochschild_prime` proved at the
ring level, lifting to `Matrix (Fin r) (Fin r) A` is mostly functoriality, and it opens the door
to the true Calabi–Yau-rank statements where the density dichotomy actually bites.
