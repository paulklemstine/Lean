## Core Vision

The existing Church–Rosser budget result says, roughly, that β-equivalent terms can be joined with sufficient reduction budget. That is already a complexity statement. The breakthrough is to reinterpret that budget **as behavioral depth** in a finite transition semantics, and then prove that **normalization cost induces a bisimulation pseudometric**.

This would open a new research direction:

- **proof complexity → metric semantics**: reduction length becomes a semantic distance;
- **operational semantics → coalgebra**: weak bisimulation depth becomes a quantitative observable;
- **lambda calculus → algorithmic verification**: one can compute certified upper bounds on behavioral distinguishability from normalization traces.

If successful, this is not “one more lambda-calculus lemma.” It is the beginning of a **resource-sensitive behavioral geometry of proofs**.

## Breakthrough Theorem Targets

You must prove at least **3 substantial theorems** with nontrivial proof structure. The following are the recommended core targets.

### Theorem 1: Joinability budget gives finite bisimulation depth

This is the fundamental bridge theorem.

#### Mathematical statement
For all lambda terms `t u`, if `t` and `u` are β-equivalent and have reduction budgets `k₁, k₂` to a common join, then there exists a finite depth `d` such that the finite transition systems generated by truncation at depth `d` are weakly bisimilar. Moreover one can take
`d ≤ k₁ + k₂` or `d ≤ max k₁ k₂` depending on the exact existing theorem interface.

#### Lean-style target
```lean
theorem weaklyBisimilarAtDepth_of_joinBudget
    {t u : LamTerm} {k₁ k₂ : ℕ} :
    JoinBudgetBound (k₁ + k₂) t u →
    WeaklyBisimilarAtDepth (k₁ + k₂) t u
```

or, if the catalog theorem gives a `max`-budget:
```lean
theorem weaklyBisimilarAtDepth_of_betaEq_budget
    {t u : LamTerm} {k₁ k₂ : ℕ} :
    BetaReducesIn k₁ t v →
    BetaReducesIn k₂ u v →
    WeaklyBisimilarAtDepth (max k₁ k₂) t u
```

#### Why this is a breakthrough
This converts **proof-theoretic convergence data** into a **coalgebraic indistinguishability witness**. It says that common reducts are not just extensional certificates—they are behavioral synchronization devices.

### Theorem 2: Normalization cost upper-bounds bisimulation depth

This is the main quantitative theorem suggested by the conjecture.

#### Mathematical statement
For simply-typed lambda terms `t, u` that normalize, if `nf_t` and `nf_u` are β-normal forms reached in minimal costs `c_t` and `c_u`, and if the normal forms are weakly bisimilar at depth `0` (or equivalent under the chosen finite semantics), then
\[
\mathrm{bisimDepth}(t,u) \le c_t + c_u.
\]
In particular, if `t` and `u` are β-equivalent simply-typed terms, then their behavioral distance is bounded by the sum of normalization costs.

#### Lean-style target
```lean
theorem bisimDepth_le_normCost_sum
    {t u : LamTerm} :
    SimplyTyped t →
    SimplyTyped u →
    HasNormalForm t →
    HasNormalForm u →
    normalizationBisimDist t u ≤ normCost t + normCost u
```

A sharper version, if available from the catalog:
```lean
theorem bisimDepth_le_of_betaEq
    {t u : LamTerm} :
    SimplyTyped t →
    SimplyTyped u →
    BetaEq t u →
    normalizationBisimDist t u ≤ normCost t + normCost u
```

#### Why this is a breakthrough
This theorem gives a **semantic Lipschitz principle for normalization**: computational effort bounds observational discrepancy. That is a new kind of theorem, with the flavor of complexity theory, semantics, and metric geometry all at once.

### Theorem 3: The induced distance is a pseudometric

If exact metric axioms are too ambitious because β-equivalent distinct terms may have distance zero, prove pseudometric axioms. This is mathematically cleaner and more likely true.

#### Mathematical statement
Define
\[
d(t,u) := \text{least depth at which } t,u \text{ become weakly bisimilar}
\]
or a computable surrogate. Then:

1. `d(t,t) = 0`
2. `d(t,u) = d(u,t)`
3. `d(t,v) ≤ d(t,u) + d(u,v)` whenever the depth composition lemma holds.

#### Lean-style target
```lean
theorem normalizationBisimDist_self (t : LamTerm) :
    normalizationBisimDist t t = 0

theorem normalizationBisimDist_comm (t u : LamTerm) :
    normalizationBisimDist t u = normalizationBisimDist u t

theorem normalizationBisimDist_triangle (t u v : LamTerm) :
    normalizationBisimDist t v
      ≤ normalizationBisimDist t u + normalizationBisimDist u v
```

#### Why this is a breakthrough
A pseudometric on lambda terms extracted from reduction semantics would create a **quantitative semantic geometry**. This is the sort of object that can seed an entire line of work: continuity of substitution, contractivity of evaluation strategies, quantitative full abstraction, and complexity-sensitive denotational semantics.

## Proof Strategy Architecture

You must not give only one path. Pursue at least 2–3 approaches, and decide explicitly which is strongest.

### Strategy A: Transport the existing Church–Rosser budget into weak bisimulation depth
Most promising for the first main theorem.

1. Extract from `betaEq_joinable_with_sufficient_budget` a witness `v` and reduction bounds from `t` and `u` to `v`.
2. Show that any pair of terms reducing to the same `v` within bounded budget are weakly bisimilar after truncation to that budget.
3. Define `normalizationBisimDist` as a least such depth and conclude the upper bound.

**Why promising:** it directly leverages the catalog theorem and converts already-certified reduction bounds into behavioral witnesses.

### Strategy B: Induction on normalization derivations
Best for the normalization-cost upper bound.

1. Define `normCost` via minimal-length normalization derivations or a recursively certified evaluator.
2. Prove by induction on the derivation of normalization that each β-step changes bisimulation depth by at most 1.
3. Sum these one-step perturbation bounds along reduction sequences from `t` and `u` to their normal forms.

**Why promising:** this creates a genuine “path metric” interpretation and may yield sharper additive estimates than the raw Church–Rosser bound.

### Strategy C: Coalgebraic truncation and relation lifting
Most conceptual; may be strongest for pseudometric axioms.

1. Define the depth-`k` finite transition system `toFTS k t`.
2. Prove monotonicity: if weak bisimulation holds at depth `k`, it also holds at any larger depth.
3. Use least-witness constructions and relation composition to prove symmetry and triangle inequality.

**Why promising:** this yields the cleanest abstract theory and makes the metric/pseudometric structure natural rather than ad hoc.

**Recommended order:** A → B → C.  
A gives the bridge theorem quickly, B gives the quantitative upper bound, C elevates the result into a reusable semantic framework.

- derivational complexity,
- bounded observational equivalence,
- cost semantics.

### 4. Dynamical systems / statistical physics
Reduction-to-normal-form can be viewed as energy dissipation toward an attractor, while bisimulation depth measures coarse-grained observational similarity. This analogy suggests entropy-like or Lyapunov-style quantities. Even a brief theorem or discussion here would be powerful.

### 5. Type theory / verification
For simply-typed terms, strong normalization makes the distance total and computable on bounded examples. That gives a practical algorithmic semantics for small typed programs.
