# Assignment: Direction 4 — Sheaf Compression on Finite Sites

## Mode: prove

Aristotle, do not treat this as a small extension of finite representability. This is the moment to fuse **probe complexity**, **Grothendieck topologies**, and **compression by generators** into a genuinely new theory:

> **Sheaf compression on finite sites**: the passage from presheaf-level probe covers to topology-respecting sheaf-level covers.

If this works, it opens a new bridge between **finite categorical compression** and the geometric logic of sheaves. The point is not merely that a presheaf can be approximated by representables, but that **the approximation survives sheafification in an optimal way**. That is the first step toward a theory of **geometric compression invariants** of sites, topoi, and finite geometric data.

Build directly on:

## Central Vision

The presheaf world is combinatorial and explicit; the sheaf world is geometric and local-to-global. The breakthrough is to prove that **minimal representable compression is not destroyed by locality constraints**.

The guiding principle is:

> A probe system that already separates a sheaf and is compatible with the topology should produce a representable cover whose sheafification remains universal among sheaf-level covers generated by those probes.

This would amount to a finite, computable avatar of the slogan:

> **“Compression by observables commutes with geometric descent.”**

That is a field-opening statement. It connects:
- **algebraic geometry**: sheafification, covers, sites, local generation,
- **topos theory**: generators and representability inside sheaf categories,
- **data compression**: minimal observable summaries,
- **topological data analysis / sensing**: reconstruction from local probes.

Application keywords: `finite sites`, `Grothendieck topology`, `sheafification`, `representable presheaves`, `compression complexity`, `geometric compression`, `topos generators`, `local-to-global reconstruction`, `categorical sensing`, `finite algebraic geometry`.

## Required New Definitions

You must introduce at least one genuinely new concept not already in the catalog. I strongly recommend defining all three:

1. **Topology-compatible probe family**
   A family of probe objects `P : Finset C` such that the induced representable-testing maps detect equality and interact well with covering sieves.

2. **Sheaf-compressed cover**
   A morphism in the sheaf category from the sheafification of a coproduct of representables into a sheaf `F`, together with a minimality condition relative to a probe family.

3. **Sheaf compression number**
   A natural number invariant measuring the minimum size of a probe-generated representable family whose sheafification surjects onto / separates / generates `F` in the appropriate finite sense.

These definitions should not be decorative. They must drive the theorem statements.

## Precise Theorem Targets

You need at least 3 substantial theorems. Here is the theorem package I want.

### Theorem 1: Descent of probe covers through sheafification

Informal statement:

Let `(C, J)` be a finite site, let `F : Cᵒᵖ ⥤ Type _` be a sheaf, and let `π : R ⟶ F` be a presheaf morphism where `R` is a finite coproduct of representables coming from a topology-compatible probe family. If `π` is probe-separating and cover-compatible, then `π` factors through the sheafification of `R`, yielding a canonical sheaf-level cover.

This is the first nontrivial bridge theorem.

### Suggested Lean 4 type signature sketch

You will need to adapt to exact Mathlib names, but target something structurally like:

```lean
theorem presheaf_cover_factors_through_sheafification
  {C : Type u} [Category C]
  (J : GrothendieckTopology C)
  [Finite (Opposite C)] 
  (F : Cᵒᵖ ⥤ Type v)
  (hF : Presheaf.IsSheaf J F)
  (P : Finset C)
  (R : Cᵒᵖ ⥤ Type v)
  (hR : IsProbeCoproduct P R)
  (π : R ⟶ F)
  (hsep : ProbeSeparating J P π)
  (hcompat : TopologyCompatibleProbes J P) :
  ∃ (π_sh : J.sheafify R ⟶ J.toSheaf F),
    π = J.toSheafify_hom R ≫ π_sh
```

If `F` is already packaged as a sheaf object in Mathlib, then replace `Presheaf.IsSheaf J F` and `J.toSheaf F` by the actual sheaf-category object and its forgetful image.

### Why it is a breakthrough
This theorem says representable compression is not just a presheaf artifact; it survives the imposition of geometric locality. That is the conceptual leap.

### Theorem 2: Universal minimality of the sheafified probe cover

Informal statement:

Assume the finite representability theorem from the catalog gives a minimal presheaf-level representable cover for a sheaf `F`. Then among all sheaf-level covers generated by the same topology-compatible probes, the sheafification of that presheaf cover is initial/minimal.

This theorem turns existence into optimality.

### Suggested Lean 4 type signature sketch

```lean
theorem sheafified_probe_cover_is_minimal
  {C : Type u} [Category C]
  (J : GrothendieckTopology C)
  [Finite (Opposite C)]
  (F : Cᵒᵖ ⥤ Type v)
  (hF : Presheaf.IsSheaf J F)
  (P : Finset C)
  (Rmin : Cᵒᵖ ⥤ Type v)
  (hRmin : IsMinimalProbeCoproduct P F Rmin)
  (hcompat : TopologyCompatibleProbes J P) :
  IsMinimalSheafCover J P F (J.sheafify Rmin)
```

A more concrete version may compare cardinalities:

```lean
theorem sheaf_compression_number_le_of_cover
  {C : Type u} [Category C]
  (J : GrothendieckTopology C)
  [Finite (Opposite C)]
  (F : Cᵒᵖ ⥤ Type v)
  (hF : Presheaf.IsSheaf J F)
  (P : Finset C)
  (S : Sheaf J (Type v))
  (σ : S.1 ⟶ F)
  (hσ : GeneratedByProbes J P S)
  (hsurj : SheafCoverLike σ) :
  sheafCompressionNumber J P F ≤ ProbeGeneratorCardinality S
```

### Why it is a breakthrough
This creates an actual **compression invariant of sheaves on finite sites**. That is the beginning of a theory, not a one-off result.

### Theorem 3: Equality of presheaf and sheaf compression under topological generation

Informal statement:

If the probe family generates covering sieves and `F` is a sheaf separated by those probes, then the minimal presheaf compression number equals the minimal sheaf compression number.

This is the decisive theorem. It upgrades the conjecture from factorization to **no-loss compression under sheafification**.

### Suggested Lean 4 type signature sketch

```lean
theorem presheaf_and_sheaf_compression_agree
  {C : Type u} [Category C]
  [CategoryTheory.FintypeCategory C]
  (J : GrothendieckTopology C)
  (F : Cᵒᵖ ⥤ Type v)
  (hF : Presheaf.IsSheaf J F)
  (P : Finset C)
  (hsep : SheafSeparatedByProbes J P F)
  (hgen : ProbesGenerateTopology J P) :
  presheafCompressionNumber P F = sheafCompressionNumber J P F
```

If equality is too ambitious in the first pass, prove both inequalities separately:

```lean
theorem sheafCompression_le_presheafCompression ...
theorem presheafCompression_le_sheafCompression ...
```

### Why it is a breakthrough
This is the theorem that says geometry imposes **no extra compression cost** when probes are topologically sufficient. That is a new principle with immediate conceptual power.

### Recommended cross-domain theorem: Poset/Alexandrov specialization

Finite sites arising from finite preorders are the ideal bridge. For a finite poset `P`, sheaves on the Alexandrov site correspond to compatible local data on upper sets. Use this to prove that sheaf compression on such a site matches a combinatorial notion of compression on the poset nerve or incidence structure.

## Proof Architecture: 3 Possible Strategies

You must not rely on trivial automation. Use induction, `rcases`, `by_contra`, and multi-step `calc`. At least 3 theorems should visibly require serious proof structure.

### Strategy A: Universal-property route through sheafification
Most promising.

1. Start with the catalog’s finite representable presheaf cover theorem.
2. Use the universal property of sheafification: any map from `R` into a sheaf `F` factors uniquely through `J.sheafify R`.
3. Prove that the probe-separating and topology-compatible hypotheses ensure that the factorization preserves the relevant minimality/separation property.

Why this is strongest:
- It uses Mathlib’s sheafification machinery directly.
- It isolates the genuinely new content into your compatibility predicates.
- It should scale to later work on stacks/topoi.

### Strategy B: Work internally in the sheaf category
1. Reinterpret finite coproducts of representables as generators of the sheaf category after sheafification.
2. Prove that probe-generated covers correspond to epimorphic families in the sheaf category.
3. Show minimality by contradiction: if a smaller sheaf cover existed, pulling back along the forgetful functor would contradict presheaf minimality.

Why this is deep:
- It reveals the topos-theoretic meaning.
- It may give a cleaner conceptual theorem.
Risk:
- More dependence on Mathlib’s category-of-sheaves API and categorical epis.

### Strategy C: Finite-site combinatorial descent
1. Encode a finite Grothendieck topology by explicit covering sieves/families.
2. Write out the sheaf condition concretely as an equalizer/gluing condition over finite covers.
3. Construct the representable cover object-by-object and prove factorization by induction over the finite covering data.

Why this is useful:
- Best for executable computation and `demo.py`.
- Good fallback if sheafification APIs are awkward.
Risk:
- More combinatorial boilerplate, but excellent for experiments.

**Recommendation:** Use Strategy A for the main theorem, Strategy C for the computational/finite examples layer, and optionally Strategy B for a conceptual strengthening.

You must include 3–5 testable scientific hypotheses. At least one should be the main conjecture, but sharpened. Here are strong candidates:

1. **Compression equality conjecture**
   For every finite site `(C, J)` and sheaf `F` separated by a topology-generating probe family `P`,
   `presheafCompressionNumber P F = sheafCompressionNumber J P F`.
   **Test:** exhaustive search over categories with at most 4 objects.

2. **Strict gap conjecture fails outside topological generation**
   If `P` does not generate covering sieves, there exists a finite site and sheaf `F` with
   `presheafCompressionNumber P F < sheafCompressionNumber J P F`.
   **Test:** search for smallest counterexample.

3. **Alexandrov rigidity conjecture**
   On finite Alexandrov sites, principal-open probes always realize minimal sheaf compression.
   **Test:** enumerate finite posets up to size 5 and compare against all probe families.

4. **Subadditivity conjecture**
   Sheaf compression number is subadditive under finite coproduct/product of sheaves:
   `κ(F ⊞ G) ≤ κ(F) + κ(G)` or analogous finite-site version.
   **Test:** compute on explicit finite sheaves.

5. **Cohomological obstruction conjecture**
   Failure of compression equality correlates with nontrivial gluing obstruction data on the site.
   **Test:** in small sites, compare compression gaps with explicitly detected nontrivial matching conditions.

Each conjecture must have a clear computational falsification route.
