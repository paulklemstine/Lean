## Core Vision

Do not merely show “some bad examples exist.” Produce a mathematically structured obstruction theory.

A compelling target is to introduce a new concept such as a **layered exchange profile** or **certificate bottleneck complex**, and prove that it lower-bounds all admissible descent trajectories. This new structure should not already exist in the catalog.

### Theorem 1: Layer forcing lower bound

**Mathematical statement.**  
For every descent system equipped with a valid layered profile, every descent trajectory from the designated start point to a terminal point has length at least the number of forced layers. This is the abstract lower-bound engine.

**Lean-style target signature** (adapt to actual descent-path definitions in the library):

```lean
theorem descent_length_ge_forcedTraversalCount
    {α : Type _}
    (L : LayerProfile α)
    (x0 xt : α)
    (p : List α)
    (hp : ValidDescentPath p)
    (hstart : p.head? = some x0)
    (hend : p.getLast? = some xt)
    (hx0 : L.layer x0 = L.top)
    (hxt : L.layer xt = L.bottom) :
    forcedTraversalCount L ≤ p.length := by
```

**Why it matters.**  
This theorem transforms shell decomposition from a proof heuristic into a reusable lower-bound principle. Once proved abstractly, every explicit adversarial construction becomes a corollary.

## Proof strategy architecture

You must give Aristotle multiple proof paths and decide which one is most promising.

### Strategy A: Abstract shell/barrier argument
1. Define a layer function `ℓ : α → ℕ` such that every admissible descent step decreases `ℓ` by at most `1`.
2. Show the chosen start point lies in layer `N` and every terminal state lies in layer `0`.
3. Conclude any path has length at least `N` by induction on path length or by a telescoping `calc` argument.

**Why promising:**  
This is the cleanest route to a reusable theorem and will likely interact best with existing monotonicity lemmas such as `depthCertificate_runtime_monotone`.

### Strategy B: Explicit product construction
1. Build the adversarial family as a Cartesian or iterated product of lower-dimensional gadgets.
2. Prove that depth-`k` certificates compose under this product.
3. Show the number of necessary layers multiplies, producing the exponent `d-k`.

**Why promising:**  
This is the most likely route to the actual sharp exponent. Product gadgets naturally create `d^(d-k)`-scale complexity and are amenable to induction on `d-k`.

### Strategy C: Rank or energy method
1. Define an integer-valued potential, rank, or energy that decreases slowly under each descent step.
2. Show the initial energy is on the order of `d^(d-k-1) D`.
3. Prove each legal move reduces energy by at most `O(1)` or `O(d^k)`.

**Why promising:**  
This can produce robust lower bounds even if exact shell combinatorics are hard to formalize. It also creates a natural bridge to statistical mechanics, where slow relaxation is governed by energy barriers.

**Most promising overall:** Strategy B built on top of Strategy A.  
Use Strategy A to prove the abstract theorem once. Then use Strategy B to manufacture explicit families whose layer count grows like `d^(d-k)` or `d^(d-k-1)`. Strategy C is an excellent fallback if product-shell exactness becomes technically difficult.

## Mathematical insight to pursue

The conjectural picture is that depth-`k` certificates expose only `k` directions of rapid progress, leaving `d-k` hidden directions that can encode a combinatorial labyrinth. The lower bound should come from making those hidden directions interact multiplicatively, not additively. That is the real content behind the exponent `d-k`.

A useful slogan: **certificate depth measures visible dimension; lower bounds arise from invisible dimension.**

Try to turn this into a formal invariant:
- visible coordinates controlled by the certificate,
- hidden coordinates arranged in a layered obstruction,
- each descent step resolves only bounded hidden information.

This is where the complexity-theoretic analogy becomes powerful: the descent process resembles a bounded-information algorithm navigating a high-entropy state space.
