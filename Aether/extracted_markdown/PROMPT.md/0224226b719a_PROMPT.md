## Assignment: Fractional Transversal Refinement — LP-Dual Threshold Prediction and Integrality Gap Dynamics

### Visionary Significance

The fractional transversal number $\tau^*(C)$ is the optimal value of a linear program — it inherits convexity, continuity under perturbation, and polynomial-time computability. The integer transversal number $\tau(C)$ is the solution to an integer program — it is stepwise, NP-hard in general, and discontinuous under small structural changes. The conjecture that $\tau^*$ is a *better predictor* of empirical phase transitions than $\tau$ is not merely a computational observation; it is a claim that **convex relaxations capture mean-field physics more faithfully than their integer counterparts**, and that the integrality gap $\tau - \tau^*$ encodes the very fluctuations that make integer predictors noisy. Proving this opens a systematic route from LP duality to asymptotic prediction theory for phase transitions in constraint satisfaction problems, with applications from statistical physics to coding theory.

### Precise Theorem Targets

**Theorem 1 (LP Duality for Transversal-Matching):** For any hypergraph $H = (V, \mathcal{E})$, the fractional transversal number equals the fractional matching number:
$$\tau^*(H) = \nu^*(H)$$
where $\nu^*(H) = \max\left\{\sum_{e \in \mathcal{E}} y_e : \sum_{e \ni v} y_e \leq 1 \;\forall v \in V,\; y_e \geq 0\right\}$.

**Theorem 2 (Heterogeneity-Induced Integrality Gap):** For any hypergraph $H = (V, \mathcal{E})$ with maximum edge size $d_{\max}$ and minimum edge size $d_{\min}$:
$$\tau^*(H) \leq \tau(H) \leq d_{\max} \cdot \tau^*(H)$$
Moreover, if $H$ is $k$-uniform (all edges have size $k$), then $\tau(H) \leq k \cdot \tau^*(H)$ with equality iff $H$ contains a projective plane of order $k-1$ as a subhypergraph. The gap $\tau(H) - \tau^*(H)$ is zero when $H$ is bipartite (2-uniform and balanced) and grows with edge-size heterogeneity.

**Theorem 3 (Fractional Smoothing — Convexity Argument):** Let $\mathcal{H}(n, \mathbf{p})$ denote the random heterogeneous hypergraph on $n$ vertices where each potential $k$-subset is an edge independently with probability $p_k$, for $k \in \{k_1, \ldots, k_r\}$. Then the fractional transversal predictor satisfies:
$$\mathrm{Var}_{\mathcal{H}}\left[|V| - \lceil\tau^*\rceil\right] \leq \mathrm{Var}_{\mathcal{H}}\left[|V| - \tau\right]$$
with strict inequality when the edge-size distribution is non-degenerate (at least two distinct sizes appear with positive probability). ### Proof Strategy (Three Paths)

**Strategy A (LP Duality + Rounding):** Prove Theorem 1 via LP strong duality (the transversal LP and matching LP are duals). Then prove Theorem 2 by randomized rounding: sample each vertex independently with probability $x_v / d_{\max}$ where $x$ is the fractional transversal, then take a union bound. The integrality gap bound $d_{\max}$ falls out. For Theorem 3, use the fact that the optimum of an LP is a concave function of the right-hand side, hence Lipschitz under perturbations of the constraint matrix.

**Strategy B (Combinatorial — Alternating Paths):** Prove Theorem 1 via the augmenting path method generalized to hypergraphs (extending König's theorem). This gives a direct combinatorial proof but requires developing the theory of alternating paths in hypergraphs, which is more involved. The advantage: it yields structural insight into *when* the gap vanishes, directly connecting to the balanced/odd-cycle condition.

**Strategy C (Probabilistic — Second Moment):** For Theorem 3 (smoothing), use the second-moment method on random heterogeneous hypergraphs. Show that $\tau^*$ concentrates tightly (variance $O(1)$) while $\tau$ has variance $\Omega(\log n)$ due to integer rounding. This is the most promising path for the variance bound because it directly quantifies the smoothing effect and connects to the statistical physics prediction that replica-symmetric solutions have smaller fluctuations than replica-symmetry-broken ones.

**Recommended: Strategy A for Theorems 1–2, Strategy C for Theorem 3.** Strategy A builds directly on existing LP duality infrastructure in Mathlib, while Strategy C captures the essential physics.

### Novel Definition: Heterogeneity Index

```lean
/-- The edge-size heterogeneity of a hypergraph, measured as the 
    variance of edge cardinalities. Zero iff uniform. -/
def edgeHeterogeneity {V : Type*} [Fintype V] [DecidableEq V]
    (H : Hypergraph V) : ℝ :=
  let d_bar := (∑ e ∈ H.edges, (e.card : ℝ)) / H.edges.card
  (∑ e ∈ H.edges, ((e.card : ℝ) - d_bar)^2) / H.edges.card

/-- A hypergraph is heterogeneous iff its edges have at least 
    two distinct sizes. -/
def IsHeterogeneous {V : Type*} [Fintype V] [DecidableEq V]
    (H : Hypergraph V) : Prop :=
    ∃ k₁ k₂, k₁ ≠ k₂ ∧ 
    (∃ e ∈ H.edges, e.card = k₁) ∧ 
    (∃ e ∈ H.edges, e.card = k₂)
```
