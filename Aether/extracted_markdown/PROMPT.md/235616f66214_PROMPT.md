## Core Breakthrough Targets

### Theorem Cluster A: Quaternionic rotation action and the `SO(3)` double cover

Define a quaternion structure over `ℝ`, define conjugation, norm, and the action of unit quaternions on pure imaginary quaternions by
\[
v \mapsto q v q^{-1}.
\]
Then prove this induces an orientation-preserving isometry of `ℝ^3`, and that the resulting map onto `SO(3)` is a surjective group homomorphism with kernel `{1, -1}`.

This is not merely “quaternions represent rotations.” The breakthrough is to **formalize the spin covering mechanism itself** and package it as a certified computational bridge from noncommutative algebra to rigid-body kinematics.

### Precise theorem statement

Let `ℍ` be the real quaternions, `Pure ℍ := {q : ℍ // re q = 0}`, and let `unitSphere := {q : ℍ // quatNorm q = 1}`. Construct a map
\[
\mathrm{rotOfUnitQuat} : unitSphere \to SO(3)
\]
via conjugation on pure quaternions.

Prove:

1. **Norm preservation on pure quaternions**
   \[
   \forall q,\ \mathrm{quatNorm}(q)=1 \to \forall v \in \mathrm{Pure}\,\mathbb H,\ 
   \mathrm{quatNorm}(q v q^{-1}) = \mathrm{quatNorm}(v).
   \]

2. **Homomorphism theorem**
   \[
   \forall q_1 q_2,\ \mathrm{rotOfUnitQuat}(q_1 q_2)=\mathrm{rotOfUnitQuat}(q_1)\circ \mathrm{rotOfUnitQuat}(q_2).
   \]

3. **Kernel theorem**
   \[
   \ker(\mathrm{rotOfUnitQuat}) = \{1,-1\}.
   \]

4. **Surjectivity theorem**
   \[
   \forall R \in SO(3),\ \exists q,\ \mathrm{quatNorm}(q)=1 \land \mathrm{rotOfUnitQuat}(q)=R.
   \]

5. **Double-cover theorem**
   \[
   unitSphere / \{\pm 1\} \simeq SO(3).
   \]

### Theorem Cluster B: Quaternion algebras over fields and a first classification theorem

Do **not** attempt the full Albert–Brauer–Hasse–Noether theorem unless the library already gives enough local-field infrastructure. Instead, prove a strong and elegant first classification result:

For a field `F` of characteristic not `2`, define the quaternion algebra
\[
(a,b)_F = F \oplus Fi \oplus Fj \oplus Fk,\qquad i^2=a,\ j^2=b,\ ij=-ji=k.
\]
Prove that over an algebraically closed field every quaternion algebra splits, and over `ℝ` there are exactly two isomorphism classes: the split algebra `M₂(ℝ)` and Hamilton’s division algebra `ℍ`.

This is the correct “classification foothold”: conceptually deep, formalizable, and it opens the door to local-global classification later.

### Precise theorem statement

1. **Split over algebraically closed fields**
   \[
   \forall F,\ \mathrm{IsAlgClosed}(F)\land \mathrm{char}(F)\neq 2
   \implies \forall a b \in F^\times,\ (a,b)_F \cong M_2(F).
   \]

2. **Real classification**
   \[
   \forall a,b \in \mathbb R^\times,\ (a,b)_{\mathbb R} \cong M_2(\mathbb R)\ \lor\ (a,b)_{\mathbb R}\cong \mathbb H.
   \]
   More sharply, classify by signs:
   \[
   (a,b)_{\mathbb R}\cong \mathbb H \iff a<0 \land b<0.
   \]

### Theorem Cluster C: Cayley–Dickson, octonions, and the exact boundary of associativity

Build on the catalog’s Cayley–Dickson dimension results, but transcend them completely. Define a recursive Cayley–Dickson construction and prove the structural threshold:

- reals, complexes, quaternions are associative;
- octonions are alternative but not associative.

This is the theorem that marks the frontier between classical algebra and exceptional algebra.

### Precise theorem statement

Let `CD n` be the `n`-fold Cayley–Dickson algebra over `ℝ`.

Prove:

1. **Associativity up to quaternions**
   \[
   \forall x y z \in CD(n),\ n \le 2 \implies (xy)z = x(yz).
   \]

2. **Alternativity for octonions**
   \[
   \forall x y \in CD(3),\ (xx)y = x(xy)\quad\text{and}\quad y(xx) = (yx)x.
   \]

3. **Failure of associativity in octonions**
   \[
   \exists x y z \in CD(3),\ (xy)z \neq x(yz).
   \]

## Proof Strategy Architecture

### Strategy A: Coordinate-explicit algebraic proof of `SU(2) → SO(3)` via conjugation
1. Define quaternions as `ℝ^4` with explicit multiplication; prove conjugation and norm identities by `ring`, `field_simp`, and multi-step `calc`.
2. Identify pure quaternions with `ℝ^3`, compute the `3×3` matrix of `v ↦ qvq⁻¹`, and prove orthogonality by direct expansion.
3. Show kernel `{±1}` by comparing coefficients of the action on basis vectors `i,j,k`; prove surjectivity by axis-angle reconstruction.

**Why promising:** Most robust in Lean because every step reduces to polynomial identities over `ℝ`. This avoids advanced Lie theory while still proving a genuinely deep theorem.

### Strategy B: Structural proof via normed algebra / inner-product preservation
1. Build quaternions as a star-algebra with multiplicative norm.
2. Show conjugation by a unit quaternion is a linear isometry on the pure subspace.
3. Upgrade “linear isometry + orientation preservation” to an element of `SO(3)`, then prove surjectivity from axis-angle decomposition.

**Why promising:** Conceptually cleaner and closer to mathematics/physics. Best if Mathlib’s linear isometry and orthogonal-group APIs cooperate.

### Strategy C: Quaternion algebra classification via norm forms
1. Define the reduced norm
   \[
   N(x_0+x_1i+x_2j+x_3k)=x_0^2-a x_1^2-b x_2^2+ab x_3^2.
   \]
2. Prove splitting is equivalent to isotropy of the norm form or existence of a nontrivial zero divisor.
3. Over `ℝ`, analyze sign patterns to distinguish definite vs indefinite forms.

**Why promising:** This converts a hard algebra-isomorphism problem into quadratic form analysis. It is the best route for the real classification theorem.
