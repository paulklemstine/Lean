## Research Brief: Coinductive Self-Similarity — Non-Well-Founded Proofs in Geometry

### Context
The original concept explored non-well-founded proofs (proofs that reference themselves) in geometry. The insight is sound: coinductive proof principles like Stream'.coinduction ARE self-referential, and geometric self-similarity IS a form of self-reference. The previous attempt captured these ideas but the formalization was incomplete.

### Why This Matters
This shows that non-well-founded proofs are not paradoxes — they are the NATURAL proof method for self-similar structures. Coinduction proves P(xs) by observing P(head(xs)) and assuming P(tail(xs)), which is exactly circular reasoning made rigorous. Geometric streams are the simplest non-trivial example where this circular structure resolves to a unique fixed point, both at the stream level (self-similarity) and at the limit level (contraction fixed points).
