**Mode:** prove

You are not being asked for a routine extension. You are being asked to decide whether the current differential-depth theory is genuinely optimal or hiding a stronger invariant. This is a structural question about symbolic differentiation, Hardy-level complexity, and the stability of asymptotic hierarchies under derivation. If the `+1` is sharp, we identify the exact obstruction. If it is not sharp, we collapse an apparent complexity jump and strengthen the whole theory of differential closure for PosEML expressions.

## Core Research Objective

Determine whether the theorem
\[
\forall e,\quad \mathrm{depth}(\mathrm{deriv}(e)) \le \mathrm{depth}(e)+1
\]
is **sharp** in an infinite-family sense, or whether it can be strengthened to
\[
\forall e,\quad \mathrm{depth}(\mathrm{deriv}(e)) \le \mathrm{depth}(e).
\]

This is not a cosmetic refinement. It decides whether symbolic differentiation is a genuinely complexity-raising operation in the PosEML grammar, or merely appears so because of unsimplified syntax.

## Precise Theorem Targets

You should aim to formalize at least one of the following two mutually illuminating outcomes, and ideally both a positive and negative theorem around them.

### Target Theorem A: Infinite sharp family
Prove there exists an infinite family of eventually positive expressions with exact `+1` depth jump:
\[
\exists F : \mathbb{N} \to \mathrm{PosEMLExpr},\;
(\forall n,\; \mathrm{EventuallyPositive}(F\,n)) \;\land\;
(\forall n,\; \mathrm{depth}(F\,n)=n) \;\land\;
(\forall n,\; \mathrm{depth}(\mathrm{deriv}(F\,n))=n+1).
\]

## Why This Would Be a Breakthrough

This problem sits at the fault line between **syntax** and **asymptotics**.

- If `depth (deriv e) ≤ depth e` always holds, then differentiation is a **Hardy-level preserving** operation, not merely a controlled perturbation. That would strengthen the current closure theory and suggest a much more rigid asymptotic semantics for PosEML expressions.
- If the `+1` is sharp, then the current theorem is optimal, but the real advance is to identify **why**: not just existence of a counterexample, but the exact combinatorial mechanism in the expression tree that forces level increase.
- In either case, you are resolving whether derivation acts like a **non-expansive operator** or a **controlled complexity amplifier** in the symbolic Hardy hierarchy.

This opens a new program: complexity theory of differential operators on transseries-like grammars.

## Proof Strategy Architecture

You must not rely on brute-force enumeration as the main proof. Use enumeration only as an exploratory tool guiding theorem discovery.

### Strategy A: Explicit recursive witness family
Most promising if sharpness is true.

1. Define a recursively nested family where multiplication and exponentiation interact so that differentiation introduces a new additive/product layer at top depth.
   - Candidate templates:
     - `F₀ = x` or `exp x`
     - `F_{n+1} = mul (exp (F_n)) (exp (F_n))`
     - or an asymmetric family where one branch’s derivative already saturates depth.
2. Prove by induction:
   - `depth (F_n) = n`
   - `EventuallyPositive (F_n)`
   - `depth (deriv (F_n)) = n+1`
3. The key technical step is a lower bound, not the upper bound. You will need lemmas showing that in the derivative of a product/exponential form, the resulting syntax tree really attains the claimed depth and cannot collapse under the current raw `deriv` definition.

Why promising: the catalog already controls the upper bound via `depth_deriv_le`; what is missing is an explicit lower-bound witness family.

---

### Strategy B: Exact recursion theorem for `depth (deriv e)`
Most promising if a classification theorem is attainable.

1. Prove precise formulas or inequalities for each constructor:
   - variable / constants
   - addition
   - multiplication
   - exponentiation
2. Introduce a syntactic invariant detecting when the product rule or chain rule forces a new outer layer.
3. Derive a theorem of the form:
   \[
   \mathrm{depth}(\mathrm{deriv}(e)) =
   \max(\text{local contributions from subterms})
   \]
   or at least an iff criterion for equality with `depth(e)+1`.

Why promising: this would subsume the existence question and produce a reusable calculus for future differential-complexity results.

---

### Strategy C: Normal-form comparison / semantic collapse
Most promising if the `+1` bound is an artifact of unsimplified syntax.

1. Define a simplification or normalization procedure for derivative expressions, especially for product-rule expansions.
2. Prove that normalized derivatives satisfy a stronger bound:
   ```lean
   theorem depth_normDeriv_le (e : PosEMLExpr) :
     depth (normDeriv e) ≤ depth e
   ```
3. Compare raw `deriv` and normalized derivative semantically or syntactically.

Why promising: even if raw syntax admits `+1`, a normalized calculus might not. This would connect symbolic complexity with compiler-style optimization and could be more important than the original question.
