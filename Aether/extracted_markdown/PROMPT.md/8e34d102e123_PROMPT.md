## Title
**Mod-p Spectral Fingerprints and Expansion Profiles of Arithmetic Simplicial Complexes**

## Core Vision

Turn the vague principle “finite-field topology remembers real expansion” into a precise, formally verified bridge between **persistent homology over `𝔽_p`**, **combinatorial Laplacians**, and **spectral expansion**. The breakthrough is not merely to correlate barcode statistics with expansion numerically, but to isolate a mathematically rigid notion of a **mod-p spectral fingerprint** and prove theorems showing that, under explicit hypotheses, these fingerprints constrain — and in structured cases determine — real spectral gap data.

This opens a new field: **arithmetic-topological spectral inference**. The point is radical: estimate analytic expansion and mixing using only finite-field linear algebra and persistence-style invariants. If successful, this creates a new computational paradigm for high-dimensional expanders, arithmetic manifolds, coding theory, and even quantum LDPC constructions, where real spectral data are hard but mod-p algebra is cheap.

## Precise Theorem Statements to Target

You need at least 3 substantial theorems. Here is a coherent package.

### Theorem 1: Monotonicity of persistent kernel dimensions along Laplacian thresholds
This is the foundational persistence theorem.

Let `L : V →ₗ[K] V` be a finite-dimensional endomorphism over a field `K`, and let `F_t` be a monotone family of `L`-stable subspaces induced by a threshold filtration. Then the persistent nullity profile is monotone under inclusion, and rank-nullity identities force a barcode-counting conservation law.

A Lean-shaped target could be:

```lean
theorem persistentNullity_monotone
  {K : Type*} [Field K]
  {V : Type*} [AddCommGroup V] [Module K V] [FiniteDimensional K V]
  (F : ℕ → Submodule K V)
  (hmono : Monotone F)
  (L : Module.End K V) :
  Monotone (fun n => FiniteDimensional.finrank K ((F n).comap L)) 
```

This exact signature may need adjustment depending on whether you use `comap`, `ker`, or restricted maps. The mathematical point is to prove a genuine persistence monotonicity statement via submodule arguments, dimension inequalities, and `calc`.

**Why it matters:** this gives a rigorous barcode surrogate inside Lean, even before full persistent homology infrastructure is built.

---

### Theorem 2: Rank-jump control implies spectral-gap lower bound surrogate
Prove that if the mod-p Laplacian filtration exhibits a uniform gap in kernel growth / rank drop across thresholds, then the corresponding real Laplacian cannot have too many near-zero modes. This is the bridge theorem.

A mathematically precise version:

For a finite simplicial complex with integer boundary matrices `∂k`, let `Lk = ∂k+1 ∂k+1ᵀ + ∂kᵀ ∂k`. Suppose that for all primes `p` in a set `P`, the reduction mod `p` satisfies a threshold-rank separation condition:
\[
\dim_{\mathbf F_p}\ker(L_{k,p}^{(\le t_1)}) - \dim_{\mathbf F_p}\ker(L_{k,p}^{(\le t_0)}) \le B
\]
uniformly. Then the multiplicity of real eigenvalues of `Lk` in `[0,\varepsilon]` is bounded above by an explicit function of `B`, the operator norm, and coefficient-height data.

This may be formalized first in matrix language rather than full simplicial generality:

```lean
theorem smallEigenvalueMultiplicity_bound_of_modp_rankControl
  {n : ℕ}
  (A : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℤ)
  (hSymm : A.IsSymm)
  (B : ℕ)
  (hmodp : ∀ p : ℕ, Nat.Prime p → 
    rankJumpBound A p B) :
  ∃ C : ℝ, smallEigenvalueMultiplicityBound A B C
```

You will likely need to define `rankJumpBound` and `smallEigenvalueMultiplicityBound` as new predicates. The theorem can initially be an abstract transfer principle: if enough reductions mod `p` constrain nullity patterns, then characteristic polynomial coefficients — hence low-lying spectrum — are constrained.

**Why it matters:** this is the first rigorous mechanism by which finite-field data controls real spectral information.

### Theorem 3: Fingerprint equality forces equality of low-degree characteristic data
This is the algebraic heart.

For integer matrices `A, B`, if for sufficiently many small primes the nullity/rank profiles of reductions of polynomial expressions in `A` and `B` agree, then the first several coefficients of their characteristic polynomials agree. From this deduce equality or closeness of low-order spectral moments.

Lean target:

```lean
theorem fingerprint_eq_imp_charpoly_coeff_eq
  {n m : ℕ}
  (A B : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℤ)
  (hfp : PrimeFingerprintEqUpTo A B m) :
  ∀ k ≤ m, charpolyCoeff A k = charpolyCoeff B k
```

Again, `PrimeFingerprintEqUpTo` and `charpolyCoeff` may be your own wrappers around existing matrix/charpoly notions.

A weaker but more Lean-realistic version is acceptable:
- equality of determinants modulo many primes implies equality over `ℤ`,
- equality of traces of powers modulo many primes implies equality over `ℤ`,
- Newton identities then transfer to characteristic coefficients.

**Why it matters:** this theorem explains *why* prime fingerprints are not just heuristic features: they encode rigid arithmetic information about the operator.

---

### Theorem 4: Cross-domain theorem — spectral moments from persistence-type rank data
Connect topology to probability / dynamics.

For a finite complex, prove that suitable averaged rank statistics of Laplacian threshold maps control return probabilities of the simplicial random walk, or heat trace coefficients.

Lean-style target:

```lean
theorem fingerprint_controls_heat_trace_surrogate
  {n : ℕ}
  (A : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℤ)
  (hpos : SymmetricPosSemidef A) :
  ∃ f : ℕ → ℚ,
    FingerprintDeterminesMoments A f ∧
    ∀ k, heatTraceMomentSurrogate A k = f k
```

Even if you cannot formalize full heat kernels, formalize a discrete surrogate:
\[
\mathrm{tr}(A^k)
\]
is determined or bounded by fingerprint data. This is already a major cross-domain bridge: **topological persistence ↔ spectral statistics ↔ random walks**.

**Why it matters:** this connects expander theory to statistical mechanics and diffusion, not merely algebraic topology.

## Proof Strategy Architecture

### Strategy A: Arithmetic linear algebra via reductions mod p
**Most promising for the core transfer theorems.**

1. Work with integer matrices representing combinatorial Laplacians or boundary-derived operators.
2. Show that nullity/rank data modulo many primes constrains minors, determinants, and traces of powers modulo many primes.
3. Use the standard arithmetic principle: if an integer is divisible by enough primes exceeding its coefficient height bound, it must vanish.
4. Deduce equality of low-degree characteristic polynomial data, then infer constraints on low-lying real spectrum.

**Why this is strongest:** it converts a topological-statistical conjecture into a rigid arithmetic recovery theorem. Lean can handle integer matrices, determinants, traces, modular reductions, and dimension/rank arguments more readily than analytic spectral asymptotics.

---

### Strategy B: Persistence as submodule calculus
**Best for the foundational monotonicity/barcode theorems.**

1. Define threshold filtrations as monotone families of submodules or kernels of restricted maps.
2. Prove dimension monotonicity using submodule inclusion and `FiniteDimensional.finrank_mono`.
3. Derive rank-nullity conservation laws and persistence inequalities via `calc` chains.
4. Package these into a reusable barcode surrogate API.

**Why it matters:** this gives a formal persistent-homology language without needing the full generality of persistence modules from the outset.

---

### Strategy C: Spectral moments and random walks
**Best for cross-domain impact.**

1. Interpret traces of powers of Laplacians/adjacency operators as counts of closed walks or diffusion moments.
2. Show fingerprint data determines or bounds these moments through mod-p rank statistics and Newton identities.
3. Translate to mixing surrogates, heat trace coefficients, or return probabilities.

**Why this is field-opening:** it links finite-field topology to probability, statistical physics, and high-dimensional Markov dynamics.

## Falsifiable Conjecture with Computational Test

State this sharply.

### Conjecture: Prime Fingerprint Determinacy
There exists `C > 0`, an integer `k ≥ 1`, and a family of bounded-degree arithmetic simplicial complexes `X_N` such that if
\[
\mathcal F_{X_N}(p,t)
\]
denotes the `k`-dimensional mod-`p` Laplacian barcode/rank fingerprint, then the collection
\[
\{\mathcal F_{X_N}(p,t) : p \le C \log |X_N|,\ t \in T_N\}
\]
determines the real `k`-Laplacian spectral gap `λ_1^{(k)}(X_N)` up to `o(1)` as `N → ∞`.

### Testable prediction
For explicit families `X_N`, train a predictor from prime fingerprints to real spectral gap. The conjecture is supported if prediction error tends to zero with `N`; it is refuted if one can exhibit two asymptotically distinct spectral-gap families with asymptotically indistinguishable prime fingerprints up to `C log |X_N|`.

Also formulate a sharper possible disproof target:
- construct non-isospectral complexes with identical low-prime fingerprints.

That counterexample search is scientifically valuable even if the main conjecture fails.
