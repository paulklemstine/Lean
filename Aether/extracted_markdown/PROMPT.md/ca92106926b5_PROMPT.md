## Core New Definitions You Must Introduce

At least one genuinely new concept is mandatory. Introduce all of the following if absent from the catalog:

1. **Weighted stereographic distance** on \(\mathbb{R}^n\) induced by inverse stereographic projection:
   \[
   d_{\mathrm{st}}(x,y) := \operatorname{dist}_{S^n}\big(\sigma^{-1}(x),\sigma^{-1}(y)\big),
   \]
   where \(\sigma^{-1} : \mathbb{R}^n \to S^n \setminus \{N\}\).

2. A computable surrogate:
   \[
   d_w(x,y) := 2 \arctan\!\left(\frac{\|x-y\|}{\sqrt{(1+\|x\|^2)(1+\|y\|^2)}}\right)
   \]
   or an equivalent formally convenient expression proven equal to spherical geodesic distance under stereographic inversion.

3. **Stereographic Čech filtration** of a finite point cloud \(X \subset S^n \setminus \{N\}\): the filtration obtained by projecting \(X\) to \(\mathbb{R}^n\) and using \(d_{\mathrm{st}}\) or the equivalent closed-form weighted metric.

4. **North-pole exclusion radius / tame hemisphere condition**: a quantitative hypothesis ensuring the point cloud stays in a compact spherical chart away from the stereographic singularity. This will be essential for algorithmic bounds and stability constants.

These definitions are not cosmetic: they are the language needed to turn conformal geometry into persistent topology.

## Proof Architecture: Multiple Strategies

You must include 2–3 plausible proof routes in comments or notes and pursue the most promising one.

### Strategy A: Direct metric identity → simplex predicate equivalence → functorial persistence
1. Prove the closed-form distance identity for inverse stereographic projection by explicit coordinate computation on the sphere.
2. Show that for every finite subset \(S \subseteq X\), the nonempty intersection of spherical \(\varepsilon\)-balls around \(S\) is equivalent under projection to the nonempty intersection of weighted \(\varepsilon\)-balls around \(\sigma(S)\).
3. Deduce equality of simplex predicates, then simplicial complex isomorphism, then filtered equivalence, then homology/persistence equivalence.

**Why promising:** This is the cleanest path in Lean because each step reduces to concrete extensional equality and transport along a vertex bijection.

---

### Strategy B: Use conformal / Möbius geometry and geodesic-ball correspondence
1. Prove that stereographic projection sends spherical circles/balls not passing through the north pole to Euclidean spheres/balls with explicit radius distortion.
2. Characterize Čech simplices by Helly-type ball intersection conditions.
3. Infer filtration equivalence from exact ball-image formulas rather than pairwise distances.

**Why promising:** This may better align with geometric intuition and could generalize to other conformal models.  
**Risk:** Harder in Lean because ball-image formulas require more geometric infrastructure.

---

### Strategy C: Prove an interleaving theorem first, then sharpen to equality
1. Establish bi-Lipschitz comparison between \(d_{\mathrm{st}}\) and Euclidean distance on bounded charts.
2. Obtain persistence interleaving/stability bounds.
3. Upgrade from interleaving to equality by proving the exact metric identity.

**Why promising:** Gives partial results early and yields an algorithmic theorem even if exact equivalence is delayed.  
**Most promising overall:** **Strategy A**, because the exact distance formula is the master key; once obtained, the rest becomes categorical transport.

## Important Correction to the Original Conjecture

Do **not** blindly assume “persistence diagrams are invariant under conformal transformations.” That statement is false in general if one changes the metric and keeps only angle information. Persistence depends on the filtration metric, not merely conformal class.

The correct theorem is subtler and more powerful:

- persistence is preserved under **exact metric transport**;
- stereographic projection is not an isometry from spherical geodesic distance to Euclidean distance;
- but it does become an isometric identification once Euclidean coordinates are equipped with the transported metric \(d_{\mathrm{st}}\), for which you must prove an explicit formula.

This correction is scientifically crucial. It transforms a potentially false slogan into a rigorous new theory.
