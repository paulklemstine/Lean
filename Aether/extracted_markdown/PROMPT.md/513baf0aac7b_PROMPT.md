## Required New Definitions

You must introduce at least one genuinely new concept not already present in the catalog. The most promising candidates are:

### 1. Parallel β-reduction
A Tait–Martin-Löf style parallel reduction relation:
```lean
inductive ParBeta : Lam → Lam → Prop
| var  : ...
| app  : ...
| lam  : ...
| beta : ...
```
This is the most canonical path to a machine-checkable Church-Rosser proof.

### 2. Depth-stable joinability
A semantic budgeting notion that isolates the combinatorial heart of the strong bisimulation proof:
```lean
def JoinableWithin (k : Nat) (t u : Lam) : Prop :=
  ∃ v, ReducesWithin k t v ∧ ReducesWithin k u v
```
This lets you track not merely confluence, but confluence with explicit bounded reduction resources.

### 3. Strong bisimulation witness relation generated by common reducts
```lean
def CRWitnessRel (d' : Nat) : Lam → Lam → Prop :=
  fun t u => ∃ v, ReducesWithin d' t v ∧ ReducesWithin d' u v
```
Then show this relation is a strong bisimulation on the bounded transition graph, once `d'` is chosen large enough.

These are not cosmetic definitions. They are the organizing abstractions that make the proof modular and reusable.

## Why this is a breakthrough

Weak bisimulation says β-equivalent terms eventually simulate each other modulo path compression. Strong bisimulation says something much sharper: **every immediate β-move from one term has a matching immediate semantic response from the other, after embedding both into a sufficiently rich bounded transition universe**.

This would yield a formal mechanism for transporting **proof-theoretic confluence** into **coalgebraic behavioral equivalence**. In effect:

- Church-Rosser becomes a bisimulation generator.
- β-equivalence classes acquire a modal semantics.
- Bounded FTS models become canonical finite observational windows into infinitary rewriting behavior.

This is the kind of result that makes different fields recognize the same object from different angles.

## Proof Architecture: 3 viable strategies

## Strategy A: Parallel reduction → diamond → confluence → bisimulation witness
**Most promising.**

### Step 1
Define `ParBeta` and prove substitution compatibility and complete development lemmas.

### Step 2
Prove `parBeta_diamond` by induction on one derivation and structural inversion on the other. This is where you will use `induction`, `rcases`, and multi-step `calc` heavily.

### Step 3
Use the diamond property to derive Church-Rosser, then define a witness relation by “having a common reduct within budget”, and prove this relation is a strong bisimulation on `toFTS d'`.

**Why this is best:**  
It cleanly separates proof theory from semantics. The parallel-reduction layer gives canonical joins, which are ideal witnesses for bisimulation matching.

---

## Strategy B: Takahashi’s complete developments / star translation
Potentially elegant, but more delicate to mechanize.

### Step 1
Define Takahashi’s star translation `t ↦ t⋆` or complete development.

### Step 2
Show every one-step β-reduct reduces to the same complete development:
```lean
BetaStep t u → MultiBeta u (star t)
```

### Step 3
Derive Church-Rosser by funneling all β-reducts of a term into a canonical target, then lift this canonical target into a strong bisimulation witness relation.

**Why it is interesting:**  
This yields a more canonical proof object and may give better computational extraction.  
**Why it is harder:**  
Substitution bookkeeping is often more painful in Lean than the parallel reduction route.

---

## Strategy C: Direct induction on β-equivalence derivation plus local peak completion
More semantic, less modular.

### Step 1
Induct on the derivation of `BetaEq t u`.

### Step 2
For each generating symmetry/transitivity/step case, prove a budget extension lemma ensuring one-step transitions can be matched after enlarging depth.

### Step 3
Assemble the local matching lemmas into a strong bisimulation on bounded FTS.

**Why it may work:**  
It interacts directly with your target theorem.  
**Why it is less promising:**  
You risk reproving fragments of Church-Rosser ad hoc, and transitivity cases can become combinatorially ugly.

## Recommended proof order

1. Define `ParBeta`.
2. Prove reflexivity and embedding of `BetaStep` into `ParBeta`.
3. Prove substitution compatibility for `ParBeta`.
4. Prove `parBeta_diamond`.
5. Derive `ChurchRosserProp`.
6. Define `JoinableWithin` or `CRWitnessRel`.
7. Prove monotonicity in the depth parameter:
   ```lean
   theorem JoinableWithin.mono ...
   theorem CRWitnessRel_mono ...
   ```
8. Prove one-step matching lemmas inside `toFTS d'`.
9. Conclude `StrongBisimilar`.
10. Package everything into the final theorem.
