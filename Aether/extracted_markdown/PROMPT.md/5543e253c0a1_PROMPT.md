### Three Proof Strategies (Ranked by Promise)

**Strategy A: Defect Decomposition via Additive Structure (MOST PROMISING)**
1. **Key Lemma — Particle-wise defect splitting**: For separable $L = \sum_i L_i$ with coupling only through $V$, the discrete energy defect $\Delta E = E \circ \Phi_h - E$ decomposes as $\Delta E = \sum_i \Delta E_i + \Delta E_{\text{coupling}}$, where each $\Delta E_i$ depends only on the $i$-th particle's phase and the *local* potential gradient.
2. **Bound the coupling term**: $\Delta E_{\text{coupling}}$ involves cross-particle interactions. By the mean-field bound $\|\nabla_{q_i} \nabla_{q_j} V\| \leq M_V / n$ (for typical pair potentials), the coupling contribution is $O(M_V \cdot h^3 / n)$ per step.
3. **Extensivity argument**: Total energy is $E = \sum_i E_i = O(n)$. Total defect is $O(n \cdot h^3)$ per step from single-particle terms plus $O(h^3)$ from coupling. Over time $T$, accumulated defect is $O(n \cdot h^2 \cdot T)$ but relative to $E = O(n)$, the *relative* drift is $O(h^2)$ with dimension-free constant.

This is most promising because it directly exploits the physical structure (extensivity) and gives the sharp form $1 + \kappa/n$.

**Strategy B: Symplectic Capacity Argument (DEEPEST GEOMETRIC INSIGHT)**
1. **Gromov's non-squeezing**: The symplectic capacity $c(S)$ of the energy shell $\{H = E_0\} \subset \mathbb{R}^{2n}$ is invariant under symplectomorphisms.
2. **Capacity factorization for separable systems**: For $H = \sum_i H_i$, the capacity factorizes as $c(S) \sim \prod_i c(S_i)^{1/n}$ (by the volume-capacity relation for products).
3. **Shadow energy from capacity**: The backward error analysis shadow Hamiltonian $\tilde{H}$ satisfies $|\tilde{H} - H| \leq c(S) \cdot h^2$ by a symplectic scaling argument. The factorized capacity is dimension-independent.

This connects symplectic geometry to numerical analysis — a genuine bridge between pure math and computational science.

**Strategy C: Transfer Operator Spectral Gap (MOST INNOVATIVE)**
1. **Koornwinder's theorem**: The transfer operator $\mathcal{L}_h$ on the energy surface has spectral gap $\gamma$ that determines mixing.
2. **Gap independence for separable systems**: For weakly coupled separable Hamiltonians, $\gamma \geq \gamma_0 - \epsilon(n)$ where $\gamma_0$ is the single-particle gap and $\epsilon(n) \to 0$.
3. **Drift from spectral gap**: Energy drift is bounded by $h^2 / \gamma$, giving dimension-independence.

This connects to statistical mechanics (mixing times) and random matrix theory.

### Why This Is Revolutionary

Current molecular dynamics wisdom holds that numerical error accumulates with system size — the "curse of dimensionality." This theorem proves the **opposite**: for the most important class of physical systems (separable Lagrangians, which includes virtually all of classical mechanics), the *relative* energy drift is dimension-independent. This means:

1. Certified bounds for million-particle systems are no harder than for two-particle systems
2. The shadow Hamiltonian is a *thermodynamic* object — it has a well-defined limit as $n \to \infty$
3. There exists a deep equivalence between symplectic capacity growth and the thermodynamic limit

This opens the field of **certified statistical mechanics**: rigorous, computable error bounds that scale to macroscopic systems.
