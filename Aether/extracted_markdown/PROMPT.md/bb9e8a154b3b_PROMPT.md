Prove a genuinely new theorem that turns the Berggren semigroup from a Diophantine object into a certified pseudorandomness mechanism. ## Primary Theorem Target: Berggren product-test bias via spectral decay

### Mathematical statement

Let `μ` be the uniform step distribution on the Berggren generators. Let `T` be the corresponding averaging operator on observables of Berggren states. Let degree-`k` product tests be functions lying in the span of `k`-fold coordinate characters/observables. Prove that if each degree-`k` eigenspace of `T` has operator norm at most `(1/2)^k`, then every centered degree-`k` product test has exponentially decaying bias under `n` Berggren steps.

The theorem should be formalized in a finite-dimensional surrogate model first: a finite state space carrying a Berggren transition operator and a graded family of test subspaces. Once that is complete, specialize to the Berggren walk encoding already present in the library.

### Proposed Lean 4 type signature

```lean
/--
If a Markov averaging operator preserves a graded family of test spaces and acts
on degree-`k` tests with operator norm at most `ρ^k`, then the bias of any
centered degree-`k` test after `n` steps decays by `(ρ^k)^n`.
This is the abstract spectral-to-fooling transfer theorem.
-/
theorem bias_bound_of_spectral_decay
    {α : Type*} [Fintype α] [DecidableEq α]
    (T : (α → ℝ) →ₗ[ℝ] (α → ℝ))
    (degSpace : ℕ → Submodule ℝ (α → ℝ))
    (ρ : ℝ)
    (hpres : ∀ k, Submodule.map T (degSpace k) ≤ degSpace k)
    (hmean_zero_pres :
      ∀ f, (∑ x, (T f) x) = ∑ x, f x)
    (hnorm :
      ∀ k, ∀ f ∈ degSpace k,
        (∑ x, f x = 0) →
        ‖T f‖ ≤ (ρ ^ k) * ‖f‖)
    (hρ : 0 ≤ ρ ∧ ρ ≤ 1) :
    ∀ k n, ∀ f ∈ degSpace k,
      (∑ x, f x = 0) →
      ‖(T^[n]) f‖ ≤ ((ρ ^ k) ^ n) * ‖f‖
```

Then specialize to the target spectral constant `ρ = 1/2`:

```lean
/--
Degree-`k` Berggren product tests are fooled with bias decaying like `(1/2)^(k n)`.
-/
theorem berggren_product_test_bias_bound
    (k n : ℕ)
    (f : BerggrenState → ℝ)
    (hf_deg : f ∈ berggrenDegreeSpace k)
    (hf_mean_zero : ∑ x, f x = 0) :
    ‖(berggrenAveragingOp^[n]) f‖ ≤ ((1 / 2 : ℝ) ^ (k * n)) * ‖f‖
```

If the exact existing Berggren state type is not yet available in a finite/Fintype form, first build the abstract theorem and then a restricted finite-window theorem on truncated Berggren words:

```lean
theorem berggren_word_prefix_bias_bound
    (L k n : ℕ)
    (f : BerggrenWordTrunc L → ℝ)
    (hf_deg : f ∈ berggrenPrefixDegreeSpace L k)
    (hf_mean_zero : ∑ x, f x = 0) :
    ‖(berggrenPrefixAveragingOp L^[n]) f‖ ≤ ((1 / 2 : ℝ) ^ (k * n)) * ‖f‖
```

This truncated theorem is already nontrivial and can serve as the formal launchpad.

## Proof strategy architecture

You should pursue at least two proof paths in parallel.

### Strategy A: Abstract operator-theoretic transfer theorem first
Most promising for Lean.

1. Define a general finite-dimensional averaging operator `T` on functions over a finite state space.
2. Define a graded family `degSpace k` of structured observables.
3. Prove the abstract iteration lemma:
   if `‖T f‖ ≤ ρ^k ‖f‖` on centered degree-`k` observables, then
   `‖T^[n] f‖ ≤ (ρ^k)^n ‖f‖`.
4. Instantiate with the Berggren walk on a truncated orbit graph or word-prefix graph.

Why promising:
- Lean likes finite-dimensional linear algebra.
- It separates the conceptual theorem from the arithmetic realization.
- Even if the full Berggren spectral decomposition is difficult, the abstract transfer theorem is already novel and reusable.

### Strategy B: Direct Fourier/product-character diagonalization on prefix states
Most exciting mathematically.

1. Encode truncated Berggren words as a finite product-type space if possible, e.g. words of length `L` in generators.
2. Define product tests as depending on at most `k` coordinates/letters or as tensor products of local observables.
3. Show the averaging operator acts diagonally or upper-triangularly on the corresponding Fourier/Walsh basis.
4. Extract eigenvalues explicitly, aiming for the `(1/2)^k` bound.

Why powerful:
- This gives the cleanest computational-complexity interpretation.
- It makes the `(1/2)^k` constant feel inevitable rather than assumed.
- It resembles classical small-bias/noise-operator theory, but in an arithmetic semigroup setting.

Potential obstacle:
- The Berggren walk may not be product-like on triple coordinates, so work on word space first and then transport to arithmetic states.

### Strategy C: Tensor-power / representation-theoretic contraction
Most visionary.

1. Interpret degree-`k` observables as lying in a `k`-fold tensor representation of the walk operator.
2. Prove the operator norm on the nontrivial tensor component is at most `(1/2)^k`.
3. Deduce bias decay by representation-theoretic projection.

Why this matters:
- This is the route to general thin semigroups.
- It may connect with expansion, transfer operators, and automorphic spectral methods.
- If successful, it gives a conceptual explanation for why low-degree arithmetic observables decay exponentially.

This is likely harder to formalize first, but it is the right long-term theorem architecture.
