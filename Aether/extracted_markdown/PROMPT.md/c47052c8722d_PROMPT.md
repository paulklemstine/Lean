## Synthesis

This cycle reconstructed, on a fully self-contained Mathlib-only foundation, the rank of
apparition `z(m) = min { k > 0 : m ∣ F k }` and pinned down its behaviour as a map between
two divisibility lattices: the lattice of *moduli* and the lattice of *indices*. The decisive
tool is the **law of apparition** `m ∣ F n ↔ z(m) ∣ n` (`fib_dvd_iff_z_dvd`), which we derive
unconditionally for every `m ≥ 1` from a from-scratch pigeonhole argument on the Fibonacci
shift map over `ZMod m` (the abstract engine behind the Pisano period, which Mathlib lacks).

Once the law of apparition is in hand, every lattice statement about `z` collapses to an
elementary divisibility statement about indices. We exploited this to prove:

- **`z_lcm`** — the unrestricted join law `z(lcm a b) = lcm(z a, z b)`, removing the
  coprimality hypothesis from the catalog's `fibEntry_mul_coprime`;
- **`z_monotone`** — `a ∣ b → z a ∣ z b`, the order-morphism property;
- **`z_gcd_dvd`** with **`z_gcd_not_exact`** — the meet (gcd) is only a *sub*-morphism, with
  `(a,b) = (4,6)` the minimal witness of strictness; and
- **`z_fib_self`** — self-indexing: `z(F n) = n` for `n ≥ 3`, the sharp converse of
  `Nat.fib_dvd`.

The honest one-line summary: **`z` is a join-morphism and a meet-sub-morphism of divisibility
lattices, fixing the Fibonacci sub-monoid by self-indexing.**

## Research directions

### 1. The exact gcd-defect is a 2-adic phenomenon

The meet bound `z(gcd a b) ∣ gcd(z a, z b)` is strict, but our `(4,6)` witness suggests the
defect is highly structured rather than random. The conjecture is that the quotient
`gcd(z a, z b) / z(gcd a b)` is always a power of `2`, controlled entirely by the carry
behaviour of the Fibonacci entry points at the prime `2` (where `z(2) = 3`, `z(4) = 6`,
`z(8) = 6`, `z(2^k)` stabilising in a 2-adically predictable way). **The key insight is** that
the only place the lattice morphism can leak is where the entry-point map `z` fails to be
injective on a `p`-adic tower, and for Fibonacci the unique such anomaly is the doubling
`z(F_6 = 8) = z(F_4·2)` collision at `p = 2`. **Why now?** We already have `z_lcm` (the join is
exact) and `z_gcd_dvd` (the meet is sub-exact), so the entire defect is isolated into a single
computable ratio; a finite `decide`-style verification over residues mod small powers of `2`,
combined with `z_lcm`, would settle the conjecture or produce a counterexample at the first
odd prime power where it fails. This is falsifiable by exhibiting any pair `(a,b)` whose defect
ratio has an odd prime factor.

### 2. Self-indexing extends to a graded fixed-point spectrum

`z_fib_self` says the Fibonacci numbers `F n` (`n ≥ 3`) are exactly the moduli whose
apparition equals their own index. Conjecture: the *fixed points* of the composite
`n ↦ z(F n)` and, more ambitiously, the solutions of `z(m) = m` form a thin, fully classified
set — precisely `m ∈ {1, 5}` (since `z(1)=1`, `z(5)=5`, `F_5 = 5`), and no others. **The key
insight is** that `z(m) = m` forces `m ∣ F m`, and by `z_fib_self` and strict growth `F m ≫ m`
for `m ≥ 6`, the modulus must be a Fibonacci number equal to its own index, which only `1` and
`5` satisfy. **Why now?** `z_fib_self` gives the converse direction `F n ∣ F k ↔ n ∣ k`
needed to turn `z(m)=m` into a Fibonacci-membership constraint; the remaining step is a clean
growth estimate already available via `Nat.fib_lt_fib`. Falsifiable by any `m ≥ 6` with
`z(m) = m`.

### 3. The join law upgrades to a full prime-power product formula

Combining `z_lcm` with the multiplicativity over coprime parts, conjecture an explicit
closed form: `z(m) = lcm_{p^e ∥ m} z(p^e)`, and further `z(p^e) = p^{max(0, e - e_0(p))}·z(p)`
where `e_0(p)` is the Wall–Sun–Sun threshold (`e_0(p) = 1` for all known primes; a
Wall–Sun–Sun prime would have `e_0(p) ≥ 2`). **The key insight is** that `z_lcm` already
reduces *all* entry-point computation to prime powers, so the only unknown left is the
single-prime tower law `z(p^{e+1}) = p · z(p^e)`, which is equivalent to the
non-Wall–Sun–Sun condition `p^2 ∤ F_{z(p)}`. **Why now?** With `z_lcm` proved, the prime-power
reduction is no longer conjectural; formalizing the tower step gives, for free, a decision
procedure for `z(m)` for every `m` not divisible by a Wall–Sun–Sun prime, linking this
elementary lattice theory to a famous open problem. Falsifiable: a prime `p` with
`z(p^2) = z(p)` would refute the tower law (and be a Wall–Sun–Sun prime).

### 4. The apparition lattice morphism transfers to every strong divisibility sequence

The proofs of `z_lcm`, `z_monotone`, `z_gcd_dvd` used only `Nat.fib_gcd` (strong
divisibility) and the existence of an entry point. Conjecture: for *any* strong divisibility
sequence `u` with `u(0) = 0`, `u(1) = 1`, and bounded residues (so entry points exist), the
entry map `z_u` is a join-morphism and meet-sub-morphism, and the self-indexing
`z_u(u(n)) = n` holds beyond a sequence-dependent threshold. **The key insight is** that none
of the four lattice theorems touch the *value* `F n` — only the gcd identity and minimality —
so the entire package is a theorem about the abstract renormalization identity
`gcd(u m, u n) = u(gcd m n)`, not about Fibonacci. **Why now?** The catalog already isolates
this abstraction (`StrongDivSeq` in `FibonacciEntryPointInvariant.lean`); porting `z_lcm` /
`z_fib_self` into that abstract setting would immediately instantiate to Mersenne/repunit
sequences `a^n - 1` and elliptic divisibility sequences. Falsifiable by any strong divisibility
sequence whose entry map fails the join law.

### 5. Apparition controls a sharp Pisano-period divisibility tower

The pigeonhole behind `fib_apparition_exists` produces, but does not name, the Pisano period
`π(m)`. Conjecture the clean tower relation `z(m) ∣ π(m) ∣ z(m) · ord_m(±1)` together with
`z(m) = π(m)` exactly when the Fibonacci–Lucas unit `F_{z(m)+1}` is `≡ 1 (mod m)` (the
"orientation-preserving" case). **The key insight is** that the same shift-map orbit that
yields `z` as the *first return to the F-axis* yields `π` as the *first return to the identity
matrix*, so `π/z` is exactly the multiplicative order of the diagonal twist `F_{z+1}` in
`(ZMod m)ˣ` — a quantity already expressible with Mathlib's `orderOf`. **Why now?** Our
`fibStep` permutation and `fibStep_iterate` already encode the orbit dynamics formally; building
`π(m)` as `orderOf (fibStep m raised to ...)` and proving the divisibility tower would give
Mathlib its first Pisano-period theory, with `z` as the cornerstone. Falsifiable by any `m`
where `z(m) ∤ π(m)` or `π(m) / z(m)` exceeds the order of the orientation unit.
