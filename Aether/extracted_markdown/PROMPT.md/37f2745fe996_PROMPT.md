## Core Mathematical Objective

Let \(G\) be a finite group and let \(\mathcal S(G)\) denote a finite family of subgroups of \(G\) (for the formalization, you may begin with `Finset (Subgroup G)` or a finite indexed family of subgroups). Let each subgroup \(H \in \mathcal S(G)\) be included independently with Bernoulli parameter \(p\), producing a random ensemble \(\omega : \mathcal S(G) \to \{0,1\}\). Define the **random subgroup pressure**
\[
\Pi_G(\omega) := \sum_{\substack{H,K \in \mathcal S(G) \\ \omega(H)=1,\ \omega(K)=1}} w_G(H,K),
\]
where \(w_G(H,K)\) is the pair-weight coming from the pressure formalism already present in the catalog, or a formally introduced proxy weight satisfying the same summability and positivity properties.

Your first breakthrough target is not just “some concentration bound,” but a theorem of the following shape:

### Theorem A — Abstract bounded-difference concentration for subgroup pressure
Let \(G\) be finite, let \(S : Finset (Subgroup G)\), and let \(w : Subgroup G → Subgroup G → ℝ\). Assume
\[
\forall H \in S,\quad \sum_{K \in S} |w(H,K)| + \sum_{K \in S} |w(K,H)| \le L.
\]
Then for the Bernoulli random ensemble on \(S\), the pressure functional
\[
\Pi(\omega)=\sum_{H \in S}\sum_{K \in S} \omega(H)\omega(K)\,w(H,K)
\]
satisfies a subgaussian concentration inequality with variance proxy \( \sum_{H\in S} c_H^2 \), where each coordinate influence \(c_H\) is bounded by \(L\). In particular,
\[
\Pr\bigl(|\Pi-\mathbb E[\Pi]| \ge t\bigr)
\le 2\exp\!\left(-\frac{2t^2}{\sum_{H\in S} c_H^2}\right).
\]

This is the formal core: once you have this, every algebraic estimate on row-sums of the pair-weight immediately yields a concentration theorem.

### Theorem B — Symmetric-group pressure concentration under inverse-index decay
Assume \(G = S_n\), and assume the pair-weight satisfies the algebraic decay bound
\[
|w_{S_n}(H,K)| \le \frac{A}{[S_n:H]^2 [S_n:K]^2}
\]
for all \(H,K\) in the chosen subgroup family \(S_n^\ast\). Then there is a constant \(C(A)\) such that
\[
\Pr\bigl(|\Pi_{S_n}-\mathbb E[\Pi_{S_n}]| \ge t\bigr)
\le 2\exp\!\left(-\frac{t^2}{C(A)\,\Sigma_n}\right),
\]
where
\[
\Sigma_n := \sum_{H \in S_n^\ast} [S_n:H]^{-4}.
\]
Your strategic objective is then to prove, for a natural family \(S_n^\ast\), that \(\Sigma_n = O(1/n)\) or better, yielding
\[
\Pr\bigl(|\Pi_{S_n}-\mathbb E[\Pi_{S_n}]| \ge t\bigr)
\le 2\exp\!\left(-\frac{t^2 n}{C}\right).
\]

This is the precise self-averaging statement. If the full \(O(1/n)\) asymptotic for all subgroups is too ambitious in one cycle, prove it first for a **structured ensemble** (e.g. point stabilizers, Young subgroups of bounded shape complexity, or maximal subgroups satisfying explicit index lower bounds), and state the full conjecture.

### Theorem C — Variance bound and thermodynamic deterministic limit
Under the same hypotheses as Theorem B,
\[
\operatorname{Var}(\Pi_{S_n}) \le C(A)\,\Sigma_n.
\]
Hence if \(\Sigma_n \to 0\), then
\[
\Pi_{S_n} - \mathbb E[\Pi_{S_n}] \to 0
\]
in \(L^2\), hence in probability. This is the formal thermodynamic-limit theorem: pressure becomes asymptotically deterministic.

A plausible Lean 4 signature skeleton:

```lean
import Mathlib
import Catalog.old.Pythagorean.SubgroupPressure

open scoped BigOperators
open Finset

variable {G : Type*} [Group G] [Fintype G] [DecidableEq (Subgroup G)]

/-- A finite subgroup ensemble with pair interaction weight. -/
structure SubgroupPressureModel (G : Type*) [Group G] [Fintype G] [DecidableEq (Subgroup G)] where
  support : Finset (Subgroup G)
  weight : Subgroup G → Subgroup G → ℝ

/-- Pressure of a deterministic ensemble indicator `χ : Subgroup G → Bool`. -/
def subgroupPressure
    (M : SubgroupPressureModel G)
    (χ : Subgroup G → Bool) : ℝ :=
  ∑ H in M.support, ∑ K in M.support,
    (if χ H then 1 else 0 : ℝ) * (if χ K then 1 else 0 : ℝ) * M.weight H K

/-- Coordinate influence of toggling a single subgroup. -/
def pressureInfluence
    (M : SubgroupPressureModel G)
    (H₀ : Subgroup G) : ℝ :=
  ∑ K in M.support, |M.weight H₀ K| + ∑ K in M.support, |M.weight K H₀|

/-- Uniform bounded-difference hypothesis. -/
def HasBoundedInfluence
    (M : SubgroupPressureModel G)
    (L : ℝ) : Prop :=
  ∀ H ∈ M.support, pressureInfluence M H ≤ L
```

## Proof Strategy Architecture

You must not pursue only one path. Build the result with layered fallback plans.

### Strategy A — Bounded differences via finite toggling geometry
Most promising for formalization.

1. Define pressure as a function on Boolean subgroup indicators.
2. Prove the **toggle bound**: changing only one subgroup affects only one row and one column of the interaction matrix.
3. Sum the coordinate sensitivities and invoke a finite bounded-differences inequality, or prove a custom exponential-moment estimate by induction on support size.

**Why this is most promising:** it separates algebra from probability. Once the deterministic Lipschitz estimate is formalized, every concentration statement becomes modular.

### Strategy B — Direct variance expansion
Good if concentration inequalities are hard to import.

1. Expand
   \[
   \Pi = \sum_{H,K} X_H X_K w(H,K),
   \]
   with \(X_H\) independent Bernoulli.
2. Compute or bound
   \[
   \mathbb E[\Pi],\quad \mathbb E[\Pi^2],\quad \operatorname{Var}(\Pi).
   \]
3. Show covariance terms vanish unless index overlaps occur in the support of the quartic expansion, yielding a finite combinatorial bound in terms of influence sums.

**Why it matters:** even without full subgaussian tails, a sharp \(L^2\) theorem already proves self-averaging and thermodynamic determinism.

### Strategy C — Free-energy / cumulant route
Most visionary; pursue if time permits.

1. Define a partition function or moment generating function for subgroup pressure.
2. Show convexity and derive susceptibility bounds from second derivatives.
3. Combine with catalog results like `susceptibility_add_of_freeEnergy_add` and `divergence_bound_of_additive_susceptibility` to transfer existing deterministic thermodynamic estimates into the random setting.

**Why this could be revolutionary:** it reframes subgroup combinatorics as a genuine disordered statistical mechanics, not just a probabilistic estimate on a sum.

## Cross-Domain Connections You Must Make Explicit

This project should not read as “probability on finite groups.” It is a bridge.

### 1. Statistical mechanics
Subgroup pressure is an algebraic free-energy density. Concentration means **self-averaging in a disordered medium**. The random subgroup ensemble is analogous to quenched disorder.

### 2. Information theory
The Bernoulli subgroup indicator vector is a random codeword on the subgroup lattice. Pressure concentration says that a complicated quadratic observable has **low effective entropy at scale \(n\)**.

### 3. Random matrix theory
Your quadratic form in independent Bernoulli variables is an algebraic cousin of concentration for random quadratic forms and trace observables. If you can identify the weight matrix \(W=(w(H,K))\), the influence bound is a row-sum norm estimate, analogous to matrix concentration controls.

### 4. Representation theory / asymptotic group theory
For \(S_n\), subgroup index growth is governed by deep asymptotic structure of permutation groups. Any theorem showing that inverse-index moments collapse with \(n\) connects thermodynamic concentration to subgroup classification.

## Conjecture with Testable Prediction

You must state at least one falsifiable conjecture and make the computational test precise.

### Conjecture — Universal self-averaging for inverse-index kernels on \(S_n\)
There exists \(C > 0\) such that for every \(n \ge 5\), every finite subgroup family \(S_n^\ast \subseteq \mathrm{Sub}(S_n)\), and every symmetric pair-kernel satisfying
\[
0 \le w(H,K) \le \frac{1}{[S_n:H]^2 [S_n:K]^2},
\]
the random pressure obeys
\[
\operatorname{Var}(\Pi_{S_n}) \le \frac{C}{n}.
\]

### Testable prediction
For sampled subgroup families of \(S_n\), \(n=5,\dots,15\), with Bernoulli inclusion probability \(p=1/2\):
- empirical variance of pressure should decay like \(O(1/n)\);
- normalized fluctuations \(\sqrt{n}\,(\Pi-\mathbb E[\Pi])\) should approach a stable law, plausibly Gaussian;
- row-sum influence statistics should predict observed concentration exponents.

A computational disproof would be:
- variance plateaus away from zero,
- or a family of subgroup supports exhibits maximal influence not shrinking with \(n\).

If the full subgroup lattice is computationally prohibitive, test structured families:
- point stabilizers,
- two-block Young subgroups,
- maximal subgroups from standard classification tables.
