## Synthesis

This cycle laid the load-bearing foundation of Baker–Norine divisor theory on a finite
graph, entirely over an ordinary Mathlib `SimpleGraph V`. The chip-firing operator was
defined as the graph Laplacian `(lap G f) v = ∑_{u ∼ v}(f v − f u)`, and we proved that
`f ↦ lap G f` is a degree-zero additive homomorphism `(V → ℤ) → Divisor V` killing the
constants (`lap_zero`, `lap_const`, `lap_add`, `lap_neg`, `lap_deg_zero`). From these
homomorphism facts, linear (chip-firing) equivalence dropped out as a genuine
`Equivalence` (`linEquiv_equivalence`, packaged as `linSetoid`), degree became an
invariant of the equivalence class (`linEquiv_deg`), and the "easy direction" of
Riemann–Roch — negative degree forces an empty linear system — fell out in three lines
(`neg_deg_no_effective_equiv`).

The structural insight of the cycle is that *the entire algebraic layer is the coset
relation of one homomorphism*: the three equivalence-relation axioms are literally
`lap_zero`/`lap_neg`/`lap_add`, and degree invariance is exactly the sum-zero property.
The sum-zero property itself reduces to the antisymmetry of `f v − f u` under the
symmetric adjacency relation — a `Finset.sum_comm` argument forcing `S = −S`, with no
handshake/degree-counting needed. The one genuinely combinatorial result,
`lap_kernel_const_of_connected`, is the discrete maximum principle: on a *connected*
graph the kernel of the Laplacian is exactly the constants. Its proof isolates
connectivity to a single step — propagating the argmax level set along walks
(`reachClosed` + `Connected.preconnected`) — while the arithmetic is the local equality
case of the maximum principle. Nothing here was disproved; the only false starts were
encoding choices (a weighted-multigraph `V → V → ℤ` carrying explicit symmetry, and a
`deg(v)·f v − ∑ f u` Laplacian) that obscured the antisymmetry that does all the work.

On the numerics side, the Brill–Noether number `ρ(g,r,d) = g − (r+1)(g−d+r)` was shown
to satisfy Serre duality (`bnNumber_serre_duality`), the genus-0 formula
(`bnNumber_genus_zero`), strict monotonicity in degree (`bnNumber_strict_mono_d`), and
an exact unit increment (`bnNumber_succ_d`). These give a clean numerical scaffold that
any future rank/Riemann–Roch formula must respect, and they connect directly to the
combinatorial layer through degree invariance.

## Results Summary

- `lap_zero`: proved — the empty firing pattern moves no chips.
- `lap_const`: proved — constant firing patterns are in the kernel (foundation for "kernel = constants").
- `lap_add`: proved — the Laplacian is additive (transitivity of linear equivalence).
- `lap_neg`: proved — the Laplacian respects negation (symmetry of linear equivalence).
- `lap_deg_zero`: proved — every Laplacian has degree zero (the sum-zero property), via antisymmetry + `Finset.sum_comm`.
- `linEquiv_equivalence` / `linSetoid`: proved — chip-firing equivalence is an `Equivalence`, so the Picard group is a well-defined quotient type.
- `linEquiv_deg`: proved — degree is a linear-equivalence invariant.
- `neg_deg_no_effective_equiv`: proved — negative-degree divisors have empty linear systems (easy direction of Baker–Norine, rank −1).
- `lap_max_principle`: proved — the Laplacian is `≥ 0` at any global maximum of `f`.
- `lapNeighborConst`: proved — at a kernel maximum, every neighbour ties the maximum (equality case).
- `reachClosed`: proved — adjacency-closed sets are reachability-closed.
- `lap_kernel_const_of_connected` / `lap_kernel_iff_const`: proved — **on a connected graph the Laplacian kernel is exactly the constant functions** (discrete maximum principle; the deep result of this cycle).
- `bnNumber_serre_duality`: proved — `ρ(g,r,d) = ρ(g, g−1−d+r, 2g−2−d)`.
- `bnNumber_genus_zero`: proved — `ρ(0,r,d) = (r+1)(d−r)`.
- `bnNumber_strict_mono_d`: proved — `ρ` is strictly increasing in `d` for `r ≥ 0`.
- `bnNumber_succ_d`: proved — `ρ(g,r,d+1) = ρ(g,r,d) + (r+1)`.

## Research Directions

### Direction 1: The graph Jacobian is a finite abelian group
**Hypothesis**: For a connected graph `G` on `Fintype V`, the quotient
`Quotient (linSetoid G)` restricted to degree-zero divisors carries an abelian group
structure inherited from pointwise `+`, and this group `Jac(G)` is *finite*.
**Test**: Define `Jac(G)` as the degree-0 part of `Quotient (linSetoid G)`; build the
`AddCommGroup` instance by lifting pointwise addition through `linEquiv_equivalence`
(addition descends because `lap` is additive); then prove `Finite (Jac G)` by exhibiting
a surjection from a bounded fundamental domain (e.g. `q`-reduced divisors).
**Why now**: The key insight is that finiteness is *forced by* `lap_kernel_const_of_connected`:
the Laplacian's kernel being one-dimensional (the constants) means its image in degree-0
divisors has finite index, which is precisely what makes `Jac(G)` finite. We already have
the setoid (`linSetoid`), additivity (`lap_add`), and degree descent (`linEquiv_deg`).
**If true**: Opens the matrix–tree theorem `|Jac(G)| = #spanning trees` and a Lean type
for the sandpile group, computable for small graphs.
**If false**: Would mean the kernel-of-Laplacian description fails to control the
cokernel — pointing to a missing connectivity or torsion hypothesis.

### Direction 2: Dhar's burning algorithm and unique `q`-reduced representatives
**Hypothesis**: Fixing a vertex `q`, every divisor class has a *unique* `q`-reduced
representative, computable by a terminating burning process on `Finset V`.
**Test**: Define `q`-reducedness (`∀ nonempty S ⊆ V∖{q}, ∃ v ∈ S, D v < outdeg_S(v)`),
implement Dhar's burning as a well-founded recursion decreasing the unburnt set, and prove
existence + uniqueness; verify on the path and cycle graphs via `#eval`.
**Why now**: The key insight is that uniqueness is the *equality case* of the maximum
principle we already proved: `lapNeighborConst` says a harmonic firing is flat across an
edge, so two `q`-reduced divisors in one class differ by a Laplacian whose firing set,
if nonempty, would violate reducedness at its boundary. Termination is tractable with
Lean 4 well-founded recursion on `Finset V`.
**If true**: Gives canonical class representatives, hence decidable equality on `Jac(G)`
and a constructive route to the full Baker–Norine rank.
**If false**: A counterexample (non-unique reduced divisor) would reveal that adjacency
alone is too weak and edge multiplicities must be tracked.

### Direction 3: Full Baker–Norine Riemann–Roch from the easy direction
**Hypothesis**: With `rank D` defined as `max k` such that `D − E` is equivalent to an
effective divisor for all effective `E` of degree `k` (and `−1` if `D` itself is not
equivalent to an effective divisor), the identity
`rank D − rank (K_G − D) = deg D − g + 1` holds, where `g = #edges − #vertices + 1`.
**Test**: Prove the two Riemann–Roch inequalities separately; the `≤` direction uses
`q`-reduced divisors (Direction 2), the `≥` direction uses a Dhar duality between `D` and
`K_G − D`. Cross-check `deg D < 0 ⇒ rank D = −1` against `neg_deg_no_effective_equiv`.
**Why now**: The key insight is that `neg_deg_no_effective_equiv` is *already* the base
case `rank = −1` for `deg < 0`, and `bnNumber_succ_d` (the exact `+(r+1)` increment)
encodes how rank should move with degree — so the numerical target is pinned down before
the combinatorics start.
**If true**: A complete formal graph Riemann–Roch, one of the few discrete analogues of a
classical theorem provable from scratch in Lean.
**If false**: The most likely failure (the `≥` direction) would localize exactly which
graph hypothesis (2-edge-connectivity? loop-freeness?) Baker–Norine secretly needs.

### Direction 4: Quantitative maximum principle and a spectral gap
**Hypothesis**: On a connected graph, a near-harmonic firing pattern is near-constant:
`‖lap G f‖_∞ ≤ ε` implies `max f − min f ≤ C(G)·ε` with `C(G)` controlled by the graph
diameter, i.e. the Laplacian has a strictly positive smallest nonzero eigenvalue.
**Test**: First prove the qualitative diameter bound `max f − min f ≤ diam(G)·‖lap G f‖_∞`
by summing the local inequalities `lapNeighborConst` gives along a geodesic; then relate
to `Matrix.IsHermitian` eigenvalues of the Laplacian matrix in Mathlib.
**Why now**: The key insight is that `lap_kernel_const_of_connected` is the `ε = 0` case
of a robust statement, and its proof already walks a path from the argmax to every vertex
— turning that walk into a *telescoping inequality* immediately yields the diameter bound.
**If true**: Connects the combinatorial divisor theory to spectral graph theory and
Cheeger/expander estimates already catalogued elsewhere in the project.
**If false**: Would show the kernel description is fragile under perturbation, isolating
disconnected or bottlenecked graphs as the obstruction.

### Direction 5: Specialization preserves the Brill–Noether obstruction
**Hypothesis**: Any rank function `r` satisfying Serre duality
`r(D) − r(K−D) = deg D − g + 1` automatically makes the Brill–Noether locus obstruction
`ρ(g,r,d)` invariant under the Serre involution, so tropical and algebraic sides share the
*same* numerical liftability condition.
**Test**: Show abstractly that `bnNumber_serre_duality` plus degree preservation
(`linEquiv_deg`-style invariance under a specialization map) forces `ρ` to agree on both
sides; formalize a `SpecializationDatum` interface (rank-non-decreasing, degree-preserving)
and derive the necessary condition `ρ(g,r,d) ≥ 0` purely tropically.
**Why now**: The key insight is that the Serre-duality identity we proved is *exactly* the
numerical invariant that any specialization must respect, so the liftability obstruction
can be checked entirely in the tropical setting before any non-Archimedean geometry enters.
**If true**: Gives a computable, formally verified necessary condition for lifting tropical
divisors to algebraic ones.
**If false**: A specialization datum violating the `ρ` identity would expose a hidden
hypothesis (e.g. faithful tropicalization) needed for Baker's specialization lemma.
