### 2. Time-translation symmetry implies energy conservation

For autonomous Lagrangians `L(q,v)` independent of time, prove conservation of the Hamiltonian/energy:
\[
E(q,v)=\sum_i v_i \frac{\partial L}{\partial v_i}(q,v)-L(q,v).
\]

Lean target:

```lean
def Energy {n : ℕ}
    (L : (Fin n → ℝ) → (Fin n → ℝ) → ℝ)
    (dL_dv : (Fin n → ℝ) → (Fin n → ℝ) → (Fin n → ℝ))
    (q v : Fin n → ℝ) : ℝ :=
  (∑ i, v i * dL_dv q v i) - L q v

theorem energy_conserved_of_time_translation
    {n : ℕ}
    (L : (Fin n → ℝ) → (Fin n → ℝ) → ℝ)
    (q : ℝ → (Fin n → ℝ))
    (hq : EulerLagrangeTrajectory L q)
    (haut : AutonomousLagrangian L) :
    ∀ t, HasDerivAt (fun s => Energy L (dL_dv L) (q s) (qdot s)) 0 t
```

This theorem is the formal heart of classical mechanics. It should not be proved by simplification; expect multi-step chain-rule/calc manipulations.

## Proof Strategy Architecture

You must include 2–3 viable proof routes in your work and pursue the most promising one.

### Strategy A: Coordinate Euler–Lagrange derivation
Most promising for this cycle.

1. Define coordinate partial derivatives `dL_dq`, `dL_dv` on `Fin n → ℝ`.
2. Define `EulerLagrangeTrajectory L q` as
   \[
   \frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial v_i}(q,\dot q)=\frac{\partial L}{\partial q_i}(q,\dot q)
   \]
   for each coordinate.
3. For a symmetry generator `ξ`, define the Noether charge
   \[
   J(q,\dot q)=\sum_i \frac{\partial L}{\partial v_i}\xi_i(q).
   \]
4. Differentiate `J` along a trajectory using chain rule and the Euler–Lagrange equations.
5. Substitute infinitesimal invariance to cancel terms and conclude derivative zero.

Why promising: it avoids full manifold machinery, is compatible with Lean’s finite-index sums, and naturally yields explicit computational algorithms.

### Strategy B: Action variation / first variation identity
Conceptually cleaner, more geometric.

1. Define the action functional on compact intervals.
2. Prove first variation formula for compactly supported variations.
3. Show that a symmetry family leaves the action invariant.
4. Differentiate invariance in the group parameter to obtain a boundary term.
5. Identify the boundary term as the Noether charge and conclude constancy.

Why powerful: closest to the classical statement of Noether’s theorem and generalizes to field theory. Why harder: function-space formalization and regularity bookkeeping may be substantial.

### Strategy C: Lie-algebraic generator formalization
Ambitious cross-domain route.

1. Encode infinitesimal symmetries as derivations or linear vector fields.
2. Show invariance as vanishing Lie derivative of the Lagrangian.
3. Derive conservation from commutation of dynamics with symmetry flow.
4. Compare the resulting conserved quantities with operator commutators from `angular_momentum_comm_xy`.

Why exciting: strongest bridge to quantum algebra and representation theory. Why risky: likely too infrastructure-heavy unless a minimal finite-dimensional version is adopted.
