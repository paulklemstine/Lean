## Assignment: Proof Strategy for the Continued Fraction / Gauss Map Spectral-Mixing Program

Mode: **prove**

This direction is worth pursuing only if we make it genuinely structural: not “another ergodic estimate,” but a formal bridge between continued fractions, modular dynamics, transfer-operator decay, and polynomial observables of digits. The right breakthrough is to isolate a class of **finite-digit cylinder observables** and prove explicit exponential decorrelation under the Gauss map, then reinterpret that as a statement about random walks on `SL₂(ℤ)`-generated continued-fraction matrices. That would open a formal pipeline from one-dimensional dynamics to arithmetic statistics and symbolic thermodynamic formalism inside Lean.

The key idea is that degree-`k` tests depending on the first `k` partial quotients are not arbitrary measurable functions: they factor through the finite truncation of the continued fraction expansion, hence through a finite cylinder partition. This turns spectral-mixing heuristics into exact finite combinatorics plus exponential error. If formalized well, this becomes a reusable theorem schema for arithmetic observables generated by Euclidean-algorithm digits.

### Primary Breakthrough Target

Define the Gauss map on `(0,1)` by
\[
T(x)=\operatorname{fract}(1/x).
\]
Let `μGauss` be the Gauss measure
\[
d\mu(x)=\frac{1}{\log 2}\frac{dx}{1+x},
\]
which is `T`-invariant. Let a **cylinder observable of depth `k`** be a bounded function depending only on the first `k` continued-fraction digits.

The theorem to formalize is:

> **Theorem (Explicit exponential decorrelation for finite continued-fraction observables).**  
> For every natural numbers `k m`, there exists a constant `Ck : ℝ≥0∞` or `ℝ` depending only on `k` such that for any bounded cylinder observables `f, g : (0,1) → ℝ` of depth at most `k`,
> \[
> \left|\int f(x)\, g(T^{m}x)\, d\muGauss - \left(\int f\, d\muGauss\right)\left(\int g\, d\muGauss\right)\right|
> \le C_k \, \rho^m \, \|f\|_\infty \|g\|_\infty
> \]
> for some `ρ < 1`, ideally extracted from a formal spectral-gap hypothesis for the transfer operator.

This is the mathematically correct shape: finite-memory observables, explicit exponential mixing, and a parameter `ρ` tied to a spectral gap. It is nontrivial, field-opening, and broad enough to support many corollaries.

## Secondary Structural Theorem: Matrix/Coset Bridge

The continued fraction expansion is generated by products of matrices
\[
A_a=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&a\end{pmatrix}, \quad a\in\mathbb N_{>0}.
\]
A digit prefix corresponds to a product of these matrices, and cylinder sets correspond to intervals determined by convergents. This gives the arithmetic bridge.

> **Theorem (Cylinder sets are modular-word shadows).**  
> For every finite digit word `w = [a₁, …, a_k]`, there exists a matrix `M_w ∈ SL₂(ℤ)` such that the continued-fraction cylinder of prefix `w` is exactly the image of `(0,1)` under the associated Möbius interval map, with endpoints given by consecutive convergents extracted from `M_w`.

Lean target:


and then a theorem connecting `wordMatrix` to convergents / cylinder intervals. This is the exact place where the “random walk on `SL₂(ℤ)`-cosets” becomes formal mathematics rather than rhetoric.

## 2–3 Proof Strategy Paths

### Strategy A: Cylinder-set combinatorics + preimage refinement
Most promising for first formal success.

1. Define finite continued-fraction cylinders by digit prefixes and prove they form a semi-ring / finite partition at each depth.
2. Show `T⁻¹` of a depth-`k` cylinder is a disjoint union of depth-`k+1` cylinders; more generally `(T^[n])⁻¹` refines prefixes by concatenation.
3. Import or axiomatize exponential convergence of cylinder probabilities to equilibrium, then lift from indicator functions to linear combinations of cylinder indicators.

Why this is promising:
- avoids full Banach-space operator theory at first;
- works directly with finite combinatorics;
- naturally matches “degree-k tests are polynomial functions of partial quotients.”

### Strategy B: Transfer operator formalization on bounded variation / Lipschitz observables
Most conceptually powerful, but heavier.

1. Define the Perron–Frobenius / Gauss transfer operator
   \[
   \mathcal L f(x)=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{1}{(x+n)^2}f\!\left(\frac{1}{x+n}\right).
   \]
2. Formalize invariance of Gauss measure and identify the eigenfunction/eigenmeasure pair.
3. Assume or prove a spectral gap on a function space, then derive exponential correlation decay for observables in that space; deduce the cylinder case as a corollary.

Why this matters:
- gives the true engine behind Gauss–Kuzmin;
- reusable for future thermodynamic formalism;
- connects directly to your catalog’s spectral-gap theorems.

This is the long-term canonical route.

### Strategy C: Matrix random walk / modular coding route
Best for cross-domain novelty.

1. Encode digit words by products of `SL₂(ℤ)` matrices and identify cylinders with Möbius images.
2. Translate correlation questions into asymptotic independence of prefix/suffix events in a matrix-word process.
3. Use spectral input on the associated shift / transfer operator to prove decorrelation.

Why this is exciting:
- makes the arithmetic/modular connection explicit;
- creates a bridge to automorphic and homogeneous dynamics;
- potentially enables future Lean formalization of geodesic coding on the modular surface.

## Cross-Domain Connections You Should Exploit

1. **Ergodic theory ↔ number theory**  
   Continued fractions encode Diophantine approximation; mixing of digit observables yields asymptotic independence phenomena for approximation events.

2. **Dynamical systems ↔ `SL₂(ℤ)` / modular forms**  
   The Gauss map is a symbolic coding of geodesic flow on the modular surface. Formalizing cylinder/matrix correspondence opens a path toward modular symbolic dynamics in Lean.

3. **Spectral theory ↔ information theory**

4. **Random matrix products ↔ statistical mechanics**

5. **Automata / symbolic dynamics ↔ proof assistants**  
   Cylinder sets and finite words are ideal for Lean. They give finite combinatorial handles on deep measure-theoretic dynamics.
