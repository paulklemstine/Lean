## Assignment: Tropical Pseudorandom Generators from Orbit Expansion

Prove a genuinely new theorem turning tropical orbit complexity into pseudorandomness. The goal is not a cosmetic variant of existing PRG lemmas, but a structural hardness-randomness bridge for tropical matrix dynamics: **orbit expansion should force fresh entropy at each time step, and universal hashing should extract that entropy into a long pseudorandom output stream.**

This is a chance to open a new interface between **tropical algebra, min-entropy methods, extractor theory, derandomization, and dynamical systems**.

---

## Breakthrough Target

### Informal theorem
Let `G` be a tropical matrix seed drawn from a sufficiently spread distribution on matrices. Suppose the tropical orbit
\[
G^0, G^1, \dots, G^T
\]
has a strong expansion/non-collapse property: no late iterate is determined by the earlier iterates, quantitatively in min-entropy. Then for a suitable family of universal hash functions `h`, the output stream
\[
h(G^0), h(G^1), \dots, h(G^T)
\]
is statistically close to uniform on a long bitstring. In other words, **tropical orbit expansion yields a pseudorandom generator**.

This would be a real breakthrough because it replaces classical algebraic PRG structure with **max-plus dynamics** and shows that tropical power growth can serve as an entropy reservoir. If formalized cleanly, this opens a program of **tropical hardness-vs-randomness**, where combinatorial orbit growth substitutes for number-theoretic or circuit-theoretic hardness assumptions.

## Precise theorem statement

You should aim for a theorem of the following shape, first in a finite/discrete abstraction that Lean can support cleanly, then specialize to tropical matrices.

### Core statistical theorem
Let `Seed` be a finite type of tropical matrix seeds, `pow : Seed → ℕ → State` the orbit state, and `hash : State → Fin m` a universal hash/extractor. Assume:

1. `G` is sampled uniformly from `Seed`.
2. For each `i ≤ T`, the conditional law of `pow G i` given the prefix
   \[
   (pow G 0, \dots, pow G (i-1))
   \]
   has min-entropy at least `k`.
3. `hash` satisfies the appropriate leftover-hash closeness bound on any source of min-entropy `k`.

Then the joint distribution of
\[
(hash (pow G 0), \dots, hash (pow G T))
\]
is within distance at most `(T+1) * ε` of uniform on `(Fin m)^(T+1)`.

This is the right theorem because it isolates the true mathematical bottleneck: **conditional entropy growth along a tropical orbit**. Once that is extracted, the PRG conclusion follows by a hybrid argument.

## Lean 4 theorem target

A plausible first formal target, abstracting away implementation details of tropical matrices, is:

```lean
theorem tropical_orbit_prg_from_conditional_minEntropy
  {Seed State Out : Type}
  [Fintype Seed] [DecidableEq Seed]
  [Fintype State] [DecidableEq State]
  [Fintype Out] [DecidableEq Out]
  (μ : PMF Seed)
  (pow : Seed → ℕ → State)
  (hash : State → Out)
  (T : ℕ)
  (k : ℝ)
  (ε : ℝ)
  (hcond :
    ∀ i ≤ T,
      k ≤ conditionalMinEntropy
        (X := fun s => pow s i)
        (Y := fun s => Fin.tuple fun j : Fin i => pow s j)
        μ)
  (hextract :
    ∀ i ≤ T,
      statDist
        (PMF.map (fun s => hash (pow s i)) μ)
        (PMF.uniformOfFintype Out) ≤ ε) :
  statDist
    (PMF.map
      (fun s : Seed => Fin.tuple fun i : Fin (T+1) => hash (pow s i)) μ)
    (PMF.uniformOfFintype (Fin (T+1) → Out))
    ≤ (T + 1) * ε
```

This exact signature may need adaptation to the probability API already present in your development, but the mathematical content should remain unchanged: **conditional entropy lower bounds + one-step extraction imply joint pseudorandomness via hybrids**.

## Stronger tropical structural theorem to pursue

The truly field-opening theorem is not only the hybrid lemma above, but a tropical dynamical source theorem:

### Structural entropy theorem
For a finite family of tropical matrices with bounded entries, if the orbit
\[
G^0,\dots,G^T
\]
is pairwise distinct and avoids eventual periodicity up to time `T`, then there exists a constant `c > 0` such that for each `i ≤ T`,
\[
H_\infty(G^i \mid G^0,\dots,G^{i-1}) \ge c
\]
under the uniform seed distribution on a sufficiently rich seed set.

This is the hard theorem. It says that **orbit expansion implies fresh conditional entropy**, not just state-space novelty. If you can prove even a weaker finite-state version — e.g. expansion implies a logarithmic lower bound on support size of the conditional fiber — that would already be important.

A Lean-friendly finite combinatorial variant is:

```lean
theorem orbit_expansion_gives_conditional_support_bound
  {Seed State : Type}
  [Fintype Seed] [DecidableEq Seed]
  [Fintype State] [DecidableEq State]
  (pow : Seed → ℕ → State)
  (T M : ℕ)
  (hexp :
    ∀ i ≤ T, ∀ y : (Fin i → State),
      M ≤ Fintype.card {s : Seed // (∀ j : Fin i, pow s j = y j)}) :
  ∀ i ≤ T,
    log (M : ℝ) ≤ conditionalMinEntropy
      (X := fun s => pow s i)
      (Y := fun s => Fin.tuple fun j : Fin i => pow s j)
      (PMF.uniformOfFintype Seed)
```

Even if your exact entropy API differs, this is the theorem shape to aim for: **fiber-size lower bounds imply conditional min-entropy lower bounds**.

## Proof strategy architecture

You should pursue this in layers. Do not start with full tropical matrix complexity. First prove the abstract entropy-to-PRG theorem, then a tropical orbit theorem that supplies the entropy hypothesis.

### Strategy A: Hybrid argument + conditional extraction
This is the most promising route.

1. **Abstract one-step extractor lemma.**  
   Prove that if `X_i` has conditional min-entropy at least `k` given the prefix `X_0,...,X_{i-1}`, then `hash(X_i)` is `ε`-close to uniform even conditioned on the previous hashed outputs or previous states, depending on your extractor formalization.

2. **Hybrid sequence.**  
   Define hybrids `H_j` where the first `j` coordinates are real outputs and the remaining are uniform. Show adjacent hybrids differ by at most `ε`.

3. **Sum errors.**  
   Apply triangle inequality to conclude total distance at most `(T+1)ε`.

Why this is strongest: it cleanly separates dynamics from extraction and is robust under future generalization to computational distinguishers, Nisan-style hybrids, and multi-source tropical extractors.

---

### Strategy B: Support-size / fiber-count route
This is the best route for the structural tropical theorem.

1. **Define prefix fibers.**  
   For each prefix value `y = (G^0,...,G^{i-1})`, define the seed fiber of matrices realizing that prefix.

2. **Show expansion prevents fiber collapse.**  
   Prove that if powers remain distinct/non-periodic, then many seeds remain compatible with a given prefix while yielding multiple possibilities for `G^i`. Even a crude lower bound on the number of possible `G^i` values given the prefix gives entropy.

3. **Convert counting to min-entropy.**  
   Use finite uniform seed distribution and support-cardinality lemmas to obtain `H∞(G^i | prefix) ≥ log M`.

Why it matters: this route reveals the true tropical combinatorics and could become a standalone theory of **entropy production in max-plus semigroup dynamics**.

---

### Strategy C: Reduction to collision bounds
This may be easier if entropy APIs are awkward.

1. Instead of conditional min-entropy directly, prove a bound on conditional collision probability of `G^i` given the prefix.
2. Use a certified leftover-hash style theorem phrased in collision entropy if one exists in your library.
3. Derive statistical pseudorandomness from collision probability bounds.

Why useful: collision bounds are often easier to establish combinatorially, especially from pairwise-distinct orbit assumptions or bounded multiplicity of transition fibers.

## Cross-domain connections to exploit

This project becomes revolutionary if you make the right bridges explicit.

### Tropical algebra × extractor theory
Classical extractors work on bit sources, affine sources, or algebraic sources. Here the source is a **dynamical tropical orbit**. That is a new source class. Formalizing it would create a new category of extractor-compatible objects.

### Max-plus dynamics × hardness-vs-randomness
Orbit expansion in semigroups can play the role usually occupied by hard functions or expander walks. This suggests a tropical analogue of the hardness-randomness paradigm: **dynamical unpredictability implies derandomization**.

### Symbolic dynamics × information theory
Conditional entropy along the orbit is exactly the language of symbolic dynamics and entropy production. A successful theorem would connect tropical matrix powers to **nonlinear entropy rate** phenomena.

### Semiring linear algebra × cryptography
If orbit-hash sequences are pseudorandom, tropical matrix iteration becomes a candidate primitive for lightweight algebraic cryptography, especially where max-plus operations are natural or hardware-efficient.

### Derandomization × combinatorial geometry
Tropical matrix powers encode shortest-path and path-weight combinatorics. Pseudorandomness extracted from those powers hints at a new geometric source of randomness from path-complexity growth.
