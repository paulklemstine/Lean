# Direction 4: Gröbner Footprint Bound for Finite Grids

You should treat this as a foundational bridge theorem between coding theory, Gröbner methods, finite-field combinatorics, and formal computational algebra. The target is not just a re-proof of a Reed–Muller minimum-distance fact: the real goal is to install in Lean a reusable **footprint machine** for finite-grid polynomial method arguments. If you succeed, you open a formal pathway toward Alon–Füredi, Combinatorial Nullstellensatz on finite products, affine Cartesian codes, and eventually symbolic-algebraic decoding bounds.

## Breakthrough theorem to formalize

Let `F` be a finite field with `Fintype.card F = q`, let `σ` be a finite variable type, and let `f : MvPolynomial σ F`. Assume `f` is reduced modulo the vanishing ideal generated by `X i ^ q - X i`, meaning every exponent in every monomial of `f` is `< q`. Let `m = lm(f)` be the leading monomial under a fixed monomial order, with exponent vector `e : σ → ℕ`. Then the support of the evaluation function of `f` on the full grid `σ → F` is bounded below by the anti-footprint of `m`:

\[
\#\{x : \sigma \to F \mid f(x)\neq 0\} \;\ge\; \prod_{i\in \sigma}(q - e_i).
\]

This is the finite-grid Gröbner footprint lower bound in its cleanest reduced-form incarnation.

A stronger aspirational formulation, if infrastructure permits, is:

\[
\#\{x \mid f(x)\neq 0\} \ge \min_{m \in \operatorname{LM}(f)} \#(\text{antiFootprint}(m)),
\]

but the first theorem is already deep and structurally sufficient.

   For exponent vector `e` with `e i < q`, the number of reduced monomials divisible by `X^e` is exactly `∏ i (q - e i)`, or equivalently the anti-footprint cardinality has that product form.

   ```lean
   theorem card_monomials_above_in_box
       {σ : Type*} [Fintype σ] [DecidableEq σ]
       (q : ℕ) (e : σ →₀ ℕ)
       (he : ∀ i, e i < q) :
       cardReducedMonomialsDivisibleBy q e = ∏ i, (q - e i) := by
     sorry
   ```

This combinatorial lemma is the beating heart of the argument.

## Proof architecture: three strategies

### Strategy A: Direct Gröbner-footprint argument via reduced monomial basis
This is the most faithful to the theorem and the most reusable long-term.

1. Construct the vector space of reduced polynomials:
   monomials with exponent box `0 ≤ a_i < q` form a basis for functions on `F^σ`.
2. For a nonzero reduced `f`, let `m = lm(f)`. Show that if `f` vanished on too many points, then after interpolation on the complement of its support one gets a reduced polynomial in the same function class with leading monomial strictly smaller, contradicting uniqueness or reduced normal form.
3. Count the monomials in the anti-footprint region to obtain the lower bound `∏ (q - e_i)`.

Why this is promising:
- It aligns with the intended theorem statement.
- It creates a general-purpose finite-grid normal-form package.
- It is the right basis for later Alon–Füredi and affine Cartesian code theorems.

### Strategy B: Induction on number of variables using univariate root bounds fiberwise
This may be easier to formalize first if monomial-order infrastructure becomes painful.

1. Write `f` as a polynomial in one distinguished variable `X_n` with coefficients in `MvPolynomial (σ \ {n}) F`.
2. Let the leading monomial have exponent `e_n` in `X_n`. For each fiber assignment to the other variables, obtain a univariate polynomial of degree at most `e_n` after suitable leading-coefficient control.
3. Use the fact that a nonzero univariate polynomial over a field has at most `e_n` roots, then induct over variables to get the product lower bound.

Why this is promising:
- It avoids deep Gröbner basis formalism at first.
- It may prove the same numerical theorem with less infrastructure.
- Afterwards, package the proof as a “shadow theorem” and only then identify it with the anti-footprint interpretation.

Risk:
- Harder to state as a true footprint theorem.
- Less directly extensible to ideals beyond the full grid.

### Strategy C: Linear-algebra / evaluation-matrix rank proof
This is conceptually powerful and connects to coding theory.

1. Index reduced monomials by exponent vectors in the `q`-box.
2. Consider the evaluation matrix on `F^σ`. Show this matrix is invertible on the reduced monomial basis, i.e. reduced polynomials and functions on the grid are isomorphic vector spaces.
3. Restrict to monomials in the anti-footprint of the leading monomial and show any function supported on fewer points than that cannot realize a polynomial with that leading term, by rank deficiency.

Why this is revolutionary:
- It recasts the footprint bound as a statement about structured Vandermonde matrices over finite Cartesian products.
- This is the bridge to affine Cartesian codes, interpolation complexity, and symbolic decoding.

Most promising overall:
- **Start with Strategy B if Lean infrastructure for monomial orders is currently weak.**
- **Aim to finish in Strategy A form.**
- If you can build enough basis/rank machinery cleanly, Strategy C becomes the conceptual crown jewel.

## Cross-domain connections you should make explicit in the development

1. **Coding theory**  
   This theorem implies the minimum-distance lower bound for generalized Reed–Muller / affine Cartesian codes by purely combinatorial algebra. The anti-footprint cardinality is the codeword support lower bound.

2. **Combinatorial Nullstellensatz and Alon–Füredi**  
   The theorem is a finite-grid precursor to stronger zero-set bounds on Cartesian products. Once formalized, it should lead naturally to:
   - lower bounds on nonzero evaluations from top monomials,
   - upper bounds on zero sets,
   - certificate-based existence theorems in additive combinatorics.

3. **Symbolic computation / Gröbner geometry**  
   This installs a finite-field version of the footprint philosophy in Lean: leading terms control function-space complexity. That is a formal algebraic geometry primitive, not just a coding lemma.

4. **Complexity theory**  
   The reduced monomial basis gives canonical normal forms for functions on finite cubes. This is directly relevant to polynomial representations of finite functions, interpolation complexity, and lower bounds for sparse representations.

5. **Tropical and idempotent analogies**  
   The “support forced by a leading combinatorial object” principle mirrors tropical support stratification. A future bridge could reinterpret anti-footprints as discrete Newton regions and transport support bounds into tropical evaluation complexity.

## Concrete implementation plan

### Phase 1: finite-grid reduction machinery
- Define reducedness: every monomial in support has coordinate exponents `< q`.
- Define `reduceModGrid` by repeated replacement of `X_i^q` with `X_i`.
- Prove evaluation preservation on all `x : σ → F` using `a^q = a`.
- Prove every polynomial reduces to a reduced one.
- Prove uniqueness of reduced representatives, likely by cardinality of the reduced basis and injectivity of evaluation.

### Phase 2: leading monomial infrastructure
- If Mathlib monomial-order support is sufficient, use it directly.
- Otherwise, define a bespoke admissible order on finitely supported exponent vectors adequate for the theorem.
- Define `leadingMonomialOfNonzero`.
- Prove reducedness implies each exponent of the leading monomial is `< q`.

### Phase 3: anti-footprint counting
- Define anti-footprint of `e` inside the `q`-box:
  exponent vectors `a` with `e_i ≤ a_i < q`.
- Prove its cardinality is `∏ (q - e_i)`.
- Connect this set to the monomial shadow/divisibility relation.

### Phase 4: main support bound
Two routes:
- direct contradiction from too-small support using interpolation and cancellation of the leading monomial;
- or induction on variables with root counting.

If direct Gröbner machinery gets stuck, prove the numerical support theorem first via induction, then later upgrade the statement to mention the anti-footprint explicitly.
