## Assignment: Dihedral groups

Mode: **prove**

Prove a genuinely new bridge theorem around cyclotomic-real subfields and dihedral symmetry, with Lean-ready statements, not vague exploration. The right target is not “show something about `cos (2π/n)`,” but to isolate the **real cyclotomic generator theorem** and then leverage it toward a finite-group/Galois correspondence formalization that can seed future work on explicit dihedral extensions.

### Research Direction
Exploit the element
\[
\alpha_n := \zeta_n + \zeta_n^{-1}
\]
in a cyclotomic extension, which corresponds analytically to \(2\cos(2\pi/n)\), and prove that it generates the maximal real subfield. This is the algebraic shadow of dihedral symmetry: inversion \(\zeta \mapsto \zeta^{-1}\) is the reflection symmetry, and the real subfield is the fixed field of that involution. The breakthrough is to formalize, in Lean, the passage from cyclic cyclotomic symmetry to dihedral Galois structure via fixed fields.

### Precise Theorem Target

You should aim for a theorem of the following shape over `ℚ`:

> For \(n \ge 3\), if \(K\) is a field containing a primitive \(n\)-th root of unity \(\zeta\), then the subfield \( \mathbb{Q}(\zeta+\zeta^{-1}) \) is the fixed field of the involution \(\zeta \mapsto \zeta^{-1}\). Consequently, if the cyclotomic extension is Galois with group \(G \cong (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^\times\), then the real subfield has degree \(\varphi(n)/2\) for \(n > 2\).

A Lean 4 target should be stated in a form close to what Mathlib can support. For example, one plausible formal target is:

A more immediately achievable theorem, and likely the correct first milestone, is the quadratic relation:

### Mathematical Framing
The key algebraic identity is:
\[
\zeta^2 - (\zeta+\zeta^{-1})\zeta + 1 = 0.
\]
So \(\zeta\) is quadratic over the field generated by \(\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\). This proves that the cyclotomic field is at most degree 2 over the real subfield. The involution \(\zeta \mapsto \zeta^{-1}\) fixes \(\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\), and morally this is the unique nontrivial symmetry cutting out the real subfield. In complex realizations, this is complex conjugation. The resulting semidirect-product intuition is dihedral: rotations from powers of \(\zeta\), reflections from inversion.

### Proof Strategy Options

#### Strategy 1: Algebraic tower + quadratic equation
Most promising.

1. Let \(F = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})\), \(E = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta)\).
2. Show \(\zeta\) is a root of the monic polynomial
   \[
   X^2 - (\zeta+\zeta^{-1})X + 1 \in F[X].
   \]
   Therefore \(E/F\) has degree at most 2.
3. Show inversion fixes \(F\), hence \(F\) lies in the fixed field.
4. Use degree/counting or uniqueness of the quadratic subextension to identify \(F\) with the fixed field.

Why this is best: it is closest to Mathlib’s existing algebra tower and integrality APIs, and avoids analytic `cos` until the final interpretation layer.

#### Strategy 2: Chebyshev-polynomial route
Potentially deeper and better for future generalization.

1. Define \(x = \zeta+\zeta^{-1}\).
2. Use the recurrence for Chebyshev polynomials to show \(\zeta^k+\zeta^{-k}\) is a polynomial in \(x\).
3. Since \(\zeta\) satisfies \(\zeta^n=1\), derive a polynomial relation for \(x\), giving a route to the minimal polynomial of \(2\cos(2\pi/n)\).
4. Connect this to explicit degree formulas and irreducibility data.

Why it matters: this opens the door to certified minimal polynomials for `2 cos (2π/n)` and to explicit constructive number theory in Lean. It is harder initially but scientifically explosive.

#### Strategy 3: Galois correspondence / order-theoretic fixed-field approach
Best for the final conceptual statement.

1. Use `galois_correspondence_orderIso` as a conceptual bridge: subgroups of the automorphism group correspond to intermediate fields.
2. Identify the order-2 subgroup generated by inversion or complex conjugation.
3. Prove the adjoined field of \(\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\) is exactly the intermediate field corresponding to that subgroup.

Why this is powerful: it upgrades a field-generation result into a structurally meaningful Galois theorem. It is likely the right final theorem once Strategy 1 is in place.

### Cross-Domain Connections
Do not leave this as pure algebraic number theory. Tie it to at least one other domain.

1. **Representation theory / harmonic analysis**  
   The quantity \(\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\) is the character of the 2-dimensional real representation of a cyclic subgroup inside a dihedral group. Formalizing this could connect field generators to character values and class functions.

2. **Dynamical systems / Chebyshev maps**  
   The recurrence \(T_m(\cos \theta)=\cos(m\theta)\) means the real cyclotomic generator sits inside polynomial dynamics. Minimal polynomials of `2 cos (2π/n)` become exact periodic orbit certificates.

3. **Algebraic circuit complexity**  
   Chebyshev/cyclotomic identities admit compact circuit representations. If you can formalize equality of two symbolic constructions of the same generator polynomial, `circuits_with_same_poly_agree` becomes relevant.

4. **Inverse Galois theory**  
   Once fixed fields of inversion are explicit, one can seek formal dihedral extensions by adjoining square roots to real cyclotomic subfields or by proving semidirect-product automorphism structures.
