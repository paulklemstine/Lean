### Theorem 2: Local optimality under primitive rewrites

You should formalize a one-step rewrite relation generated by AC + scalar distributivity and show that canonical form is **locally irreducible with respect to cost decrease**.

**Mathematical statement:**
No primitive AC/distributive rearrangement applied to a canonical normal form strictly decreases the sharing-aware cost.

**Lean-shaped target:**

theorem normalizeCanon_locally_optimal
    {α : Type u} [DecidableEq α]

**Why this matters:** it is the analog of “local optimality” in variational problems, now for extraction from rewrite spaces.

## Proof Strategy Architecture

You must give Aristotle multiple proof routes and choose among them.

### Strategy A: Quotient-to-multiset reduction
1. Define a flattening map from AC sums/products into multisets/lists modulo permutation.
2. Prove `normalizeCanon` computes the sorted/aggregated multiset representative.
3. Show sharing-aware cost is minimized by aggregation because duplicate subexpressions collapse in the canonical DAG view.

**Why promising:** this converts a hard rewrite search problem into a combinatorial extremal statement on multisets and support cardinalities. It is the most likely route for the fragment theorem.

---

### Strategy B: Rewrite-theoretic local-to-global optimality
1. Define primitive rewrite steps and prove canonical forms are local minima for the cost.
2. Prove every equivalent term rewrites to canonical form without increasing a suitable potential.
3. Deduce global minimality by well-founded descent or Newman-style confluence plus monotonicity.

**Why promising:** conceptually powerful, and it generalizes to future extraction theories. But it may require a carefully engineered cost potential stronger than plain size.

---

### Strategy C: DAG semantics and support cardinality
1. Interpret a tensor expression as a finite DAG of subterms modulo hash-consing.
2. Prove canonical aggregation merges all equal monomials/coefficient-carrying subterms.
3. Show any equivalent expression must realize at least the same support set, so distinct-subterm count is minimized by the canonical DAG.

**Why promising:** this is closest to real e-graphs and optimizer extraction. It may be the cleanest conceptual explanation of “maximal sharing,” though it requires more new definitions.

**Recommendation:** pursue **Strategy A first** for a theorem on a strong fragment, then layer **Strategy C** for the conceptual extraction interpretation. Strategy B is ideal if the catalog already has a robust one-step rewrite relation.
