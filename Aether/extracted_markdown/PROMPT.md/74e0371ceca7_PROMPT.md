## Hypothesis 1: Ray Class Precursor Hypothesis

### Visionary theorem target

Formalize a ray-class-style quotient at finite level and prove that it surjects onto the ordinary class group. This is the first structural bridge from Hilbert class fields to full abelian class field theory.

The key breakthrough is not merely defining a quotient: it is proving that the “congruence refinement” of principal ideals gives a strictly finer arithmetic invariant while still projecting canonically to the ordinary ideal class group. Once this exists in Lean, conductor-sensitive reciprocity becomes formally reachable.

### Precise mathematical statement

Let `K` be a number field and let `𝔪` be a nonzero ideal of `𝓞 K`. Define the subgroup
\[
P_{1,\mathfrak m} \subseteq I^\mathfrak m_K
\]
of principal fractional ideals generated by elements \(a \in K^\times\) satisfying the finite congruence condition
\[
a \equiv 1 \pmod{\mathfrak m}
\]
and coprimality to `𝔪`, where \(I^\mathfrak m_K\) denotes the group of fractional ideals coprime to `𝔪`.

Define the ray class precursor quotient
\[
\mathrm{RayClassGroup}(K,\mathfrak m) := I^\mathfrak m_K / P_{1,\mathfrak m}.
\]

Then prove:

1. There is a canonical surjective homomorphism
   \[
   \pi_{\mathfrak m} : \mathrm{RayClassGroup}(K,\mathfrak m) \twoheadrightarrow \mathrm{ClassGroup}(\mathcal O_K).
   \]

2. If `𝔪 ≠ ⊤` is nontrivial, then
   \[
   \#\mathrm{RayClassGroup}(K,\mathfrak m) \ge \#\mathrm{ClassGroup}(\mathcal O_K).
   \]

3. In the special case `K = ℚ(√-5)` and `𝔪 = (2)`, instantiate the construction and prove the map exists and is surjective.

The theorem is deliberately one step short of full Artin reciprocity: it isolates the algebraic quotient and projection theorem that every future formal ray class field construction will need.

### Proof strategy architecture

#### Strategy A: Quotient-by-subgroup architecture from the ideal group
Most promising.

1. Define the group of fractional ideals coprime to `m` as a subgroup of `FractionalIdeal K⁰ (𝓞 K)` or an equivalent existing ideal group object.
2. Define the “principal congruent to 1 mod m” subgroup via generators `a : Kˣ` satisfying a modular condition on integral representatives.
3. Use the universal property of quotient groups to define the map to `ClassGroup (𝓞 K)` by forgetting the congruence condition.
4. Surjectivity should follow because every ideal class has an integral ideal representative, and every nonzero ideal is coprime to `m` after multiplying by a suitable principal ideal or by choosing a representative in the same class avoiding the finite set of bad primes.

Why this is promising: it aligns directly with existing class group constructions in Mathlib, and the theorem only needs a projection, not full reciprocity.

#### Strategy B: Present ray classes as a kernel-refinement of the class group presentation
1. Start from the existing presentation of `ClassGroup (𝓞 K)` as ideals modulo principal ideals.
2. Refine the principal-ideal relation by restricting to generators congruent to `1 mod m`.
3. Obtain the projection by quotient functoriality from the inclusion
   \[
   P_{1,\mathfrak m} \subseteq P_K.
   \]
4. Deduce the cardinal inequality from the quotient map.

Why it may be easier: this avoids constructing the coprime ideal group first if Mathlib already gives a clean quotient presentation of class groups.  
Why it may be harder: the coprimality condition is hidden in classical ray class definitions, so some localization bookkeeping may reappear later anyway.

#### Strategy C: Finite-support prime ideal presentation
1. Represent ideals by finite products over prime ideals.
2. Define “coprime to `m`” by vanishing valuations on primes dividing `m`.
3. Define the congruence subgroup valuation-theoretically.
4. Build the quotient concretely.

Why it is interesting: this connects directly to idèle-style future formalization.  
Why it is less promising now: too much infrastructure overhead unless the catalog already contains valuation-heavy ideal factorization lemmas.

## Hypothesis 2: Capitulation Detection via Transfer Map

### Visionary theorem target

Formalize the transfer map and prove a concrete capitulation theorem in a cyclic unramified extension of prime degree. This would be the first Lean bridge between:
- ideal class groups,
- subgroup transfer in finite group theory,
- and capitulation of ideal classes in extensions.

This is conceptually explosive: it turns a classical “mysterious” phenomenon of ideal classes becoming principal upstairs into a computable morphism in group theory.

### Precise mathematical statement

Let \(L/K\) be a cyclic unramified extension of prime degree \(p\), and let \(H\) be the Hilbert class field of \(K\). Suppose \(L\) corresponds, under class field theory, to a quotient of \(\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal O_K)\) of order \(p\).

Then the capitulation kernel
\[
\ker\!\left(\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal O_K)\to \mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal O_L)\right)
\]
is identified with the image of the transfer map from the appropriate Galois subgroup quotient:
\[
\operatorname{Ver} : \mathrm{Gal}(H/L)^{\mathrm{ab}} \to \mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal O_K),
\]
at least in the concrete cyclic prime-degree case realized inside the existing formal Hilbert class field infrastructure.

A weaker but still major theorem target, suitable for current infrastructure, is:

> For a finite group `G` with subgroup `U` of finite index, define the transfer homomorphism `transfer : G →* Uᵃᵇ`. Prove that in the special case where `G` is abelian and `U` has prime index `p`, the transfer agrees with the `p`-th power map modulo the canonical identification.

Then connect this theorem to the capitulation kernel in one explicit arithmetic example already present in the catalog.

### Lean 4 formalization target

Two-tier target: first the pure group theory, then the arithmetic application.

```lean

def classGroupMapOfExtension
  (K L : Type*) [Field K] [Field L] [NumberField K] [NumberField L]
  [Algebra K L] :
```

If the full arithmetic theorem is too ambitious in one cycle, prove instead:
1. the transfer map exists,
2. the abelian prime-index formula,
3. a formally checked lemma showing that this formula matches the expected capitulation kernel size in an explicit cyclic example.

That still constitutes a nontrivial bridge theorem.

### Proof strategy architecture

#### Strategy A: Build transfer via coset actions and prove independence of representatives
Most faithful to the classical definition.

1. Choose a transversal `s : G ⧸ U → G`.
2. Define
   \[
   V(g)=\prod_{x \in G/U} s(gx)^{-1} g s(x)
   \]
   in `U`, then descend to `Abelianization U`.
3. Prove independence from the choice of transversal after abelianization.
4. Specialize to `CommGroup G`; simplify the product to `g^[G:U]`.

Why this is promising: once transfer lands in abelianization, representative-dependence vanishes more easily. This is the classical robust route.

#### Strategy B: Use norm-on-group-algebra intuition to derive transfer abstractly
1. Interpret transfer as the multiplicative norm along the finite `G`-set `G/U`.
2. Define it functorially via permutation action.
3. In the abelian case, identify the norm with exponentiation by the orbit cardinality.

Why it is visionary: this ties transfer to categorical norm maps and later to cohomology/Tate theory.  
Why it may be harder now: requires more category-action machinery than the direct combinatorial definition.

#### Strategy C: Arithmetic-first strategy through existing Hilbert class field data
1. If the catalog already has `Gal(H/K) ≃ ClassGroup (𝓞 K)`, transport the transfer map from Galois groups to class groups.
2. Define the extension map on class groups and compare kernels via the correspondence theorem.
3. Avoid some pure group infrastructure by working only in the abelian finite Galois context.

Why it may work: the arithmetic context is already abelian, so the transfer formula simplifies dramatically.  
Why it may fail: if Galois subgroup APIs are less mature than finite-group APIs, the transport overhead could dominate.

### Key catalog building blocks to exploit

Look for existing theorems on:
- `Abelianization`,
- finite-index subgroup quotients,
- finite group actions on cosets,
- class group maps induced by extension of ideals,
- Hilbert class field Galois/class group equivalence,
- kernels/ranges of group homomorphisms,
- cardinality lemmas for finite quotients.

If the catalog already includes a theorem identifying `Gal(H/K)` with `ClassGroup (𝓞 K)`, use it as the arithmetic endpoint of the transfer formalization.

### Cross-domain connections

- **Finite group theory ↔ algebraic number theory:** transfer becomes the formal mechanism behind capitulation.
- **Cohomology ↔ class field theory:** the transfer is the shadow of corestriction in group cohomology; formalizing it prepares the ground for Tate cohomology in Lean.
- **Representation theory ↔ arithmetic norms:** transfer is a multiplicative norm over the permutation representation on cosets.
- **Computation ↔ explicit examples:** cyclic prime-degree cases are ideal for extraction and verification against computational algebra systems.
- **Higher reciprocity ↔ formal proof engineering:** once transfer exists, genus theory and ambiguous class number formulas become realistic targets.

### Application keywords

`capitulation`, `transfer map`, `Verlagerung`, `Hilbert class field`, `abelianization`, `cyclic unramified extension`, `prime degree`, `group cohomology`, `corestriction`, `ambiguous class number formula`

### Success criterion

A sorry-free proof of either:
- the full concrete capitulation equality in one explicit cyclic prime-degree example, or
- the transfer map plus its prime-index abelian formula, together with a formal arithmetic comparison theorem reducing capitulation to transfer in the available Hilbert class field framework.

### Failure criterion

A precise obstruction showing that one of the following is not yet expressible with current infrastructure:
- transversal-independent transfer,
- extension-induced class group maps,
- transport across the class field correspondence.

Again, such failure is valuable if it isolates the missing API.

### Revolutionary significance

This would formalize the first deep interaction between **group-theoretic transfer** and **arithmetic ideal-class capitulation**. It opens routes to:
- genus theory,
- ambiguous class number formulas,
- transfer kernels and class towers,
- cohomological class field theory,
- and eventually Iwasawa-theoretic descent arguments.

This is not a local theorem. It is the seed of formal arithmetic cohomology.
