## Assignment: Lehmer's Mahler Measure Problem

**Mode:** `formalize` + `prove`

Prove genuinely new, non-trivial theorems around Mahler measure, but do not waste cycles trying to settle Lehmer’s conjecture outright in one jump. Instead, build the formal infrastructure that turns the conjecture into a mathematically sharp, machine-checkable frontier: define Mahler measure in Lean 4 for integer polynomials, prove its Jensen/root-factorization formula, connect it to logarithmic height and entropy of companion-matrix dynamics, and isolate a formally verified reduction principle showing exactly where the non-cyclotomic difficulty lives.

This is not bookkeeping. If you succeed, you create the first formal bridge between arithmetic complexity, spectral dynamics, and entropy for one of the deepest open problems in Diophantine analysis.

---

## Core Vision

Lehmer’s polynomial
\[
L(X)=X^{10}+X^9-X^7-X^6-X^5-X^4-X^3+X+1
\]
has Mahler measure approximately \(1.17628\ldots\), and Lehmer’s problem asks whether every non-cyclotomic integer polynomial has Mahler measure bounded away from \(1\) by at least this value.

You should **not** merely restate this conjecture. You should formalize the landscape around it so powerfully that future attacks become inevitable. The breakthrough target is a certified equivalence web:

- Mahler measure as a product over roots outside the unit circle,
- logarithmic Mahler measure as an average over the complex unit circle,
- entropy of the toral/algebraic dynamical system attached to a monic polynomial as the same logarithmic quantity,
- cyclotomicity characterized by zero logarithmic Mahler measure under suitable hypotheses,
- lower bounds reduced to spectral and height-theoretic obstructions.

This opens a new field direction: **formal arithmetic dynamics of integer polynomials**.

## Primary Theorem Targets

### Target 1: Define logarithmic Mahler measure and prove root-factorization

For a nonzero monic polynomial \(P \in \mathbb{Z}[X]\), with complex roots \(\alpha_i\) counted with multiplicity,
\[
\log M(P)=\sum_i \max(0,\log |\alpha_i|).
\]

This is the indispensable theorem. Without it, everything else is rhetoric.

### Lean 4 formalization target
A realistic signature, allowing some flexibility in representation of roots:

```lean
noncomputable def logMahlerMeasure (P : Polynomial ℤ) : ℝ := ...

theorem logMahlerMeasure_eq_sum_roots
    (P : Polynomial ℤ)
    (hP0 : P ≠ 0)
    (hmonic : P.Monic) :
    logMahlerMeasure P
      = ∑ z in P.map (Int.castRingHom ℂ) |>.roots.toFinset,
          max 0 (Real.log ‖z‖) := ...
```

If `roots.toFinset` loses multiplicity too aggressively for your chosen API, switch to `Multiset`:

```lean
theorem logMahlerMeasure_eq_sum_roots'
    (P : Polynomial ℤ)
    (hP0 : P ≠ 0)
    (hmonic : P.Monic) :
    logMahlerMeasure P
      = ((P.map (Int.castRingHom ℂ)).roots).map
          (fun z => max 0 (Real.log ‖z‖)) |>.sum := ...
```

You may need to define Mahler measure first over `Polynomial ℂ` and then specialize to `ℤ`.

### Target 2: Cyclotomic polynomials have Mahler measure 1

For every cyclotomic polynomial \(\Phi_n\),
\[
M(\Phi_n)=1,\qquad \log M(\Phi_n)=0.
\]

### Target 3: Entropy bridge for companion-matrix dynamics

Let \(P\in \mathbb{Z}[X]\) be monic of degree \(d\), and let \(C_P\) be its companion matrix over \(\mathbb{C}\). Then the logarithmic Mahler measure equals the sum of logs of moduli of eigenvalues outside the unit circle. In a suitable dynamical formalization, this is the spectral entropy of the associated linear map.

\[
\log M(P)=\sum_{\lambda \in \mathrm{Spec}(C_P)} \max(0,\log |\lambda|).
\]

### Lean 4 target
```lean
noncomputable def companionMatrixInt (P : Polynomial ℤ) : Matrix (Fin P.natDegree) (Fin P.natDegree) ℤ := ...

theorem logMahlerMeasure_eq_companion_spectral_sum
    (P : Polynomial ℤ)
    (hmonic : P.Monic)
    (hdeg : 0 < P.natDegree) :
    logMahlerMeasure P
      = ∑ λ in (Polynomial.mapMatrix
          (Int.castRingHom ℂ)
          (companionMatrixInt P)).charpoly.roots.toFinset,
          max 0 (Real.log ‖λ‖) := ...
```

A more robust alternative is to avoid explicit eigenvalue APIs and prove equality via characteristic polynomial of the companion matrix:

```lean
theorem logMahlerMeasure_eq_logMahlerMeasure_charpoly_companion
    (P : Polynomial ℤ)
    (hmonic : P.Monic)
    (hdeg : 0 < P.natDegree) :
    logMahlerMeasure P
      = logMahlerMeasure
          ((Matrix.companion (P.map (Int.castRingHom ℂ))).charpoly.map
            (Complex.ofRealCLM.toRingHom)) := ...
```

If the matrix API is cumbersome, prove the algebraic statement first and state entropy as a conjectural/formal interface theorem.

For monic integer polynomials,
\[

\[
\log M(P)=0 \implies P \text{ is cyclotomic up to powers of } X.

theorem logMahlerMeasure_eq_zero_iff_all_roots_norm_le_one

## Proof Architecture: 3 Strategic Routes

### Strategy A: Root-factorization first, then everything else
**Most promising.**

1. Define `logMahlerMeasure` by roots for `Polynomial ℂ`:
   \[
   \log M(P) := \log |a_d| + \sum_i \max(0,\log |\alpha_i|).
   \]
   Then specialize to monic integer polynomials, where the leading coefficient term vanishes.

2. Prove basic algebraic invariances:
   - multiplicativity under polynomial multiplication,
   - invariance under multiplication by roots of unity / cyclotomic factors,
   - nonnegativity for monic integer polynomials.

3. Derive cyclotomic and entropy consequences from this formula.

**Why this is strongest:** it avoids difficult measure-theoretic integration over the unit circle at the start and works directly with Mathlib’s polynomial root APIs.

---

### Strategy B: Jensen’s formula / circle integral route
**Most conceptually profound, but more API-heavy.**

1. Define
   \[
   \log M(P) := \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log|P(e^{it})|\,dt
   \]
   for complex polynomials, or as a restricted version sufficient for integer polynomials with no unit-circle zeros.

2. Use Jensen’s formula to prove equality with the root-factorization formula.

3. Then deduce arithmetic and dynamical consequences.

**Why this matters:** this route reveals Mahler measure as an information/energy average, which is the right gateway to entropy and statistical mechanics analogies.

**Why it is harder:** Lean’s analysis stack may make the full integral formalization expensive. Use this as a medium-term path, not necessarily the first theorem to land.

---

### Strategy C: Companion-matrix and spectral-radius route
**Best for cross-domain novelty.**

1. Define the companion matrix and prove its characteristic polynomial is \(P\).

2. Show roots of \(P\) coincide with eigenvalues of the companion matrix over \(\mathbb{C}\).

3. Define a spectral entropy functional
   \[
   h_{\mathrm{spec}}(A):=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathrm{Spec}(A)}\max(0,\log|\lambda|),
   \]
   then prove
   \[
   h_{\mathrm{spec}}(C_P)=\log M(P).
   \]

**Why this is revolutionary:** it reframes Lehmer’s problem as a lower-bound problem for entropy production in algebraic dynamics. This connects Diophantine approximation to dynamical systems in a formally checkable way.

## Cross-Domain Connections You Must Exploit

### 1. Arithmetic dynamics
The companion matrix of a monic integer polynomial defines an endomorphism of a rational vector space / lattice model. Its expanding directions are measured exactly by Mahler measure. This is the algebraic-dynamical heart of Lehmer’s problem.

Use `fixed_point_entropy_upper_bound` conceptually: even if the theorem is from a speculative file, mine its entropy formalism. Try to define an entropy-like invariant for companion dynamics and show Mahler measure controls or equals it.

### 2. Height theory
For an algebraic integer \(\alpha\), the logarithmic Weil height of \(\alpha\) is closely related to the Mahler measure of its minimal polynomial:
\[
\deg(\alpha)\, h(\alpha)=\log M(\mathrm{minpoly}_\alpha).
\]
A formal version of even a weak inequality here would be major. If full algebraic-number infrastructure is too heavy, prove it for roots of monic irreducible integer polynomials as a structured conjectural interface.

### 3. Entropy / thermodynamic formalism
Mahler measure is an average logarithmic energy on the unit circle. This creates a bridge to statistical mechanics and thermodynamic formalism. The theorem `fixed_point_entropy_upper_bound` suggests an avenue: certify that algebraic complexity of a polynomial bounds or equals a spectral entropy quantity.

### 4. Tropical and ultrametric viewpoints
Use `tropSupport_is_smallest_closed_support` and `ultrametric_proof_dynamics_diagonal_stability` conceptually, not decoratively. The bold conjectural direction is that archimedean Mahler measure and non-archimedean/tropical support data are two shadows of one global complexity invariant. Even a toy theorem comparing support size or coefficient geometry to a lower bound on positive root escape would be novel.

### 5. Lattice reduction analogy
`reduceOnce_measure_nonincreasing` hints at monotonicity of complexity under reduction operations. Seek a theorem that coefficient-reduction or factor-stripping operations do not increase log Mahler measure. This can become an algorithmic preprocessor for Lehmer searches.
