## Assignment: Direction 2 — Non-Cancellation Certificates and Coefficient-Aware Bounds

**Mode:** `prove`

Build a coefficient-sensitive bridge from combinatorial support lower bounds to genuine arithmetic-circuit lower bounds. The goal is not to repackage support-shadow statements, but to prove that under a mathematically sharp genericity certificate, the Hessian support is *forced* to realize the full second shadow, so the support-complexity lower bound becomes an honest lower bound for actual polynomials.

This would be a breakthrough because it attacks the central weakness of support-only arguments: cancellations. If you can formally isolate a verifiable regime in which cancellations provably cannot occur, then every combinatorial lower bound in the shadow model becomes a coefficient-aware lower bound in arithmetic complexity. That is the missing interface between tropical/combinatorial complexity and classical algebraic circuit complexity.

---

## Core Mathematical Objective

You should introduce a new formal notion of **non-cancellation certificate** for a polynomial and prove that it upgrades the shadow lower bound from supports to actual Hessian entries.

The conceptual target is:

> For a polynomial over a characteristic-zero domain, if every coefficient whose exponent lies in the second shadow is nonzero, then every Hessian monomial predicted by the support shadow actually occurs with nonzero coefficient. Consequently, any lower bound derived from the quadratic leaf shadow applies to the real polynomial, not merely its support skeleton.

This should be formalized at three levels:

1. **Exact support realization theorem**: Hessian-entry support equals second shadow under a coefficient nonvanishing hypothesis.
2. **Coefficient-aware lower bound theorem**: the shadow complexity lower bound applies to actual polynomials satisfying the certificate.
3. **Genericity theorem / conjectural genericity schema**: over an infinite field of characteristic zero, the certificate holds on a Zariski-open condition, or at minimum formulate this as a precise falsifiable conjecture with computational tests.

## Precise Theorem Targets

You should prove at least **3 nontrivial theorems**, all with multi-step reasoning. Use induction / `rcases` / `by_contra` / `field_simp` / long `calc` chains where appropriate.

### Theorem 1: Exact non-cancellation for Hessian coefficients
This should be the foundational bridge theorem.

**Mathematical statement**

Let `p` be a multivariate polynomial over `ℚ`. Assume every coefficient of `p` indexed by an exponent in the second shadow of `support p` is nonzero. Then for every pair of variables `i, j` and every exponent `d`, the coefficient of `X^d` in `∂ᵢ∂ⱼ p` is nonzero iff `d` lies in the quadratic leaf set / second shadow predicted by the support.

**Lean-style target**
```lean
theorem nonzero_hessian_coeff_iff_shadow
    {σ : Type*} [Fintype σ] [DecidableEq σ]
    (p : MvPolynomial σ ℚ)
    (hcert : NonCancellationCert p) :
    ∀ i j d,
      MvPolynomial.coeff d (MvPolynomial.pderiv i (MvPolynomial.pderiv j p)) ≠ 0 ↔
      d ∈ WeightedSupportShadow.quadLeafSet (MvPolynomial.support p) i j
```

**Build on catalog**
- `WeightedSupportShadow.nonzeroQuadLeafSet_eq_shadow`
- `WeightedSupportShadow.coeff_pderiv_pderiv_ne_zero_iff`

**Why this matters**
This theorem says the Hessian support is not merely bounded by the second shadow — it is *exactly equal* to it. That destroys the usual loophole where support combinatorics overpredicts actual algebra due to cancellation.

### Theorem 2: Coefficient-aware lift of the shadow lower bound
This is the actual complexity theorem.

**Mathematical statement**

Assume `p` satisfies the non-cancellation certificate. If the weighted support shadow yields a lower bound `L` on the number of quadratic leaves / support-circuit resources needed to realize the Hessian pattern, then the same `L` lower-bounds the corresponding arithmetic complexity of `p`.

You may need to formulate this relative to whatever complexity notion the catalog already supports.

**Lean-style target**
```lean
theorem coefficient_aware_shadow_lower_bound
    {σ : Type*} [Fintype σ] [DecidableEq σ]
    (p : MvPolynomial σ ℚ)
    (hcert : NonCancellationCert p) :
    WeightedSupportShadow.shadowLowerBound (MvPolynomial.support p) ≤
      ArithmeticComplexity.circuitSize p
```

If `ArithmeticComplexity.circuitSize` does not yet exist in usable form, prove instead a theorem of the shape:
```lean
theorem coefficient_aware_shadow_lower_bound'
    {σ : Type*} [Fintype σ] [DecidableEq σ]
    (p : MvPolynomial σ ℚ)
    (hcert : NonCancellationCert p) :
    WeightedSupportShadow.shadowLowerBound (MvPolynomial.support p) ≤
      SupportCircuit.realizationCost p
```
where `SupportCircuit.realizationCost p` is a new abstraction connecting actual circuits to support circuits.

**Why this matters**
This is the theorem that converts a combinatorial lower bound into a genuine arithmetic lower bound. It is the point where the theory becomes relevant to real circuit complexity.

### Theorem 3: Genericity via coefficient parameter space
You should either prove a rigorous theorem in a finite-dimensional coefficient family, or state a sharp conjecture plus computational test if the full theorem is too large.

A good theorem is:

**Mathematical statement**

Fix a finite support set `S`. In the affine space of polynomials supported on `S`, the subset of coefficient assignments satisfying the non-cancellation certificate is the complement of a finite union of coordinate hyperplanes, hence Zariski open and dense over any infinite field.

**Lean-style target**
If full Zariski-topology formalization is too expensive, prove the algebraic core:
```lean
theorem nonCancellationCert_generic_on_fixed_support
    {σ : Type*} [Fintype σ] [DecidableEq σ]
    (S : Finset (σ →₀ ℕ)) :
    ∃ U : Finset (σ →₀ ℕ),
      ∀ a : (σ →₀ ℕ) → ℚ,
        (∀ d ∈ U, a d ≠ 0) →
        NonCancellationCert (∑ d in S, MvPolynomial.C (a d) * MvPolynomial.monomial d 1)
```

A stronger version would identify `U` explicitly as the ancestor set of the second shadow.

**Why this matters**
This reframes the non-cancellation hypothesis as *generic rather than exceptional*. Then the coefficient-aware lower bound is not a special-case theorem but a theorem about a dense open set of polynomials.

## Proof Strategy Architecture

You must pursue one main strategy and mention alternatives.

### Strategy A — Direct coefficient transport through second derivatives
Most promising.

1. Use `coeff_pderiv_pderiv_ne_zero_iff` to characterize exactly when a Hessian coefficient can be nonzero in terms of an ancestor exponent in `p`.
2. Use the non-cancellation certificate to show that every such ancestor coefficient is indeed nonzero, so no predicted Hessian monomial disappears.
3. Invoke `nonzeroQuadLeafSet_eq_shadow` to identify the resulting nonzero coefficient set with the combinatorial second shadow, then transfer the existing shadow lower bound to the actual polynomial.

**Why most promising:** it leverages existing catalog lemmas exactly where they are strongest: coefficient-level characterization and support-shadow equality. It minimizes new infrastructure while producing a conceptually major theorem.

### Strategy B — Finset/combinatorial shadow induction
A good backup.

1. Define the ancestor map from Hessian exponents to source exponents.
2. Prove by induction on total degree or support size that every shadow exponent has a unique ancestor under second differentiation.
3. Deduce exact support realization by reducing nonvanishing to the ancestor coefficient.

**Why useful:** if the catalog lemmas are not in the right normal form, this route reconstructs the combinatorial heart from first principles.

### Strategy C — Parameter-space genericity argument
For the genericity theorem.

1. Fix a finite support `S` and parameterize coefficients by functions `a : S → ℚ`.
2. Identify the certificate as finitely many inequalities `a e ≠ 0`.
3. Conclude that the good locus is the complement of finitely many coordinate hyperplanes; over an infinite field this is dense/open informally, or at least nonempty with explicit witnesses formally.

**Why important:** this explains why the certificate is mathematically natural rather than ad hoc.

## Conjecture With Testable Prediction

### Conjecture: Generic Hessian shadow exactness over characteristic zero
For any finite support set `S ⊆ ℕ^n`, there exists a Zariski-open dense set of coefficient assignments on `S` such that for every polynomial `p` with support exactly `S` in this set, every Hessian entry has support exactly equal to the second shadow predicted from `S`.

Possible formal statement:
```lean
conjecture generic_hessian_support_exact
    {σ : Type*} [Fintype σ] [DecidableEq σ]
    (S : Finset (σ →₀ ℕ)) :
    GenericOnSupport S
      (fun p =>
        ∀ i j,
          MvPolynomial.support (MvPolynomial.pderiv i (MvPolynomial.pderiv j p)) =
          WeightedSupportShadow.quadLeafSet S i j)
```

### Computational test
For randomly sampled sparse supports `S` and random nonzero rational coefficients:
- compute the predicted second shadow;
- compute actual Hessian-entry supports;
- check equality across all `(i,j)`;
- search for counterexamples when coefficients are allowed to be zero or when characteristic is small.

**Falsifiable prediction**
- Over `ℚ` with random nonzero coefficients: equality should hold overwhelmingly, plausibly always under the certificate.
- Over finite fields of small characteristic: failures should appear due to scalar annihilation (e.g. second derivative coefficient factors becoming zero mod `p`).

This gives a sharp science-style discriminator between characteristic zero and small characteristic behavior.

## Characteristic-Zero Insight You Should Exploit

Do not leave the characteristic assumption as decoration. The second derivative coefficient of a monomial is scaled by factors like
- `d i * d j` for `i ≠ j`,
- `d i * (d i - 1)` for `i = j`.

Over `ℚ`, these factors vanish exactly when the combinatorics says differentiation should kill the monomial. Over positive characteristic, they may vanish spuriously. This is the deep reason the coefficient-aware bridge is natural in characteristic zero and subtle in finite characteristic.

A valuable theorem or lemma here is:

```lean
theorem hessian_scalar_nonzero_of_shadow_condition
    {σ : Type*} [DecidableEq σ]
    (d : σ →₀ ℕ) (i j : σ)
    (h : ShadowSecondEligible d i j) :
    hessianScalar d i j ≠ (0 : ℚ)
```

This is where `field_simp`, arithmetic lemmas, and case splitting on `i = j` can produce a genuinely nontrivial proof.
