## Precise Theorem Target

Let \(E \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n\). At scale \(\delta > 0\), let:
- \(\mathcal Q_\delta(E)\) be a finite family of \(\delta\)-cubes covering \(E\),
- \(\mathcal T_\delta\) be a finite family of \(\delta\)-tubes, one for each direction in a \(\delta\)-net of \(S^{n-1}\),
- each tube \(T \in \mathcal T_\delta\) is required to intersect the cover of \(E\),
- \(N_\delta(E) := |\mathcal Q_\delta(E)|\),
- \(M_\delta := |\mathcal T_\delta|\),
- and define the pairwise intersection parameter
  \[
  \mathrm{PairEnergy}_\delta(\mathcal T_\delta)
  := \sum_{T,U \in \mathcal T_\delta} |\{Q \in \mathcal Q_\delta(E) : Q \cap T \neq \emptyset,\; Q \cap U \neq \emptyset\}|.
  \]

Assume each tube meets at least \(L_\delta\) cubes of the cover. Then a Cauchy–Schwarz/double-counting mechanism should yield
\[
N_\delta(E) \;\ge\; \frac{(M_\delta L_\delta)^2}{\mathrm{PairEnergy}_\delta(\mathcal T_\delta)}.
\]

If furthermore:
- \(M_\delta \gtrsim \delta^{-(n-1)}\),
- \(L_\delta \gtrsim \delta^{-1}\),
- \(\mathrm{PairEnergy}_\delta(\mathcal T_\delta) \lesssim \delta^{-(n+\alpha)}\),

then
\[
N_\delta(E) \gtrsim \delta^{-(n-\alpha)}.
\]
This implies the lower Minkowski dimension is at least \(n-\alpha\), and under appropriate comparison hypotheses, the Hausdorff dimension is at least \(n-\alpha\).

### Breakthrough theorem to formalize first

You should **not** begin by formalizing the full Besicovitch-set statement. First isolate and prove the discrete engine.

### Theorem A: Discrete incidence lower bound
For any finite family of cells and tubes with incidence relation \(I(Q,T)\), if every tube is incident to at least \(L\) cells and the pairwise energy
\[
\sum_{T,U} |\{Q : I(Q,T)\land I(Q,U)\}| \le P,
\]
then the number of cells satisfies
\[
|\mathcal Q| \ge \frac{(|\mathcal T|L)^2}{P}.
\]

This is the combinatorial heart. It is general, reusable, and likely easiest to certify in Lean.

### Theorem B: Scale-exponent bootstrap
Suppose for all sufficiently small \(\delta\), a discretization satisfies
- \(M_\delta \ge c_M \delta^{-(n-1)}\),
- \(L_\delta \ge c_L \delta^{-1}\),
- \(P_\delta \le C_P \delta^{-(n+\alpha)}\),
- \(N_\delta(E)\) is bounded below by Theorem A.

Then there exists \(C>0\) such that
\[
N_\delta(E) \ge C \delta^{-(n-\alpha)}
\]
for all sufficiently small \(\delta\).

This theorem extracts the exponent arithmetic.

### Theorem C: Dimension transfer theorem
Assume \(E\) is bounded and there exists \(s\in\mathbb R\) and \(C>0\) such that for all sufficiently small \(\delta\),
\[
N_\delta(E) \ge C \delta^{-s}.
\]
Then the lower Minkowski dimension of \(E\) is at least \(s\). If you can leverage existing Mathlib definitions/lemmas relating Hausdorff dimension and box-counting dimension, derive
\[
s \le \dim_H(E)
\]
under the strongest available formal hypotheses.

If the full Hausdorff comparison is currently too library-heavy, prove the lower Minkowski bound cleanly and state the Hausdorff step as a sharply formulated conjectural extension. But push hard: if Mathlib already contains enough dimension infrastructure, this is where the result becomes field-opening.

## Exact Mathematical Statement You Should Aim to Prove

### Main discrete theorem
For all finite sets of cells \(Q\) and tubes \(T\), and incidence relation \(I \subseteq Q \times T\),
\[
\left(\sum_{t \in T} \deg(t)\right)^2
\le |Q| \sum_{t,u \in T} |\{q \in Q : I(q,t)\wedge I(q,u)\}|.
\]
Hence if \(\deg(t)\ge L\) for every \(t\), then
\[
|Q| \ge \frac{(|T|L)^2}{\sum_{t,u} |\{q : I(q,t)\wedge I(q,u)\}|}.
\]

This is the exact finite theorem from which the geometric application flows.

### Main geometric corollary
Let \(E \subseteq [0,1]^n\). Suppose for every sufficiently small \(\delta\), there is a finite discretization \((\mathcal Q_\delta,\mathcal T_\delta)\) such that:
1. \(\mathcal Q_\delta\) is a \(\delta\)-cube cover of \(E\),
2. \(|\mathcal T_\delta| \ge c_M \delta^{-(n-1)}\),
3. every \(T\in\mathcal T_\delta\) meets at least \(c_L \delta^{-1}\) cubes,
4. the pair-energy satisfies
   \[
   \sum_{T,U \in \mathcal T_\delta}
   |\{Q \in \mathcal Q_\delta : Q\cap T\neq\emptyset,\ Q\cap U\neq\emptyset\}|
   \le C_P \delta^{-(n+\alpha)}.
   \]
Then the lower box dimension of \(E\) is at least \(n-\alpha\).

### Ambitious extension
If you can connect lower box dimension to Hausdorff dimension under additional regularity assumptions on the discretization, formulate and prove:
\[
\dim_H(E)\ge n-\alpha.
\]

Be honest about what is fully proved and what remains conjectural, but push the frontier.

## Proof Strategy Architecture

### Strategy A: Pure double-counting + Cauchy–Schwarz + exponent bookkeeping
This is the most promising route.

1. **Incidence function.**
   Define
   \[
   f(q):=|\{t : I(q,t)\}|.
   \]
   Then
   \[
   \sum_q f(q)=\sum_t \deg(t).
   \]

2. **Energy identity.**
   Observe
   \[
   \sum_q f(q)^2
   = \sum_{t,u} |\{q : I(q,t)\wedge I(q,u)\}|.
   \]

3. **Cauchy–Schwarz.**
   Apply
   \[
   \left(\sum_q f(q)\right)^2 \le |Q| \sum_q f(q)^2.
   \]
   This yields Theorem A.

4. **Scale substitution.**
   Insert \(M_\delta\), \(L_\delta\), \(P_\delta\) asymptotics and simplify exponents:
   \[
   \frac{(\delta^{-(n-1)}\delta^{-1})^2}{\delta^{-(n+\alpha)}}=\delta^{-(n-\alpha)}.
   \]

5. **Dimension passage.**
   Translate lower covering-number growth to lower box dimension, then to Hausdorff dimension if available.

Why this is best: it is structurally robust, library-light, and formalizes a universal inequality rather than a delicate geometric estimate.

### Strategy B: Bipartite graph energy formalization
Recast the entire problem as a theorem about a finite bipartite graph \(G=(Q,T,E)\).

1. Define left vertices = cells, right vertices = tubes.
2. Pair-energy becomes the sum of codegrees on the right:
   \[
   \sum_{t,u} \operatorname{codeg}(t,u).
   \]
3. Prove the lower bound using graph degree moments.
4. Then instantiate the graph with geometric data.

Why this is attractive: it reveals the theorem as a graph-theoretic phenomenon, opening immediate transfer to additive combinatorics, sparse matrices, and communication complexity. This is the strongest cross-domain abstraction.

### Strategy C: Measure-theoretic shadow of the discrete argument
If you can access finite-measure machinery, define
\[
F(x)=\sum_{T\in\mathcal T_\delta} 1_T(x)
\]
or the cell-count analogue, then use
\[
\|F\|_1^2 \le |\operatorname{supp}F| \|F\|_2^2.
\]
This is the \(L^1\)-\(L^2\) incarnation of Strategy A.

Why it matters: this connects directly to harmonic analysis and may later support formalized maximal-function arguments. However, it is probably heavier than necessary for the first breakthrough.

5. **Finite Euclidean discretization ↔ finite field analogies**  
   Once the theorem is graph-theoretic, the same formal engine may apply to finite-field Kakeya incidence systems.
