## Core Vision

The usual renormalization group acts on couplings by coarse-graining. Here, the proposal is that a coupling parameter `g : ℝ` is first compactified to a projective/energy sphere, then evolved by changing the stereographic pole, and finally returned to affine coordinates. The resulting map should behave like a geometric renormalization step.

The breakthrough target is to show that:

1. **Iterated pole-change maps form a nontrivial dynamical system** on coupling space.
2. **Fixed points of this system are geometric critical couplings**.
3. **The linearization of the pole-change map at a fixed point defines a geometric beta observable**, and under suitable assumptions it matches the infinitesimal RG beta function.
4. **Energy conservation / Hamiltonian invariants constrain the RG flow**, creating a rigorous bridge between conformal compactification and physical scale flow.

If successful, this opens a new field: **geometric renormalization via stereographic dynamics**, with downstream implications for conformal field theory, exactly solvable models, Hamiltonian systems, and algorithmic symbolic RG.

### Theorem 1: Nontriviality of moving-pole RG

## Proof Strategy Architecture

You must include multi-step proofs; no trivial automation-only file. Here are 3 strategy routes.

### Strategy A: Algebraic/Möbius classification of pole dynamics
Most promising for the first theorem cluster.

1. **Expand `rgUpdate` explicitly** using the definition of `pole_map`.
2. **Use `pole_map_is_involution`** to simplify repeated pole applications and isolate the truly nontrivial two-pole composition.
3. **Reduce fixed-point and identity questions to rational/algebraic equations**, then solve them with `field_simp`, `ring`, `nlinarith`, and structured case splits on singularities.

Why promising: if `pole_map` is rational/Möbius, all core statements become exact algebraic identities and Lean is strong here.

---

### Strategy B: Dynamical systems viewpoint via linearization
Best for the derivative / beta-function theorem.

1. Prove that `rgUpdate a b` is differentiable away from singularities.
2. Compute `deriv (rgUpdate a b)` by chain rule and explicit derivative formulas for `pole_map`.
3. At fixed points, simplify the derivative expression and derive local attraction/repulsion criteria.

Why promising: this gives the physically meaningful “beta = linearization” bridge. Even a partial derivative formula is publishable if exact.

---

### Strategy C: Energy/Hamiltonian compatibility route
Best for the cross-domain theorem.

1. Start from `energy_derivative_zero_of_solution` to obtain a conserved scalar quantity along trajectories.
2. Show `rgUpdate` acts by a conformal/projective reparameterization that preserves or controls this scalar.
3. Conclude invariance or boundedness of the RG observable along physical solutions.

Why promising: this is the deepest conceptual bridge and least likely to be already in the literature or catalog.
