## Core Research Objective

### Precise theorem statement

Let `G` be a finite connected graph, let `S ⊆ V(G)` be nonempty and separated in the sense already developed in the catalog, and let `L_S` denote the Laplacian restricted to the `S`-coordinates modulo constants / normalization constraints. Let `CanonicalKernelSpan(G,S)` be the additive subgroup generated by the canonical harmonic kernel generators attached to `S`, and let `Constants` denote the constant functions. Then prove:

> **Main Theorem (constructive SNF correspondence).**  
> For every finite connected graph `G` and every nonempty separated subset `S`, there exists an explicit additive equivalence
> \[
> \Phi_{G,S} :
> \bigl(\mathrm{CanonicalKernelSpan}(G,S) / \mathrm{Constants}\bigr)
> \;\cong\;
> \mathbb Z^{|S|} / \operatorname{Im}(L_S),
> \]
> and moreover if `U L_S V = D` is a Smith normal form decomposition, then `Φ_{G,S}` is computable from the transition matrices `U,V`, with the induced coordinates on the quotient identifying the invariant factors of the canonical kernel quotient with the nonzero diagonal entries of `D`.

This should not be an abstract nonconstructive isomorphism. The point is to **track generators through the SNF basis change** and prove that the resulting map is independent of choices up to canonical equivalence.

## Proof architecture: 3 strategy paths

### Strategy A: Quotient-lattice comparison through harmonic uniqueness
**Most promising.**

1. Define the map from canonical generators to integer boundary data modulo the Laplacian image.
2. Prove kernel = constants by contradiction using separation and normalized harmonic uniqueness from the catalog.
3. Prove surjectivity by constructing a harmonic lift for each boundary datum and showing it lies in the canonical span.

**Why this is strongest:** it uses exactly the catalog’s mature machinery (`harmonic_normalized_unique`, separation results, firing equivalence transport) and produces the conceptual theorem before introducing SNF coordinates. Then SNF is a corollary that enriches the classification.

---

### Strategy B: Smith normal form first, then identify the tropical basis
1. Compute an SNF decomposition `U L_S V = D`.
2. Show the canonical kernel generators define a lattice map into `ℤ^|S|`.
3. Prove that in SNF coordinates the image has the same invariant factors, and use unimodularity of `U,V` to construct the equivalence.

**Why it is powerful:** this gives the most algorithmic result and directly produces the computational method.  
**Risk:** Mathlib’s SNF API may be more cumbersome than the harmonic side, so this may be harder formally at first.

---

### Strategy C: Categorical / exact-sequence route
1. Place constants, canonical kernel span, integer vertex functions, and Laplacian image into a short exact sequence.
2. Identify the quotient with a cokernel via universal properties.
3. Then specialize to SNF to extract invariant factors.

**Why it matters:** this is the cleanest mathematical narrative and would generalize best to divisors, Jacobians, and sheaf-theoretic Laplacians.  
**Risk:** it may require more category-theoretic scaffolding than needed for the first breakthrough.

**Recommendation:** Prove the theorem via Strategy A, enrich it algorithmically via Strategy B, and explain Strategy C in `FUTURE_DIRECTIONS.md` as the route to higher-dimensional tropical homology.

Then your theorem identifies the space of “charge-balanced harmonic modes modulo gauge” with the arithmetic torsion of the network. This is a genuine bridge between tropical graph theory and discrete electrostatics / topological phases on networks.
