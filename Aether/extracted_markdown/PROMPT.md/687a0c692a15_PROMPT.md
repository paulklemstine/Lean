### 2. Focal rigidity under certified witness equivalence

The real breakthrough starts here. You need a theorem saying that if two geodesic families have the same focal profile, then they induce the same nontrivial divisor witness data. Since “same focal profile” can be encoded in multiple ways, I recommend first proving a version at the level of equality of minimizer sets or equality of tropical potentials.

A robust first formal version is:

```lean
def FocalSet
    {N : ℕ} (L : BerggrenLensData N) (S : Finset PrimitiveTriple) : Finset PrimitiveTriple :=
  L.V.filter (fun v => IsFocalMinimizer L S v)

def SameFactorPartition
    {N : ℕ} (L : BerggrenLensData N)
    (S T : Finset PrimitiveTriple) : Prop :=
  ∀ d : Nat, (∃ v ∈ FocalSet L S, L.factorWitness v d) ↔
             (∃ v ∈ FocalSet L T, L.factorWitness v d)
```

Then prove a rigidity theorem of the form:

```lean
theorem focal_rigidity_of_equal_potential
    {N : ℕ} (L : BerggrenLensData N)
    (S T : Finset PrimitiveTriple)
    (hEq : ∀ v, v ∈ L.V → tropicalPotential L S v = tropicalPotential L T v) :
    SameFactorPartition L S T
```

This is already substantial: it says tropical-optical indistinguishability forces arithmetic indistinguishability at the factor-partition level.

A stronger version, closer to the informal brief, would assume a “split-nontriviality” hypothesis on Gram defects:

```lean
def NontrivialGramSplit
    {N : ℕ} (L : BerggrenLensData N) (S : Finset PrimitiveTriple) : Prop :=
  ∃ u ∈ S, ∃ v ∈ S, L.gramDefect u ≠ L.gramDefect v
```

and then prove uniqueness/canonicity of the factor partition attached to the focal set.

### 3. Factor extraction from strict focal split

This is the theorem that turns geometry into arithmetic. It should say: if the focal structure exhibits a strict split and the witness predicate is sound, then one can extract a genuine nontrivial factorization of `N`.

First define a soundness interface:

```lean
def FactorWitnessSound
    {N : ℕ} (L : BerggrenLensData N) : Prop :=
  ∀ v d, L.factorWitness v d → d ∣ N ∧ 1 < d ∧ d < N
```

Then define a strict split condition, for example by existence of two focal minimizers supporting incompatible witnesses:

```lean
def StrictFocalSplit
    {N : ℕ} (L : BerggrenLensData N) (S : Finset PrimitiveTriple) : Prop :=
  ∃ v₁ ∈ FocalSet L S, ∃ v₂ ∈ FocalSet L S, v₁ ≠ v₂ ∧
    ∃ d e, L.factorWitness v₁ d ∧ L.factorWitness v₂ e ∧ d * e = N
```

Now prove:

```lean
theorem extract_factors_of_strict_focal_split
    {N : ℕ} (hN : 1 < N) (L : BerggrenLensData N)
    (hsound : FactorWitnessSound L)
    (S : Finset PrimitiveTriple)
    (hsplit : StrictFocalSplit L S) :
    ∃ d e : Nat, d * e = N ∧ 1 < d ∧ d < N ∧ 1 < e ∧ e < N
```

This theorem is the heart of the program. It says tropical geodesic decoding is not a heuristic: under a certified split hypothesis, it produces a bona fide factor certificate.

## Most Promising Proof Architectures

You asked for 2–3 proof strategy steps. Here are three distinct routes; pursue them in this order.

### Strategy A: Abstract finite optimization first, arithmetic second
**Most promising for Lean progress.**

1. **Build an abstract finite tropical optimization layer.**  
   Ignore Pythagorean specifics initially. Prove existence of minimizers, stability under equal potentials, and extraction lemmas from witness predicates on arbitrary finite weighted structures.

2. **Instantiate with Berggren lens data.**  
   Show that your concrete Berggren complex satisfies the hypotheses of the abstract layer. This modularizes the hard arithmetic definitions from the optimization proofs.

3. **Import certified extraction machinery.**  
   Connect witness predicates to factor certificates using `berggren_certified_extraction_pipeline`.

**Why this is best:** it isolates the theorem-proving burden into finite combinatorics and order theory, which Lean handles well. It also lets you prove genuinely new theorems even if the arithmetic lens definitions remain partially abstract at first.

### Strategy B: Gram-defect monotonicity along Berggren edges
**Most exciting mathematically if it works.**

1. Define a concrete `gramDefect : PrimitiveTriple → ℤ` or `ℕ` using residue-compatible quadratic discrepancy relative to `N`.

2. Prove a **monotonicity or convexity lemma** along Berggren transforms: descendants move defect in controlled ways, ideally with a min-plus subadditivity property.

3. Deduce that equal focal profiles force equal defect partitions, and then prove that nontrivial defect splitting determines divisor witnesses.

**Why this could be revolutionary:** it would make the Berggren tree itself into a renormalization flow for factor information. That is much stronger than a generic finite optimization argument and would create a reusable arithmetic dynamics framework.

---

### Strategy C: Certified robustness transfer from ML-style margin bounds
**Cross-pollination route; surprisingly powerful.**

1. Treat focal minimizers as “predicted classes” in a min-plus classifier on triples.  
   A strict focal gap becomes a **margin**.

2. Use `certified_robustness_from_margin_and_lipschitz` analogically: prove that under a Lipschitz-type bound on tropical potential, focal minimizers are stable under local perturbations of residue or defect data.

3. Upgrade stability to rigidity: if two geodesic families share focal profiles within a certified margin, then they must yield the same factor witness set.

**Why this matters:** it imports certification technology from geometric deep learning into arithmetic decoding. That is exactly the kind of impossible-looking bridge that creates a new area.
