**Mode:** `prove`

## Core breakthrough theorem to target

Let `K₀ ⊆ K₁ ⊆ ... ⊆ K_n` be a filtration of finite simplicial complexes obtained by adding exactly one simplex at each step, ordered by nondecreasing weight. For each degree `d`, define the **tropical Morse event** at step `i` to be:
- `birth` if `β_d(K_{i+1}) = β_d(K_i) + 1`,
- `death` if `β_{d-1}(K_{i+1}) = β_{d-1}(K_i) - 1`,
- `neutral` otherwise.

The central conjectural theorem should be sharpened into a precise formal statement:

> **Theorem (simplex insertion dichotomy in degree `d`)**  
> Let `K' = K ∪ {σ}` where `σ` is a newly added `d`-simplex and all proper faces of `σ` already belong to `K`. Then exactly one of the following holds:
> 1. the boundary class `∂σ` is nontrivial in `H_{d-1}(K)`, and adjoining `σ` kills that class, so `β_{d-1}(K') = β_{d-1}(K) - 1` and `β_d(K') = β_d(K)`;
> 2. the boundary class `∂σ` is trivial in `H_{d-1}(K)`, and adjoining `σ` creates a new `d`-cycle, so `β_{d-1}(K') = β_{d-1}(K)` and `β_d(K') = β_d(K) + 1`.
>
> In particular,
> \[
> (\beta_d(K')-\beta_d(K)) - (\beta_{d-1}(K')-\beta_{d-1}(K)) = 1,
> \]
> and the event is uniquely classified as either a degree-`d` birth or a degree-`(d-1)` death.

This is the higher-dimensional analog of the graph edge insertion dichotomy, but now the proof must use actual chain-complex reasoning, not graph counting. This is the point where the theory becomes real.

## Proof strategy architecture

### Strategy A: Relative homology / long exact sequence approach
This is the conceptually cleanest route and likely the most scalable.

1. Model the insertion `K ⊆ K' = K ∪ {σ}` as a pair `(K', K)` with quotient chain complex supported in degree `d`.
2. Show `H_q(K',K)=0` for `q ≠ d` and `H_d(K',K) ≅ 𝕜` (or `ℤ` if you can manage torsion carefully, but a field is much easier).
3. Use exactness of the long exact sequence:
   \[
   H_d(K) \to H_d(K') \to H_d(K',K) \xrightarrow{\partial} H_{d-1}(K) \to H_{d-1}(K')
   \]
   and classify the two possibilities by whether the connecting map sends the generator to zero or not.

**Why this is most promising:** it exposes the theorem as a structural fact of homological algebra, not a combinatorial accident. It also scales to arbitrary degree and immediately explains the birth/death dichotomy.

### Strategy B: Rank-nullity on boundary matrices
This is computationally explicit and ideal for algorithm extraction.

1. Represent the filtered complex via boundary matrices `∂_d`.
2. Analyze how adjoining one `d`-simplex adds one column to `∂_d` and one basis vector to `C_d`.
3. Prove that either the new column increases `rank(∂_d)` by 1, killing one `(d-1)`-class, or it lies in the existing image constraints and contributes one new kernel dimension, creating a `d`-class.

**Why this is powerful:** it leads directly to a verified algorithm for classifying simplex insertions and computing tropical event data. This should drive `demo.py`.

### Strategy C: Discrete Morse / tropical energy landscape interpretation
This is the visionary route.

1. Define a tropical energy associated to simplex weight and boundary support.
2. Interpret insertion events as crossing a tropical critical threshold.
3. Show that the criticality condition is equivalent to the homological birth/death criterion.

**Why this matters:** this is the route to the future theory, but it should likely come after the relative-homology proof. Use it to motivate definitions and to formulate conjectures, not as the first proof attempt unless the supporting catalog is unexpectedly strong.
