## Central Breakthrough Target

### Precise theorem statement

Formalize a metric or pseudometric on tropical persistence barcodes and prove a Lipschitz stability theorem controlled by local graph complexity.

A mathematically sharp target is:

> **Theorem (Tropical barcode stability under vertex-set perturbation).**  
> Let `G` be a finite simple graph of maximum degree at most `D`. Let `F, F' : ℝ → Set V` be two monotone vertex filtrations on `G`, and assume their indexing functions are uniformly `ε`-close in the sense that corresponding vertices enter the filtration at times differing by at most `ε`. Then the tropical barcode distance between the associated tropical persistence barcodes is bounded by
> \[
> d_T(\operatorname{TPB}(F), \operatorname{TPB}(F')) \le C(D)\,\varepsilon,
> \]
> where `C(D)` depends only on the degree bound, and ideally one can take `C(D) = D + 1` or another explicit linear function of `D`.

This should not remain at the slogan level. You should define the distance and prove the bound.

## Proof architecture: three viable strategies

You must pursue one strategy fully, but document the alternatives in comments or the paper.

### Strategy A: Atomic event induction via single-vertex perturbations
1. Prove the one-vertex perturbation lemma: moving one vertex’s filtration time changes `β₁` by at most the number of incident edges and changes `κ_q` by at most the number of touched components.
2. Sum these local changes along a path from `f` to `g`, obtained by reordering vertex events one at a time.
3. Convert cumulative event discrepancy into a barcode-distance bound via reconstruction.

**Why this is promising:** It directly uses the decomposition `δ = β₁ + κ_q` and mirrors the event-based infrastructure already present in `FiltrationPersistence.lean`. This is likely the shortest path to a real theorem in Lean.

### Strategy B: Interleaving-style proof
1. Define an `ε`-interleaving notion for tropical filtrations or tropical event profiles.
2. Show that `ε`-close vertex entrance functions induce a `(Cε)`-interleaving on the tropical kernel-dimension process.
3. Deduce a bottleneck-style stability theorem from interleaving.

**Why this is deeper:** If successful, this opens a full tropical analogue of the classical interleaving/stability paradigm in persistent homology. It is more conceptually revolutionary, but likely requires more new definitions.

### Strategy C: Reconstruction-first proof
1. Use the barcode reconstruction theorem from the catalog to express the barcode entirely in terms of event increments of `δ`.
2. Prove Lipschitz control directly on the reconstruction data rather than on the barcode objects themselves.
3. Lift the bound to the barcode metric by proving reconstruction is nonexpansive.

**Why this may be Lean-friendly:** It avoids building a sophisticated matching theory too early. If the catalog’s reconstruction theorem is already strong, this may be the cleanest formal route.

**Recommendation:** Start with **Strategy A**, then extract the profile/reconstruction layer from **Strategy C** to obtain the clean final theorem. Strategy B should be recorded as the conceptual next step in `FUTURE_DIRECTIONS.md`.

## Mathematical insight you should exploit

The decomposition
\[
\delta(S) = \beta_1(S) + \kappa_q(S)
\]
is not just bookkeeping. It splits the tropical invariant into:

- a **topological term** `β₁`, sensitive to cycle creation and destruction,
- a **visibility / interaction term** `κ_q`, encoding how the tropical structure sees components or reachable regions.

For a single vertex insertion:
- `β₁` can increase only through incident edges closing independent cycles, hence bounded by local degree;
- `κ_q` changes only through components touched by the new vertex, also locally bounded.

That is exactly the mechanism needed for a stability theorem with a constant depending on maximum degree rather than graph size. If you prove this cleanly, you have created the first robust perturbation theory for tropical persistence on graphs.

### 1. Spectral graph theory
Degree bounds imply Laplacian norm bounds. Therefore tropical barcode stability can be reinterpreted as a spectral-regularity phenomenon for graph filtrations.

### 2. Topological data analysis
This is a tropical analogue of the Cohen–Steiner–Edelsbrunner–Harer stability philosophy. The difference is that your invariant is enriched by tropical visibility structure, not just homological persistence.

### 3. Discrete geometry / metric geometry
The filtration perturbation is a metric perturbation on entrance-time functions. Stability says the barcode is a Lipschitz observable of a combinatorial metric structure.

### 4. Statistical mechanics / network science
A stable tropical barcode becomes a robust observable for noisy graph processes, percolation-style growth, and random network evolution.
