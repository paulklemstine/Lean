## Primary Theorem Target

### Core mathematical statement

Let:
- `X` be a finite type,
- `cl : Set X → Set X` be a closure operator (extensive, monotone, idempotent),
- `I` a finite index type,
- `U : I → Set X` a finite family of `cl`-closed sets whose union generates `X` in the closure sense:
  `cl (⋃ i, U i) = Set.univ`,
- `F` a presheaf of idempotent semimodules on the poset of `cl`-closed subsets,
- `sᵢ ∈ F(Uᵢ)` local predictors.

Assume:
1. **pairwise compatibility**: restrictions of `sᵢ` and `sⱼ` to `cl`-closed overlaps agree,
2. **finite acyclic descent / Helly-style nerve exactness**: compatible local data on the cover has at most one amalgamation, and every finite compatible family with vanishing overlap defect glues,
3. **tropical extension functional** `E : F(X) → α` (for an ordered idempotent codomain) satisfies:
   - `E(s) = ⨆ i defect(restrict s (U i), sᵢ)`,
   - `E(s) = ⊥` iff all restrictions agree exactly.

Then there exists a unique `s : F(X)` such that all restrictions recover `sᵢ`, and this `s` is the unique minimizer of `E`.

This theorem should be stated in Lean in a finite, structurally realizable way.

## Definitions to introduce cleanly

You should create finite, Lean-friendly definitions rather than overgeneralized category-theoretic ones.

### 1. Closure operator / nucleus
A structure such as:

```lean
structure FinClosureSpace (X : Type*) where
  cl : Set X → Set X
  extensive : ∀ s, s ⊆ cl s
  monotone : ∀ ⦃s t⦄, s ⊆ t → cl s ⊆ cl t
  idempotent : ∀ s, cl (cl s) = cl s
```

### 2. Closed cover and closure nerve
Define the nerve combinatorially:
- vertices: indices `i : I`,
- simplices: finite subsets `J : Finset I` such that `⋂ j ∈ J, U j` is nonempty or closure-nontrivial.

You may only need **pairwise overlap nonemptiness** and a finite Helly-style condition, not full simplicial machinery.

### 3. Presheaf of idempotent concept semimodules
If full semimodule over a tropical semiring is too heavy, start with:
- ordered idempotent commutative monoid / semilattice structure on sections,
- restriction maps preserving inf/sup.

A lightweight structure suffices for the theorem if the variational functional only uses order/idempotence.

### 4. Tropical disagreement functional
Define:
- local defect,
- global extension cost as supremum/max of local defects,
- exact consistency as defect `= ⊥` or `= 0`.

This makes the optimization theorem straightforward.

## Proof strategy architecture

You must include at least 2–3 plausible proof routes in the file comments or surrounding documentation, and pursue the most Lean-feasible one first.

### Strategy A: Direct finite descent on the closure lattice
**Most promising for Lean.**

1. Define a finite presheaf on `cl`-closed sets with explicit restriction maps.
2. Express compatibility only on pairwise overlaps.
3. Postulate or prove a finite gluing principle from a Helly-style closure property of the cover.
4. Derive existence and uniqueness of a global section by explicit construction on `Set.univ`.
5. Show that the tropical disagreement functional vanishes exactly on glued sections.
6. Use uniqueness of gluing to deduce uniqueness of the minimizer.

Why promising:
- avoids heavy category theory,
- works with finite types and `Set`,
- lets you encode all descent data as elementary equalities.

### Strategy B: Variational reconstruction first, descent second
1. Define the global extension functional `E(g)` as the supremum of local tropical defects.
2. Prove that `E(g)=⊥` iff `g` restricts exactly to all local predictors.
3. Show existence of a minimizer using finite generation / finite decomposition.
4. Prove that any minimizer with value `⊥` yields a glued section.
5. Use uniqueness of exact gluing to conclude uniqueness of the minimizer.

Why valuable:
- gives the optimization theorem as the primary object,
- aligns directly with machine learning interpretation,
- makes algorithm extraction easier.

Potential issue:
- existence of minimizers can be technically heavier unless codomain/section spaces are finite.

### Strategy C: Nerve-based combinatorial exactness
1. Define a finite nerve of the closed cover.
2. Formalize a Helly-acyclic assumption ensuring pairwise consistency propagates to all finite intersections.
3. Prove a finite Čech-style equalizer theorem: global sections are the equalizer of product and overlap-restriction maps.
4. Transport this equalizer characterization into tropical minimization.

Why revolutionary:
- this is the mathematically deepest formulation,
- it points toward future cohomological learning theory.

Why less promising initially:
- simplicial/Čech formalization overhead in Lean may be nontrivial.

**Recommendation:** Prove Strategy A fully, derive Strategy B as a corollary, and leave Strategy C partially scaffolded as the next frontier.
