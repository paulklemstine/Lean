## Precise theorem statements to formalize

You must prove at least **3 deep theorems** beyond catalog correctness. The following are the recommended flagship results.

### Theorem 1: Special soundness / witness extraction
This is the central theorem and should be treated as the primary breakthrough.

**Mathematical statement.**  
Let `G` be a cyclic group generated by `g` of order `q`. Suppose two accepting Schnorr transcripts
`(a, c₁, z₁)` and `(a, c₂, z₂)` exist for the same statement `y`, with `c₁ ≠ c₂`, and assume `c₁ - c₂` is invertible modulo `q`. Then there exists `w mod q` such that `y = g^w`, and moreover
`w ≡ (z₁ - z₂) * (c₁ - c₂)⁻¹ [ZMOD q]`.

**Lean 4 target signature.**
A plausible signature is:

```lean
theorem schnorr_special_soundness_extract
    {G : Type*} [CommGroup G]
    (g y a : G) (q c₁ c₂ z₁ z₂ : ℕ)
    (hg : orderOf g = q)
    (hacc₁ : g ^ z₁ = a * y ^ c₁)
    (hacc₂ : g ^ z₂ = a * y ^ c₂)
    (hneq : c₁ ≠ c₂)
    (hinv : IsUnit ((c₁ : ZMod q) - (c₂ : ZMod q))) :
    ∃ w : ZMod q, y = g ^ (w.val)
```

If exponentiation through `ZMod q` is awkward, introduce a wrapper definition for exponent representatives and prove invariance under congruence mod `q`.

A more refined theorem should expose the extractor’s exact output:

```lean
theorem schnorr_extractor_correct
    {G : Type*} [CommGroup G]
    (g y a : G) (q : ℕ)
    (c₁ c₂ z₁ z₂ : ZMod q)
    (hg : orderOf g = q)
    (hacc₁ : g ^ z₁.val = a * y ^ c₁.val)
    (hacc₂ : g ^ z₂.val = a * y ^ c₂.val)
    (hneq : c₁ ≠ c₂) :
    y = g ^ (((z₁ - z₂) / (c₁ - c₂)).val)
```

You may need to replace `/` with multiplication by `inv` in the field case when `q` is prime, which is often cleaner.

**Why this is a breakthrough.**  
This upgrades Schnorr from a verified protocol to a verified **proof of knowledge**. That is the conceptual leap needed for serious mechanized cryptography.

### Theorem 2: Perfect honest-verifier zero-knowledge as transcript distribution equivalence
Do not state HVZK merely as “there exists a simulator.” Formalize **distributional equality** between real and simulated transcripts under uniform sampling.

**Mathematical statement.**  
For Schnorr over a finite cyclic group of order `q`, if `r,c` are sampled uniformly and the simulator outputs
`a = g^z * (y^c)⁻¹`, then the joint distribution of accepting transcripts `(a,c,z)` produced by the simulator equals the distribution of real transcripts generated using witness `x` and random commitment nonce `r`.

**Lean 4 target signature.**
Something like:

```lean
theorem schnorr_hvzK_distribution_eq
    {G : Type*} [CommGroup G] [Fintype G] [DecidableEq G]
    (g : G) (q : ℕ) (x : ℕ)
    (hg : orderOf g = q) :
    realTranscriptDist g q x = simulatedTranscriptDist g q x
```

If full probability distribution equality is too heavy with current Mathlib APIs, prove a pointwise counting version:

```lean
theorem schnorr_hvzK_counting
    {G : Type*} [CommGroup G] [Fintype G] [DecidableEq G]
    (g : G) (q : ℕ) (x : ℕ)
    (hg : orderOf g = q) :
    ∀ t, realTranscriptCount g q x t = simulatedTranscriptCount g q x t
```

This is still mathematically deep and can later be lifted to distributions.

**Why this matters.**  
This makes zero-knowledge not a slogan but a certified **measure-preserving symmetry**. It opens the door to machine-checked simulation arguments for far more advanced protocols.

### Theorem 3: Fiat–Shamir transcript soundness via collision/reprogramming barrier
Since the catalog already has `fiat_shamir_schnorr_correct`, your new theorem should identify a nontrivial security consequence in the random oracle model.

**Mathematical statement.**  
Assume a prover outputs two accepting Fiat–Shamir transcripts with the same commitment but distinct hash challenges. Then one can extract a witness. Equivalently, any successful forgery without witness induces either a random-oracle collision/reprogramming inconsistency or a break of special soundness.

This is a formalized **forking-style lemma fragment**, not full asymptotic reduction complexity if that is too large.

**Lean 4 target signature.**
For a finite oracle model:

```lean
theorem fiat_shamir_fork_extract
    {G : Type*} [CommGroup G]
    (g y : G) (q : ℕ)
    (H₁ H₂ : G → ZMod q)
    (a : G) (z₁ z₂ : ZMod q)
    (hg : orderOf g = q)
    (hchal : H₁ a ≠ H₂ a)
    (hacc₁ : g ^ z₁.val = a * y ^ (H₁ a).val)
    (hacc₂ : g ^ z₂.val = a * y ^ (H₂ a).val) :
    ∃ w : ZMod q, y = g ^ w.val
```

A stronger formulation can factor through your `schnorr_special_soundness_extract`.

**Why this is a breakthrough.**  
It formalizes the exact hinge on which the Fiat–Shamir heuristic turns: transcript re-use plus oracle variation yields extraction. This is the mechanized nucleus of ROM security.

### Theorem 4: Entropy preservation / zero-information theorem (cross-domain)
This is the required cross-domain connection: cryptography + information theory / probability.

Use the catalog theorem `idempotent_oracle_zero_information` as inspiration and the phrase “zero information” as a structural guide, but produce a theorem genuinely specialized to Schnorr.

**Mathematical statement.**  
In the honest-verifier setting, for fixed public statement `y = g^x`, the simulated transcript distribution is independent of the witness `x` except through `y`; equivalently, conditional mutual information between the witness and transcript given the statement vanishes.

A finite counting proxy is acceptable:

```lean
theorem schnorr_transcript_witness_independence
    {G : Type*} [CommGroup G] [Fintype G] [DecidableEq G]
    (g : G) (q : ℕ)
    (x₁ x₂ : ℕ)
    (hx : g ^ x₁ = g ^ x₂) :
    simulatedTranscriptDist g q x₁ = simulatedTranscriptDist g q x₂
```

Or a counting form:

```lean
theorem schnorr_zero_information_given_statement
    {G : Type*} [CommGroup G] [Fintype G] [DecidableEq G]
    (g : G) (q : ℕ) (x₁ x₂ : ℕ)
    (hy : g ^ x₁ = g ^ x₂) :
    ∀ t, transcriptWeight g q x₁ t = transcriptWeight g q x₂ t
```

**Why this matters.**  
This reframes zero-knowledge as an **information-theoretic invariance law**, linking cryptography to entropy and statistical mechanics. That cross-domain bridge is exactly the kind of formal mathematics that opens new territory.

## Proof strategy architecture

You must include at least 2–3 proof strategy steps per flagship theorem. The following routes are recommended.

### Strategy A: Algebraic extraction via transcript subtraction
Best for Theorem 1 and Theorem 3.

1. From acceptance equations
   `g^z₁ = a * y^c₁` and `g^z₂ = a * y^c₂`,
   eliminate `a` by multiplying one equation by the inverse of the other.
2. Obtain
   `g^(z₁ - z₂) = y^(c₁ - c₂)`
   in an appropriate modular exponent sense.
3. Use cyclicity and invertibility of `c₁ - c₂ mod q` to solve explicitly for the discrete log witness:
   `y = g^((z₁-z₂)/(c₁-c₂))`.

Why promising: it is algebraically canonical, maps directly onto Lean’s `calc` style, and naturally uses `rcases`, multi-step rewriting, and modular arithmetic lemmas.

Key tactics likely needed: `rcases`, `have`, `calc`, `field_simp` or modular inverse lemmas, group cancellation, `by_contra` for uniqueness/invertibility contradictions.

---

### Strategy B: Quotient-by-order / `ZMod q` linearization
Best for Theorem 1 and entropy theorems.

1. Push all exponents into `ZMod q` using `orderOf g = q`, proving exponent congruence invariance:
   if `m ≡ n [ZMOD q]` then `g^m = g^n`.
2. Rewrite transcript equations as affine linear equations over `ZMod q`:
   `z = r + c*x`.
3. Perform all extraction and simulator proofs in the finite field/ring `ZMod q`, where cancellation and inversion are cleaner.

Why promising: it turns nonlinear-looking group equalities into linear algebra over finite rings. This is the mathematically cleanest route and best for future generalization to Σ-protocols.

---

### Strategy C: Counting/bijection proof of HVZK
Best for Theorem 2 and Theorem 4.

1. Define the real transcript map `(r,c) ↦ (g^r, c, r + cx mod q)`.
2. Define the simulator map `(z,c) ↦ (g^z * (y^c)⁻¹, c, z)`.
3. Construct an explicit bijection between the sampling spaces showing transcript multiplicities agree pointwise.

Why promising: avoids heavy probabilistic infrastructure while proving a strong finite-distribution theorem. This is ideal in Lean if full measure-theoretic APIs become cumbersome.
