## Precise Formal Target

Work with a finite group `G` and a symmetric probability kernel `μ : G → ℝ` supported on a generating set, normalized so that `∑ g, μ g = 1` and `μ g = μ g⁻¹`. Let `T_μ` be the averaging operator on functions `G → ℂ` or equivalently on the group algebra:
\[
(T_\mu f)(x) = \sum_{g \in G} \mu(g)\, f(g^{-1}x).
\]

Define the associated quantum channel on operators diagonal in the group basis, and then extend to a canonical channel on the full matrix algebra by conjugation averaging:
\[
\Phi_\mu(\rho) = \sum_{g \in G} \mu(g)\, U_g \rho U_g^\dagger,
\]
where `U_g` is the left-regular permutation unitary.

Your primary theorem should identify the purity decay of iterates of this channel with spectral moments / return probabilities of the underlying walk.

### Theorem 1: Purity as a spectral moment / return probability

For the left-regular averaging operator attached to a symmetric probability measure on a finite group, prove that the Hilbert–Schmidt norm squared of the channel iterate on a basis state equals the return probability after `2k` steps.

Mathematically:
\[
\operatorname{tr}\!\left(\Phi_\mu^k(|e\rangle\langle e|)^2\right)
= \sum_{x \in G} (\mu^{*k}(x))^2
= (\mu^{*2k})(e).
\]

This is the exact quantum incarnation of the moment kernel.

### Theorem 2: Exponential purity decay from spectral gap

Assume the normalized walk operator has spectral gap `λ > 0` on the orthogonal complement of constants. Then for every density matrix `ρ`,
\[
\operatorname{tr}\!\left((\Phi_\mu^k(\rho)-I/|G|)^2\right)
\le (1-\lambda)^{2k}\,
\operatorname{tr}\!\left((\rho-I/|G|)^2\right).
\]

This is stronger and cleaner than the original conjectural form with an unspecified `C_k`: it exhibits the maximally mixed state as the unique fixed point in the regular sector and shows strict Hilbert–Schmidt contraction at a rate dictated by the classical gap.

### Suggested Lean 4 type signature
```lean

  (μ : G → ℝ)
  (λ : ℝ)
  (hprob : (∑ g, μ g) = 1)
  (hsym : ∀ g, μ g = μ g⁻¹)
  (hnonneg : ∀ g, 0 ≤ μ g)
  (hgap : spectralGapBound G μ λ)
  (ρ : Matrix G G ℂ)
  (k : ℕ) :
```

### Why this matters

## Proof Strategy Architecture

You must present and execute 2–3 proof routes, even if one becomes the main route.

### Strategy A — Direct convolution / matrix-entry route
1. Define `basisProj e = |e⟩⟨e|` and compute the matrix entries of `Φ^k(basisProj e)`.
2. Show these entries are diagonal with diagonal values `μ^{*k}(x)` for the classical walk distribution.
3. Compute purity:
   \[
   \operatorname{tr}(\rho_k^2) = \sum_x (\mu^{*k}(x))^2.
   \]
4. Use symmetry of `μ` to identify this with `(\mu^{*2k})(e)`.

**Why promising:** This route is elementary, constructive, and likely most compatible with Lean’s finite sums, `Matrix.trace`, and induction on `k`.

### Strategy B — Operator-theoretic / Hilbert–Schmidt contraction route
1. View `Φ_μ` as a self-adjoint operator on the Hilbert–Schmidt space because `μ(g)=μ(g⁻¹)`.
2. Show the maximally mixed state is fixed and the orthogonal complement contracts by the second eigenvalue bound.
3. Deduce the centered purity decay theorem from spectral radius control.

**Why promising:** This is the conceptually strongest route for the gap-decay theorem, though it may require more linear-algebra infrastructure.

### Strategy C — Representation-theoretic route
1. Decompose the regular representation into irreducibles.
2. Express `Φ_μ` blockwise as averaging against representation matrices.
3. Relate purity decay to character moments and then to the normalized trace of powers.

**Why promising:** This is the most revolutionary route if Mathlib support permits; it reveals the hidden representation theory behind the channel. Even partial implementation would produce a compelling `RESEARCH_PAPER.md`.

**Recommendation:** Use Strategy A as the formal backbone, Strategy B for the general contraction theorem, and discuss Strategy C in the paper as the structural explanation and future extension path.
