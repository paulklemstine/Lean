## Precise Mathematical Target

Let `Gen := {A,B,C}` be the three Berggren generators, and let `Word := FreeMonoid Gen`. Let `Triple` denote primitive Pythagorean triples equipped with a rooted Berggren-tree structure. A Berggren quantum walk should consist of:

- a finite-dimensional complex inner product space `H`,
- a distinguished initial state `ψ0 : H`,
- for each generator `g : Gen`, a unitary operator `U g : H ≃ₗᵢ[ℂ] H`,
- an output/observation map from Berggren-tree vertices or rooted cylinders to amplitudes,
- compatibility with rooted path semantics:
  \[
  \mathrm{amp}(w) = \langle \psi_{\mathrm{obs}}, U(w)\psi_0 \rangle
  \]
  for words `w : Word`, where `U(w)` is the product along the word.

Define the **triple-path amplitude semimodule** `M_U` to be the span of all rooted cylinder amplitude functionals generated by words in `A,B,C`, equipped with the shift action by left concatenation and the induced positive amplitude form
\[
\langle [u],[v]\rangle := \overline{\mathrm{amp}(u)}\,\mathrm{amp}(v)
\quad\text{or, more generally,}\quad
K(u,v)=\langle U(u)\psi_0,U(v)\psi_0\rangle .
\]

You should formalize a finite realization theorem and a reconstruction theorem.

## Proof Strategy Architecture

## Strategy A: GNS / positive-kernel realization on the Berggren monoid
**Most promising.**

1. Define the amplitude kernel
   \[
   K(u,v)=H(u,v)
   \]
   on Berggren words. Show positivity and Hermitian symmetry from the moment-table hypotheses.

2. Quotient the free finitely supported complex functions on words by the radical of the sesquilinear form induced by `K`. This gives a finite-dimensional pre-Hilbert space because of stable-rank closure.

3. Define left-shift operators corresponding to `A,B,C` by
   \[
   S_g [\delta_w] := [\delta_{gw}].
   \]
   Use the unitary shift relations in the moment table to prove each `S_g` is isometric, and then unitary on the reduced finite-dimensional quotient.

4. Take `ψ0 := [\delta_1]`, define observation vector from the zeroth row/column, and prove realization of the table.

5. Prove minimality by construction: the cyclic span of `ψ0` under the generated shifts is the whole space.

6. Prove uniqueness up to gauge by the standard intertwiner argument: two minimal realizations with identical kernels admit a unique unitary intertwiner sending cyclic vectors to cyclic vectors up to phase.

**Why this is best:**  
It simultaneously handles existence, minimality, and uniqueness. It is the exact noncommutative analogue of classical Hankel/GNS realization and should formalize cleanly in Lean because positivity and quotient-by-radical are standard structural moves.

---

## Strategy B: Finite Hankel factorization + rank-stable realization
1. Define a truncated Berggren Hankel matrix indexed by prefix/suffix words up to length `N`.
2. Prove that stable rank `r` implies factorization
   \[
   H(u,v)=\langle x_u, x_v\rangle
   \]
   through an `r`-dimensional complex space.
3. Show Berggren consistency implies there exist linear maps `T_A,T_B,T_C` satisfying `x_{gw}=T_g x_w`.
4. Use the unitary identities to prove each `T_g` is unitary.
5. Build the minimal realization from the reachable span of the factor vectors.

**Why this is valuable:**  
This route is algorithmically closer to a certified reconstruction procedure. If you want executable reconstruction in Lean, this is likely the best path to an actual `reconstructWalk`.

**Why it is slightly less promising as a first theorem:**  
Factorization lemmas over truncated positive semidefinite complex matrices may require more matrix infrastructure and basis bookkeeping than the quotient/GNS route.

---

## Strategy C: Category-theoretic duality via representable amplitude functors
1. Define the category of finite observable Berggren walks with intertwiners.
2. Define the category of reduced unitary semimodules with positive amplitude form.
3. Construct a functor sending a walk to its amplitude semimodule.
4. Construct a realization functor from semimodules to walks by choosing a cyclic realization.
5. Prove unit/counit are isomorphisms under minimality/reducedness.

**Why this matters:**  
This yields the truly revolutionary statement: not merely every semimodule is realizable, but the two theories are *the same theory* in opposite directions.

**Why this should probably come second:**  
Once the realization and uniqueness theorems are in place, the equivalence proof becomes natural and much easier to organize.
