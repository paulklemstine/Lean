# Mode: prove

## Breakthrough Objective

Establish a genuine **converse compilation theorem** for tropical formulas over annotated words: prove that a tropically recognizable series is definable by a tropical formula **if and only if** its weighted transition structure is formula-rigid in a precise algebraic sense. The right target is not a vague “closure” statement, but an exact structural characterization that turns recognizability into syntax.

This would be a field-opening result because it upgrades the current direction from a one-way compiler into a **semantic completeness theorem**: automata semantics and tropical formula semantics become equivalent on the nose for a sharply identified class. That is the tropical analogue of the deepest results connecting automata, logic, and algebra in classical language theory.

The existing theorem
- `tropical_recognizable_iff_finite_nerode`
  from `Tropical/MyhillNerode.lean`

is the crucial launchpad: recognizability is already characterized by finite Nerode structure. The revolutionary step is to identify when that finite tropical Nerode structure is not merely finite, but **formula-presentable**.

## Precise Mathematical Target

Work over a finite alphabet of annotations. Let `σ` be a finite type of annotations, and let weighted series be functions
`List σ → ℕ∞` or `List σ → ℕ` depending on what is already developed; if both are feasible, prioritize `ℕ∞` for min-plus semantics and fall back to `ℕ` if library friction is too high.

Define a class of tropical formulas built from:
- constants,
- letter tests / atomic annotated predicates,
- tropical addition (`+`),
- tropical minimum (`min`),
- and concatenation-style composition if already present in the codebase.

Then define the semantic evaluation of a formula as a series on annotated words.

The central theorem should be one of the following equivalent-strength formulations.

### Primary theorem: formula-definability iff finite min-generated derivative semimodule

Informal statement:

> A tropical series `S : List σ → ℕ∞` is definable by a tropical formula iff its set of left derivatives is contained in a finitely generated sub-semimodule of the tropical semimodule of series, generated using pointwise tropical addition and finite minima by derivatives corresponding to atomic formula contexts.

This is the strongest and most conceptual version.

### More Lean-friendly theorem: formula-definability iff finite formula-closed Nerode quotient

Informal statement:

> A tropically recognizable series is definable by a tropical formula iff its Nerode quotient admits a finite presentation in which every transition acts by a tropical affine map generated from constants, identity, tropical addition, and min.

This is likely the best formalization target for Lean.

### Even more concrete theorem: unambiguous acyclic converse

If the full characterization is too large for one cycle, first prove the decisive nontrivial special case:

> Every tropically recognizable series realized by a finite **acyclic** min-plus weighted automaton over annotated words is definable by a tropical formula, and conversely every tropical formula denotes a series recognized by a finite acyclic min-plus weighted automaton.

This is already substantial: it identifies formulas exactly with acyclic recognizable behavior, a tropical analogue of straight-line circuit semantics.

## Proof Architecture: Three Viable Strategies

## Strategy A: Derivatives → finite syntax via dynamic programming
This is the most promising route.

### Step 1
Define left derivatives of a tropical series and show:
- every formula-definable series has only finitely many derivatives up to formula-equivalence,
- every derivative remains formula-definable.

### Step 2
Starting from a finite derivative set `Q`, impose a transition normal form:
for each state/derivative `q` and annotation `a`, the derivative by `a` is representable as a tropical affine combination of elements of `Q`.

### Step 3
Construct formulas for each `q ∈ Q` simultaneously by well-founded recursion on:
- acyclic height if the quotient graph is acyclic, or
- SCC condensation plus tropical fixed-point elimination if cycles occur in a controlled way.

Why this is promising:
- It builds directly on `tropical_recognizable_iff_finite_nerode`.
- It produces a canonical finite basis.
- It matches the standard automata-to-expression conversion philosophy, but in tropical algebra.

## Strategy B: Matrix semantics and tropical linear representation
This is more algebraic and may scale better conceptually.

### Step 1
Formalize recognizable series as
`λ w, α ⊗ M(w) ⊗ β`
for tropical matrices over `WithTop ℕ`.

### Step 2
Characterize formula-definable series as those whose matrix representation factors through a finitely generated tropical semimodule closed under coordinatewise min and tropical scalar shift.

### Step 3
Show that this factorization is equivalent to a finite affine derivative presentation, then transfer to syntax.

Why this matters:
- It connects your theorem to tropical linear algebra and weighted automata theory.
- It may yield stronger corollaries, like effective minimization and rank bounds.

Risk:
- Lean matrix formalization may create overhead unless the automaton representation already exists.

## Strategy C: Acyclic base case + counterexample-guided refinement
This is the pragmatic route if full generality stalls.

### Step 1
Prove exact equivalence between formulas and acyclic tropical automata.

### Step 2
Search for cyclic recognizable series not definable by formulas as currently defined.
If found, isolate the obstruction:
- cycle mean,
- non-well-founded min recursion,
- or failure of finite affine closure.

### Step 3
Upgrade the theorem into an exact characterization:
formula-definable iff recognizable and obstruction-free.

Why this is powerful:
- Even a sharp obstruction theorem is a breakthrough.
- A negative characterization can be more important than a partial positive one.

## Cross-Domain Connections You Should Exploit

### 1. Descriptive complexity and weighted logic
This theorem is a tropical analogue of Büchi–Elgot–Trakhtenbrot style equivalences:
- automata ↔ logic ↔ algebra,
but now in the min-plus world.
If formalized cleanly, it opens a path toward:
- tropical MSO fragments,
- weighted first-order definability,
- and complexity stratification of tropical computations.

### 2. Tropical circuit complexity
Formula-definable series are the tropical analogue of **formula-size computations**, while recognizable series correspond more closely to finite-state dynamic programs. Your characterization theorem separates:
- finite-state representability,
- formula representability,
- and potentially circuit representability.
This is a new complexity hierarchy in tropical semantics.

### 3. Dynamic programming and shortest paths
Min-plus automata are shortest-path machines. Tropical formulas are straight-line shortest-path expressions. Your theorem becomes a classification of which path-cost functions admit **loop-free symbolic certificates**. That has algorithmic implications for:
- program analysis,
- sequence alignment,
- Viterbi-style decoding,
- and explainable dynamic programming.

### 4. Idempotent analysis and control
Recognizable tropical series appear in discrete event systems and optimal control. Formula-definability corresponds to finitely explainable policy cost landscapes. This creates a bridge from formal language theory to:
- max-plus control,
- schedulability,
- and symbolic verification.
