## Target 1: First Explicit Ray Class Group Computation in Lean

### Breakthrough theorem

For the quadratic number field \(K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5})\), let \(\mathcal O_K\) be its ring of integers and let \(\mathfrak m = (2)\). Define the ray class group modulo \(\mathfrak m\) as the quotient of the group of nonzero fractional ideals coprime to \(\mathfrak m\) by the subgroup of principal ideals generated by elements congruent to \(1 \bmod \mathfrak m\). Prove that this ray class group has order \(4\), and that the natural surjection to the ordinary class group has kernel of order \(2\).

This is not a toy computation. \(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5})\) is the first place where unique factorization fails, the class group is nontrivial, and ray refinement detects arithmetic invisible to the ordinary ideal class group. A fully formal proof here would be the first proof-assistant-certified explicit ray class group computation of a genuinely nontrivial modulus over a non-UFD quadratic order.

### Precise theorem statement

Let \(K := \mathrm{QuadraticField}(-5)\), \(R := \mathcal O_K\), and \(\mathfrak m := \mathrm{Ideal.span}\{(2 : R)\}\).

Prove the existence of a concrete ray class group object and the cardinality theorem:
- \( \# \mathrm{Cl}_{\mathfrak m}(R) = 4\),
- the canonical map \( \mathrm{Cl}_{\mathfrak m}(R) \to \mathrm{Cl}(R)\) is surjective,
- its kernel has cardinality \(2\).

## Target 2: Choice-Free General Transfer via Abelianization

### Breakthrough theorem

Formalize the group-theoretic transfer
\[
\mathrm{Ver}_{G/U} : G \to U^{\mathrm{ab}}
\]
for a finite-index subgroup \(U \le G\), prove that it is independent of the choice of transversal after passage to the abelianization, and prove that when \(G\) is abelian it reduces to the \([G:U]\)-th power map on \(U\).

This is the mechanism behind capitulation, norm-compatibility, and eventually cohomological formulations of class field theory. If you formalize this properly, you unlock non-abelian shadows of reciprocity inside Lean.

### Precise theorem statement

Let \(G\) be a finite group and \(U \le G\) a subgroup of finite index. For any choice of transversal \(t : G/U \to G\), define
\[
\mathrm{Ver}(g)
=
\prod_{s \in G/U}
\overline{t(gs)^{-1} g t(s)}
\in U^{\mathrm{ab}},
\]
where the bar denotes the class in the abelianization and the product is taken in the finite commutative group \(U^{\mathrm{ab}}\).

Prove:
1. This expression is well-defined.
2. It is independent of the choice of transversal.
3. It defines a group homomorphism \(G \to U^{\mathrm{ab}}\).
4. If \(G\) is abelian, then under the canonical identification \(U^{\mathrm{ab}} \cong U\), it is \(g \mapsto g^{[G:U]}\) restricted appropriately.

### Lean 4 formalization target

A target signature should look like:

```lean
noncomputable def transfer
    (G : Type*) [Group G] [Fintype (Quotient (Subgroup.leftRel U))]
    (U : Subgroup G) : G →* Abelianization U := ...
```

with key theorems:

```lean
theorem transfer_independent_of_transversal
    (t₁ t₂ : Quotient (Subgroup.leftRel U) → G)
    (ht₁ : IsTransversal U t₁) (ht₂ : IsTransversal U t₂) :
    transferWith U t₁ = transferWith U t₂
```

```lean
theorem transfer_eq_abelianTransfer_of_comm
    (G : Type*) [CommGroup G] [FiniteIndex U] :
    transfer G U =
      (Abelianization.equivOfComm U).symm.toMonoidHom.comp (abelianTransfer U)
```

If your current API has a theorem `abelianTransfer`, build directly on it rather than re-proving the abelian case from scratch.

### Proof strategies

#### Strategy A: Permutation-of-cosets proof
1. Show that left multiplication by \(g\) permutes the coset space \(G/U\).
2. Compare the products attached to two transversals using the correction factors \(u_s \in U\) with \(t_2(s)=t_1(s)u_s\).
3. After passing to `Abelianization U`, the correction terms telescope or cancel because the product is commutative.

**Why this is most promising:** This is the classical proof and is perfectly aligned with Lean’s finite product machinery over `Finset`.

#### Strategy B: Universal property of abelianization
1. Define a transversal-dependent map into \(U\).
2. Show the commutator defect is exactly what prevents choice-independence.
3. Descend to `Abelianization U` using its universal property and prove equality there.

**Why this is conceptually powerful:** It isolates the exact reason transfer lives naturally in the abelianization, not in \(U\) itself.

#### Strategy C: Action-groupoid perspective
1. Regard coset representatives as sections of a quotient map.
2. Interpret the transfer term as a multiplicative holonomy around the action of \(g\) on the finite orbit set.
3. Show section changes induce conjugate coboundary corrections, which vanish in abelianization.

**Why this matters:** This perspective is the right one if you want later to connect transfer to cohomology and Mackey functor structures.

### Cross-domain connections
- **Algebraic topology**: transfer maps appear in covering spaces and stable homotopy.
- **Representation theory**: Verlagerung interacts with determinant-like invariants and induction/restriction.
- **Cohomology**: this is the degree-zero shadow of corestriction in group cohomology.
- **Class field theory**: transfer is the group-theoretic core of capitulation phenomena.

\[

#### Strategy C: Via transfer comparison
