## Proof strategy architecture

### Strategy 1: Abstract implication scaffolding first, analytic instantiation second
1. Define predicates `RHFor`, `PrimeCountingSqrtError`, `MertensSqrtBound`, `ExplicitFormulaControl`, `ReciprocalZetaBoundaryControl`.
2. Prove purely logical implication chains between them.
3. Instantiate these predicates with actual zeta/prime-counting objects wherever Mathlib permits.

**Why this is promising:** it minimizes `sorry` by separating theorem architecture from analytic gaps. Even if full RH equivalences are unreachable today, the bridge theorems become reusable infrastructure.

---

### Strategy 2: Finite-dimensional Hilbert–Pólya surrogate via Hermitian matrices
1. Prove that Hermitian matrices have real spectrum in the exact API available in Mathlib.
2. Encode the map `λ ↦ 1/2 + I*λ` and show all resulting roots lie on the critical line.
3. Package this as a generic theorem for any self-adjoint spectral model.

**Why this is promising:** Mathlib is comparatively strong in linear algebra. This route can yield a genuinely new formal theorem family now, with zero dependence on deep analytic number theory infrastructure.

---

### Strategy 3: Arithmetic finite truncations and explicit sums
1. Define finite Euler-product or Dirichlet-polynomial approximants:
   ```lean
   def zetaTrunc (N : ℕ) (s : ℂ) : ℂ := ∑ n in Finset.Icc 1 N, (n : ℂ) ^ (-s)
   ```
   or a variant that is easier to formalize.
2. Prove symmetry/approximation lemmas for these truncations.
3. Study when roots of symmetrized truncations obey critical-line constraints under coefficient positivity or self-inversive conditions.

**Why this is promising:** finite zeta models can reveal operator-theoretic or polynomial-theoretic structure inaccessible in the full function. This is the best route to original theorem discovery.

**Most promising overall:** Strategy 2 + Strategy 1. Use spectral theorems to produce strong, exact formal results now, while building an abstract implication interface for future analytic instantiations.

## Cross-domain connections to exploit

### 1. Quantum chaos / random matrix theory
Formalize finite Hermitian ensembles or deterministic Hermitian matrices as surrogates for zero statistics. The theorem you can actually prove is not “GUE implies RH,” but:

- Hermitian spectral data induces critical-line root placement for shifted characteristic polynomials.
- Eigenvalue spacing statistics of finite models can be computed and compared experimentally to zeta-truncation roots.

This opens a Lean-compatible route toward **formal quantum chaos heuristics**.

### 2. Complexity theory / verification
Define finite certificates that a polynomial has all roots on a vertical line by reducing to real-rootedness after a shift. This turns a deep analytic condition into a **checkable algebraic certificate**, suggesting algorithmic verification of RH surrogates for truncations.

### 3. Signal processing / control theory
Self-inversive and stable polynomials are close cousins of critical-line / unit-circle root constraints. Translate between:
- roots on `Re(s)=1/2`,
- roots on the imaginary axis after shifting,
- roots on the unit circle after Möbius transform.

This could yield a breakthrough theorem pipeline:
**critical line ↔ imaginary axis ↔ unit circle ↔ paraunitary/self-inversive polynomial theory**.

### 4. Tropical / ultrametric intuition
Although RH is archimedean, finite Euler products and valuation bounds can be studied p-adically or tropically. This may produce new “valuation shadows” of zero-free regions, even if not the full theorem.
