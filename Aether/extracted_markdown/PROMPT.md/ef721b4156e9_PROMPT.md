## Assignment: Algebra–Tropical–Geometry Tropical Voronoi–Lattice Realization Duality via Idempotent Distance Semimodules and Certified Minimal Decoder Reconstruction

**Mode:** prove

Prove a genuinely new finite duality theorem that turns tropical nearest-site geometry into an algebraic classification theorem for idempotent distance semimodules. The goal is not a variant of known tropical convexity, but a structural equivalence: a finitely generated separated tropical distance semimodule should *be* exactly the algebraic shadow of a finite essential tropical Voronoi decoder complex, with a canonical and reconstructible minimal site presentation. This would open a new algebra–geometry–decoding interface: semimodule invariants become decoder invariants, and geometric reconstruction becomes an algebraic certification problem.

This is potentially field-opening because it would give a **representation theorem for tropical decoding objects** analogous in spirit to how matroids, polyhedra, or Hecke modules admit multiple avatars. Here the avatars are:

- algebraic: finitely generated idempotent semimodules of tropical distance profiles,
- geometric: finite weighted tropical Voronoi complexes,
- algorithmic: certified minimal decoder reconstruction data,
- combinatorial: extremal rays / residuation face incidence.

The breakthrough is the converse direction: not merely that sites induce profile modules, but that the module remembers the minimal site arrangement up to tropical equivalence.

## Precise Main Theorem Target

Let `S := Tropical` be the min-plus tropical semiring. Let profiles on a finite ambient space `X` be functions `X → S`. For a finite family of weighted sites `(p_i, w_i)`, define
\[
d_i(x) := w_i \oplus \operatorname{dist}_{trop}(x,p_i),
\]
where in min-plus notation this is
\[
d_i(x) = w_i + \operatorname{dist}_{trop}(x,p_i).
\]
Let `M` be the tropical semimodule generated by the finite family `{d_i}` under tropical scaling and tropical addition. Impose:

1. **Separatedness:** distinct generators define distinct minimality regions;
2. **Essentiality / irredundancy:** no generator lies in the tropical span of the others in a way preserving all decoder cells;
3. **Distance-profile axiom:** generators are representable by weighted tropical distance functions.

Define the decoder cell of generator `d_i` by
\[
C_i := \{x \in X \mid d_i(x) \le d_j(x)\ \forall j\}.
\]
The tropical Voronoi decoder complex `V(M)` is the finite cell complex formed by all nonempty intersections of these principal minimality regions and their tie loci.

### Main realization–reconstruction duality
Prove that every finite separated essential tropical distance semimodule admits a canonical realization by a finite weighted tropical Voronoi decoder complex, and conversely every finite essential tropical Voronoi decoder complex determines, up to tropical isometry/equivalence and permutation, a unique irredundant generating family of distance rays.

### Minimality theorem
Prove that the minimal number of generators of `M` equals the number of extremal indecomposable distance rays of `M`, and that these rays correspond bijectively to essential decoder cells.

### Certified reconstruction theorem
Prove that from the finite residuation/incidence data of `M` one can reconstruct the unique minimal weighted site arrangement realizing `M` up to tropical equivalence.

## Exact Mathematical Core You Should Establish

### Theorem A: Finite realization duality
For every finite ambient type `X` and every finitely generated separated essential tropical distance semimodule `M ⊆ (X → Tropical)`, there exists a finite weighted site family `(p_i,w_i)_{i∈I}` such that
\[
M = \operatorname{tropSpan}\{x \mapsto w_i + \operatorname{dist}(x,p_i) : i \in I\},
\]
and the induced decoder regions
\[
C_i = \{x : d_i(x) \le d_j(x)\ \forall j\}
\]
form an essential finite tropical Voronoi decoder complex canonically attached to `M`.

### Theorem B: Reconstruction converse
If two irredundant separated weighted site families generate the same tropical distance semimodule, then they are equivalent up to tropical isometry/equivalence, permutation of generators, and tropical additive normalization on weights. In particular, the irredundant generator family is unique as a set of extremal distance rays.

### Theorem C: Minimality = extremality
The cardinality of any irredundant generating family of `M` equals the number of extremal indecomposable distance rays of `M`, and these extremal rays are exactly the profiles whose decoder cells are essential/nonempty and not unions of lower residuated faces.

### Theorem D: Certified algorithmic reconstruction
From a finite table of tropical pairwise inequalities/residuation relations among generators, one can reconstruct the minimal generator family and hence the unique essential decoder complex. Formalize this as a theorem of existence and uniqueness of a certified output, even if the executable algorithm is deferred.

## Proof Strategy Options

### Strategy 1: Extremal decomposition → Voronoi cells → uniqueness
**Most promising.**

1. Prove that every finitely generated separated distance semimodule admits a finite canonical set of extremal indecomposable generators, using `finite_tropical_choquet_canonical`.
2. Show each extremal generator defines a nonempty essential decoder cell, and non-extremal generators correspond to unions / tropical combinations of others, hence redundant cells.
3. Prove that equality of semimodules identifies the same extremal rays, so any two irredundant site families must coincide up to tropical equivalence.

Why most promising:
- It converts geometry into a finite algebraic extremal decomposition problem.
- It naturally yields the minimality theorem.
- It is Lean-friendly because finite extremal sets and canonical decompositions are easier to formalize than geometric Voronoi incidence from scratch.

### Strategy 2: Residuation and Galois connection approach
1. Define a residuation preorder on profiles:
   \[
   f \preceq_M g \iff \exists \lambda,\ f \le \lambda \odot g \text{ in } M
   \]
   or an appropriate tropical residuation scalar.
2. Show decoder cells are principal faces in the residuation poset, and essential cells correspond to join-irreducible / meet-prime elements.
3. Reconstruct generators from principal residuated faces, then prove these are exactly the extremal distance rays.

Why valuable:
- Gives the strongest reconstruction theorem from finite incidence data.
- Produces a combinatorial certificate object.
- Connects naturally to order theory and idempotent analysis.

Risk:
- More setup, and formalizing residuation cleanly may take work.

### Strategy 3: Direct finite metric embedding / reconstruction
1. Start from a finite inequality table between abstract generators and points of `X`.
2. Show consistency conditions imply representability by weighted tropical sites.
3. Construct the site family explicitly and prove minimality by contradiction using cell collapse.

Why useful:
- Closest to an algorithmic corollary.
- May produce executable content earlier.

Risk:
- Harder to prove canonical uniqueness at the right level of abstraction.
- More combinatorial casework.

Recommended plan: **Strategy 1 for the structural theorem**, then use **Strategy 2** to sharpen the certified reconstruction corollary.
