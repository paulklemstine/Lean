## Assignment: Phase Transition in Proof Compression for Formal Arithmetic

**Mode:** prove / discover

Prove a genuinely new theorem that turns the vague “proof explosion under normalization” intuition into a formal universality statement inside Lean 4, even if first in a sharply idealized model. The goal is not an empirical benchmark report alone, but a mathematically rigid theorem schema that could become the founding result of a new interface between proof complexity, normalization theory, and formalized arithmetic.

### Research Direction

The central vision is to isolate a **phase transition law for proof compression**: a theorem saying that once a proof family crosses a structural threshold associated with search complexity or cut-elimination depth, normalization ceases to be a benign syntactic cleanup and becomes an unavoidable source of superpolynomial or exponential blowup.

The original conjecture is too ambitious to attack in one jump because “all complete deterministic normalizers for all sound finitely axiomatized theories extending EA” mixes semantic, syntactic, and machine-model issues. The breakthrough move is to formalize a **model theorem** that already exhibits the dichotomy in a clean class of proof systems and statement families, then derive falsifiable hypotheses for the full conjecture.

### Precise Theorem Target

Define a formal class of proof encodings for bounded arithmetic-style total search principles, together with:

- `rawProofLength : Proof φ → ℕ`
- `normProofLength : Proof φ → ℕ`, where `normProofLength p := rawProofLength (normalize p)`
- `ShortestRaw φ := sInf {ℓ | ∃ p : Proof φ, rawProofLength p = ℓ}`
- `ShortestNorm φ := sInf {ℓ | ∃ p : Proof φ, normProofLength p = ℓ}`

Then target a theorem of the following form for a **specific, fully formalizable family** of true `Π₂` statements encoding bounded search:

> There exists a family `φ : ℕ → Sentence`, constants `c,C,α > 0`, and a deterministic complete normalizer `N` on a restricted proof calculus `𝒫`, such that:
>
> 1. `ShortestRaw (φ n) ≤ C * n^c` for all sufficiently large `n`;
> 2. `ShortestNorm (φ n) ≥ 2^(n^α)` for infinitely many `n`;
> 3. equivalently, in terms of raw length,
>    `ShortestNorm (φ n) ≥ exp((ShortestRaw (φ n))^α')`
>    for some `α' > 0` and infinitely many `n`.

This already establishes a genuine phase-separation theorem.

A stronger second-stage target is a **gap theorem**:

> For a natural complete family `φ_n` encoding a total-search principle in the chosen calculus, exactly one of the following holds:
>
> - there exists `k` such that `ShortestNorm (φ n) ≤ (ShortestRaw (φ n))^k` eventually;
> - there exist `α > 0` and infinitely many `n` such that  
>   `ShortestNorm (φ n) ≥ exp((ShortestRaw (φ n))^α)`.
>
> No family in the chosen completeness class has stable asymptotics
> `exp((log L(n))^2)`, `exp(√(log L(n)))`, or any other genuinely intermediate distortion regime.

Even proving this for one nontrivial proof system / normalizer pair would be a field-opening result.

### Most Promising Concrete Instantiation

Do **not** begin with full first-order arithmetic. Instead, define an idealized but nontrivial proof language where normalization corresponds to a canonical search-tree expansion. Then leverage the existing catalog theorem

- `exponential_search_lower_bound`

as the engine that turns search complexity into normalized proof lower bounds.

The likely breakthrough theorem is:

> **Search-to-Normalization Transfer Theorem.**  
> In a proof calculus where normalized proofs for total-search statements induce explicit deterministic witness-search trees of branching factor `b ≥ 1` and depth `d`, any lower bound of the form `b^d` on search implies a lower bound of the same order on normalized proof length. Therefore any family with succinct non-normal proofs but exponential search complexity exhibits exponential normalization blowup.

This is the bridge theorem that converts your catalog lower bound into a proof-complexity theorem.

### Proof Strategy A: Search-Extraction from Normal Forms
**Most promising.**

This is the cleanest path because it directly leverages the catalog lower bound.

Step 1. Formalize a restricted proof calculus for `Π₂` total-search statements where a normalized proof must make witness choices explicit.  
Think of formulas `∀ x ≤ t(n), ∃ y ≤ s(n), R(x,y,n)` with decidable `R`. In the normalized form, witness-producing structure cannot remain hidden behind cuts/lemmas.

Step 2. Prove that every normalized proof canonically determines a deterministic search tree whose leaves correspond to candidate witnesses or branch failures.  
This is the key structural theorem.

Step 3. Apply `exponential_search_lower_bound` to obtain a lower bound on the tree size, then transfer this to normalized proof length using a size-dominance lemma.

Why this is promising: it avoids the impossible task of characterizing arbitrary normalization globally. Instead, it identifies one proof-system feature—**explicit witness extraction after normalization**—that forces proof length to simulate search.

### Proof Strategy B: Cut-Elimination / Normalization Blowup via Herbrand Expansion

Step 1. Formalize a Herbrand-style expansion theorem for a bounded fragment of arithmetic in Lean: normalized proofs of `Π₂` statements induce finite disjunctions of explicit witness instances.

Step 2. Show that for a carefully chosen family `φ_n`, any such Herbrand expansion must contain exponentially many instances, by reducing witness correctness to a complete deterministic search problem.

Step 3. Construct short raw proofs using cuts/abbreviations/derived lemmas that compress these exponentially many cases.

Why this is exciting: it ties your result to classical proof theory—cut elimination, Herbrand complexity, and witness extraction—making the theorem legible to both proof theorists and formal methods researchers.

Why it is less immediately promising than Strategy A: Herbrand formalization in Lean can become technically heavy unless the formula class is very tightly controlled.

### Proof Strategy C: Information Bottleneck / Communication View of Normalization

Step 1. Interpret raw proofs as compressed certificates and normalized proofs as locally checkable explicit protocols.

Step 2. Define an invariant—certificate entropy, branching entropy, or communication width—showing that normalization destroys global sharing and forces local explicitness.

Step 3. Prove a lower bound by reduction to deterministic communication/search complexity.

Why this matters: if successful, it would connect proof normalization to communication complexity and information theory, opening a genuinely new language for proof compression.

Why it is riskier: more conceptually ambitious, but harder to land as a first Lean theorem unless the information measure is made extremely combinatorial.

### Deeper Mathematical Insight

The real mathematical object here is not “proof length” alone, but a **renormalization flow on proof representations**. Raw proofs can share subarguments globally, use cuts, and hide witness construction in abstract lemmas. Normalization forces locality: shared structure is duplicated, hidden witnesses become explicit, and abstract existence arguments are unfolded into search. This suggests a new invariant:

- **search dimension** of a proof family,
- **witness entropy** of a statement family,
- or **normalization susceptibility** of a proof system.

A theorem showing only polynomial vs stretched/exponential distortion in a complete family would resemble universality phenomena from statistical mechanics: microscopically different normalizers collapse into a few macroscopic asymptotic phases.

That is why this is bigger than proof engineering. It proposes a **phase diagram of formal reasoning**.

### Lean Design Advice

You will likely need an abstract interface rather than a full syntax of arithmetic at first.

Define structures such as:

```lean
structure ProofSystem where
  Proof : Sentence → Type
  length : {φ : Sentence} → Proof φ → ℕ

structure Normalizer (P : ProofSystem) where
  normalize : {φ : Sentence} → P.Proof φ → P.Proof φ

structure SearchTree where
  size : ℕ
  depth : ℕ
  branches : ℕ
```

Then define extraction and domination properties as typeclasses or hypotheses. This lets you prove the transfer theorem abstractly and instantiate concrete calculi later.
