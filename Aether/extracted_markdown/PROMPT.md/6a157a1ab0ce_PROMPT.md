### Target Theorem 1: Tropical Stone Reconstruction

Let `S` be a finitely generated idempotent commutative semiring equipped with a tropical multiplication law and enough prime congruences to separate elements:
\[
\forall a b : S,\ a \neq b \to \exists p \in \mathrm{Spec}_c(S),\ \neg(a \sim_p b).
\]
Define
\[
D(a,b) := \{p \in \mathrm{Spec}_c(S) : \neg(a \sim_p b)\}.
\]
Let `η_S` send `a : S` to its evaluation profile on prime congruences, or equivalently to the basic tropical clopen data determined by the family `D(a,b)`.

**Theorem (Stone-type reconstruction):**  
If `S` is finitely generated, idempotent, and spectrally separated, then the evaluation map
\[
\eta_S : S \to \mathrm{ClopTrop}(\mathrm{Spec}_c(S))
\]
is injective, respects semiring operations, and its image generates the target algebra under finite tropical combinations. In a finite/discrete spectral model, this yields a reconstruction equivalence between `S` and the semiring of spectral tropical clopen functions generated by evaluations.

### Target Theorem 2: Spectral Hardness Separation

Define a class of attacks modeled on tropical linear inversion / residuation-compression algorithms. The attack class should be mathematically checkable: maps built from finitely many uses of tropical multiplication, transpose/residuation primitives, bounded compression, and observable extraction. Let certificate complexity be the minimal size of a finite spectral subbasis needed to separate `x` and `y`.

For `x y : S`, define a **spectral certificate** as a finite family of prime congruences or basic opens witnessing
\[
\forall p \in C,\ \neg(x \sim_p y),
\]
with `C` sufficient relative to the attack class.

**Theorem (Hardness separation):**  
Assume `x ≠ y` and there exists a finite sound-complete spectral certificate `C` separating them. Then any attack in the chosen tropical linear/residuation-compression class fails to produce a collision witness identifying `x` and `y` below the certificate complexity bound. Equivalently: spectral non-collapse implies a lower bound on inversion/collision-finding complexity for that attack class.

## Definitions to Architect Carefully

You must choose definitions that are strong enough to be nontrivial, but weak enough to formalize now.

### 1. Prime congruence
A prime congruence should tropicalize the usual semiring prime condition. Good options:

- **Cancellation-prime formulation:** if `a*b ~ a*c`, then `b ~ c` or `a` lies in a singular radical.
- **Product-separation formulation:** if `a*b ~ c*d`, then one of finitely many factorwise collapses occurs.
- **Observer-prime formulation:** pull back a prime-like observer quotient from existing catalog infrastructure.

Most promising formally: **observer-prime / cancellation-prime hybrid**, because it can exploit existing “prime-like observer” theorems while preserving a direct algebraic interpretation.

### 2. Spectrum and topology
Use basic opens `D(a,b)`. This is the right semiring analogue of Zariski/Stone separation because equality modulo congruence, not ideal membership, is the native notion.

### 3. Spectral clopen algebra
If topological generality becomes a burden, define the codomain first as the semiring generated by basic opens inside `Set (SpecC S)` with tropical operations interpreted as union/intersection or min/max on a finite Boolean/tropical codomain. Then prove the finite Stone theorem there. You can generalize later.

### 4. Attack class
Do not leave “natural class” vague. A good formal class is:

- built from `tropMul`,
- transpose or residuation operations already in catalog,
- bounded-depth composition,
- finite observable extraction,
- compression maps preserving upper bounds (`tropMul_preserves_bound`-style hypotheses).

This attack class should be broad enough to matter, narrow enough for proof by induction on attack syntax.

## Proof Strategy Architecture

### Strategy A: Finite spectral representation via separating congruences
Most promising for Lean.

1. **Build the finite-spectrum algebra.**  
   For finitely generated `S`, define the family of prime congruences relevant to generators or bounded certificates. Use `D(a,b)` to define a semiring-valued evaluation map into a function space over `SpecC S`.

2. **Prove injectivity from separation.**  
   Show that if `η(a) = η(b)`, then every prime congruence identifies `a,b`, contradicting the separation axiom. This is the exact tropical Stone step.

3. **Show generation/density by basic opens.**  
   Prove the image of `η` generates all certificate-relevant clopen functions by induction on finite tropical combinations. In the finite case, “dense” should be replaced by a precise algebraic generation statement.

Why this is strongest: it reduces topology to finite combinatorics and matches Lean’s strengths.

---

### Strategy B: Quotient-first reconstruction through observer semantics
Potentially elegant if the catalog already has observer/congruence quotient machinery.

1. **Associate to each prime congruence a quotient semiring** `S/p`.
2. **Embed `S` into the product** of all `S/p` over the spectrum, using separation to prove injectivity.
3. **Identify the image** with the spectral clopen/evaluation algebra by characterizing which tuples are locally realizable on basic opens.

Why promising: this mirrors subdirect representation theorems and may avoid heavy topological formalization. It also naturally connects to cryptographic observables as quotient semantics.

---

### Strategy C: Attack lower bounds by syntactic induction on admissible attacks
Best for Theorem 2.

1. **Define attack syntax and cost.**  
   Include primitive operations corresponding to catalog lemmas: `tropMul`, witness extraction, transpose/residuation, and bound-preserving compression.

2. **Prove invariance of spectral indistinguishability.**  
   If two inputs are separated by all primes in a certificate, then every admissible low-cost attack preserves the inability to collapse them into a collision witness unless it “uses up” enough certificate complexity.

3. **Derive lower bound.**  
   Induct on attack derivations to show that any successful inversion/collision attack induces a collapse of some certificate prime, contradiction.

Why this is the cryptographic heart: it converts algebraic non-collapse into complexity lower bounds.
