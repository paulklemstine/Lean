## Research Direction

Treat a “surgery” operation on a tropical matrix as a **two-edge perturbation**:
replace a matrix `A` by
\[
A'_{ij} = \min\big(A_{ij},\, u_i + v_j,\, u'_i + v'_j\big),
\]
or, in a more localized version, modify exactly two entries
\[
A' = A \;\text{with}\; A'_{i_1 j_1} = \min(A_{i_1 j_1}, c_1),\quad
A'_{i_2 j_2} = \min(A_{i_2 j_2}, c_2).
\]
This is the tropical analogue of a rank-2 update, but in min-plus algebra the right invariant is not classical rank: it is **cycle mean / spectral radius / tropical eigenvalue**. The goal is to prove that surgery is a controlled spectral operation.

This is not merely perturbation theory. If successful, it opens a **tropical interlacing theory** for min-plus matrices, with consequences for shortest-path sensitivity, discrete event systems, weighted automata, and tropical control.

## Core Breakthrough Target

### Precise theorem statement

Define the tropical surgery update by
\[
\operatorname{surgery}(A,U,V,U',V')(i,j)
:= \min\big(A(i,j),\, U(i)+V(j),\, U'(i)+V'(j)\big).
\]

The breakthrough theorem to formalize is:

> **Theorem (rank-2 tropical spectral monotonicity and bound).**  
> Let `A : Fin n → Fin n → ℝ` with `n ≥ 1`. Let
> \[
> B(i,j)=\min(A(i,j), U(i)+V(j), U'(i)+V'(j)).
> \]
> Then:
> 1. `tropicalSpectralRadius B ≤ tropicalSpectralRadius A`.
> 2. `tropicalSpectralRadius B ≤ min (tropicalSpectralRadius A) (maxEntryBound U V U' V')`, for a suitable explicit scalar bound extracted from the update templates.
> 3. If every cycle of `A` realizing the tropical spectral radius avoids the support where surgery is strict, then  
>    `tropicalSpectralRadius B = tropicalSpectralRadius A`.

The first clause is the non-negotiable core theorem. The third clause is the conceptual breakthrough: **spectral invariance under off-critical surgery**.

If the catalog’s notion of “spectral radius” is encoded as a theorem-level bound rather than a definition, prove a formally weaker but still meaningful theorem:

> If `B i j ≤ A i j` for all `i j`, then every certified tropical spectral upper bound for `A` is also a certified tropical spectral upper bound for `B`.

This still creates a reusable monotonicity API.

## Why this would be a breakthrough

Classical matrix theory has rank-one updates, Weyl inequalities, and interlacing. Tropical linear algebra has many spectral statements, but lacks a robust **perturbation calculus for low-complexity min-plus updates**. A theorem showing that rank-2 tropical surgery is spectrally monotone, and is spectrally inert away from critical cycles, would be the seed of:

- tropical eigenvalue perturbation theory,
- sensitivity analysis for shortest-path and scheduling systems,
- certified spectral control in min-plus dynamical systems,
- a tropical analogue of low-rank preconditioning.

This is exactly the kind of theorem that turns a collection of isolated lemmas into a new subfield.

## How to build on the verified catalog theorems

Use the existing theorems as structural anchors, not as decorations.

1. **`tropical_spectral_radius_le_eigenvalue`**  
   Use this to pass from a candidate tropical eigenvalue for `A` to an upper bound on the surgery matrix `B`.  
   The idea: if `λ` is certified for `A`, then entrywise decrease should make the same `λ` admissible for `B`. This gives monotonicity.

2. **`tropical_spectral_bound`**  
   This is likely the cleanest bridge theorem if it already packages a finite-dimensional spectral upper bound for matrices on `Fin (n+1)`.  
   First prove an auxiliary monotonicity lemma:
   ```lean
   (∀ i j, B i j ≤ A i j) → tropical_spectral_bound B ≤ tropical_spectral_bound A
   ```
   Then instantiate `B := tropicalRankTwoSurgery A u v u' v'`.

3. **`spectral_tropical_bound`**  
   This theorem may encode a 2×2 or four-parameter spectral inequality. Use it to test the rank-2 update on `Fin 2` first.  
   A highly strategic move is:
   - prove the theorem in dimension 2 explicitly,
   - identify the exact shape of the inequality,
   - generalize by reduction to cycle means or principal 2×2 minors.

4. **`tropical_plus_distributes_over_min`**  
   Use this to normalize min-plus expressions when proving that surgery matrices preserve subeigenvector inequalities.  
   This is especially useful if your proof manipulates expressions of the form
   \[
   x_i + \min(a,b) = \min(x_i+a, x_i+b).
   \]

5. **`tropical_and_bound`**  
   Even if from another domain, it may help combine two independent lower/upper constraints into one scalar bound.  
   Use it if you derive separate spectral constraints from the two rank-one components and need a combined estimate.

## Proof strategy options

### Strategy A: Entrywise monotonicity → spectral monotonicity
**Most promising** if `tropical_spectral_bound` behaves monotonically.

1. Define the surgery matrix and prove pointwise:
   ```lean
   tropicalRankTwoSurgery A u v u' v' i j ≤ A i j
   ```
2. Prove a general lemma: entrywise smaller tropical matrices have no larger spectral bound.
3. Apply the lemma to obtain the rank-2 surgery spectral inequality.

Why this is promising: it is robust, dimension-free, and likely aligns with the current catalog.

---

### Strategy B: Subeigenvector transport
Best if the library has a notion of tropical eigenvalue/subeigenvalue.

1. Suppose `λ` is a tropical eigenvalue upper certificate for `A`, witnessed by some vector `x`.
2. Show the same `x` certifies `λ` for the surgery matrix `B`, because every matrix entry only decreases.
3. Use `tropical_spectral_radius_le_eigenvalue` to conclude:
   \[
   \rho(B) \le \lambda.
   \]
   Then specialize `λ` to the spectral certificate for `A`.

Why this is deep: it reveals the true mechanism — surgery preserves subeigenvectors.

---

### Strategy C: Critical-cycle analysis and equality criterion
Most ambitious; attempt after proving monotonicity.

1. Formalize the set of edges where surgery is strict:
   \[
   S = \{(i,j) : B(i,j) < A(i,j)\}.
   \]
2. Define or reason about critical cycles realizing the tropical spectral radius of `A`.
3. Prove: if no critical cycle uses an edge from `S`, then all maximizing cycle means are unchanged, hence
   \[
   \rho(B)=\rho(A).
   \]

Why this matters: this is the tropical analogue of “low-rank perturbations away from the top eigenspace do not change the leading eigenvalue.”

## Cross-domain connections

Do not keep this inside tropical algebra. Explicitly connect it outward.

### 1. Graph algorithms / shortest paths
A min-plus matrix is a weighted directed graph. Surgery is a **two-edge weight decrease**.  
Your theorem becomes:

- decreasing two edge weights cannot increase the minimum cycle mean;
- if no optimal cycle uses those edges, the minimum cycle mean is unchanged.

This is shortest-path sensitivity and cycle optimization in disguise.

### 2. Discrete event systems / control
Min-plus linear systems model manufacturing, synchronization, and railway scheduling.  
Rank-2 surgery corresponds to adding two faster channels or reducing two delays.  
Your theorem gives a **certified performance improvement bound**.

### 3. Weighted automata / formal languages
Min-plus matrices encode transition costs. Surgery changes two transition templates.  
Spectral monotonicity translates to monotonicity of asymptotic average word cost.

### 4. Tropical geometry
Rank-one tropical matrices are outer sums defining tropical hyperplanes.  
Taking minima with two such outer sums is a **piecewise-linear surgery** on a tropical linear map.  
Spectral invariance under off-critical surgery suggests a new relation between tropical eigenspaces and stable cells of tropical polyhedral complexes.

### 5. Mathematical physics
Minimum cycle mean is the zero-temperature limit of a partition function / ground-state energy per step.  
Rank-2 surgery is a localized energy defect.  
Your equality theorem is a tropical analogue of **defect invisibility outside the ground-state support**.
