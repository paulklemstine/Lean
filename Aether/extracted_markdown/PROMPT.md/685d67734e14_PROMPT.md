## 2–3 Proof Strategy Paths

### Strategy A: Finite combinatorial Hodge decomposition
1. Define a cellular coboundary operator and a Laplacian on cochains of the finite tropical complex.
2. Characterize tropical Hodge classes as harmonic integral `(p,p)` representatives in the finite model.
3. Show that balanced weighted codimension-`p` subvarieties produce exactly these harmonic integral classes.

Why promising: finite-dimensional linear algebra over `ℤ`/`ℚ` is highly compatible with Lean and Mathlib. This route gives explicit witnesses and computational content.

### Strategy B: Divisor-first via tropical piecewise-linear functions
1. Define tropical Cartier divisors as equivalence classes of integral piecewise-linear functions.
2. Show every codimension-1 tropical Hodge class arises as the divisor of such a function.
3. Identify the divisor with a balanced tropical subvariety and conclude representability.

Why promising: the codimension-1 case is structurally simpler and mirrors the classical Lefschetz `(1,1)` theorem. This is likely the fastest route to a publishable formal theorem.

### Strategy C: Idempotent algebra and Hilbert-basis generation
1. Use `idempotent_hilbert_basis_theorem` as a conceptual template: finitely generated tropical semimodules of cycle data should admit finite generating balanced cycles.
2. Show the monoid/semimodule of effective balanced codimension-`p` cycles is finitely generated under your finite-complex hypotheses.
3. Prove every integral tropical Hodge class lies in the image of the generated cycle-class cone.

Why promising: this is the boldest cross-catalog route. It leverages existing idempotent algebraic infrastructure and could yield an algorithmic generation theorem, not just existence.

**Recommended order:** B first, then A, then C.  
B gives a sharp theorem quickly. A generalizes the conceptual framework. C is the field-opening upgrade that connects tropical Hodge theory to idempotent algebra and finite generation.
