## Core Mathematical Insight: Correct the Naive Local Model

The raw map
\[
x \mapsto \mathbb Z_p / x \mathbb Z_p
\]
cannot produce the full Cohen–Lenstra distribution on finite abelian p-groups, because every quotient of the rank-1 module \(\mathbb Z_p\) is cyclic. So the first breakthrough is to **formalize and prove the obstruction**, then replace the naive rank-1 model by a **random matrix / random endomorphism model** over \(\mathbb Z_p^n\), and finally pass to a stable restricted-product law.

This is already scientifically valuable: it separates a seductive but false local model from the mathematically viable one, and turns the heuristic into a theorem-shaped program.

### Theorem 2: finite-level normalization of cokernel weights

For fixed \(n,k\), let \(M_n(\mathbb Z/p^k\mathbb Z)\) be the finite ring of \(n\times n\) matrices. For each finite abelian \(p\)-group \(G\) of exponent dividing \(p^k\) and generated by at most \(n\) elements, define
\[
\mu_{n,k}(G)
= \frac{\#\{A \in M_n(\mathbb Z/p^k\mathbb Z) : \operatorname{coker}(A) \cong G\}}
        {\#M_n(\mathbb Z/p^k\mathbb Z)}.
\]
Prove that this is a probability distribution:
\[
\sum_G \mu_{n,k}(G) = 1.
\]

This theorem is finite, exact, and formalizable.

### Theorem 4: cyclic local law from valuations

This theorem captures the part of the naive model that is actually true.

For Haar-random \(x \in \mathbb Z_p\), the valuation \(v_p(x)\) has geometric distribution:
\[
\mathbb P(v_p(x)=n)=p^{-n}(1-p^{-1}),
\]
and
\[
\mathbb Z_p/x\mathbb Z_p \cong \mathbb Z/p^{v_p(x)}\mathbb Z.
\]
Therefore the rank-1 pushforward law is exactly the geometric law on cyclic \(p\)-groups.

## Proof Strategy Architecture

You must provide deep proofs, not superficial automation. Here are three viable proof paths.

### Strategy A: finite-level first, then pass to inverse/restricted limits
**Most promising.**

1. Work over \(R_k = \mathbb Z/p^k\mathbb Z\), where all spaces are finite and countable by cardinality.
2. Define cokernel classes via invariant factor partitions bounded by \(k\) and length \(\le n\).
3. Prove normalization by partitioning all matrices according to the isomorphism type of their cokernel.
4. Build product laws over finite prime sets using `finite_product_card` and related catalog theorems.
5. Only after this finite skeleton is complete, formulate the asymptotic/stable conjectural Cohen–Lenstra limit.

**Why most promising:** it avoids immediate dependence on full Haar measure infrastructure for profinite spaces while still capturing the exact combinatorial essence of the heuristic.

### Strategy B: module-theoretic classification over a DVR
1. Prove every finite quotient of \(\mathbb Z_p^n\) corresponds to a partition \(\lambda\) with at most \(n\) parts.
2. Analyze random endomorphisms of free rank-\(n\) modules over \(\mathbb Z_p\).
3. Push Haar measure on \(\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb Z_p}(\mathbb Z_p^n)\) to quotient types and show finite-dimensional cylinder approximations agree with mod-\(p^k\) counts.

**Why powerful:** this is conceptually closest to the true random-matrix origin of Cohen–Lenstra.  
**Why harder:** likely heavier dependence on available Mathlib support for \(p\)-adics, modules, and Haar pushforwards.

### Strategy C: classify the failure of the naive scalar model, then derive the corrected model
1. Prove rank-1 quotients are cyclic.
2. Show the induced law is geometric on cyclic groups via valuation counts.
3. Introduce rank-\(n\) endomorphism cokernel laws as the minimal correction.
4. Prove finite-product compatibility across primes.

**Why important:** this tells a compelling scientific story: not just what works, but why the naive heuristic fails and how mathematics repairs it.
