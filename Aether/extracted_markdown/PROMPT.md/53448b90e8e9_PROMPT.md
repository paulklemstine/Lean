## Assignment: Direction 4: Dynamical Systems Perspective on Repeated Squaring

*For the map f(x) = x² on ℤ/nℤ is a dynamical system whose fixed points are the idempotents — and the idempotents are the ghosts of the factorization.*

---

### The Deep Observation

The repeated squaring map $f_n: x \mapsto x^2 \bmod n$ is an endomorphism of the multiplicative monoid $(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}, \cdot)$. Its **functional graph** $G(f_n)$ — the directed graph with edges $x \to f_n(x)$ — is the dynamical fingerprint of $n$. The central revelation is that the **fixed points of $f_n$ are exactly the idempotents of the ring**, and by CRT, the number of idempotents is $2^{\omega(n)}$ where $\omega(n)$ counts distinct prime factors. A prime has exactly 2 idempotents $\{0, 1\}$; a composite $n = pq$ has 4, including the "spectral idempotents" $e, 1-e$ that encode the factorization. These extra fixed points are **bifurcation nodes** — additional attractors that fragment the phase space into basins whose boundaries carry the factorization.

This is not merely a restatement of CRT. It is the recognition that **primality is a dynamical property**: $n$ is prime if and only if the functional graph of $f_n$ has exactly two connected components in its subgraph of periodic points (the cycles). Every extra idempotent spawns an extra basin of attraction, and the orbit of a single random point, observed long enough, cannot help but reveal which basin it belongs to — and therefore whether $n$ is prime.

### Precise Theorem Targets

#### Theorem 1: Idempotent Count via Dynamical Fixed Points

```lean
theorem idempotent_count_fixed_points (n : ℕ) (hn : n > 1) :
    Finset.card {x : ZMod n | x * x = x} = 2 ^ (Nat.factorization n).toFinset.card :=
  sorry
```

*Proof Strategy A (CRT + Chinese counting)*: By CRT, $x^2 = x$ in $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ iff $x^2 = x$ in each $\mathbb{Z}/p_i^{a_i}\mathbb{Z}$. In each $\mathbb{Z}/p^a\mathbb{Z}$, the only solutions are $0$ and $1$ (since $x(x-1) \equiv 0 \bmod p^a$ and $\gcd(x, x-1) = 1$ forces $p^a | x$ or $p^a | (x-1)$). By CRT independence, the total count is $2^{\omega(n)}$. **This is the most direct path** — it reduces to a standard CRT argument plus the local irreducibility of $x(x-1)$.

*Proof Strategy B (Ring-theoretic)*: The idempotents of a product ring $R \times S$ are pairs $(e_R, e_S)$ of idempotents. By induction on the number of prime factors and the fact that $\mathbb{Z}/p^a\mathbb{Z}$ is a local ring (hence has only trivial idempotents), the result follows. **More structural but requires building the idempotent theory of local rings.**

#### Theorem 2: CRT Orbit Decomposition (Structural)

```lean
theorem crt_orbit_decomposition {n p q : ℕ} (hpq : Nat.Coprime p q) (hn : n = p * q) :
    ∀ a : ZMod n,
    let f := fun x : ZMod n => x * x
    let fp := fun x : ZMod p => x * x
    let fq := fun x : ZMod q => x * x
    -- The orbit type (preperiod, period) of a under f equals
    -- the lcm-max of the orbit types under fp and fq
    orbitType f a = maxOrbitType (orbitType fp (a mod p)) (orbitType fq (a mod q)) :=
  sorry
```

where `maxOrbitType (ρ₁, λ₁) (ρ₂, λ₂) = (max ρ₁ ρ₂, lcm λ₁ λ₂)`.

*Proof Strategy*: The orbit of $a$ under $f_n$ maps componentwise to the orbits of $a \bmod p$ under $f_p$ and $a \bmod q$ under $f_q$ by CRT. The pre-period of the product orbit is $\max(\rho_1, \rho_2)$ because both components must enter their cycles. The period is $\text{lcm}(\lambda_1, \lambda_2)$ because the product orbit repeats iff both component orbits repeat simultaneously. **Key lemma**: the CRT isomorphism is an equivariant map with respect to the squaring endomorphism on both sides.

```lean
    ∃ e : ZMod n, e * e = e ∧ e ≠ 0 ∧ e ≠ 1 :=

```

```lean
theorem composite_orbit_type_lower_bound (n : ℕ) (hn : n > 1) (hcomp : ¬Nat.Prime n) 
    (hω : (Nat.factorization n).toFinset.card ≥ 2) :
    -- The number of distinct orbit types (preperiod, period) in (Z/nZ)*
    -- under squaring is at least 3
    Finset.card (orbitTypeSet n).toFinset ≥ 3 :=
  sorry
```

*Proof Strategy*: From Theorem 1, composite $n$ with $\omega(n) \geq 2$ has at least 4 idempotents, hence at least 2 non-trivial ones ($e \neq 0, 1$). Each non-trivial idempotent $e$ is a fixed point of $f_n$, so its orbit type is $(0, 1)$. But $e$ and $1$ are in different basins of attraction (since $e$ is idempotent and $1-e$ is also idempotent, with $e(1-e) = 0$). Elements near $e$ vs near $1$ vs near $0$ will generically have different pre-periods, giving at least 3 distinct orbit types. **The critical step** is showing that elements in the "shadow" of different idempotents have different orbit characteristics — this follows from the CRT decomposition and the fact that the idempotents project to different components.

### Cross-Domain Connections

1. **Dynamical Systems → Number Theory**: The functional graph $G(f_n)$ is a **phase portrait** whose topology (number of basins, cycle structure, convergence rates) encodes the arithmetic of $n$. This is the first step toward a **topological dynamics of factorization**.

2. **Ergodic Theory → Cryptography**: The mixing rate of $f_n$ on $(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^*$ is governed by the order of $2$ modulo the group orders $p_i - 1$. For RSA moduli $n = pq$, the **desynchronization rate** between the $p$-component and $q$-component under iteration determines the security against cycle-detection attacks. This connects to the Ruelle–Perron–Frobenius theory of transfer operators.

3. **Information Theory → Primality**: Define the **orbit entropy** $H(n) = -\sum_{(\rho,\lambda)} p_{\rho,\lambda} \log p_{\rho,\lambda}$ where $p_{\rho,\lambda}$ is the probability that a random $a \in (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^*$ has orbit type $(\rho, \lambda)$. By CRT independence (Theorem 2), for $n = pq$ with $\gcd(p-1, q-1) = 2$: $H(pq) \geq H(p) + H(q) - \log 2$ (**superadditivity of orbit entropy**). Primes cannot achieve this entropy — it is a **dynamical certificate of compositeness**.

4. **Spectral Graph Theory → Factorization**: The Laplacian spectrum of $G(f_n)$ encodes the cycle structure. For composite $n$, the spectral gap is generically smaller than for primes of comparable size (more basins = more low-frequency modes). This connects factorization to **Ramanujan-type spectral bounds**.

### Revolutionary Significance

This opens the field of **arithmetic dynamics of endomorphisms**: the study of how algebraic properties of a ring $R$ are encoded in the dynamical behavior of its endomorphisms. The specific case of $x \mapsto x^2$ on $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ is the tip of the iceberg:

- **Generalization**: For any endomorphism $\phi$ of a finite ring $R$, the functional graph $G(\phi)$ carries information about the ideal structure of $R$. The idempotents of $R$ are always among the periodic points of any endomorphism.
- **Algorithmic consequence**: An orbit-structure-based primality test that examines the *topology* of the functional graph (number of basins, cycle structure) rather than searching for witnesses. This is conceptually different from Miller–Rabin: it is a **topological test**, not an algebraic one.
- **Physical analogy**: The basins of attraction of the idempotents under squaring are analogous to the **energy minima** in a spin glass. The factorization of $n$ determines the "energy landscape," and the orbit of a random point is a "thermal trajectory" that must settle into one of the minima.
