## Central Mathematical Objective

### Main conjectural breakthrough theorem
Let \(p\) be a homogeneous polynomial in \(n\) variables with nonnegative coefficients. Write its coefficient function on exponent vectors as
\[
w_p : \alpha \mapsto \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}
\]
or in Lean as a map on finitely supported exponent vectors. Assume:

1. `Homogeneous p d`
2. all coefficients are nonnegative
3. the support of \(p\) is \(M\)-convex
4. `ValuatedExchange p 1`

Then for every variable \(i\), the partial derivative \(\partial_i p\) also satisfies
\[
\mathrm{ValuatedExchange}(\partial_i p, 1).
\]

This would mean the \(K=1\) exchange cone is **closed under all coordinate derivations**.

### Precise theorem statement to target
You should formalize a theorem of the following shape, adapting names to the actual catalog definitions:

```lean
theorem valuatedExchange_one_deriv_closed
  {σ : Type*} [Fintype σ] [DecidableEq σ]
  (p : MvPolynomial σ ℝ)
  (d : ℕ)
  (hp_hom : p.IsHomogeneousOfDegree d)
  (hp_nonneg : ∀ s, 0 ≤ MvPolynomial.coeff s p)
  (hp_mconvex : MConvexSupport p)
  (hp_vx : ValuatedExchange p 1) :
  ∀ i : σ, ValuatedExchange (MvPolynomial.pderiv i p) 1
```

If the catalog uses a coefficient-family representation instead of `MvPolynomial`, also prove the coefficient-level analogue; in fact, that may be the more robust formal statement:

```lean
theorem valuatedExchange_one_on_derivative_coeffs
  {σ : Type*} [Fintype σ] [DecidableEq σ]
  (w : (σ →₀ ℕ) → ℝ)
  (d : ℕ)
  (hw_hom : HomogeneousWeight d w)
  (hw_nonneg : ∀ m, 0 ≤ w m)
  (hw_mconvex : MConvexFunctionSupport w)
  (hw_vx : ValuatedExchangeWeight w 1) :
  ∀ i : σ, ValuatedExchangeWeight (partialDerivativeWeight i w) 1
```

You should also isolate and prove the degree-2 base case as a standalone theorem:

```lean
theorem valuatedExchange_one_deriv_closed_degree_two
  {σ : Type*} [Fintype σ] [DecidableEq σ]
  (p : MvPolynomial σ ℝ)
  (hp_hom : p.IsHomogeneousOfDegree 2)

```

This degree-2 theorem is not merely warm-up: it is the first nontrivial test of whether the closure principle reflects a genuine exchange-geometric mechanism rather than hidden Lorentzian structure.

## Why this is a breakthrough

If true, this theorem would identify \(K=1\) valuated exchange as a **differentially stable positivity notion**. That matters because derivative closure is one of the defining signatures of deep convexity/concavity classes:

- stable polynomials survive specialization and differentiation,
- Lorentzian polynomials survive differentiation,
- now valuated exchange at the sharp constant \(1\) may survive differentiation too.

This would open a new field: **exchange-positive algebraic combinatorics**, where discrete convex support geometry and coefficient inequalities interact directly with differential operators. It could become a lighter-weight alternative to Lorentzian machinery for proving:

- ultra log-concavity,
- negative dependence shadows,
- Hodge-type inequalities for combinatorial generating functions,
- structural closure under deletion/contraction-like operations.

The conceptual leap is that differentiation is the algebraic shadow of **contraction** in matroid theory. So proving derivative closure says the exchange inequality is stable under a fundamental combinatorial operation.

## Proof strategies

## Strategy A: Degree-2 anchor + induction on degree
**Most promising if catalog Theorem 5 is strong and reusable.**

### Step 1
Prove that if `p` is homogeneous of degree `2`, then for every variable `i`, `pderiv i p` is homogeneous of degree `1` and has nonnegative coefficients. Its support is a subset of singleton exponents / linear support.

### Step 2
Invoke the catalog theorem analogous to `valuatedExchange_of_linear_nonneg` to conclude `ValuatedExchange (pderiv i p) 1`.

### Step 3
For general degree, induct on `d`. Show each derivative coefficient is a linear rescaling of a degree-`d` coefficient:
\[
c_{\partial_i p}(\alpha)= (\alpha_i+1)c_p(\alpha+e_i).
\]
Then transport the exchange inequality from `p` to `∂_i p` by analyzing how the rescaling factor behaves under the exchange move. The key subgoal is to show the multiplicative factors preserve the `max/min` structure needed by the \(K=1\) inequality.

**Why promising:** it leverages already-certified linear-support results and turns the general theorem into a clean coefficient identity plus a discrete inequality on exponents.

---

## Strategy B: Support contraction + matroidal exchange geometry
**Most conceptually powerful.**

### Step 1
Prove that differentiating in coordinate `i` corresponds to contracting along `i` at the support level:
\[
\operatorname{supp}(\partial_i p)=\{ \alpha \mid \alpha+e_i \in \operatorname{supp}(p)\ \text{and}\ (\alpha_i+1)c_p(\alpha+e_i)\neq 0\}.
\]

### Step 2
Show that if `support p` is \(M\)-convex, then the contraction shadow is again \(M\)-convex (or satisfies the exact exchange axiom needed in the derivative proof).

### Step 3
Lift this support-level contraction theorem to coefficients. The derivative exchange inequality should then be interpreted as the original exchange inequality on `α+e_i`, `β+e_i` with carefully chosen exchange indices.

**Why promising:** this directly mirrors matroid contraction, making the theorem structurally inevitable rather than coefficient-accidental. It also creates reusable infrastructure for deletion/contraction operations on valuated supports.

## Strategy C: Bridge through Lorentzian recognition
**Best if the catalog Lorentzian recognition theorem is especially complete.**

### Step 1
Identify a certified theorem in `LorentzianRecognitionComplete.lean` showing that your hypotheses imply Lorentzianity, or prove an implication in a restricted regime (e.g. weighted uniform matroids, degree 2, or strongly Rayleigh-type coefficient arrays).

### Step 2
Apply the Brändén–Huh derivative closure theorem already formalized or formalizable in the catalog to obtain that all first derivatives are Lorentzian.

### Step 3
Prove that in the presence of \(M\)-convex support and nonnegative coefficients, Lorentzianity of the derivative implies `ValuatedExchange _ 1`.

**Why promising:** this imports a known robust closure theorem, but the real contribution is the reverse bridge: showing \(K=1\) exchange is the “visible discrete shadow” of derivative-stable Lorentzian structure.

**Why less direct:** it risks reducing your result to an implication chain that may be too dependent on stronger hypotheses than desired. Use it either as a proof in special cases or as a cross-domain theorem if the full conjecture resists direct proof.
