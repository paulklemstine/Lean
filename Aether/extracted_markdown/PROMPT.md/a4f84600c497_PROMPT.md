## Precise Theorem Targets

You should introduce at least one genuinely new definition. A good candidate is a prime-sensitive torsion profile on finite abelian groups or on homology groups of finite complexes.

### New definition 1: prime torsion weight
For a finite abelian group `A`, define
\[
\mathrm{ptw}_\ell(A) := v_\ell(\#A),
\]
the ℓ-adic valuation of its cardinality.

If direct finite cardinality of torsion homology is awkward in Lean, define first on finite abelian groups represented as `ZMod n` products, or on finitely generated abelian groups with finite torsion part extracted through Smith normal form data.

### New definition 2: torsion echo profile
For a finite simplicial complex `K` and dimension `k`, define the torsion echo at prime `ℓ`
\[
\mathrm{echo}_\ell(K,k) := v_\ell\!\left(\#\,\mathrm{Tor}\, H_k(K;\mathbb Z)\right).
\]
If full singular/simplicial homology over `ℤ` is too heavy, define an algebraic surrogate from boundary matrices:
\[
\mathrm{echoMatrix}_\ell(\partial_{k+1},\partial_k)
\]
using Smith invariants of the chain complex.

### New definition 3: prime-separation predicate
\[
\mathrm{PrimeSeparated}(K,k) :\!\iff \exists \ell_1\neq \ell_2,\ \mathrm{echo}_{\ell_1}(K,k)\neq \mathrm{echo}_{\ell_2}(K,k).
\]
This is the formal nucleus of the conjectural non-universality phenomenon.

### Theorem 3: mod-ℓ lower bound from torsion echo
This is the crossroad theorem connecting integral torsion to field-coefficient topology.

A mathematically meaningful statement is:
for a finitely generated abelian homology group `H`, if `echo_ℓ(H) > 0`, then
\[
\dim_{\mathbf F_\ell}(H \otimes \mathbf F_\ell) \ge \operatorname{rank}(H)+1.
\]
Equivalently, ℓ-torsion forces a strict increase in mod-ℓ homology relative to the rational rank.

## Main Conjecture to State Precisely

You must formalize a falsifiable conjecture, even if only as markdown and computational specification.

### Arithmetic Non-Universality Conjecture for Random Flag Complexes
Let `X(n,p)` be the clique complex of `G(n,p)`. Then there exist `k ≥ 1`, primes `ℓ ≠ q`, and a critical-window scaling
\[
p(n)=n^{-1/(k+1)}\cdot \lambda(n)
\]
with `λ(n)` bounded away from `0` and `∞`, such that for some normalization `a_n > 0`,
\[
\frac{v_\ell(|\operatorname{Tor} H_k(X(n,p);\mathbb Z)|)}{a_n}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac{v_q(|\operatorname{Tor} H_k(X(n,p);\mathbb Z)|)}{a_n}
\]
do not converge to the same limiting law.

### Computational refutation criterion
The conjecture is refuted if for every tested pair of distinct primes `ℓ,q`, and every tested critical-window scaling, the empirical distributions of normalized torsion echoes become statistically indistinguishable as `n` increases.

### Computational support criterion
The conjecture is supported if there exist `k, ℓ, q` and a critical-window regime where the Kolmogorov–Smirnov distance, Wasserstein distance, or moment profile between the normalized empirical laws stabilizes away from zero across growing `n`.

## Proof Strategy Architecture

You must pursue 2–3 proof avenues, not a single hint.

### Strategy A: Smith normal form as the arithmetic microscope
1. Model homology torsion from integer boundary matrices via Smith diagonal entries.
2. Show torsion echo is exactly the sum of ℓ-adic valuations of nontrivial Smith invariants.
3. Derive prime separation by constructing matrices whose Smith diagonals contain different prime powers.
4. Prove mod-ℓ rank jump results by reducing Smith data modulo `ℓ`.

**Why this is most promising:** Mathlib already has strong linear algebra and matrix infrastructure. Smith-normal-form style arguments convert topological torsion questions into explicit arithmetic matrix statements that are far more formalization-friendly than full asymptotic topology.

### Strategy B: Universal coefficient bridge
1. Formalize the relation between integral homology and homology over `ZMod ℓ`.
2. Show positive ℓ-torsion forces additional mod-ℓ dimension beyond rational rank.
3. Use this to define experimentally accessible prime signatures in random complexes.
4. Interpret prime-sensitive torsion as a failure of field-coefficient universality.

**Why it is powerful:** this creates the bridge to probabilistic topology and makes the theory measurable in simulations, not just abstractly definable.

### Strategy C: Euler/combinatorial constraints and sparse random matrices
1. Express low-dimensional chain groups via simplex counts in flag complexes.
2. Analyze boundary matrices as sparse signed incidence matrices.
3. Prove deterministic torsion vanishing/nonvanishing criteria from matrix patterns: unimodularity, block decomposition, divisibility structure.
4. Use these criteria to motivate why different primes might appear with different frequencies in random windows.

**Why this opens the field:** it connects random topology with arithmetic statistics of sparse integer matrices, a domain with deep links to sandpile groups, random cokernels, and Cohen–Lenstra-type heuristics.

**Recommended order:** Start with Strategy A, use Strategy B for the key cross-domain theorem, and use Strategy C to motivate the random-complex experiments and future asymptotics.

## Cross-Domain Connections You Must Exploit

At least one theorem and part of the exposition must connect to another domain.

### 1. Arithmetic statistics
Prime torsion echoes are a topological analogue of prime bias in class groups and random cokernels. This suggests a bridge to Cohen–Lenstra heuristics, Wood’s random matrix models, and distributional arithmetic invariants.

### 2. Spectral graph theory / random matrices
Flag complexes come from graphs. Their boundary operators inherit sparse random structure from clique incidence. Prime-sensitive torsion may correspond to arithmetic defects invisible to real or complex spectra.

### 3. Information theory
Define a finite-sample observable such as the empirical distribution of `echo_ℓ(K,k)` across random draws. Compare primes using KL divergence, total variation, or Wasserstein distance. This turns the conjecture into a statistical distinguishability problem.

### 4. Physics-inspired viewpoint
Interpret torsion echo as an arithmetic “hidden order parameter” for topological phase transitions. Betti numbers detect continuous degrees of freedom; torsion echo detects discrete, quantized obstructions.

### 5. Computational complexity
Computing integer homology is algorithmically harder than field homology. A verified algorithm that isolates prime-sensitive torsion efficiently would contribute to certified computational topology.
