### New Definitions to Introduce

Define a structure expressing the canonical tropical-kernel data on a subset `S`:

## Recommended Lean 4 Theorem Signatures

The exact signatures may need adaptation to the catalog’s graph model, but your file should contain theorem statements of roughly this precision.

### Theorem 1: Canonical generators are independent modulo constants

```lean
theorem canonicalKernel_independent_mod_constants
    {V : Type*} [Fintype V] [DecidableEq V]
    (G : SimpleGraph V) (S : Finset V)
    (hconn : G.Connected)
    (hS : S.Nonempty)
    (hsep : SeparatedOn G S)
    :
    LinearIndependent ℤ
      (fun i : CanonicalKernelIndex G S =>
        (CanonicalKernelGenerator.repr G S i :
          ((V → ℤ) ⧸ AddSubgroup.closure {f | ∃ c : ℤ, f = fun _ => c})))
```

If quotient-linear algebra over `ℤ` is awkward, reformulate using:
- pairwise non-equivalence modulo constants,
- independence in a chosen normalized slice,
- or linear independence over `ℚ` after coercion.

### Theorem 2: Canonical kernel span models the restricted critical group

```lean
theorem canonicalKernelSpan_quot_equiv_restrictedCriticalGroup
    {V : Type*} [Fintype V] [DecidableEq V]
    (G : SimpleGraph V) (S : Finset V)
    (hconn : G.Connected)
    (hS : S.Nonempty)
    (hsep : SeparatedOn G S)
    :
    Nonempty
      (CanonicalKernelSpan G S ≃+ RestrictedCriticalGroup G S)
```

If a full equivalence is too ambitious in one cycle, prove the two halves separately:
1. a surjective map from canonical tropical generators to the quotient,
2. injectivity modulo constants and firing relations.

But the target should be an actual additive equivalence.

### Theorem 3: Rank/generator-count agreement with restricted Laplacian arithmetic

```lean
theorem canonicalKernel_generator_count_eq_rank
    {V : Type*} [Fintype V] [DecidableEq V]

    (hconn : G.Connected)
```

If `Module.rank ℤ` is not the right arithmetic invariant in the formalization, replace it with:

### Cross-domain theorem: harmonic rigidity gives chip-firing uniqueness on tree attachments

```lean
theorem harmonic_tree_attachment_forces_unique_firing_extension
    {V : Type*} [Fintype V] [DecidableEq V]
    (G : SimpleGraph V) (S T : Finset V)
    (hconn : G.Connected)
    (hsep : SeparatedOn G S)
    (htree : IsTreeAttachmentAlong G S T)

```

This is the theorem that turns tropical rigidity into chip-firing propagation and is exactly the kind of nontrivial bridge result this project needs.

## Mathematical Strategy: 3 Proof Routes

You must include at least 2–3 serious proof strategies in your planning comments or documentation, and then execute the strongest one. Here are the recommended routes.

### Strategy A — Quotient-lattice comparison via restricted Laplacian
**Most promising.**

1. Define the restricted divisor group on `S` and the restricted Laplacian image.
2. Use `constant_isHarmonicOn` to identify constants as the only unavoidable degeneracy.
3. Use separation to prove every canonical generator has a unique normalized representative.
4. Construct a homomorphism from canonical generators into the critical-group quotient.
5. Prove injectivity using harmonic rigidity (`harmonic_leaf_rigidity`) and by contradiction: if two canonical generators differ by a firing move, then the normalized harmonic representative collapses.
6. Prove surjectivity by showing every divisor class admits a harmonic/tropically reduced representative.

**Why this is most promising:** it converts a geometric uniqueness statement into a lattice quotient statement, matching the known arithmetic nature of the critical group.

### Strategy B — Smith normal form through tropical normalization
1. Formalize the restricted Laplacian matrix on `S`.
2. Produce a normalized family of harmonic kernel generators.
3. Show the transition matrix from canonical generators to a divisor basis is unimodular on the free part.
4. Compare diagonal invariants with Smith normal form.
5. Deduce cardinality/rank and generator-count equality.

**Why it matters:** this gives the sharpest arithmetic statement and ties directly to computation, but may require heavier matrix infrastructure.

### Strategy C — Inductive graph decomposition by leaf pruning and cycle core
1. Decompose connected graphs into a cycle core plus tree attachments.
2. Use `harmonic_leaf_rigidity` to show canonical generator data is determined by the cycle core.
3. Prove chip-firing classes extend uniquely along leaves.
4. Induct on the number of pruned leaves to transfer the correspondence from the core to the full graph.

**Why this is attractive:** it is structurally elegant and likely to produce the strongest conceptual theorem, especially for a “separation hypothesis” tailored to forbid pathological overlap.

## Separation Hypothesis: Make It Precise

Do not leave `SeparatedOn G S` as hand-wavy prose. It should be formal enough to drive proofs. For example, one of the following formulations may work:

1. **Support-separation version:** distinct canonical kernel generators have disjoint or minimally intersecting support on `S`.
2. **Path-separation version:** every pair of vertices in `S` is distinguished by a unique harmonic profile along paths/cycle cuts.
3. **Restriction-faithfulness version:** if two harmonic functions agree on a distinguished subset of `S`, then they differ only by a constant.

The third is often easiest to use in Lean because it becomes a clean extensional principle:
```lean
def SeparatedOn (G : SimpleGraph V) (S : Finset V) : Prop :=
  ∀ ⦃f g : V → ℤ⦄,
    IsHarmonicOn G S f →
    IsHarmonicOn G S g →
    NormalizedOn S f →
    NormalizedOn S g →
    (∀ v ∈ S, f v = g v) →
    f = g
```
or a weaker modulo-constants version if needed.

A good theorem to prove early is that your separation hypothesis implies normalized uniqueness modulo constants.
