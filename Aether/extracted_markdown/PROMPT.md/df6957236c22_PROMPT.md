## Theorem Cluster A: Sharp Polynomial Lower Growth Along the Minimal Branch

The previous “exponential depth-growth” hypothesis is too optimistic globally. The mathematically interesting phenomenon is that the **minimal hypotenuse at depth n is governed by the slow branch**, and this should be made exact.

### Target Theorem A1: Quadratic lower bound for all words

Let `cOfWord w` denote the hypotenuse of the primitive triple obtained by applying the Berggren word `w` to the root `(3,4,5)`.

**Precise statement:**
There exists an explicit constant `C > 0` such that for every Berggren word `w` of length `n`,
\[
cOfWord(w) \ge C n^2 + O(n),
\]
and in fact one can choose an explicit elementary bound such as
\[
cOfWord(w) \ge 2n^2 + 2n + 5.
\]
Even better: prove the exact formula for the all-`A` branch and show it minimizes hypotenuse at fixed depth.

### Breakthrough version
Prove that among all words of length `n`, the word `A^n` yields the smallest hypotenuse, and compute it exactly:
\[
c(A^n) = 2n^2 + 4n + 5.
\]
Hence
\[
\min_{\lvert w\rvert = n} cOfWord(w) = 2n^2 + 4n + 5.
\]

This is the first genuine extremal theorem for the Berggren dynamics: not just growth, but **exact depth-optimal arithmetic complexity**.

### Why this is a breakthrough
This converts the free semigroup theorem into a **quantitative symbolic-arithmetic dictionary**. It says depth in the symbolic tree corresponds to quadratic Diophantine complexity in the minimal case. That is a formal theorem about orbit geometry, not mere enumeration.

### Proof strategies

#### Strategy A: Exact recurrence on the A-branch + monotonic domination
1. Compute the matrix action of `A` explicitly on triples and derive recurrences for `(a_n,b_n,c_n)`.
2. Solve the recurrence exactly for `c_n`, obtaining `2n^2 + 4n + 5`.
3. Prove each generator increases `c` by at least as much as `A` does, in a state-dependent sense; then show replacing any letter by `A` weakly decreases the resulting hypotenuse.

**Why promising:** It stays elementary and close to the formalized semigroup action. If the catalog already has matrix-action lemmas and positivity/primitive invariants, this is the shortest path.

#### Strategy B: Euclid-parameter transport
1. Identify the action of Berggren generators on Euclid parameters `(m,n)` for primitive triples.
2. Show depth corresponds to compositions of affine or unimodular maps on the positive cone.
3. Prove the `A`-branch minimizes the quadratic form `m^2 + n^2` among all depth-`n` parameter images.

**Why promising:** This reveals the hidden geometry. If successful, it opens a general framework for extremal questions via positive cones and quadratic forms.

#### Strategy C: Monovariant / tropicalization viewpoint
1. Define a weighted potential on triples, e.g. `Φ(a,b,c) = c` or a more flexible linear functional.
2. Show each generator acts by a positive matrix, and `A` is pointwise minimal on the positive cone after ordering coordinates appropriately.
3. Deduce extremality of repeated `A` by a semigroup comparison theorem.

**Why promising:** This abstracts the argument and may generalize to other matrix trees beyond Pythagorean triples.

## Theorem Cluster B: Finite-Quotient Mixing and Modular Equidistribution

The original equidistribution hypothesis should be sharpened. “Admissible residue classes” should not be defined by prime factorization conditions modulo `m`; that is global and mismatched to finite quotient dynamics. The correct formal object is the orbit of the root triple under the Berggren semigroup modulo `m`.

### Target Theorem B1: Uniform distribution on the finite reachable orbit under a primitivity hypothesis

Let `S_m` be the finite set of reachable residues in `(Z/mZ)^3` obtained from the Berggren semigroup action on the root modulo `m`. Let `μ_n(x)` be the proportion of words of length `n` mapping the root to residue `x`.

**Precise statement:**
Assume the directed graph on `S_m` induced by the three generators is strongly connected and aperiodic. Then
\[
\mu_n(x) \to \frac{1}{|S_m|}
\quad\text{as } n\to\infty
\]
for every `x ∈ S_m`.

This is a finite-state Markov-chain theorem specialized to Berggren dynamics, but formalized in a way that turns arithmetic semigroup actions into exact modular statistics.

### Lean 4 target signature
You may need `ZMod m` and finite-state transition machinery.

```lean
def ModTriple (m : ℕ) := ZMod m × ZMod m × ZMod m

def applyWordMod (m : ℕ) : Word → ModTriple m → ModTriple m
def rootMod (m : ℕ) : ModTriple m := (3, 4, 5)

def reachableMod (m : ℕ) : Finset (ModTriple m) := sorry

  ((List.replicateM n [Gen.A, Gen.B, Gen.C]).countP

    (hsc : StronglyConnected (berggrenGraph m))
    (hap : Aperiodic (berggrenGraph m)) :
    Filter.Tendsto (fun n => mu m n x) Filter.atTop
      (nhds (1 / (reachableMod m).card : ℚ)) := by
  sorry
```

If asymptotic limits in `ℚ` are too awkward, first prove exact stationarity of the uniform distribution and convergence in a finite-dimensional stochastic operator norm.

### Why this is a breakthrough
This would be one of the cleanest formally verified examples of **arithmetic mixing in a thin semigroup orbit**. It reframes the Berggren tree as a finite-quotient dynamical system and creates a pathway toward affine sieve-style statements.

### Proof strategies

#### Strategy A: Finite Markov chain formalization
1. Build the directed multigraph on reachable residues modulo `m`.
2. Define the averaging operator
   \[
   T f(x)=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{g\in\{A,B,C\}} f(gx).
   \]
3. Invoke or formalize the Perron–Frobenius/finite irreducible aperiodic Markov chain convergence theorem.

**Why promising:** Conceptually clean and modular. Once done, it becomes reusable infrastructure for many arithmetic dynamical systems.

#### Strategy B: Semigroup averaging via adjacency matrices
1. Represent transitions by a stochastic matrix `P_m`.
2. Show row-stochasticity and that uniform measure on the reachable strongly connected component is stationary if the graph is regular in the right sense.
3. Deduce convergence from powers `P_m^n`.

**Why promising:** More concrete for Lean. Matrix powers over `ℚ` or `ℝ` may be easier than abstract Markov-chain APIs.

#### Strategy C: Representation-theoretic finite quotient analysis
1. Study the semigroup generated by the three matrices inside `Mat₃(ZMod m)`.
2. Identify orbit decomposition and possible obstructions to mixing.
3. Use this to classify moduli `m` for which strong connectivity and aperiodicity hold.

**Why promising:** Harder, but scientifically richer. It turns the theorem into a classification project rather than a black-box convergence result.

## Theorem Cluster C: Modular Reachability and Orbit Classification

Before equidistribution, one needs the finite quotient orbit itself.

### Target Theorem C1: Reachable-set characterization modulo small coprime moduli

For fixed `m` coprime to `2`, classify the set
\[
S_m = \Gamma \cdot (3,4,5) \subseteq (\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^3,
\]
where `Γ` is the Berggren semigroup.

At minimum, prove:
- `S_m` is finite and computable;
- each generator preserves the quadratic relation `a^2 + b^2 = c^2`;
- each generator preserves primitivity modulo units where meaningful;
- for small `m` (e.g. `3,5,7,11`), `S_m` equals the set of residues satisfying the modular Pythagorean relation plus an explicit parity/congruence condition.

### Lean 4 target signature
```lean

    (m : ℕ) (w : Word) :

    t.1^2 + t.2.1^2 = t.2.2^2 := by
  sorry

theorem reachableMod_finite (m : ℕ) :
    (reachableMod m).Finite := by
  sorry
```

For concrete moduli:
```lean
theorem reachableMod_eq_solutionSet_mod5 :
    reachableMod 5 = solutionSetMod 5 := by
  sorry
```

### Why this matters
This is the arithmetic-geometric skeleton underlying the equidistribution theorem. It is also the formal entry point into **thin orbits modulo q**, a central object in modern number theory.

### Proof strategies

#### Strategy A: Direct matrix congruence computation
1. Reduce Berggren matrices mod `m`.
2. Verify preservation of the quadratic cone by direct algebra.
3. Enumerate reachable states and compare to a definable solution set.

#### Strategy B: Orthogonal-group viewpoint
1. Show each Berggren matrix lies in the integral orthogonal group of `x^2+y^2-z^2`.
2. Reduce mod `m`.
3. Interpret the orbit as a semigroup orbit inside the modular light cone.

**Why promising:** This creates a conceptual bridge to arithmetic groups and hyperbolic geometry.

## Cross-Domain Connections You Should Exploit

### 1. Symbolic dynamics
The free semigroup theorem says the Berggren tree is a one-sided shift on three symbols with no collisions. Growth theorems convert symbolic depth into arithmetic size. This is a formal analogue of coding geodesics or continued fractions.

### 2. Thin groups and affine sieve
The Berggren semigroup is a toy model for thin-orbit arithmetic. Modular orbit classification and equidistribution are exactly the finite-quotient shadows needed before one can even dream of a formal affine sieve.

### 3. Hyperbolic / Lorentzian geometry
The equation `a^2 + b^2 = c^2` is the light cone of the quadratic form `x^2+y^2-z^2`. Berggren matrices act by integral Lorentz isometries. This geometric reinterpretation may simplify invariant proofs.

### 4. Automata and algorithmics
Once modular reachability is formalized, the Berggren tree becomes a finite-state automaton modulo `m`. This enables certified BFS enumeration, residue filters, and exact counting algorithms.

### 5. Spectral theory and mixing
Finite quotient averaging operators connect directly to Perron–Frobenius and expander-style heuristics. Even if only finite-state in this cycle, the conceptual leap is major.
