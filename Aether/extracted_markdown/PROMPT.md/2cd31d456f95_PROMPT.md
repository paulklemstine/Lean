## Core theorem target

### Mathematical statement

Let `q` be an odd prime power, let `G = Sp₄(𝔽_q)`, and let `s ∈ G` be a regular semisimple toral element lying in an `𝔽_q`-rational maximal torus `T`. Suppose `(s,t)` is a certified generating pair in the sense of `ClassicalGenCertificate`. Let `μ` be the symmetric probability measure on `G` supported on `{s, s⁻¹, t, t⁻¹}`. Denote by `λ₂(μ)` the operator norm of the averaging operator on the orthogonal complement of constants in `ℓ²(G)`.

**Breakthrough theorem to prove:**

> There exist explicit constants `C > 0` and `ε₀ > 0`, independent of odd prime powers `q`, such that for every odd prime power `q`, every certified pair `(s,t)` in `Sp₄(𝔽_q)` with `s` regular toral and lying in a maximal torus `T` satisfying the Deligne–Lusztig character-ratio bound
> \[
> \forall \rho \in \mathrm{Irr}(G),\ \rho \neq 1,\qquad
> \left|\frac{\chi_\rho(s)}{\chi_\rho(1)}\right| \le C q^{-1},
> \]
> one has
> \[
> \lambda_2(\mu) \le 1 - \varepsilon_0.
> \]
> In particular, the Cayley graph `Cay(G, {s,s⁻¹,t,t⁻¹})` is a family of uniform expanders as `q` varies.

This is the “if Deligne–Lusztig ratio bounds hold, then uniform spectral gap follows” theorem. It is already a major formal contribution because it isolates the exact representation-theoretic input needed for higher-rank expansion.

### Stronger theorem target

Push beyond the conditional theorem if possible:

> For a distinguished family of regular toral generators `s_q ∈ Sp₄(𝔽_q)` arising from a fixed torus type, there exist explicit `C, ε₀ > 0` such that the above character-ratio hypothesis holds uniformly in `q`, hence the corresponding certified Cayley graphs form a uniform expander family.

This would be a field-opening result: the first formalized higher-rank spectral gap theorem driven by Deligne–Lusztig input.

## Proof strategy architecture

You must give Aristotle multiple routes. Here are the main ones.

### Strategy A — Abstract representation-theoretic transference (most promising)
This is the cleanest and most formalization-friendly route.

1. **Abstract spectral estimate.**
   Prove for any finite group and symmetric measure `μ` that
   \[
   \lambda_2(\mu) \le \sup_{\rho \neq 1} \left\| \widehat{\mu}(\rho)\right\|,
   \]
   and for a conjugacy-supported measure or certified pair measure, bound this by normalized character averages.

2. **Inject quasirandomness.**
   Use the lower bound on nontrivial irreducible dimensions from the `Sp₄` catalog / literature interface to control the Diaconis–Shahshahani sum.

3. **Consume Deligne–Lusztig certificate.**
   Show that a uniform `C/q` character-ratio bound yields a uniform spectral gap after summing over irreducibles.

**Why this is most promising:** it isolates the deepest missing ingredient into a reusable certificate. Even if full Deligne–Lusztig formalization is incomplete, the architecture still produces major theorems and a robust foundation for later geometry.

### Strategy B — Explicit conjugacy class analysis inside `Sp₄(𝔽_q)`
1. Classify the regular semisimple torus types relevant to `Sp₄`.
2. For each torus type, derive explicit character-ratio bounds from known formulas.
3. Push these formulas directly into the random walk operator estimate.

**Why it is valuable:** this could produce sharper constants and a more concrete theorem for small `q`.  
**Why it is harder:** the case split burden is high, and Lean may resist direct encoding of all torus-type combinatorics unless you aggressively modularize.

### Strategy C — Comparison with Hecke operators / spherical functions
1. Model the walk generated by a toral element and a companion generator as a low-rank Hecke-type operator.
2. Compare its spectrum with spherical function bounds on the finite symplectic building.
3. Transfer building expansion to the Cayley graph.

**Why this is exciting:** it opens a bridge to automorphic forms and finite buildings.  
**Why it is risky now:** the formal infrastructure for Hecke algebras/buildings may be thinner than what the catalog currently supports.

**Recommendation:** pursue **Strategy A first**, use B for explicit constants and computational validation, and keep C as a visionary extension in `FUTURE_DIRECTIONS.md`.
