## Precise Theorem Statement

Let `T_N` be the finite truncation to depth `N` of the Berggren tree generated from `(3,4,5)` by the three standard Berggren matrices. Let `W_≤ N` denote words over `{1,2,3}` of length at most `N`, identified with vertices of `T_N`. Let `R` be a commutative semiring or ring suitable for finite linear combinations, and let `M_N := (T_N →₀ R)` be the finitely supported signal module on vertices.

Define branch-extension operators
- `U_i : M_N →ₗ[R] M_N` for `i ∈ {1,2,3}`,
given by truncated pushforward along the `i`-th child map,
and define Hecke/correspondence operators
- `H_w : M_N →ₗ[R] M_N`
by averaging over descendants/extensions with prefix/suffix constraints determined by a word `w`.

Introduce a residue invariant
- `ρ_K : T_N → Res_K`
for some finite congruence target extracted from `(a,b,c) mod K`,
and let `M_N[ξ]` be the submodule of signals supported on a fixed residue block `ξ : Res_K`.

Define a class of multiplicative triple-characters
- `χ : T_N → S`
factoring through branch word residue data and/or congruence data of `(a,b,c)`,
such that on each residue block they diagonalize the commuting family `{H_w}`.

### Main theorem package

You should aim to formalize a theorem of the following shape:

1. **Residue-block invariance and commutation**
   For a suitable finite family of correspondence operators `H_w` (for words `w` in a chosen finite admissible set), each `H_w` preserves residue blocks `M_N[ξ]`, and on each block these operators pairwise commute.

2. **Canonical character decomposition on each block**
   For each residue block `M_N[ξ]`, there exists a finite family of triple-characters `Χ_ξ` such that every `χ ∈ Χ_ξ` is a simultaneous eigenvector for all `H_w`, and these characters span the branch-periodic submodule of `M_N[ξ]`.

3. **Moment separation / injectivity**
   If `f,g` are branch-periodic signals of bounded branch complexity on `M_N[ξ]`, and
   \[
   \forall \chi \in Χ_ξ,\quad \mathrm{moment}(f,\chi)=\mathrm{moment}(g,\chi),
   \]
   then `f = g`.

4. **Certified period reconstruction**
   There exists a finite reconstruction procedure which, given the finite character moments of a branch-periodic signal `f`, recovers:
   - the minimal branch period,
   - the support pattern inside the truncated tree,
   - and the coefficients of `f`,
   with a proof certificate of uniqueness.

## Most Promising Mathematical Model

The most viable formal route is to represent a vertex by its generating branch word, with evaluation to the actual triple as a secondary map. This separates:
- **tree combinatorics** from
- **arithmetic residue data**.

Then define the spectral theory first on a finite quotient of branch words, and only afterward transport it through the Berggren evaluation map.

This is strategically superior because the Berggren child operators are noncommutative on the full tree, but branch-periodic signals factor through a finite quotient where one can manufacture a commuting correspondence algebra by averaging over word classes. ## 2–3 Proof Strategy Paths

### Strategy A: Finite quotient of branch words + character table
**Most promising.**

1. Define a finite quotient `Q` of words by:
   - depth truncation,
   - branch period equivalence,
   - residue equivalence of the associated triples modulo `K`.
2. Show the chosen `H_w` descend to endomorphisms of functions on `Q`.
3. Prove these endomorphisms commute, hence are simultaneously diagonalizable over `ℂ` or another splitting coefficient ring.
4. Identify eigenvectors as multiplicative characters on the finite quotient monoid/group-like object.
5. Deduce injectivity of moments from invertibility of the finite character table.

Why this is best:
- it reduces the theorem to finite linear algebra;
- it aligns perfectly with existing finite spectral reconstruction infrastructure;
- it is Lean-friendly because all objects become finite types / matrices / finite sums.

### Strategy B: Incidence algebra / correspondence algebra on the truncated tree
1. Define `H_w` as incidence correspondences on prefix-descendant relations in `T_N`.
2. Restrict to residue blocks where the correspondence algebra closes.
3. Show the branch-periodic submodule is a semisimple module for this algebra.
4. Construct characters from one-dimensional modules induced by residue data and branch monoid quotients.

Why useful:
- this is conceptually deepest;
- it makes the “Hecke” terminology mathematically honest;
- it could lead to future Satake-like classification statements on arithmetic trees.

Risk:
- heavier algebraic overhead in Lean.

### Strategy C: Transport from a certified spectral reconstruction theorem
Use the catalog theorem

- `finite_spectral_reconstruction_bridge : theorem finite_spectral_rec`

as the engine.

1. Build a finite spectral object associated to branch-periodic Berggren signals.
2. Verify its hypotheses by proving:
   - finiteness,
   - operator commutation,
   - character separation.
3. Invoke the bridge theorem to obtain reconstruction.
4. Then refine the conclusion to identify the recovered hidden parameter specifically as the minimal branch period.

Why useful:
- maximizes leverage from verified infrastructure;
- likely minimizes `sorry`;
- gives a clean certified algorithmic conclusion quickly.

Best plan overall:
- **Use Strategy A as the main proof architecture**, and
- **use Strategy C as the final reconstruction layer**.
