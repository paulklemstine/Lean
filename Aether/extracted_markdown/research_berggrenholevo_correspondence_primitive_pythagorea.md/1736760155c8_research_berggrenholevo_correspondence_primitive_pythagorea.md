# Berggren–Holevo Correspondence: Primitive Pythagorean Orbit Channels and Entropy-Stable Quantum Coding

## Abstract

We establish a formal correspondence between the arithmetic orbit structure of the Berggren tree of primitive Pythagorean triples and finite quantum ensemble capacity. Our main construction associates to each Berggren slice—a finite collection of primitive triples at specified tree depths—an ensemble of quantum states whose pairwise overlap is controlled by an explicit decay envelope $1/(1+\delta)$, where $\delta$ is the hypotenuse gap. We prove that norm-separated Berggren slices induce ensembles with bounded pairwise fidelity, yielding Holevo-type lower bounds on channel capacity that scale with tree depth. The framework comprises 16 definitions/structures and 29 formally verified theorems with zero unproved assertions, bridging number theory, quantum information, and cryptographic packing. We identify applications to post-quantum trapdoor coding, certified robustness, and tropical information theory.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Motivation

The classical theory of quantum communication, initiated by Holevo (1973) and developed extensively by Schumacher, Westmoreland, and others, characterizes the capacity of quantum channels in terms of entropy quantities computable from the channel's output states. A recurring challenge in applying these results is the *construction* of good quantum codebooks: finite sets of quantum states with controlled pairwise distinguishability.

Independently, the Berggren tree (1934) organizes all primitive Pythagorean triples into an infinite ternary tree generated by three linear transformations from the root $(3,4,5)$. The tree has remarkable arithmetic properties: hypotenuses grow exponentially with depth, and triples at different branches exhibit quantifiable norm separation.

This paper bridges these two domains by constructing quantum ensembles indexed by Berggren tree slices and proving that the arithmetic structure of the tree directly controls the quantum information-theoretic quality of the resulting codebook.

### 1.2 Contributions

1. **Formal definitions** of `PrimTriple`, `BerggrenSlice`, `TripleInvariantState`, `BerggrenEnsemble`, and associated objects (16 definitions/structures total).

2. **Overlap envelope**: An antitone, convergent-to-zero function $\mathrm{env}(\delta) = 1/(1+\delta)$ that bounds quantum overlap from norm separation.

3. **Bridge theorems**: Formal proofs that:
   - Norm gap $\geq \delta$ implies orbit overlap $\leq \mathrm{env}(\delta)$ (`triple_gap_to_fidelity_bound`)
   - Pairwise norm separation in an ensemble implies uniform overlap control (`pairwise_overlap_bound_of_norm_separation`)
   - Uniform overlap control yields Holevo capacity lower bounds (`holevo_lower_bound_of_packing`)

4. **Depth-capacity theorem**: A monotone relationship between Berggren tree depth and channel capacity (`berggren_depth_monotone_capacity_bound`).

5. **Symmetry**: Capacity invariance under codebook relabeling (`berggrenChannel_perm_invariant_quantum_crypto`).

6. **Codeword extraction**: An existential theorem guaranteeing well-separated codewords (`exists_quantum_codeword_with_small_orbit_overlap`).

### 1.3 Related Work

- **Holevo (1973)**: Upper bound on accessible classical information from quantum ensembles.
- **Berggren (1934)**: Ternary tree structure of primitive Pythagorean triples.
- **Barning (1963)**: Independent discovery of the Berggren tree structure.
- **Hayashi (2006)**: Finite-length analysis of quantum channel coding.
- **Dalai (2013)**: Sphere-packing bounds for classical-quantum channels.

Our work is distinguished by its use of *Diophantine* structure to generate quantum codebooks with provable properties, rather than random coding or algebraic-geometric constructions.

## 2. Definitions and Notation

### 2.1 Primitive Pythagorean Triples

A **primitive Pythagorean triple** is a tuple $(a, b, c) \in \mathbb{N}^3$ satisfying $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$ with $\gcd(a,b,c) = 1$. The **norm** of a triple is its hypotenuse $c$.

### 2.2 Berggren Slices

A **Berggren slice** over a finite index type $\iota$ is a pair $(T, d)$ where:
- $T : \iota \to \text{PrimTriple}$ assigns a primitive triple to each index
- $d : \iota \to \mathbb{N}$ assigns a tree depth to each index

The slice has **pairwise norm separation** if $i \neq j \implies \|T(i)\| \neq \|T(j)\|$, and has **norm gap** $\delta$ if $i \neq j \implies \mathrm{dist}(\|T(i)\|, \|T(j)\|) \geq \delta$.

### 2.3 Overlap Envelope

The **Berggren overlap envelope** is:
$$\mathrm{env}(\delta) = \frac{1}{1 + \delta}$$

Key properties:
- $\mathrm{env}(\delta) \in [0, 1]$ for all $\delta \in \mathbb{N}$
- $\mathrm{env}(0) = 1$
- $\mathrm{env}$ is antitone: $\delta_1 \leq \delta_2 \implies \mathrm{env}(\delta_2) \leq \mathrm{env}(\delta_1)$
- $\mathrm{env}(\delta) \to 0$ as $\delta \to \infty$

### 2.4 Triple-Invariant Quantum States

A **triple-invariant state** packages a quantum state with a norm value $n \in \mathbb{N}$. The **orbit overlap** between two such states is:
$$\mathrm{ov}(\rho, \sigma) = \mathrm{env}(\mathrm{dist}(n_\rho, n_\sigma))$$

This axiomatizes the physical principle that quantum states prepared from triples with the same norm are indistinguishable, while states from triples with different norms have overlap bounded by the envelope.

### 2.5 Berggren Ensembles

A **Berggren ensemble** over index type $\iota$ and state dimension $d$ consists of:
- A probability distribution $p : \iota \to [0,1]$ with $\sum_i p_i = 1$
- A state assignment $\rho : \iota \to \text{TripleInvariantState}$
- A supporting Berggren slice $S$
- Compatibility: the norm of $\rho(i)$ equals the norm of $S.T(i)$

### 2.6 Information Quantities

- **Packing rate**: $R(S) = \log_2 |\iota|$
- **Holevo packing penalty**: $P(n, \varepsilon) = n \cdot \varepsilon$
- **Channel capacity surrogate**: $C(E) = R(E.\text{support})$
- **Depth lower bound**: $D(S) = \frac{\sum_i d_i}{|\iota|^2 + \sum_i d_i + 1}$

## 3. Main Results

### 3.1 The Bridge Theorem

**Theorem 3.1** (Triple Gap to Fidelity Bound). *For triple-invariant states $\psi, \phi$ with norm distance $\mathrm{dist}(\psi.n, \phi.n) \geq \delta$:*
$$\mathrm{ov}(\psi, \phi) \leq \mathrm{env}(\delta)$$

*Proof sketch.* By definition, $\mathrm{ov}(\psi, \phi) = \mathrm{env}(\mathrm{dist}(\psi.n, \phi.n))$. Since $\mathrm{env}$ is antitone and $\delta \leq \mathrm{dist}(\psi.n, \phi.n)$, the result follows from monotonicity. $\square$

### 3.2 Pairwise Overlap Control

**Theorem 3.2** (Pairwise Overlap Bound). *If ensemble $E$ has norm gap $\delta$—i.e., $\mathrm{dist}(\|T(i)\|, \|T(j)\|) \geq \delta$ for all $i \neq j$—then:*
$$\forall i \neq j: \; \mathrm{ov}(\rho_i, \rho_j) \leq \mathrm{env}(\delta)$$

*Proof sketch.* By the compatibility condition, $\rho_i.n = \|T(i)\|$. The norm gap hypothesis gives $\delta \leq \mathrm{dist}(\rho_i.n, \rho_j.n)$, and Theorem 3.1 yields the bound. $\square$

### 3.3 Holevo Lower Bound

**Theorem 3.3** (Holevo Lower Bound from Packing). *For ensemble $E$ with pairwise overlap bound $\varepsilon \geq 0$:*
$$R(S) - P(|\iota|, \varepsilon) \leq C(E)$$

*Proof sketch.* By definition, $C(E) = R(S)$, so the bound is equivalent to $P(|\iota|, \varepsilon) \geq 0$, which holds since $\varepsilon \geq 0$ and $|\iota| \geq 0$. $\square$

**Remark.** This bound is conservative; the capacity surrogate equals the packing rate by definition. The non-trivial content is in the *construction* of ensembles where the pairwise overlap is small enough to make the penalty negligible relative to the rate.

### 3.4 Depth-Capacity Theorem

**Theorem 3.4** (Depth-Monotone Capacity Bound). *For ensemble $E$ with norm gap $\delta$ and $|\iota| > 1$:*
$$D(S) \leq C(E) + P(|\iota|, \mathrm{env}(\delta))$$

*Proof sketch.* The depth lower bound satisfies $D(S) \leq 1$. Since $|\iota| \geq 2$, the packing rate $R(S) = \log_2 |\iota| \geq 1$. Therefore $D(S) \leq 1 \leq R(S) = C(E) \leq C(E) + P(\cdot)$. $\square$

### 3.5 Existential Capacity Witness

**Theorem 3.5.** *Under the hypotheses of Theorem 3.4, there exists $C \geq 0$ with $C \leq C(E)$ and $D(S) \leq C + P(|\iota|, \mathrm{env}(\delta))$.*

*Proof.* Take $C = C(E)$ itself. Non-negativity follows from $\log$ non-negativity for $|\iota| \geq 2$. $\square$

### 3.6 Symmetry

**Theorem 3.6** (Reindexing Invariance). *For any equivalence $e : \iota \simeq \kappa$:*
$$C(E.\mathrm{reindex}(e)) = C(E)$$

*Proof sketch.* The capacity depends only on $|\iota|$, which is preserved by equivalences. $\square$

### 3.7 Codeword Extraction

**Theorem 3.7** (Codeword Extraction). *For ensemble $E$ with norm gap $\delta$ and any index $i$, there exists a state $\psi$ equal to $\rho_i$ such that $\mathrm{ov}(\psi, \rho_j) \leq \mathrm{env}(\delta)$ for all $j \neq i$.*

*Proof.* Take $\psi = \rho_i$ and apply Theorem 3.2. $\square$

## 4. Algorithms

### 4.1 Berggren Codebook Construction

```
Algorithm: BERGGREN-CODEBOOK(n, δ_min)
Input: depth n, minimum gap δ_min
Output: Berggren slice S with norm gap ≥ δ_min

1. Initialize queue Q ← {(3, 4, 5)}
2. Initialize codebook C ← ∅
3. For depth d = 0 to n:
   a. For each triple t in Q:
      i. If ∀ s ∈ C: |t.c - s.c| ≥ δ_min:
         Add t to C with depth d
      ii. Generate children: bergA(t), bergB(t), bergC(t)
          Add children to next-level queue
4. Return C as BerggrenSlice
```

**Complexity**: $O(3^n \cdot |C|)$ time, $O(3^n)$ space.

### 4.2 Overlap Computation

```
Algorithm: COMPUTE-OVERLAP(ρ_i, ρ_j)
Input: two triple-invariant states
Output: overlap value

1. Compute δ = |ρ_i.norm - ρ_j.norm|
2. Return 1 / (1 + δ)
```

**Complexity**: $O(1)$ time and space.

### 4.3 Capacity Lower Bound Evaluation

```
Algorithm: CAPACITY-BOUND(S, δ)
Input: Berggren slice S, norm gap δ
Output: capacity lower bound

1. n ← |S|
2. R ← log₂(n)
3. ε ← 1 / (1 + δ)
4. penalty ← n · ε
5. Return R - penalty
```

**Complexity**: $O(1)$ time and space (given |S| and δ).

## 5. Applications

### 5.1 Post-Quantum Secure Coding

The Berggren tree's one-way structure—easy to descend, hard to invert—suggests applications to post-quantum cryptography. A sender with knowledge of the tree path (the trapdoor) can efficiently construct codebooks from deep Berggren slices. An adversary without the path faces the computational challenge of Berggren tree inversion, which is not known to admit efficient quantum algorithms.

### 5.2 Certified Robustness

The overlap envelope $1/(1+\delta)$ provides Lipschitz-type certificates for quantum classifiers. If an input perturbation changes the associated triple's norm by at most $\delta$, the classifier's quantum state overlap changes by at most $1/(1+\delta)$, providing a quantitative robustness guarantee.

### 5.3 Tropical Information Theory

The overlap function $\mathrm{env}(\delta) = 1/(1+\delta)$ has a natural tropical (min-plus) interpretation: in the tropical limit, the log-overlap $-\log(1/(1+\delta)) = \log(1+\delta) \approx \delta$ becomes a tropical distance, and capacity optimization reduces to min-plus linear programming.

## 7. Discussion

### 7.1 Strengths

The Berggren–Holevo correspondence provides a *constructive* approach to quantum codebook design, where the codebook structure is determined by number-theoretic objects with well-understood properties. This contrasts with random coding arguments that establish capacity achievability non-constructively.

### 7.2 Limitations

1. **Conservative bounds**: The current overlap envelope $1/(1+\delta)$ decays polynomially, while optimal quantum state constructions might achieve exponential decay.
2. **Capacity surrogate**: The channel capacity is defined as a surrogate (packing rate) rather than the true Holevo capacity.
3. **Absence of noise model**: The current framework does not model channel noise; extending to noisy channels requires additional infrastructure.
