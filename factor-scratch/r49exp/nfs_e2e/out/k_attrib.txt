Model, from P(p^k | a^2-b^3) = alpha_p / p^k with alpha_p = 2 - 1/p for k >= 2 and
= 1/p at k = 1 (p = 2 measured separately):
    P(e=0) = 1 - alpha/p^2
    P(e=1) = 1/p - alpha/p^2
    P(e>=2) = alpha(1-1/p)/p^e
  so the density RATIO true/uniform is 1 at e=0,  (p-alpha)/(p-1)  at e=1,  and
  exactly alpha = 2-1/p at EVERY e >= 2.  A ratio that is flat in e >= 2 is the
  signature of the paper's law; any deviation is the zero-zero subspace or a
  small-prime structure effect.

IS THE k>=6 GAIN THE ZERO-ZERO SUBSPACE?
==============================================================================
For a relation whose largest factor-base exponent is k, the paper's section 4 says the
k >= 6 excess over 2-1/p comes from a = 0 (mod p^ceil(k/2)) and b = 0 (mod p^ceil(k/3)).
We take the relations with max exponent >= 6, find WHICH prime attains it, and test
the zero-zero configuration directly on the (a,b) that produced them.

  relations with max FB exponent >= 6:  2,889 of 13,818
    attributed prime: p=2:2,189, p=3:563, p=5:92, p=7:37, p=11:5, p=13:1, p=19:2
    zero-zero configuration holds for 1,694 of 2,889 (58.6%)

  For reference, under a UNIFORM model a = 0 mod p^3 has probability p^-3 and
    p=2: P(p^3 | a and p^2 | b) = 3.125e-02  -> expected hits 90.28
