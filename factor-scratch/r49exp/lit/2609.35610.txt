ON THE LARGEST PRIME FACTORS OF p − 1 AND p + 1

arXiv:2609.35610v1 [math.NT] 28 Sep 2026

GENHENG ZHAO
Abstract. We prove that each of the inequalities P + (p+1) > P + (p−1)
and P + (p − 1) > P + (p + 1) holds for a positive proportion of primes.
The argument combines a logarithmic balance identity with a uniform
Selberg sieve over a thin cofactor strip. The same transfer mechanism
applies to either shift and converts a one-sided large-prime-factor reservoir into an unconditional ordering result in both directions.

1. Introduction
For n > 1, let P + (n) denote its largest prime factor, and set P + (1) = 1.
All logarithms are natural. We write

The comparison of largest prime factors at neighbouring arguments has a
long history. The Erdős–Turán conjecture predicts that P + (n) < P + (n + 1)
holds for a set of integers of asymptotic density 1/2. Erdős and Pomerance
[3] established a positive lower density in 1978. Subsequent quantitative
refinements include the published bound 0.2017 of Lü and Wang [6]. In a
different direction, Teräväinen [9, Theorem 1.16] proved that the logarithmic
density is 1/2, and Tao–Teräväinen [8, Corollary 1.16] obtained the ordinary
density 1/2 at all scales outside an exceptional set of logarithmic density
zero. Neither statement asserts the ordinary density limit at every scale.
These results concern an unrestricted integer variable; restricting the middle argument to primes introduces an additional distribution problem and
does not follow by restriction of an integer-density estimate. For the primevariable ordering considered here, Wang proved a positive-proportion result
under the Elliott–Halberstam conjecture [11]; his survey [10] discusses the
prime-variable problem. Our purpose is to obtain unconditional positiveproportion conclusions in both directions.

A second feature is that the sieve estimate is uniform in cofactors which
grow with x. We apply a classical Selberg upper-bound theorem whose constants depend only on fixed local sieve conditions, and verify those conditions
and the remainder uniformly. Thus no prime-tuple asymptotic or unverified
dependence on growing coefficients is needed. Combining the two controls
and choosing a small fixed η completes the transfer.
For comparison, Yang’s recent preprint [12, Theorem 1.6] treats Py+ (n),
the largest prime factor of n not exceeding y. It gives a positive-proportion
ordering result for shifted primes when y = xα and 0 < α < 17/32, which
does not directly imply the untruncated ordering considered here. Cofactor
sieves also occur in that work; the transfer above relies on their combination
with logarithmic balance and a thin strip.
