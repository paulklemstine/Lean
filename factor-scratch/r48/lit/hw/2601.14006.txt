COMPUTING CRYSTALLINE COHOMOLOGY AND p-DIVISIBLE
GROUPS FOR CURVES OVER FINITE FIELDS

arXiv:2601.14006v1 [math.NT] 20 Jan 2026

JEREMY BOOHER

Abstract. Let X be a smooth projective curve over a finite field of characteristic p. We describe and implement a practical algorithm for computing
the p-divisible group Jac(X)[p∞ ] via computing its Dieudonné module, or
equivalently computing the Frobenius and Verschiebung operators on the first
crystalline cohomology of X. We build on Tuitman’s p-adic point counting
algorithm, which computes the rigid cohomology of X and requires a “nice”
lift of X to be provided.

1. Introduction
Let X be a smooth projective curve of genus g over a finite field Fq with q =
pr . The p-divisible group Jac(X)[p∞ ] is built out of the finite flat group schemes
Jac(X)[pn ]. It determines the zeta function, Newton polygon, and Ekedahl-Oort
type of X, and is a geometric generalization of the p-part of the class group of
Fq (X). Indeed, the p-primary part of the class group is the Fq -points of the (not
necessarily étale) Jac(X)[p∞ ].
Equivalently, one can consider the first crystalline cohomology H1crys (X). Set
Zq = W (Fq ), the unique unramified extension of Zp with residue field Fq , and let
Qq be the field of fractions. Then H1crys (X) is a free Zq -module of rank 2g and is
equipped with Frobenius and Verschiebung endomorphisms F and V . It is naturally
isomorphic to the Dieudonné module of Jac(X)[p∞ ], so determining H1crys (X) with
the actions of F and V is equivalent to determining the p-divisible group. (See
Section 3 for a review of the cohomology theories and p-divisible groups.)
We introduce an efficient and practical algorithm for computing H1crys (X). More
precisely, given an integer N we compute the Dieudonné module of Jac(X)[pN ], or
equivalently matrices for F and V on H1crys (X) with p-adic precision N . We build
on p-adic point counting algorithms which work by approximating the Frobenius
morphism on the rigid cohomology of X, in particular work of Tuitman [Tui16,
Tui17]. Tuitman works with a plane curve model Q(x, y) = 0 for X, and exploits
the projection maps to the coordinate axes.
Our main contribution is to realize the crystalline cohomology as the de Rham

for a version of the point counting algorithm which works around it.

5.2. How Much Precision is Needed? Computationally, all p-adic numbers are
represented by integer or rational approximations. Tuitman’s algorithm approximates a lift of the Frobenius map on overconvergent functions and then the matrix
[F ]rig for the Frobenius on the rigid cohomology of X. All of these approximations
are done modulo a fixed power pNint : here Nint is the internal precision of Tuitman’s
algorithm. We begin by reviewing a few ideas about p-adic precision.
As discussed in Remark 3.1, we can rewrite elements of Bcrys as a linear combination of the elements of Brig using the inclusion from Fact 3.6. This also involves
working modulo pNint , and gives us a matrix C ∈ Mat2g,2g (Qq ) representing the
change of basis from Bcrys to Brig . Understanding how accurate the computed
values for [F ]crys and C are based on Nint is essential.
When approximating a p-adic number, the absolute precision is a if the computed value is correct modulo pa , i.e. if the valuation of the difference between the
computed and correct values is known to be at least a. Thus we speak of computing

a p-adic matrix is the minimum precision (resp. valuation) of the entries.

There are two reasonable approaches to addressing the error regarding precision
caused by Λrig and Λ′rig differing.
(1) Correctly compute a basis for Λ′rig and use that basis as Brig throughout
the implementation of the algorithm.
(2) Use the current basis Brig for Λrig and account for the loss of precision
caused by denominators in the entries of pr.
Both are reasonable: we choose to implement the second in order to make
this work more self-contained, treating Tuitman’s algorithm and implementation
as black boxes as much as possible.
Remark 5.11. An additional reason to choose the second approach is that it is
non-trivial to actually compute a basis for Λ′rig . As explained in [Tui16, §4.1], this
is a problem in linear algebra over Zq , and can be approached using Smith normal
form like some of the questions in Section 4. However, especially when working with
approximations to p-adic numbers this is tricky to do, and an incorrect workaround
in [Tui19] is what caused the underlying issue that Λrig ̸= Λ′rig .
The fundamental mathematical problem is that for a matrix A with coefficients
in a domain R with field of fractions K, computing a K-basis the kernel of A does
not necessarily give a R-basis for the kernel even if the coefficients of the basis
elements lie in R.
As discussed in Section 4, in practice we compute Bcrys over a number field.
Tuitman’s implementation has the option to either compute Brig over a number
field or to compute a p-adic approximation: we choose the former option. This
is important as otherwise Brig and vp (pr) can (and do) depend on the internal
precision Nint . These precomputations allow us to know γ1 , γ2 , γ3 (Notation 5.2)
before selecting an internal precision Nint and computing [F ]rig , which is the most
expensive step.
