                                                   Abstract. Let n > 1 be an integer such that X0 (n) has genus 0, and let K be a field of char-
                                                   acteristic 0 or relatively prime to 6n. In this article, we explicitly classify the isogeny graphs
                                                   of all rational elliptic curves that admit a non-trivial isogeny over Q. We achieve this by
                                                   introducing 56 parameterized families of elliptic curves Cn,i (t, d) defined over K(t, d), which
                                                   have the following two properties for a fixed n: the elliptic curves Cn,i (t, d) are isogenous
                                                   over K(t, d), and there are integers k1 and k2 such that the j-invariants of Cn,k1 (t, d) and
                                                   Cn,k2 (t, d) are given by the Fricke parameterizations. As a consequence, we show that if E
                                                   is an elliptic curve over a number field K with isogeny class degree divisible by n ∈ {4, 6, 9},
                                                   then there is a quadratic twist of E that is semistable at all primes p of K such that p ∤ n.

Theorem 1. Let n > 1 be an integer such that X0 (n) has genus 0 and let K be a field of
characteristic 0 or relatively prime to 6n. Suppose further that E1 and E2 are n-isogenous
elliptic curves over K. If the j-invariants of E1 and E2 are not both identically 0 or 1728,
then there exists a t ∈ K and d ∈ K × /(K × )2 such that Ei is K-isomorphic to Cn,ki (t, d) (see
Table 5). Moreover, the isogeny class of Ei contains {[Cn,i (t, d)]K }i .
   The construction of these families was motivated by work on explicit isogenies of prime
degree by Cremona, Elkies, Watkins, and Tsukazaki [8, 9, 38]. One motivation of these works
was the following question: given an elliptic curve E1 over a field K such that E1 admits
a K-rational isogeny of prime degree ℓ, how does one compute the Weierstrass model of
the ℓ-isogenous elliptic curve over K? One answer is given through Elkies’s Algorithm [13,
Chapter 25, Algorithm 28], which outputs the ℓ-isogenous elliptic curves to E1 /K so long as
ℓ > 2 and K is a field having characteristic 0 or greater than ℓ + 2.
   In their preprint [8], Cremona and Watkins replaced the analytic methods of Elkies with
algebraic methods in the case when X0 (ℓ) has genus 0. Namely, for a field K of characteristic
not equal to 2, 3, ℓ, they used the Fricke parameterizations to construct elliptic curves Eℓ (t)
over K(t) (notation as in [38]) such that the j-invariant of Eℓ (t) is jℓ,1 (t). They then computed
the kernel polynomial of a K-rational ℓ-isogeny admitted by Eℓ (t).
   In his thesis [38], Tsukazaki continued the study of Eℓ (t) and presented an algorithm for
computing the ℓ-isogenous elliptic curves for a given elliptic curve E1 over some field K with
characteristic not equal to 2, 3, ℓ. Specifically, the algorithm solves for solutions in K to the
equation jℓ,1 (t) = j(E1 ). For a solution t0 , the algorithm outputs a U ∈ K such that E1
is K-isomorphic EℓU (t0 ), where EℓU (t0 ) is the quadratic twist of Eℓ (t0 ) by U . We note that
special care must be taken when j(E1 ) = 0, 1728, since Eℓ (t0 ) is a singular curve. By means
of the kernel polynomial of the ℓ-isogeny of Eℓ (t0 ) and Kohel’s formula [25], one then obtains
an isogenous elliptic curve E2 such that j(E2 ) = jℓ,2 (t0 ). A consequence of our work is that
Kohel’s formulas are no longer required, and in addition, we obtain a subset of the K-isogeny
class of E1 upon computing t0 , U . For the precise statements, see Section 6.
   We note that Theorem 1 is also of theoretical interest, as it asserts that for an integer
n > 1 with X0 (n) having genus 0, the study of n-isogenous elliptic curves whose j-invariants
are not both identically 0 or 1728 over a field K of characteristic 0 or relatively prime to
6n is equivalent to understanding the parameterized families {Cn,i (t, d)}i . In Section 4, we
showcase this reasoning to prove the following result:
Theorem 2. Let K be a number field. If E/K is an elliptic curve with isogeny class degree
divisible by n ∈ {4, 6, 9}, then there exists a quadratic twist E d of E with the property that
E d is semistable at all primes p of K such that p ∤ n.
Theorem 2 is analogous to results about elliptic curves with a torsion point. Frey [11] showed
that if E/K has a torsion point of prime order ℓ ≥ 5, then E is semistable at all primes p of K
such that p ∤ ℓ. In [2], the author showed that if E has a torsion point of order n ∈ {6, 8, 9},
then E is semistable at all primes p of K such that p ∤ n. We note that the n = 6 case was
originally due to Flexor and Oesterlé [10].

                     6. Algorithms for Computing Isogeny Classes
   Let ℓ be a prime such that X0 (ℓ) has genus 0. Suppose further that K is a field of
characteristic not equal to 2, 3, or ℓ. For an elliptic curve E defined over K, Tsukazaki

to E over K. The algorithm builds on the work of Cremona and Watkins by computing
the kernel polynomial of a K-rational ℓ-isogeny admitted by E. The Weierstrass model of
the ℓ-isogenous elliptic curves are then obtained via Kohel’s formulas [25]. We note that
Algorithms 2, 3, and 4 of Tsukazaki correspond to the cases when j(E) 6= 0, 1728, j(E) = 0,
and j(E) = 1728, respectively.
