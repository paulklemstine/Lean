# r112 / stange_kernel — the true success rate of the Stange Q-kernel method

**Date:** 2026-10-04. **Axis:** re-measure, from a minimal self-contained
implementation, the success rate of Stange's Q-kernel factoring method
(arXiv:2211.06821, Algorithm 2.2) on RSA moduli, with ground truth.

## Claim

**The method's success rate is the classical order-finding constant
`P = 20/27 = 0.7407`, not a property of the Q-kernel. The Q-kernel supplies a
multiple of `ord(g)` and nothing else; success is *exactly*
`P(v2(ord_p g) != v2(ord_q g))` and is statistically independent of the index
`h = G/ord(g)`, of `b`, of `c`, and of `n`.**

## Falsifier

The claim is dead if any of the following holds:

- **F1** — success is NOT predicted by the v2-law. Measured 200/200 at 2^26 and
  135/135 in the main battery; F1 **survived the attempt to kill it**.
- **F2** — success rate depends on `h = G/ord(g)`, i.e. the linear algebra does
  carry information the order trick does not. Measured flat (109/145 at h=1
  through 1/1 at h=40). F2 **survived the attempt to kill it**.
- **F3** — the measured rate is significantly different from 20/27. Measured
  108/135 = 0.800, **z = +1.57**. F3 **survived the attempt to kill it**.
- **F4** — the method does not transfer at all (rate ~0). F4 **died**: it does
  factor, ~80% per attempt. *(This is the r48 result, re-confirmed here.)*

## What was implemented, and one correction to the task brief

The task brief paraphrased the method as "a linear form `l(m) = alpha*m + beta`
over a multiplicative subgroup generated by `g`, where `g^alpha * m mod N` is
small", with a subgroup of order `l` obtained by taking an `l`-th root of unity.

**That is not Stange's method.** Stange takes no `l`-th root of unity, forms no
subgroup of prescribed order, and contains no "smallness" test anywhere in
Algorithm 2.2. `core.py` implements the published method: a `b x (b+c)` relation
matrix over the factor base, the right kernel over **Q**, `alpha_t = sum_j
(b_t)_j x_j`, `G = gcd(alpha_1..alpha_c)`, then the standard order strip + gcd.
The `l(m) = alpha*m+beta` / "small residue" description appears to be a
conflation with a different (index-calculus-flavoured) construction; if that
specific variant is wanted it is a separate, untested axis and is **not**
measured here.

## What distinguishes success from failure

**Answer: nothing about the linear algebra.** Success is predicted *perfectly*
by the v2-law and carries no information about `h`.

From `diagnose`, 2^26, b=8, c=5, 200 trials, seed 20261004: **152/200 = 0.760**,
and:

- `verified == v2_differ` in **200/200** trials. Perfect prediction.
- Success rate **by h**: h=1 → 109/145 (0.752); h=2 → 15/21; h=3 → 14/15;
  h=4 → 4/7; h=5 → 2/3; h=8/11/16/17/40 → all 1/1. Flat. Even when the kernel
  returns a multiple 40× the true order, factoring still succeeds whenever the
  v2-law says it should — because the strip phase removes `h` before the gcd.
- `h = 1` (the exact-order case, which is what Hypothesis 3.1 is about) occurs
  145/200 = 0.725 and is **uncorrelated with success**.

So `h` — the quantity r48 spent a round refuting Hypothesis 3.1 about — is
**irrelevant to whether the method factors.** The mechanism is arithmetic:
striping a multiple of `ord(g)` down to `ord(g)` is deterministic, and the gcd
then splits iff the two prime-power orders differ in their 2-adic valuation,
which is the classical `P = 20/27`.

## A reproducibility bug found and fixed (worth propagating)

The first attempt seeded per-config substreams with
`random.Random((seed, label).__hash__())`. Python randomizes `str`/`tuple`
hashing per process (PYTHONHASHSEED), so **two "same-seed" passes disagreed:
28/40 vs 33/40 on the first config.** Replaced with a sha256-derived substream
(`substream()`), and T9 was added to catch it. All counts above are post-fix.

This is the FANOUT_BRIEF §1.3 trap in a form not yet on the list. The r48 code
is **not** affected (it used plain integer seeds, `grep` confirms no
`__hash__()` anywhere under `factor-scratch/r48/`), so the 181/240 headline is
unaffected.

## Honest verdict

**SUPPORTED — the ~75-80% figure is real but is misattributed.** On RSA moduli
of 20–40 bits the Stange Q-kernel method factors at **108/135 = 80.0%** per
attempt (twice, seeded, ground-truth-checked, negative-controlled), which is
statistically indistinguishable from the classical **20/27 = 74.07%**
(z = +1.57). It does not transfer to cryptographically meaningful sizes: the
proved regime caps `b` at ~27 for n = 10^20 (the r48 regime gap, unre-measured
here because it is not reachable).

**The Q-kernel contributes no success probability.** It is an expensive way to
compute *a* multiple of `ord(g)`; the plain "strip and gcd" that every order
routine already ends with delivers the same 20/27. The linear-algebra phase —
the entire novel content of the paper — is **irrelevant to factoring success**
and should not be credited with the 75%. The author's own observation that she
"could not find this variation in the literature" may still be true and worth
something; but 75% is not evidence for it.

**NOT CLAIMED:** anything at RSA scale (untested, and the regime gap says
untestable by this method); that the method is new; any asymptotic advantage.
