1. Proof Strategy Bias
The prompt directs specific proof methods, excluding entire mathematical traditions.
Directive: "Use concrete types (Nat, Real, Finset, Matrix)"
Use concrete types (Nat, Real, Finset, Matrix). Avoid trivial tautologies.
Steers away from abstract algebra, category theory, type-theoretic approaches, and constructive mathematics that doesn't use these specific types. "Concrete" is itself a philosophical stance — much of modern math is abstract.
Directive: "If a direct proof fails, try the contrapositive, a constructive witness, or structural induction"
Excludes: proof by coinduction, categorical methods (adjunctions, universal properties), homotopy-theoretic proofs, computational reflection, tactic-based automation, probabilistic methods, and topological arguments.
Directive: "At least 3 theorems with deep proof tactics: induction, rcases, by_contra, field_simp, or multi-step calc"
Equates "deep" with specific Lean tactics. A theorem proved by a single elegant `simp` call that required a novel `@[simp]` lemma is arguably deeper than one forced through `rcases`. The metric confuses syntactic proof length with mathematical depth.
2. Domain & Connection Bias
Hardcoded examples bias research toward specific domain connections.
Example: "Domain Bridges: NumberTheory <-> Tropical, Algebra <-> Physics"
**Domain Bridges**: NumberTheory <-> Tropical, Algebra <-> Physics
These specific examples anchor Aristotle's thinking toward tropical math and physics connections even when the research direction is about something entirely different. They're presented as if they're the "right" kind of bridges.
Catalog reference priority: "FINAL/ entries are vetted, high-quality — prioritize these"
Creates a feedback loop: existing high-quality work gets referenced more, making future work build on the same foundations. Novel directions that don't connect to existing FINAL entries get deprioritized.
3. Anti-Triviality Overcorrection
The rules that prevent trivial output may also suppress elegant simplicity.
Rule: "Do NOT prove commutativity/associativity for standard algebraic structures"
In non-standard settings (quantum groups, tropical semirings, non-associative algebras), commutativity IS the deep result. This rule would suppress proving that a novel structure is commutative — which might be the whole point.
Rule: "If a result seems obvious, prove something STRONGER"
Encourages over-engineering. Sometimes the "obvious" result IS the important one (e.g., Fermat's Last Theorem is just "no integer solutions" — simple statement, deep proof). Forcing "stronger" results can produce unnecessarily general theorems that are harder to connect to reality.
Rule: "No proofs that are just `by simp` or `by trivial`"
Some deep results have simple proofs once the right definitions are in place. The Elegance Criterion in mathematics VALUES simple proofs. A great definition that makes a theorem trivial IS the contribution.
4. Output Format Bias
The prompt prescribes exact deliverables rather than letting research direction emerge.
Mandatory: "Lean 4 proofs, FUTURE_DIRECTIONS.md, RESEARCH_PAPER.md, ARTICLE.md, algorithm, demo.py, 1-3 interactive HTML widgets"
Forces every research cycle into the same template. A cycle that discovers a negative result (a conjecture is FALSE) shouldn't need a demo.py. A purely theoretical result may not need an algorithm. The format forces quantity over appropriateness.
"Scientific American style" for ARTICLE.md
Not all research fits a pop-sci narrative. Pure category theory, negative results, and technical advances may be better served by a different voice. The Taboo Topics section correctly bans verification-focused articles, but the style mandate is still constraining.
FUTURE_DIRECTIONS.md: "3-5 directions (1-2 grand_challenge + 2-3 extension)"
Prescribes the exact number and type of directions. What if a cycle only produces 1 deep direction? Or 7 small ones? What if they're all "refutation" directions rather than "grand challenges"? The template forces a specific research posture.
5. Research Model Bias
The prompt treats research as single-shot proof production, not iterative experimentation.
The prompt says: "Discover what matters. Prove what you can. Define what needs defining."
This is a one-shot model: Aristotle gets one prompt, produces one output. There's no mechanism for: generating multiple hypotheses, running experiments to test them, analyzing which failed and why, updating beliefs, iterating. The "team" mode is described but not supported by the infrastructure — it's just text in the prompt, not actual multi-agent coordination.
FUTURE_DIRECTIONS.md says: "Each direction must be self-contained"
While practical for the next cycle, this discourages directions that explicitly BUILD on each other. Real research programs often require a sequence of interdependent steps — you can't test hypothesis B until you've validated A.
The novelty estimate comes from concept.novelty, which was set by the dispatch system
The prompt doesn't include a mechanism for Aristotle to assess novelty independently — it inherits a pre-assigned score. This means the research direction's "novelty" is judged by the dispatcher, not by the researcher who would actually pursue it.
6. Cultural & Structural Biases
Minor but worth noting.
"Soli Deo Gloria"
A Latin religious phrase meaning "Glory to God alone." While historically associated with the Reformation, it's an odd fit for a secular scientific research system. It adds a cultural signal that may be unwelcome or distracting.
Concept description is truncated to 2000 chars, math framing to 1000 chars
Complex research directions may need more space to properly frame their ideas. The truncation could cut off crucial context or motivation, leading Aristotle to pursue a shallower version of what was intended.
"You are Aristotle"
Role-playing as a specific historical figure comes with implicit assumptions about what kind of research "Aristotle" would do. It frames the task as philosophical proof rather than, say, empirical experiment or computational exploration.
